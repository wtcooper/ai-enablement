## Processes (outside-in)
The unit-specific facts are the report path, the specialist's one-time parameter test, 1.5 h hands-on, no review notes and a 2-day senior queue. The rest is outside-in.

| Process | Key steps | Work types |
|---|---|---|
| ITGC operating-effectiveness test (user access recertification; one case) | Request listing via portal; compare to active users; confirm reviewer and date; write up; senior review | coordination-scheduling, review-assurance, document-production |

| Deficiency evaluation and reliance | Classify exceptions; document under AS 1215; AS 2605 where others' work is used | expert-judgment |

Not yet known: how many tests follow this pattern, how many non-ITGC controls are in scope, and how the specialist's validation is recorded.

## Likely bottleneck and waste
Typical for the field, not observed here.
- **Bottleneck:** senior review queue, tighter in busy season (typical; the case showed about 2 days). Evidence-request waits are also typical (#03).
- **Waste:** the case suggests re-proving report completeness is avoided. Possible residual waste is manual copy-paste and tick-marking (#07) and validation that lives with individual specialists (typical: not reusable or recorded).
- **Rework:** the field's main review-note driver is IPE and evidence sufficiency (module section 2). The SOX-only figures (about 30% notes, two-thirds on IPE) are not applied here.
- **Inspection risk:** the practice had two PCAOB comments on ITGC evidence; the line of origin is unknown.

The main opportunity is to make the validated-once pattern explicit, standardised, recorded and lendable to the SOX-only line. Lookup and drafting assistance comes second, and the client-data approval path (3-4 months) gates it. AI secondary review (#10) is parked. Delegate-level agent testing (#05) is only a later target picture; the practice fails the five delegation gates.
