# People and change in the portfolio (cross-cutting)

Read this when a portfolio decision touches people: choosing gatekeepers in a partnership, stopping an item a
sponsor cares about, writing role evolution at `g3`, the redeploy-or-reduce decision, or a user who fears for their
role. The full method is the suite's shared file,
`../../../transformation-advisor/references/method/people-and-change.md` (honest framing §3, role evolution §4,
redeploy or reduce §5, the junior pipeline §6, communicating change §7, a user who fears for their role §8). This
file adds what is specific to running a portfolio.

## Portfolio-specific guidance

**Stopping is about the item, not the people.** Record a Kill with the evidence and the item's reasons; never
attribute it to a named person's performance (rule 1). Say what is reused, so the team's work carries forward.

**Influence without authority.** In a partnership, the people whose time and practice change co-own the firm.
Empson's research, as reviewed by Flood, describes partnership as limiting the use of formal power and names a
"leadership constellation" of senior leaders, practice heads, business-services heads and key influencers
[JOTWELL-EMPSON-2013]. Put practice heads among the gatekeepers, because they control partner and staff time, and
take decisions in the forum with them present, not by memo.

**Turn objections into gate evidence.** Ask each skeptic what result would change their mind and add it to the next
gate's evidence (an inference from the partner sources) [TRI-AIPS-2026].

**Role evolution at `g3`.** Required for team-level and higher items (rule 2): tasks moving, new work, the capacity
choice. The redeploy-or-reduce decision itself is recorded in step 6, at a review, on realised value. Say only what
the firm controls about roles; never reassure vaguely.

**Junior work.** When an item automates tasks juniors learn from, flag it at `g2` and use the shared junior-task
audit (shared file §6).
