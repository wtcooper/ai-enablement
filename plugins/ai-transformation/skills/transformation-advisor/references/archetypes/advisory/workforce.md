advisory and outsourced services. Never set headcount targets for people; describe the evidence and the choices.

## 1. Roles

| Line | Typical roles | Where AI lands first (module §7) |
|---|---|---|
| CAS | Staff and senior accountants, controllers, CAS managers, fractional CFOs, payroll specialists, offshore pods | Coding and reconciliation move to exception review |
| Risk and controls | Internal-audit seniors and managers, SOX testers, IT auditors, risk partners | Test execution and RCM drafting; review grows |

## 2. Leverage and the shape of teams

- **More clients per person is the stated target in CAS.** Pilot defines success for firms using its agents as more
  clients per accountant and less review time per close (CAS `#03`). BST & Co.'s CAS leader went from about 1,700 to
  500-600 billable hours a year while CAS revenue grew 47%, not attributed to AI [JOFA-CASTIPS-2025].
- **The problem statement has shifted.** Woodard reports firms moving from "we can't find people" to "how do we do it
  with fewer people" [WOODARD-TWR-2025].
- **Pyramid to diamond.** Gartner says AI is turning the finance pyramid into a diamond by eliminating entry-level
  roles; that is Gartner's view of client finance functions [GARTNER-FPALEADER-2026]. In FP&A, a CFO of an unnamed global media
  and information company, quoted by KPMG, says a flux-analysis agent took variance commentary from 40 people to five
  reviewers (self-reported) [KPMG-CFOREMODEL-2026].
- **Seniors move up a layer.** CohnReznick expects senior accountants to do more review and client time (CAS `#03`);
  RSM treats its testing agent as a digital employee that can make intern-like mistakes, with auditors reviewing
  completed controls (risk-controls `#06`).
- **No source gives staffing data tied to AI** in transactions, valuation, forensic or private client. Treat statements
  as views, not measurements.

## 3. The junior pipeline: what the data say

| Evidence | Finding | Who measured, scope |
|---|---|---|
| Labour market | Employment of 22-25-year-olds in AI-exposed occupations 19% below trend, mainly through reduced hiring; "early, descriptive" | Stanford Digital Economy Lab with ADP payroll data, all occupations, August 2026 [STANFORD-CANARIES-2026] |
| UK Big Four intake | Graduate schemes down 6-29% from 2023 to 2024 (KPMG 29%, Deloitte 18%, EY 11%, PwC 6%); UK accountancy graduate adverts 44% lower in 2025 than in 2023 (Indeed) | Telegraph figures via Scottish Financial News, UK only; AI, offshoring and cost cutting all cited [SCOTFIN-BIG4-2025] |
| Counter-evidence | All Big Four plan to hire at least 1,000 juniors this year, EY 1,600; advisers cite the 2008 lesson, when cutting intake caused management shortages about five years later | ICAEW, April 2026 [ICAEW-JUNIOR-2026] |
| Expectation | 68% of respondents to an ICAEW mid-tier study expect AI to reduce the need for some early-career roles; it is unclear how much AI caused Big Four cuts | ICAEW, June 2026 (underlying report not opened) [ICAEW-VOICES-2026] |
| Small and mid-sized employers | A third of new accounting and finance hires quit within a year; senior-to-entry hiring ratio 3:1 | BambooHR survey of 1,248 US respondents; the CFO's reading links quits to an unclear entry-level remit [CFOBREW-JUNIORS-2026] |
| Firms' hiring | 77% report at least one hiring struggle; hardest to fill are 1+ years (46%) and 5+ years (42%); 56% say a new entry-level hire takes six months or more to reach full billable capacity | Intuit survey, 725 professionals [INTUIT-ACCTTECH-2026] |
| Postings | Nearly 7% of Big Four postings in 2025 required AI expertise, against under 2% in 2022 | Financial Times analysis via Computerworld [COMPUTERWORLD-FT-2026] |
| Valuation | Speakers at a Big Four panel said firms hire fewer entry-level analysts as AI automates mechanical work | BVWire report of a panel [BVR-KPMGTREND-2026] |
| Internal audit | Functions reporting staff cuts rose from 11% (2024) to 18% (2025); the report cites budgets, not AI | Internal Audit Foundation [IIA-PULSE-2026] |
| Graduates' view | 91% of respondents believe graduates are more likely to join firms using AI | A belief, Karbon survey [KARBON-SOAI-2026] |

**Reading it.** The labour data are not accounting-specific, the intake data are UK-only, and firms say they are
still hiring juniors. No source shows an advisory firm abandoning early-career hiring.

## 4. Offshore and outsourced delivery

- 29% of responding firms offshore; nearly three-quarters of firms with net client fees of $10M or more offshore some
  work (AICPA PCPS and CPA.com survey, as reported) [ACCTODAY-OUTSOURCING-2026].
- 80% of Intuit's respondents outsourced at least one service, and 65% plan to increase [INTUIT-ACCTTECH-2026].
- CohnReznick rebuilt offshore pods around documented processes, AI and onshore review, so offshore output becomes
  review work; mid-level roles are harder to fill (CAS `#15`).
- Bonadio's outsourced accounting leader describes an "evaporation of traditional bookkeepers" [ACCTODAY-BONADIO-2026].
- When making an AI business case, compare against both the onshore and the offshore cost of the work.

## 5. How roles are evolving

- **From keying to reviewing (CAS).** Choi and Xie find accountant time moving from data entry to communication and
  quality assurance, with experienced accountants gaining more (CAS `#01`). A Hiline manager says AI reconciliations
  "flipped" her role to reviewing exceptions (CAS `#02`).
- **From preparer to supervisor, and tester to controls adviser (risk).** KPMG's SOX leader says "the concept of
  auditor reliance is going to go away" (risk-controls `#16`). Jones Lang LaSalle's CAE says auditors who built careers
  on workpapers must accept handing that work to technology [IIA-UPSKILL-2026].
- **From spreading to analysis, if firms keep juniors (transactions).** Opio's CEO and Pease Bell describe junior time
  moving to analysis and challenge; no staffing data exist (transactions module §7).
- **From producing to interpreting (FP&A).** KPMG sees talent needs moving from a reporting factory toward
  interpreting AI output and advising leaders [KPMG-CFOREMODEL-2026].
- **Experts carry new duties (forensic, valuation).** Disclosure, verification logs and readiness to be asked about AI
  use [KTS-EXPERTAI-2026]; "AI literacy" is becoming a required skill and the signing valuer remains responsible
  [BVR-ENDICOTT-2026].
- **Controllers own the tooling (in-house and family offices).** An in-house controller built a ticketing flow for
  scanned mail and invoices (CAS `#14`); a family-office controller posting asks the hire to assess AI-assisted
  tooling (private-client module §7).
- **The relationship stays human.** A fractional CFO says AI makes clients more comfortable with part-time finance
  leadership and makes relationships stickier [CFODIVE-FRACTIONAL-2026]; Kaufman Rossin's family-office director says
  clients still want to talk to a real human [KAUFMAN-FO-2026].

## 6. The apprenticeship question

The question for every line: if AI does the first pass on coding, testing, spreading, tracing or comparables
research, where do juniors learn the judgment that work used to teach? The sources name four risks:

1. **Juniors trust AI too readily.** Junior staff accepted uncertain AI output more, and relying on non-consensus AI
   suggestions can raise error risk (CAS `#01`) [STANFORD-JAR-2026]. Juniors asked to oversee AI too early may approve
   bad output [ICAEW-VOICES-2026].
2. **Skills erode.** A NACVA panel warned of "cognitive debt", where overreliance erodes the skills needed to spot AI
   errors [BVR-COGDEBT-2026]; the FRC names erosion of human knowledge as a risk it did not address [FRC-GENAI-2026].
3. **The reviewer bench thins.** Forvis Mazars US says depleting junior ranks will create holes in executive-level
   insight later, and over-reliance yields people who cannot tell thin from sound work [FORVIS-QOE-2026].
4. **Entry-level remit blurs.** BambooHR's CFO links early quits to an unclear entry-level role as seniors use AI for
   work juniors used to do [CFOBREW-JUNIORS-2026].

**Designs in the sources (none measured).** Juniors review AI output and spot anomalies instead of keying data, per
Robert Half [ICAEW-JUNIOR-2026]. BVR suggests juniors use its grounded assistant first, then seniors review how they
applied the concept (valuation `#04`). Route the full redesign to professional-services-talent-learning-07 and
[professional-services workforce](../professional-services/workforce.md) §6.

## 7. Skills and new roles

- **What firms hire for.** 38% of Intuit's respondents name strategic judgment as the expertise they prioritise most
  and 22% AI fluency; for entry-level signals, technology certifications (53%) and AI, automation and data skills (53%)
  edge CPA status (50%) [INTUIT-ACCTTECH-2026].
- **Hybrid profiles are scarce.** The hardest internal-audit hire is someone who is both auditor and technologist
  [SIKICH-IAPRIORITIES-2026]; KPMG says auditing a language model is not core CPA training [KPMG-SOX2030-2026]; RSM's
  AI-risk posting asks for Python, GenAI skills and AI-audit certifications (risk-controls `#17`).
- **New roles.** Agent owners and AI leads (CAS `#21`, `#22`); citizen data scientists, AI product managers and
  knowledge engineers in finance teams, per Gartner [GARTNER-FPALEADER-2026]; vendors hiring accountants to write agent
  procedures (CAS `#03`).
- **Expertise sold to model builders.** An expert network recruiting for an AI lab offers experienced TAS managers $100 an hour to
  create and evaluate AI-made QoE exhibits and deal slides [GC-ETHOS-JOB-2026]; an AI training-data startup recruits forensic accountants at $75-150 an hour
  [AFTERQUERY-JOB-2026]. Market signals, not firm practice.

## 8. Questions to ask

- "If AI does the first pass on coding, testing or spreading, how will a second-year learn what a wrong answer looks
  like?"
- "Who reviews AI output today, at what seniority, and do you track what they change?"
- "Has your entry-level intake, offshore mix or senior-to-junior ratio changed in the last two years, and why?"
- "Which hybrid roles (agent owner, data lead, AI-risk auditor) do you need, and are you hiring or training for them?"
- "What would your people stop doing, and what would they start, if the plan works?" Use
  [people-and-change.md](../../method/people-and-change.md) for the change conversation.
