# Advisory economics

Last researched 2026-10-21. Use this in Frame and Make the case, and whenever fees, margins, capital or ownership come
up. It covers how advisory and outsourced services make money and how AI pressures that. The firm-wide picture (fee
mix, realisation, partner economics) is in [professional-services economics](../professional-services/economics.md).
Source keys in brackets resolve in [sources.md](sources.md). **No firm discloses fee, realisation or margin data tied
to AI;** every margin figure below is practitioner testimony or a company claim.

## 1. How the business makes money

| Line | How it is usually sold | What the sources say |
|---|---|---|
| CAS | Recurring, mostly fixed monthly fee or subscription, by service level | The JofA states 90% of CAS practices sell fixed-fee recurring services, naming no source [JOFA-CASTIPS-2025] |
| Fractional CFO and controllership | Time-based in the one source | The fractional CFO CFO Dive interviewed bills hourly [CFODIVE-FRACTIONAL-2026] |
| Risk and controls | Co-sourced or outsourced testing hours; readiness and governance projects | 28% of companies used outsourced providers for SOX testing [KPMG-SOX2030-2026] |
| Transactions | A price per QoE or diligence project | List prices from $1,000 (AI-native self-service) to a broker's estimate of $25,000-$200,000 [SHEPI-PRICING-2026; CTACQ-QOE-2026] |
| Valuation | Per report; recurring portfolio and fund marks | Stout and Kroll announced recurring valuation platforms (valuation `#14`) |
| Forensic and disputes | Matter-based expert and investigation work | No pricing evidence found in the sources |
| FP&A and technology advisory | Projects, implementations, managed FP&A | ISG lists outcome-linked commercial structures among leading indicators for finance outsourcing [ISG-FAOFPA-2026] |
| Private client | Outsourced family-office accounting and reporting; advice | 80% of offices outsource mainly for expertise, 31% cite cost (Campden and AlTi via Armanino) [ARMANINO-FOTRENDS-2026] |

**The feeder model.** Withum says its advisory practice grew from a small CAS team to "$70 million in revenue in the
space" in seven years and that advisory "sells best next to CAS" [ACCTODAY-CASKEYS-2026]. No independent dataset
measures whether CAS converts into advisory fees.

## 2. Scale, growth and demand

- **CAS growth.** 85% of 88 responding Top 100 firms reported a boost in CAS [ACCTODAY-BONADIO-2026]. The 2024 CAS
  Benchmark Survey (CPA.com and AICPA PCPS, 200+ respondents) found median growth of 17% and projected CAS revenue to
  double over three years, a forecast [JOFA-CASTIPS-2025]. The 2026 report is due in December 2026 [CPACOM-CASBENCH-2026].
- **Advisory is not a one-way growth story.** In 2025 Top 100 data, growth fell to 4.89%, with the largest firms
  slowing "largely due to declines in advisory services" while smaller firms grew 14-15% [WOODARD-TWR-2025].
- **Client demand, as practitioners see it.** More than half of 725 accounting and bookkeeping professionals report
  higher client demand in every financial category (financial management 61%); 86% expect AI to increase advisory
  capacity in the next 12 months [INTUIT-ACCTTECH-2026].
- **Fractional and interim finance.** Interim C-suite requests are up 151% since 2021 and interim CFO demand 14% year
  on year (Business Talent Group, cited by CFO Dive) [CFODIVE-FRACTIONAL-2026].
- **Middle-market clients' own AI spend.** Among 1,030 AI-using US and Canadian middle-market leaders, 58% plan to
  invest $1M or more this fiscal year and 84% expect spending to rise; among those raising spend, 40% reallocate from
  external consulting [RSM-MMAI-2026; CPAPA-RSMMM-2026]. RSM sells consulting, and the survey does not say who replaces
  that consulting.
- **Governance demand.** Grant Thornton Advisors' CEO says AI governance work is already driving demand and will grow
  as regulators give guidance, a forecast without figures [GT-CBIZ-2026].

## 3. Key metrics and benchmarks

| Metric | Figure | Provenance |
|---|---|---|
| CAS margins | "Over 40%" at some firms; 60-70% gross margin in Woodard's consulting data | Practitioner testimony, unaudited [WOODARD-TWR-2025] |
| Billable hours per CAS person | About 1,700 to 500-600 a year at BST & Co. while CAS revenue grew 47%; not attributed to AI | Self-reported, one firm [JOFA-CASTIPS-2025] |
| Median net hourly rate (all CPA firm work) | $170, up nearly 7% over two years | Bloomberg Law reporting an AICPA survey; not in the AICPA release read [BBLAW-PRICING-2026] |

| Firms that offshore | 29% of responding firms | AICPA PCPS and CPA.com, as reported [ACCTODAY-OUTSOURCING-2026] |

in each module's §5. Ask for the firm's own numbers before quoting any of these.

## 4. Fees and pricing models

**Practice has moved less than intent.**
- In a BILL and NewtonX survey of 200+ firm leaders (late 2025), fixed fee (66%) and hourly (60%) dominated, and 68%
  were ruling out new pricing strategies or undecided; "roughly one in five" of the firms already offering
  subscription or value pricing said AI drove the shift [BILL-AIAMBITION-2026]. Vendor-sponsored.
- The AICPA's 2025 MAP survey reports a "slow but steady increase" in value and fixed pricing, with hourly billing
  declining; the release gives no percentages [AICPA-MAP-2025].
- 69% of 1,000+ US tax professionals anticipate moving to value-based, hybrid or fixed fees as AI commodifies routine
  knowledge [CPACOM-BLUEJ-2026]. Blue J is a vendor; the sample is tax-centred.

**Named-firm moves are pilots, not results.** RKL has onboarded "several dozen" clients on a value-based model and is
piloting it; Wipfli Advisory is adding metrics such as client satisfaction and cycle time; PwC's US tax leader says
time is "becoming less and less of relevance"; KPMG's vice chair of advisory calls value-based pricing around agentic
work "incredibly important" [BBLAW-PRICING-2026].

**Outcome-linked fees.** A firm CEO forecasts advisory fees tied to measurable outcomes and lists what they need:
baselines, target ranges with floors and ceilings, simple formulas and data straight from systems. He notes audit
firms cannot accept contingent fees tied to financial results [BBTAX-SH-2026]. Digits charges firms only for clients
whose books reach 95% "zero-touch"; that is the vendor's price to the firm, not the firm's price to its client
[DIGITS-OUTCOME-2026]. Contingent fees are barred for SEC issuer audit clients and restricted under the AICPA Code for
audit and review clients [SEC-RULE2-01-2026; NYSSCPA-CONTFEE-2015]. A firm needs a rule on which clients are eligible
before it advertises outcome pricing ([guardrails.md](guardrails.md) §4).

**Price points in deals.** Shepi lists a $1,000 self-service QoE and $5,000 done-for-you; a searcher quotes $500 to
$25,000+; a broker page estimates $25,000-$200,000 [SHEPI-PRICING-2026; SEARCHFUNDER-COLUMBIA-2026; CTACQ-QOE-2026].
Forvis Mazars US says an EBITDA adjustment can be worth "many times" the fee [FORVIS-QOE-2026].

## 5. Cost structure, including AI cost

- **People first.** Staff cost dominates; leverage, offshore pods and hiring are in [workforce.md](workforce.md). About
  29% of firms offshore [ACCTODAY-OUTSOURCING-2026]; CohnReznick rebuilt offshore pods around documented processes, AI
  and onshore review (CAS `#15`).
- **Technology stack.** A typical firm runs about 10 apps, and 41% say tools are fully integrated
  [INTUIT-ACCTTECH-2026]. Integration, not model quality, is the blocker the survey names.
- **Firm AI investment (announced, firm-reported).** RSM: $1B multiyear AI and digital-core investment
  [RSM-IMPACT-2026]. Grant Thornton Advisors: $1B over three years [GT-1B-2025]. BDO USA: $1B+ over five years
  [BDO-AI-2025]. Aprio: $300M over five years, with Fieldguide as agent partner [APRIO-FIELDGUIDE-2026]. CBIZ: an
  agent-native platform on Microsoft Foundry, training for 9,500+ people [CBIZ-MICROSOFT-2026].
- **Attribution is hard.** KPMG's vice chair says AI investment costs often cannot be attributed to individual
  projects [BBLAW-PRICING-2026]. Grant Thornton's CEO says scale matters because AI investment is expensive [GT-CBIZ-2026].
- **Vendor terms move cost and risk.** Outcome pricing (Digits) shifts automation risk to the vendor
  [DIGITS-OUTCOME-2026]; vendor failure shifts it back: Botkeeper announced its closure in February 2026 and Xendoo
  bought the platform [CPAPA-XENDOO-2026]. Keep exports and an exit plan.
- **Review is a cost line.** Verification averages 26% of work time in one vendor survey [CFODIVE-DATARAILS-2026]; a
  pilot should measure added review time, not only time saved [JOFA-CASAI-2026].

## 6. Pricing pressure from AI

**Who keeps the saving is the live question.** An RSM partner asks whether halving a report's production time is "all
margin for me or all price reduction for our client" (his illustration, not a measured result) [BBLAW-PRICING-2026].
Under fixed fees or subscriptions the firm keeps the gain until the client renegotiates; in tax matters the IRS OPR
says billing for time not actually spent, or double billing for AI-assisted tasks, may violate Circular 230 section
10.27 depending on the facts, and savings should be passed on openly [IRS-OPR-2026]. That reading covers matters
before the IRS, not advisory work generally. Route the firm's own pricing design to professional-services
scoping-pricing.

**Pressure from clients.** Thomson Reuters reports 78% of corporate clients rate AI-enabled quality from providers as
very important or essential, 6% say most providers deliver it, and 32% will reconsider relationships within 12 months
[TR-FOPPR-2026]. That is stated intent across legal and accounting, not switching. Three in five accountants say
clients ask for proof of AI data protection always or frequently [INTUIT-ACCTTECH-2026]. 41% name trust and liability
as the top reason clients will keep paying for human expertise [INTUIT-ACCTTECH-2026].

**Pressure from cheaper products.** Forvis Mazars US says AI is increasing lower-quality, lower-cost QoE products
[FORVIS-QOE-2026]. AI-native QoE tiers sell at list prices (transactions `#05`); AI-native bookkeeping sells direct to
small businesses (CAS `#16`). Withum's Jim Bourke says larger firms are "coming down market" using AI
[ACCTODAY-CASKEYS-2026].

**Claims of margin gain are unmeasured.** Grant Thornton Advisors' CEO says "we're seeing margin expansion" and share
gains, without figures [GT-CBIZ-2026]. Multiplier says its AI tools more than doubled a 12-person tax firm's margins, a
company claim [TECHCRUNCH-MULTIPLIER-2025]. Listed professional-services shares fell on automation fears earlier in
2026, per Bloomberg Tax [GT-CBIZ-2026].

## 7. Capital, consolidation and ownership

Detail on firm ownership and partner economics is in [professional-services economics](../professional-services/economics.md) §7.
This section covers what changes for advisory.

- **Scale.** IFAC counts 177 direct PE investments facilitating 875 roll-up acquisitions, 1,052 transactions in
  2015-2025; in 2025 each direct investment produced 7.6 further deals, four times the 2021 level. More than half of PE
  transactions do not involve firms offering audit or assurance [CPAPA-IFACPE-2026]. CPA Trendlines tracks 525
  events, 473 acquired firms, about 72 platforms and about $49B of enterprise value on about $24B revenue to
  2026-05-31, calling its count "a floor, not a ceiling" [CPATRENDLINES-TRACKER-2026].
- **What buyers are buying.** In April 2026 deals tilted toward capabilities such as forensic, valuation,
  cybersecurity and IT transformation (EisnerAmper buying KLG Business Valuators & Forensic Accountants; J.S. Held buying CMM; Sikich buying
  Jefferson Wells U.S.). The tracker reads this as PE-backed platforms buying "defensible advisory niches"; that
  reading is the tracker's [CPATRENDLINES-TRACKER-2026].
- **Capital to fund AI.** Grant Thornton Advisors' announced $5B CBIZ acquisition is expected to close in Q4 2026; its
  CEO ties it to funding AI and training [GT-CBIZ-2026]. Crowe closed KKR's investment on 2026-08-07; Crowe LLP stays
  the licensed attest firm and Crowe Advisory LLC provides non-attest services [CROWE-KKR-2026].
- **AI-native roll-ups.** Current (formerly Crete) reports $428M revenue, 67 offices and No. 28 on the 2026 Top 100,
  backed by Thrive Holdings, and says AI lets local firms offer "Fortune 500-caliber" advisory [ACCTODAY-CURRENT-2026];
  Reuters reported a 2025 plan to spend over $500M buying firms and equip them with OpenAI-powered tools
  [REUTERS-CRETE-2025]. Multiplier raised $27.5M [TECHCRUNCH-MULTIPLIER-2025].
- **Independence consequence.** Outside capital comes with an alternative practice structure, so the advisory arm
  can grow while the attest arm limits what it can sell to attest clients. The AICPA ethics committee was still
  working toward a second exposure draft in August 2026, including a proposed ban on attest-client investment in
  firms [JOFA-PEEC-2026]; IESBA opened a PE workstream in March 2026 [IESBA-PE-2026]. The rule is not final.

## 8. What to measure in a conversation

- Revenue mix: recurring (CAS, managed FP&A, fund marks) versus project and matter work; fee basis per line.
- Clients per accountant, hours per client a month, close days; hours per control; days to first databook.
- Review hours added by AI, reviewer change rates, and where the saved hours went.
- AI cost per client or engagement (licences, platform fees, outcome-based vendor fees) against the fee.
- Share of clients that are also attest clients, by line, and any outcome-linked fees in the book.
- Pipeline: which clean CAS clients bought advisory in the last year, and at what fee basis.
