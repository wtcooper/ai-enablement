# State of AI in professional services

Last researched 2026-10-19; figures are as published up to that date. Use this in onboarding to bring in one real
example early, in Frame to set a realistic baseline, and whenever a leader says "everyone else is already doing it".
Keep three things apart: what is **measured**, what is **announced**, and what is **forecast**. Most surveys here are
run by firms that sell AI or practice software (Thomson Reuters, Clio, Kantata, Deltek, Loopio, Introhive); labels say
so. Source keys in brackets resolve in §9 or in [sources.md](sources.md).

## 1. Headlines

- **Adoption is near-universal in law; value is not.** 94% of law firms in ILTA's 2026 survey are "engaged with
  GenAI", up 14 points, but more than half issue Copilot licences only on request [ILTA-TECHSURVEY-2026]. Only 38% of
  896 architecture and engineering firms report measurable benefit [DELTEK-CLARITY-2026].
- **Use is spreading through projects.** SPI reports 43.6% of projects at 245 services firms now use AI, up from 23.1%
  a year earlier [SPI-AI-2026].
- **Savings are claimed; returns are not measured.** 84.8% of those firms say AI saves time [SPI-AI-2026]; 18% of
  respondents say their organisations collect AI ROI metrics [TR-AIPS-2026]; at Legalweek, firms asked by clients what AI saved said "we're still
  gathering the data" [TRI-LEGALWEEK-2026].
- **Clients are the pressure point.** 78% of corporate clients rate AI-enabled quality gains important or essential;
  6% say most providers deliver them [TR-FOP-REPORT-2026].
- **Shadow use is common.** 34% of 1,816 professionals use AI their organisation has not sanctioned [TR-FOP-REPORT-2026];
  54% of consultants in a LexisNexis survey use unapproved tools [LEXISNEXIS-FOW-2026].
- **"Agentic" is the 2026 vocabulary, ahead of practice.** At least 18 of 107 Legalweek 2026 sessions touched agentic
  AI [EDRM-LEGALWEEK-2026], while the Law Society found very limited use of fully autonomous agents in legal practice
  [LAWSOC-WHATAGENTIC-2026].

## 4. Quality evidence that exists

- **Randomised trial, law.** 133 patent lawyers at 11 US firms: AI raised blinded-scored quality by 0.34 SD at 10
  days and 0.38 SD at 90 days; the lasting unaided skill gain went to seniors, and juniors showed no average gain.
  Conducted and funded by Google (several authors are Google employees); NBER working paper [NBER-AUTOR-2026].
- **Field experiment, consulting.** 758 BCG consultants with GPT-4 (2023): 12.2% more tasks and 25.1% faster inside
  AI's capability, 19% less likely to be correct on a task outside it [HBS-DELLACQUA-2023].
- **Tool benchmarks (tools alone, not firm deployments).** Legal AI tools beat a lawyer baseline on document Q&A and
  summarisation, lawyers led on redlining [VALS-VLAIRFEB-2025]; leading legal research tools hallucinated 17% to 33%
  of the time in 2024 [STANFORD-MAGESH-2024].
- **Acceptance telemetry, one vendor.** Microsoft's Legal Agent: about 70% of suggestions kept across 1,800+ users
  (vendor-published; an acceptance measure, not an error rate) [MSFT-COPILOTLEGAL-2026].
- **Failure count.** 2,202 court decisions worldwide on hallucinated content as of 19 October 2026, 1,501 in the US; a
  count of decisions, not a rate [CHARLOTIN-DB-2026].
- **No deployer error rates.** No firm or in-house team publishes an error, override or parallel-run rate for
  AI-assisted work ([quality-review](modules/quality-review.md)).

## 5. How maturity is spread

- **By firm size (law).** Formal GenAI policy: 57% of firms under 50 lawyers, 98% at 350-699 (91% over 700). Training
  before use: 11% under 50, 72% over 700. Human review of work product required: 31% under 50, 83% at 350-699. Client
  approval required: 8% under 50, 38% at 350-699 [ILTA-TECHSURVEY-2026].
- **Strategy versus practice.** Over three-quarters of TRI's stand-out lawyers say their firm has an AI strategy, but
  only 25% strongly agree there is a plan for monetising AI, and just over 10% strongly agree their practice area is
  confident it will succeed [TRI-STANDOUT-2026]. Where a named strategy exists, 66% say AI meets or exceeds expectations,
  against 22% without one [TR-FOP-REPORT-2026].
- **Partners as the gap.** About a third of heavy-AI-user stand-out partners had discussed AI with 60% or more of their
  clients; embedded daily users report significant impact about nine times as often as minimal users [TRI-STANDOUT-2026].
  In a training-provider survey, 55% (74% in law firms) named partners the least prepared group [LAW360-GA-2026].
- **Consulting and project firms.** SPI found in 2025 "a market adopting AI fast but struggling to turn its pilots into
  performance" [SPI-AI-2025]; in 2026 it says profit "only goes to firms that have built the right foundations" [SPI-AI-2026].
- **Resource management.** 52.7% not equipped to manage hybrid human-agent teams; 4% well equipped [KANTATA-RMI-2026].
- **Data skew.** Evidence leans to large law firms and vendor customers. Mid-tier consulting and accounting firms,
  engineering and architecture beyond Deltek, and non-legal shared-service teams are thinly covered.

| Stage (see [ARCHETYPE.md](ARCHETYPE.md)) | Where the evidence places most organizations |
|---|---|
| Curious | Smaller law firms without policy or training; individuals using public tools outside policy |
| Experimenting | Most firms with a licensed tool used on request and anecdotal savings |
| Adopting | Large law firms with training gates, firmwide platforms and matter-level use; some consultancies on PSA agents |
| Scaling | A few: review gates, AI cost metering and pricing changes in named firms; no published value measures |
| AI-native | New entrants and stated targets only |

## 6. Vendors and platforms

- **Labs entered legal in five months:** Anthropic (May), Google (August), OpenAI (September) and Microsoft (15 October
  2026) each launched a legal offering [LAWSITES-MSFT-2026].
- **Specialist legal platforms are capitalised:** Harvey raised $550m at a $15.5bn valuation on 9 September 2026
  [HARVEY-NEWSROOM-2026]. Legora gives 140,000 users at 2,000+ firms and in-house teams in one release
  [LEGORA-HLC-2026] and 130,000+ at 2,100+ on its newsroom [LEGORA-NEWSROOM-2026]; the counts differ.
- **PSA vendors ship named agents** for estimates, SOWs, staffing and project management (Certinia Veda, Kantata Agent
  Studio, Planview) [CERTINIA-VEDA-2026; ITBRIEF-KANTATA-2026; SDTIMES-PLANVIEW-2026].
- **Labs move toward implementation services:** OpenAI launched a majority-owned Deployment Company on 11 May 2026
  (press-reported) [DECRYPT-DEPLOYCO-2026].

## 7. What is not measured

- No independent firm-level study of realisation, margin or write-downs before and after AI.
- No error, override or parallel-run rate from a named firm for drafting, review, staffing, pricing or billing tools.
- No public data on PwC One uptake or prices, or on AI-native firms' volumes and margins.
- No measure of work actually moved in house by legal departments; only stated intentions.
- Primary pages for McKinsey, BCG, Bain, Deloitte, EY and KPMG were not reachable; their figures rest on press.

## 8. Using this in a conversation

- Lead with the closest peer example from the relevant module, its grade, and who measured it.
- When a leader says "everyone is doing it", separate use (high) from measured value (rare) and from strategy that
  reaches clients (thin).
- When a leader says "AI doesn't work for us", point to the one randomised trial and the BCG experiment: quality gains
  are real inside the tool's range, and errors rise outside it [NBER-AUTOR-2026; HBS-DELLACQUA-2023].
- Treat vendor surveys as direction, not size.
