## 1. How the business makes money

- **Recurring, deadline-driven compliance.** Returns, provisions, indirect tax filings and TP documentation recur every
  year. In one vendor survey of 639 firm respondents, more than 75% named business and individual tax-return
  preparation as their most profitable service [TR-SOTP-2025].
- **Small firms price mostly by the return or a fixed fee; large firms still lean on time.** Hours saved by AI show up
  as margin where the unit of sale is the return, and as a fee question where it is the hour (§3, §7).
- **Advisory is where pricing is being rethought.** Top US firms are "brainstorming" ways to charge on value and
  outcomes as AI cuts billable hours [BTAX-PRICING-2026].
- **Retail preparation is a seasonal labour business.** H&R Block's compensation and benefits were about 40% of revenue
  in fiscal 2026 (computed from its income statement) [HRB-FY2026].
- **In-house tax is a cost centre.** Its economics are headcount, technology share of budget and outside-adviser spend,
  usually approved by finance (§8).
- **Software vendors capture part of the gain.** Thomson Reuters's tax, audit and accounting professionals segment grew
  8% organically in Q2 2026 with a 38.7% adjusted EBITDA margin [TR-Q2-2026].

## 3. Fees and pricing models

**Small and mid-size practices**
- In a July 2025 survey of 219 US firms on the Ignition platform (its own customers), fixed fee is the most common
  tax-prep model at 37%, "minimum fee plus complexity" 23%, value pricing 19% and hourly 3% [IGNITION-2025].
- The most common fee band for a basic individual return was US$400-599 (27% of firms); for business returns
  US$1,000-1,499 (29%) [IGNITION-2025]. NSA's 2023 averages: Form 1040 itemised with state US$392, Form 1065 US$815,
  Form 1120 US$1,004 [NSA-2023]. The two surveys are not comparable.
- 80% of Ignition respondents planned fee rises for 2026, by an average of 5-10% [CPAPA-IGNITION-2025].
- AICPA MAP (fiscal 2024): median net client fees up 6.7%; a slow shift to value and fixed pricing [AICPA-MAP-2025].
  Bloomberg Tax, citing a 2025 AICPA survey, gives a median net hourly rate of US$170 [BTAX-PRICING-2026].

**Large firms**
- Top US firms are "developing" value- and outcome-based models; PwC's US tax leader says time is "becoming less and
  less" relevant; RSM's partner asks whether a 20-hour report cut to 10 is "margin for me or … price reduction for our
  client"; RKL has "several dozen" clients on a value-based pilot [BTAX-PRICING-2026]. These are statements, not
  published price changes.
- **Contingent fees are limited by rule.** Circular 230 section 10.27(b) bars contingent fees for most matters before the
  IRS, with exceptions for examinations, certain refund claims and judicial proceedings [ECFR-10.27].
- **Managed services and co-sourcing.** KPMG's survey says 40% of companies have adopted managed services at scale in
  tax (vendor-published) [KPMG-MS-2026]; EY says 54% of tax leaders in its 2024 survey are rethinking operating models, with
  co-sourcing the main change considered [EY-TAXNEWS-2025].
- **Software pricing.** Research AI is priced per seat (Blue J lists US$1,498 a year per user) [BLUEJ-PRICING-2026];
  one 4,500-return firm puts AI preparation at "$100, $150 or potentially more per return"
  (Black Ore's stated average is US$100-150, built from per-page fees) [WCG-2026].

## 4. Cost structure

- **People.** Median new-graduate salaries rose to US$60,834 (bachelor's) and US$67,750 (master's) in two years
  [AICPA-MAP-2025]. PwC's global headcount fell by 5,600 to 364,000 in the year to 30 Jun 2025 [CPAPA-PWCREV-2025]. Top 100
  firms are leaning to lateral hires and non-accountants [ATODAY-TOP100-2026]. See [workforce.md](workforce.md).
- **Technology.** RSM's US$1bn technology and AI plan (myRSM Tax builds on it) [RSM-MYRSM-2025]; Deloitte US$3bn
  through 2030 [DELOITTE-REV-2026]; Grant Thornton Advisors US$1bn [GT-CBIZ-2026]; EY more than US$1bn a year on
  AI-first platforms [CPAPA-EYREV-2025]. More than 90% of firms planned to raise technology spend by up to 20% (AICPA, as
  quoted by Bloomberg Tax) [BTAX-PRICING-2026].
- **The cost of the AI itself.** TEI attendees discussed "rising token costs that could entirely change the economic
  case for automation"; no figures were given [TEI-TAXTECH-2026]. Nobody has benchmarked it.
- **Capacity is what vendors sell against.** The article on Black Ore's launch cites the BLS for about 300,000 net
  departures from the profession in two years; Black Ore's own 125-million-hour annual shortfall was not attributed to
  any source [BLACKORE-2026].

## 5. Offshoring

- 86% of IPA 100 firms employ offshore staff (83% the year before), offshore staff average 8.2% of headcount, 68%
  offshore tax returns, and all 25 largest plan to increase it [IPA-OFFSHORE-2026].
- CBIZ added 250+ India-based professionals by acquiring BINDZ, which it calls a margin lever (company statement)
  [CBIZ-Q2-2026].
- Disclosure to a preparer outside the US needs specific taxpayer consent (Rev. Proc. 2013-14) [IRS-RP2013-14].
- In house, 8% of 170 US departments plan to outsource more as their main answer to capacity limits [TR-CORPTECHRPT-2026].
- **For the business case:** with returns already offshore, an AI case measured against onshore cost overstates the
  saving. No source publishes a cost per return for offshore against AI-prepared work.

## 6. Private equity, roll-ups and ownership

- **Scale.** 10 of the top 20 US firms are, or will soon be, PE-owned [CFOBREW-2026]. Crowe sold a majority stake to KKR
  (reported at nearly US$3bn, announced 11 Jun 2026) and Eide Bailly took Reverence Capital investment (23 Jun 2026);
  both put tax and advisory in the investor-backed entity and keep attest separate [CPAPA-CROWE-2026; EIDE-2026]. Grant
  Thornton Advisors agreed on 29 Jul 2026 to buy CBIZ at a US$5.0bn enterprise value [GT-CBIZ-2026].
- **Valuation.** Advisers quoted by Accounting Today call 5-7x EBITDA "good", 9-10x or even 11x "outstanding", with
  multiples up "one point per year" since PE arrived [ATODAY-WORTH-2026]. CPA Trendlines, drawing on a letter from TaxDome's
  founders (a software vendor), says PE investors pay more for firms that use AI to expand capacity "rather than cut
  headcount" [CPATL-PEVAL-2026].
- **Independence rules are being rewritten** for alternative practice structures; a second AICPA exposure draft was
  under discussion in Aug 2026 [PEEC-2025; PEEC-2026].
- **AI-native roll-ups.** Multiplier raised US$27.5m and says AI more than doubled the margin of a 12-person boutique it
  bought (company claim) [MULTIPLIER-2025]. Crete (Thrive-backed) planned "over $500 million" of acquisitions over two
  years, with over US$300m revenue at Jun 2025 [CRETE-REUTERS-2025]. Accrual, which Reuters described in 2025 as a
  General Catalyst firm that buys companies, launched in Feb 2026 as a preparation platform sold to firms (US$75m;
  claims prep time down more than 85%) [CRETE-REUTERS-2025; CPAPA-ACCRUAL-2026].
- **Not every owner wants it.** 57% of tax, audit and accounting professionals in one vendor survey said PE is not on
  their radar [TR-TFG-2025].

## 7. Pricing pressure from AI

- **Regulator.** OPR Alert 2026-19 (24 Jun 2026) says billing manual time not actually spent, or double billing for
  AI-assisted tasks, may breach section 10.27(a) on unconscionable fees "depending on the facts", and that savings should be credited openly
  [IRS-OPR-2026]. The AICPA says this appears not to allow value pricing and "overlooks" AI costs, and has asked the IRS
  to clarify; no reply was found [AICPA-FEES-2026].
- **Clients (adjacent).** KPMG International pressed its own auditor to pass on AI savings; the audit fee fell 14% in
  dollar terms. One small audit fee, not tax [IRISHTIMES-KPMG-2026].
- **Sellers.** Nearly half of tax and audit professionals feel pressure to generate financial gains from AI, and a third
  say their firm has not adapted its commercial model (vendor survey) [TR-FOP-2026].
- **Capacity instead of price cuts.** Vendors frame the gain as serving more clients with the same headcount
  [BLACKORE-2026]; Thomson Reuters offers it as one of three paths (elevate, scale, reimagine) [TR-FOP-2026].
- **No measured fee decline in tax.** List prices and fee-rise plans are up and Big Four tax lines grew (§2, §3). No
  before-and-after fee study on the same tax work exists.

## 8. In-house budgets

- About 39% of 170 US corporate tax technology decision-makers reported a budget increase in the past year; 69% expect
  increases over 3-5 years (a similar expectation the year before was not met); half bought no new tax technology;
  dissatisfaction with the stack rose to 56% from 34% [TR-CORPTECHRPT-2026]. Only 27% can secure budget when needed and
  9% call themselves active AI users [TR-UNDERSTAFF-2026].
- Technology averages about 15.4% of the department budget (288 departments, 2025) [TR-CORPTAX-2025]. Median external
  tax spend was US$450,000 in a 251-respondent 2024 survey [TR-SOTCD-2024].
- Resourcing shows up in risk: 43% of well-resourced versus 26% of under-resourced departments are very confident of
  timely forecasting (37% versus 24% of avoiding penalties), and 56% of mostly reactive departments incurred penalties
  against 31% of mostly proactive ones (self-reported) [TR-CORPTAX-2025].
- **No cost per return, provision or filing is published.** The IRS is surveying business compliance burden for tax
  year 2025; no results yet [IRS-BTB-2026]. Build the baseline from the department's own time data.

## 9. Key metrics for the conversation

| Metric | Why it matters with AI | Benchmark in the sources |
|---|---|---|
| Unit of billing per service | Decides who keeps the saving | 37% fixed, 3% hourly for small-firm prep [IGNITION-2025] |
| Effective rate per hour on fixed-fee work | Shows whether savings become margin | Not published |
| Hours per return, provision or local file | The baseline for any case | Not published at firm level |
| Offshore share and cost per return | The real comparison for AI preparation | 68% of IPA 100 offshore returns [IPA-OFFSHORE-2026]; no cost data |
| Review hours and notes per return | Where AI time savings are lost or kept | Not published |
| Notices, amended returns, penalties | Quality and risk | Reactive departments report penalties more often [TR-CORPTAX-2025] |
| AI cost per run, seat or return | A new variable cost | Seat and per-return prices only (§3) |
| In house: technology share and adviser spend | Funding source for the case | About 15.4%; median US$450,000 [TR-CORPTAX-2025; TR-SOTCD-2024] |
