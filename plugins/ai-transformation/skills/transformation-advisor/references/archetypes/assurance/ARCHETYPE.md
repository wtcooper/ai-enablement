# Assurance (archetype)

Last researched 2026-10-17 (mid-market modules 2026-10-21). The index for the assurance archetype: read it once onboarding
points to assurance, then load only the files the conversation needs. Source keys in brackets resolve in [sources.md](sources.md).

## What it covers

External audit and other assurance: audits of financial statements and of internal control over financial reporting (ICFR, including
SOX 404 integrated audits); private-company audits, reviews and compilations; benefit plan, nonprofit and single audits; SOC and
other attestation; sustainability assurance; and assurance over AI systems. The client buys an independent opinion or conclusion that
others rely on, given under professional standards and independence rules, which an audit regulator or peer reviewer can re-examine.

## Who it serves

- **Firms of every size:** the Big Four networks, mid-tier and private-equity-backed firms, small practices, and
  AI-native entrants. Their starting points differ sharply (see [state-of-ai.md](state-of-ai.md)).
- **In-house internal audit and SOX teams.** Their controls testing, risk assessment and reporting overlap with
  external audit, and in controls testing they are often further along with agents than external auditors.
- **Typical users:** engagement and practice partners, managing partners, innovation or digital audit leaders,
  quality and risk partners, SOC and sustainability practice leads, chief audit executives (CAEs) and SOX leads.

## What it extends

It extends **[professional-services](../professional-services/ARCHETYPE.md)**: pursuit, acceptance, scoping and pricing, staffing, delivery,
quality review, billing and knowledge management all still apply. Assurance changes four things: auditing standards (ISA, PCAOB, AICPA, ISSA
5000) set the value chain's steps; independence rules limit the services, vendors and owners allowed; inspectors or peer reviewers re-examine
the work; and the human auditor stays accountable for the opinion whatever the tools [FRC-NEWS-2026; NFRA-CAALLEY-2026; JOFA-AIPA-2026].

## Recognition cues

Match on what is delivered, to whom and who regulates it, not on job titles. Cues that point to assurance:
- "We audit ...", "sign the opinion", "year-end", "busy season", "fieldwork", "the file", "workpapers", "PBC list".
- Inspections, findings, peer review, PCAOB, FRC or "the regulator pulled one of our files".
- Engagement partner, EQR, independence clearance, partner rotation, audit committee reporting.
- SOX testing, ITGCs, walkthroughs, the RCM, deficiencies or a material weakness (external or internal).
- SOC 1 or SOC 2 reports, Type 2, Section 3, Trust Services Criteria, bridge letters.
- Limited assurance on ESG or CSRD reports, ISSA 5000, GHG verification.
- Certifying or attesting AI systems: ISO/IEC 42001, AIUC-1, an ISAE 3000 opinion on AI governance.
- Mid-market practices: private-company audits, SSARS reviews and compilations, DAS or Guided Assurance; benefit plan
  audits (ERISA, Form 5500, recordkeeper SOC 1s); nonprofit and single audits (Uniform Guidance, Yellow Book, SEFA).
- From a CAE: the audit universe, the annual plan, three lines, co-sourcing, reporting to the audit committee.

Not assurance: tax compliance and provision (tax); consulting or advisory work with no opinion (professional-services);
finance teams that prepare statements or sustainability reports (only the effect on assurance belongs here).

as of 2026-10-21. **Use thin modules for risk awareness and interview probes, not as proof points.** Quote their
examples as ideas or early signals, and say plainly that no named firm has published a measured result. Data and
evidence acquisition (getting client data into the file) has no module; see [value-chain.md](value-chain.md) §4.

## Journey-stage markers (assurance)

Stage names and "ready when" gates follow [onboarding.md](../../method/onboarding.md) §5; these are the field-specific
versions. Place each scope (team, practice, firm) by markers you can see or count, not by self-rating.

| Stage | Markers in an assurance firm or internal audit team | Ready for the next stage when |
|---|---|---|
| **Curious** | No documented transformation plan (75% of 54 US firms surveyed) [CPACOM-ATR-2025]; individual chat use for memos and research; no tool inventory or certification; policy is a ban, or silent on client data | An acceptable-use rule covers client data and confidentiality; named people use AI weekly on real, non-confidential work; one quick win shown to peers |
| **Experimenting** | Enterprise assistant or guidance copilot under a policy [CAQ-RSM-2026]; point tools on some engagements (confirmations, foot-and-proof, trial-balance import) [CPACOM-ATR-2025]; GenAI for drafting more than testing; AI use not visible on the file | One workflow (for example evidence matching, a disclosure checklist or a defined control set) has an owner, a baseline (hours per selection or control, review notes), a written human control point and an approved tool, certified for that use |
| **Adopting** | Tools certified per use case and recertified when the model changes [FRC-GENAI-2026; IFIAR-TTF-2026]; control points written into methodology; tool, version and use recorded on the file [FRC-AIAUDIT-2025]; defined engagement types run the tool by default; reviewers trained on AI output; AI use explained to audit committees | Two or more workflows in production; the second reused the first's data connection or review checklist; value and quality impact recorded in one place; a named central owner for AI in the practice |
| **Scaling** | Agents across audit phases on a shared platform [EY-CANVAS-2026; DELOITTE-OMNIA-2026]; network and member-firm monitoring with clear ownership [IFIAR-TTF-2026]; client data arrives through standard connectors; staffing tied to AI capacity; active regulator dialogue (sandbox, comment letters) | Value is measured in engagement margin or the P&L, and leaders are asking what to sell, to whom and at what price |
| **AI-native** | Methodology built around whole populations with follow-up by exception [KPMG-SOX-2026]; fee model or service changed by AI (continuous or subscription assurance, assurance over AI); firms built AI-first [REPODO-2026; MODUS-2026] | Re-diagnose at each review. No firm has yet published a measured cost or quality advantage, so treat AI-native claims with scepticism |

**Delegation.** Plan delegated (agentic) work only at Adopting or later, behind the five gates in onboarding §5. The
assurance reference case is internal audit agents testing defined SOX control attributes with cited evidence, exceptions
routed to a reviewer who signs off (controls-testing `#05`; figures self- or vendor-reported). IFIAR suggests firms
assign named people to each decision checkpoint and review autonomous decisions overall [IFIAR-TTF-2026].
