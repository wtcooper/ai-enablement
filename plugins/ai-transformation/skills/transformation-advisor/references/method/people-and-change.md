# People and change (cross-cutting)

Read this when you write role evolution and the capacity choice (Make the case), when a user expresses
fear about their role, when you plan communication and training (Plan), and when rolling up workforce
effects across units. It makes rules 1, 2 and 8 in `SKILL.md` concrete. Keys in brackets are in §13.

## 1. Goal

Be candid about what AI does to work, at the level of tasks, roles and capacity, and help the user lead
the change rather than be done to by it. Every opportunity at team scope and above leaves this phase with
tasks moving, new work and an explicit capacity choice. No person is ever singled out.

## 2. The three rules, made concrete

**Rule 1: never target individuals.** Work at the level of role groups, task bundles and capacity.

| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| "The tester role spends about 40% of its hours on vouching; this frees roughly 1–2 FTE-equivalent of capacity" (illustrative) | "Cut two testers" or "Sam's work can go" |
| State reduction as capacity and cost, with a mechanism: outside spend, hiring pace, attrition, role redesign | Set a target number of people to remove |
| Name owners of actions by role, or by name where the user offers it | Record views on anyone's performance, even if the user offers them |
| Say that any decision to reduce roles goes through the organization's own HR, legal and consultation processes | Advise on which people are affected |

If asked to rank or select people, decline and redirect in one breath: "I can't help choose or rate
individuals. I can show which tasks in each role change, how much capacity that frees, and the options for
using it, so the decision rests on roles and criteria you can explain."

**Rule 2: always include role evolution and the capacity choice** (team scope and above). For each
opportunity, write `role_evolution`:

- `tasks_moving`: the tasks that leave people, taken from the task bundle (§4).
- `new_work`: what people do instead (reviewing, exceptions, rule and evaluation-set upkeep, client work).
- `capacity_use`: starts with `redeploy` or `reduce` (or `redeploy and reduce` with the split), followed by
  the destination or mechanism, for example "redeploy: staff gaps and earlier risk work".

Ask the choice openly: "If this frees time, do you want to use it for growth, quality, backlog or new
services, or to reduce cost? Both are legitimate; the plan has to say which." Then run the checks in §5.

**Rule 8: when a user fears for their own role, acknowledge it honestly, don't falsely reassure, and help
them position themselves as the person leading the change.** The script is in §8.

## 3. Honest framing of job impact

Use these to answer "will AI take my job?" without dismissing or inflating the fear. Quote the provenance.

- **No economy-wide job loss found so far.** Several independent analyses find no broad displacement
  attributable to AI through 2025–26 [YALE-BUDGETLAB-2025; STANFORD-CANARIES-2026].
- **The visible effect is on entry-level hiring.** US payroll data put employment of 22–25-year-olds in the
  most AI-exposed occupations 19% below where it would have been had it kept pace with less-exposed peers
  (descriptive, not causal) [STANFORD-CANARIES-2026]. Junior employment at firms adopting generative AI was
  about 9% lower after six quarters, senior employment on trend (independent working paper) [HARVARD-HOSSEINI-2026].
- **It depends on how AI is used.** Declines concentrate where AI substitutes for tasks; where it
  complements people, employment is flat or rising [STANFORD-CANARIES-2026].
- **Firms report hiring less more often than firing.** In a regional Fed survey of service firms, 4%
  reported AI-related layoffs in the past six months, about 15% hiring fewer people than otherwise and about 13% hiring more
  (independent, one region) [NYFED-2026].
- **Exposure is not a forecast.** Exposure scores say what AI could speed up, not what will be adopted or
  cut; never present an exposure percentage as a headcount forecast [ANTHROPIC-LABOR-2026].
- **Fear is common and partly about judgment.** In Anthropic's interviews with a general-workforce sample
  (n=1,000), 55% voiced anxiety about AI's effect on their future and 69% mentioned stigma around using it at
  work (vendor, self-selected) [ANTHROPIC-INTERVIEWER-2025].

## 4. Role evolution: from tasks to roles

1. **Break each role into a task bundle** with rough time shares before anyone says "this job" [ANTHROPIC-LABOR-2026].
2. **Mark each task** against the options kept: improve usually leaves the task with the person; automate
   and delegate move it; reinvent can change the role itself. Say this when the user picks a lens.
3. **Ask the people who do the work** which tasks they would gladly hand over, share, or keep, and why. In
   Stanford's survey, the top reason for wanting automation was freeing time for higher-value work (69% of
   responses), and workers preferred more human involvement than experts judged necessary (independent)
   [STANFORD-WORKBANK-2026].
4. **Ask which tasks define their professional identity.** Change starts with an ending; name what people
   lose before asking them to adopt something new [BRIDGES-ND; ANTHROPIC-INTERVIEWER-2025].
5. **Write the new work**: verification and review, exception handling, keeping rules and evaluation sets
   current, client time, judgment earlier in the career. AI users name quality control of AI output and
   critical thinking among the skills growing in importance (vendor survey) [MICROSOFT-WTI-2026].
6. **Name the skills and the path**: what people need to be able to do in 12 months, how they will learn it,
   and who protects the time.

## 5. Redeploy or reduce

| Option | Fits when | Check before writing it into the case |
|---|---|---|
| **Redeploy** to growth, quality, backlog or new services | There is demand for the extra output or a measurable quality gap | A named destination, an owner and an output metric. Companies measuring AI against growth outcomes reported strong ROI more often than those measuring efficiency alone (35% versus 13%; association, self-reported) [WHARTON-2026] |
| **Reduce outside spend** (contractors, co-source, outsourcing) | Work currently bought in could be done with released capacity | Often the first real money, without touching roles [MIT-NANDA-2025] |
| **Reduce through hiring pace or attrition** | Demand is flat and turnover is normal | The effect on the junior pipeline in three to five years (§6) |
| **Reduce roles** | Results are measured, not expected, and demand will not absorb the capacity | Realized, not potential; a rollback assumption if quality drops; the organization's consultation duties |

Four checks for any reduction (advisor synthesis from the sources cited):

- **Realized or expected?** Cutting on AI's potential rather than its performance is reported as common and
  risky [HBR-DAVENPORT-2026, summary only]. Of EY's respondents with gains, 17% said they led to reduced
  headcount; more reinvested (self-reported) [EY-2025].
- **Quality rollback.** Klarna froze hiring and shrank largely through attrition while AI took on customer
  service; its CEO later said the AI service was "lower quality" and it began recruiting human agents again
  (company statements via press) [KLARNA-2025]. Gartner forecasts that by 2027 half of companies that
  attributed headcount cuts to AI will rehire staff for similar work, often under new job titles (a forecast,
  made in a customer-service context) [GARTNER-ITNEWS-2026].
- **Pipeline.** Who learns the judgment the removed tasks used to teach?
- **The honest reason.** "What would we say about this change if AI were not part of the explanation?" AI
  was named in about 4.5% of reported US job cuts in the first 11 months of 2025 (outplacement data, via
  Oxford Economics) [OXFORD-FORTUNE-2026].

## 6. The junior pipeline

- **Junior-task audit.** Label each task juniors do as production, training, or both. "Both" tasks need a
  replacement learning route before they are automated (advisor synthesis; junior work serves capacity, risk
  and training at once [TR-INSTITUTE-2026]).
- **Explain-back.** Ask juniors to explain AI output back before it is used. In an RCT, AI-assisted learners
  scored 17% lower on mastery, though those who asked conceptual questions kept more (vendor RCT) [ANTHROPIC-SKILLS-2026].
- **Earlier, supervised judgment.** Move juniors toward investigating exceptions and verification with
  supervision; rotations into AI-enabled roles are a proposal, not yet an evaluated practice [TR-INSTITUTE-2026].
- **Mentoring.** Ask who juniors still learn from; AI as the first stop can thin out questions to colleagues [ANTHROPIC-WORK-2025].
- **Roll-up check.** At unit or organization scope, add up each unit's hiring-pace assumptions; individually
  small slowdowns can hollow out the pyramid together.

## 7. Communicating change

- **What we know, what we don't, what we commit to.** Draft it with the user and test it on a skeptic.
  Never promise "no job losses" unless the commitment is real and funded (advisor synthesis). Public
  examples of candor differ: Amazon's CEO said AI would reduce its corporate workforce while admitting the
  net was hard to know [AMAZON-JASSY-2025]; Accenture said upskilling came first and that people it could not
  reskill would leave [ACCENTURE-2025]. They show explicitness, not best practice.
- **Managers first.** Test readiness with one question: "What will you say when someone asks if their job
  is safe?" Prosci reports only 35% of organizations prepare managers adequately (vendor) [PROSCI-2026].
- **Check it landed.** Ask a few staff to restate what leadership said; leaders tend to believe more has
  landed than staff report [PROSCI-2026].
- **Lower the stigma.** Leaders use AI visibly; ask "who uses AI and does not say so?" [ANTHROPIC-INTERVIEWER-2025].
- **Show early wins** that people can see and relate to the change [KOTTER-1995].
- **Partner-led firms.** When partners or senior experts must back the change, use the stakeholder map, partner briefing, objection handling and leader expectations in [leading-partners.md](leading-partners.md).

## 8. Responding to fear

When the user says something like "Am I automating myself out of a job?":

1. **Acknowledge it specifically.** "That's a fair worry. Some of the tasks in your role are exactly the
   ones this changes."
2. **Don't falsely reassure.** Never say "your job is safe" or "AI won't replace anyone". You don't know,
   and the user will hear it as spin.
3. **Give the honest picture, briefly.** Which of their tasks are exposed and which are not; what the
   evidence shows (§3); what is unknown.
4. **Separate what they control.** The organization decides headcount; they decide how well they understand
   and shape the new way of working.
5. **Help them lead it**, with concrete moves: own the redesign of their process; design how AI output is
   checked (write the checklist, the evaluation cases); capture the know-how only they have; measure the
   results and report them; build skills on their own real tasks; be the visible example for the team.
   Be honest that the evidence that leading AI change helps careers is correlational, not proven.
6. **Bring it back to the work** and record their concern in the people plan, if they agree.

Don't change the subject to benefits, use statistics as reassurance, or make promises for the organization.

## 9. Diagnosing the change

Use these models as structure, not as evidence; their outcome claims come mainly from the firms that sell
them. Don't quote "70% of change programmes fail": its origin could not be traced.

- **ADKAR** per role group: awareness, desire, knowledge, ability, reinforcement. Find the first weak element
  and put effort there [PROSCI-ADKAR].
- **Bridges**: endings, neutral zone, new beginnings. Name what is being lost [BRIDGES-ND].
- **Kotter**: urgency, a guiding coalition, visible short-term wins, anchoring [KOTTER-1995].
- **Psychological safety**: "How safe is it here to say 'the AI got this wrong'?" Team safety was associated
  with learning behavior in a pre-AI field study of 51 teams [EDMONDSON-1999].

## 10. Question bank

Tags: **[T]** team, **[U/O]** unit or organization. Core questions first.

**Core**
- If this frees time, what do you want it used for: growth, quality, backlog, new services, or lower cost? [T][U/O]
- Which tasks in each role would move, and what would people do instead? [T][U/O]
- What have your people heard so far about AI and their jobs? [T][U/O]
- Which tasks do juniors learn from, and how will they learn if those tasks go? [T][U/O]
- What can you honestly commit to your people, and who will keep that commitment? [T][U/O]

**Follow-ups**
- Which tasks would your team gladly hand over, and which define how they see their work? [T]
- How safe is it on your team to say "the AI got this wrong" or "I don't know how to use this"? [T]
- Who uses AI quietly, and why don't they say so? [T]
- Is any saving in the case already real, or still expected? [T][U/O]
- What would you say about this change if AI were not part of the explanation? [U/O]
- What will your managers say when someone asks if their job is safe? [U/O]
- If quality drops, what is the plan to bring people back in? [U/O]
- Across units, how many plan to slow hiring, and what does that do to the pyramid? [U/O] *Scaling*

## 11. Common traps

| Trap | How the advisor avoids it |
|---|---|
| **Silence** | People fill it with anxiety; draft the known / unknown / committed note early [PROSCI-2026] |
| **False reassurance** | Commit only to what is real and funded |
| **Exposure quoted as a forecast** | Pair it with actual use and local evidence |
| **Cutting on potential** | Realized versus expected; phase reductions behind measured results |
| **Hollowing the pipeline** | Junior-task audit and a roll-up check on hiring pace |
| **Automating the learning** | Explain-back and supervised stretch work |
| **Training as a tool tour** | Practice on people's own tasks, with protected time |
| **Change models as proof** | Use them to structure questions, not to promise outcomes |
| **Promising career gains** | The evidence is correlational; offer leading the change as a reasonable bet |

## 12. Worked example: the audit practice

*Capacity choice for `ct-07` (evidence matching).* The partner chose "redeploy: staff gaps and earlier risk
work", consistent with the Frame stance; contractor hours in the season may fall; no roles reduced.
*Role evolution.* Staff and seniors: vouching documents to samples moves to the tool; new work is
investigating mismatches and earlier risk work. *Junior pipeline.* Vouching is a "both" task: it teaches
what good evidence looks like. Juniors explain back a sample of matches each engagement and investigate
mismatches under a senior; one firm reports first-years now doing complex revenue testing under guidance
(self-reported, archetype module).

*Rule 1 in practice.* The partner asked, "Which two seniors would I need least?" The advisor declined to
choose people and showed instead that the senior role's vouching share falls by about a third, freeing
capacity the practice had already said it would use for staff gaps.

*Rule 8 in practice.* A senior manager using the plugin said: "My job is mostly reviewing. If AI reviews,
what's left for me?" The advisor acknowledged that review is changing, did not promise the role is safe,
noted that review is currently the practice's bottleneck and that someone has to decide what the AI checks
and how, and suggested they write the review checklist (F1), own the re-performance results, and present
them at the 90-day review.

## 13. Output and sources

**Output.** In `opportunities.yaml`, complete `role_evolution` (`tasks_moving`, `new_work`, `capacity_use`)
for every opportunity at team scope and above. In `transformation-plan.md`, a People and change section
with: a table by role group (tasks moving, new work, skills, capacity choice); the capacity decision rule;
the junior pipeline plan; the communication plan (message, audience, when, who); training by role;
commitments and owners; people indicators; and, at unit or organization scope, the roll-up of hiring-pace
effects and one consistent message. Never record named individuals beyond action owners.
