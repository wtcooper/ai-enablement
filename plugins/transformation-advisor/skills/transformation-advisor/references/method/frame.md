# Frame (phase 1)

Read this on entering Frame. It sets the ambition, why now, what success looks like and the value levers. At unit or
organization level it also sets business-model exposure and the settings every child unit inherits. Source keys in
brackets are listed in §9. Tags: **[T]** individual or team, **[U/O]** unit or organization, **[all]** any scope.

## 1. Goal and exit criteria

**Goal.** Agree what the transformation is for, how success will be judged, and what is off limits, so later phases
look in the right places and score options the same way.

**Exit when all of these are true:**
- **Ambition** in one or two sentences. Where the scope can credibly support it, it includes a growth, quality or risk
  objective beside efficiency.
- **Why now:** the two or three pressures that make this urgent.
- **Three to five success measures,** each with a metric, today's value (or where it will come from) and who would accept it as proof.
- **Value levers** chosen from the seven defaults, each with a metric and a weight; weights sum to 1.
- **Scoring weights** confirmed, or changed with a stated reason.
- **Guardrails and boundaries:** rules that cannot bend, and areas the user does not want touched.
- **Owner and sponsor** named by role, with what each can decide.
- **Capacity stance (provisional):** the default use of freed capacity, redeploy or reduce, to be settled per
  opportunity in Make the case. Discuss roles and capacity, never named people or headcount targets for individuals.
- **[U/O]** An **exposure sort** of each sub-unit (AI threatens it, changes it, or mainly helps it) and the value tree.
- **What to map next:** two or three processes [T], or the value-chain stages [U/O].

## 2. How to run it

1. **Start from the profile's goals.** Ask the twelve-month success question (§3, F1). Play it back as an ambition sentence.
2. **Test the breadth of the ambition.** If it is efficiency only, ask what the scope could do or sell that it cannot
   today (F2, F3). Do not force growth where the user's remit cannot deliver it; note it instead.
3. **Ask why now.** For fee-based work, ask how the work is priced and what happens to revenue if effort halves (F6).
4. **Set success measures.** Ask what is measured today and what finance or leadership would accept (F4, F5).
5. **Propose levers and weights** from the goals (§4). Let the user adjust. Explain each lever in a line.
6. **Fix boundaries and guardrails** (F9, F10). Turn constraints from the profile into rules.
7. **Name the owner and sponsor** and what they can decide (F11, F12). At unit level, ask what will stand in for the
   forcing functions a private-equity owner would have (F13).
8. **[U/O] Sort exposure** sub-unit by sub-unit (F14–F17), then sketch the value tree: levers at the top, the
   sub-units or value-chain stages that drive each lever underneath.
9. **Choose what to map** by pain, frequency, impact and readiness (F18). Write the leader's beliefs about how the work
   runs as hypotheses to test in Map: leaders describe work as imagined, the people doing it know work as done [HOLLNAGEL-2017].
10. **Play back the frame,** write the settings and the Frame section, and name Map as next.

With senior leaders, prepare their vocabulary from the archetype first, open with an easy question, then press gently
for specifics when answers stay general [EMPSON-2018].

## 3. Question bank

Core questions first. Ask in this plain wording, one at a time.

| # | Question | Scope | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| F1 | If this goes well, what will be different in twelve months, for your clients and for your people? | [all] | The ambition. Play it back as one sentence |
| F2 | Is that mainly about doing the same work with less effort, or also about better quality, more revenue or something new? | [all] | Efficiency versus wider ambition [MCKINSEY-SOAI-2025] |
| F3 | If the tools were free and quality guaranteed, which piece of work, from request to delivery, would you rebuild? | [all] | Workflow thinking versus tool thinking |
| F4 | What are you measured on today, how is it measured, and how often is it reviewed? | [all] | Existing measures beat new ones |
| F5 | What would your finance lead or leadership accept as proof that this worked? | [all] | The standard of proof |
| F6 | How is the work paid for? If it took half the effort, what would happen to revenue or budget? | [all] | Pricing exposure; essential for hourly and fixed-fee work [SAGE-2026; BAIN-DILIGENCE-2025] |
| F7 | Why now? What is pushing hardest: margins, growth, staff, quality, clients, competitors or regulation? | [all] | Why now |
| F8 | Which of these limits you most today: data, systems, skills, rules, ownership, budget or trust? | [all] | Binding constraint |
| F9 | What must not change: rules, client commitments, independence, confidentiality? Which steps must stay with a person? | [all] | Guardrails |
| F10 | Is there anything you would rather we did not touch, and why? | [all] | Boundaries; often reveals politics or past failures |
| F11 | Who owns AI outcomes here: the business, technology, both, or no one? What can they decide? | [U/O] | Ownership [BCG-GAP-2025] |
| F12 | How much does the most senior sponsor use AI in their own work? | [U/O] | Real versus delegated sponsorship |
| F13 | Who will hold this plan to account, with what budget, and by when? | [U/O] | Substitutes for owner control, incentives and a fixed date [ACHARYA-2009] |
| F14 | In one sentence, why should your area be worth more, or less, in three years? | [U/O] | The thesis [BDEMERSON-2026] |
| F15 | Which parts of your area could AI threaten, which could it change a lot, and which would it mainly help? | [U/O] | Exposure sort [BAIN-DILIGENCE-2025] |
| F16 | If a well-funded newcomer rebuilt your core service with today's AI, what would they build, and how fast? | [U/O] | AI-native entrant |
| F17 | If the work gets cheaper to do, who keeps the gain: you, your internal clients, or your customers? | [U/O] | Whether gains are competed away [BAIN-DILIGENCE-2025] |
| F18 | Which parts of the work take the most effort, cause the most complaints, or get redone most often? | [all] | Choosing what to map |

**Follow-ups.** What happened to the last improvement or technology project here? [U/O] · Where do you suspect the
written procedure and real practice differ? [U/O] · Is there a stated multi-year target for AI, and who set it? [U/O]
· Where are many people doing near-identical knowledge tasks? [all] [BAIN-DILIGENCE-2025] · If you had to split your area
into parts that each need their own plan, where are the natural seams: service, client type or process? [U/O] (feeds Break down).

**By journey stage.**
- *Curious:* keep it light. One ambition sentence, two or three levers. Success means people using AI weekly on real
  work, a visible quick win and a safe-use rule.
- *Experimenting:* success means one shared workflow with a baseline. Ask F6 and "where do saved hours go?" now.
- *Adopting:* success means owned workflows in production with measured value. Add quality and risk levers.
- *Scaling:* success means value tracked across a portfolio and reuse. Ask F11, F13 and the operating-model questions.
- *AI-native:* exposure, pricing and new services lead (F14–F17).

## 4. Value levers and scoring weights

**Levers.** The seven defaults, the metric each usually takes in knowledge work, and the proof it needs. The seven are
this advisor's working set, not a published standard; each needs a different metric and level of proof. Quality and risk
are often the real value in professional work and the levers most often left out.

| Lever | Typical metric | Proof needed |
|---|---|---|
| capacity | Hours released and where they go; items per person | Operating data baseline, plus a named destination for the hours |
| speed | Cycle time, request to delivery; time an item waits | Timestamps on real cases |
| quality | Rework rate, review notes, error or return rate | A written definition of a defect, checked on a sample |
| growth | Revenue, win rate, new services, realisation | An attribution method (comparison group or staged start) |
| cost | Cost to serve; external spend (contractors, outsourcing) | Finance-signed baseline |
| risk | Findings, incidents, exceptions caught | An expected-loss view or incident history |
| experience | Client and staff satisfaction, effort | A survey or interview instrument |

**Weights by stage (advisor heuristic, not evidence).** Early stages usually weight capacity and speed; the middle
stages add quality and risk; later stages add growth and new services.

**Scoring weights.** Defaults: value 0.30, feasibility 0.20, data readiness 0.15, risk 0.15 (higher score means lower
risk), time to value 0.10, strategic fit 0.10. Change them only with a reason the user agrees, and keep the sum at 1.
Common reasons (advisor heuristics): regulated or inspection-exposed work raises risk; a Curious team that needs early
wins raises time to value; a Scaling portfolio raises strategic fit. Children inherit these settings unless they override them.

## 5. Expert moves

| Move | When | What it reveals |
|---|---|---|
| **Ask for success in P&L or KPI terms at twelve months** (F1, F5) | Opening Frame | Whether the ambition is efficiency only. McKinsey's high performers named growth (82%) and innovation (79%) objectives far more often than others (50% each); self-reported and correlational [MCKINSEY-SOAI-2025] |
| **Ask what they would rebuild if tools were free** (F3) before asking which tools they want | Early Frame | Workflow versus tool thinking. 55% of high performers reported fundamentally redesigned workflows against 20% of others (self-reported) [MCKINSEY-SOAI-2025] |
| **Ask how the work is priced and what happens if effort halves** (F6) | Fee-based work, Experimenting and above | Margin compression hidden by an efficiency plan. Sage's worked arithmetic: a job cut from 8 hours to 3 loses more than half its revenue if billed hourly; on a fixed fee it frees 5 hours that earn nothing unless they are put to productive use [SAGE-2026] |
| **Ask who captures the gain** (F17) | Services lines, internal service units | Whether clients or internal customers will expect lower fees or budgets [BAIN-DILIGENCE-2025] |
| **Write the thesis in one sentence** (F14) | [U/O] | Why the unit is worth more or less; forces specificity [BDEMERSON-2026] |
| **Run a full-potential pass:** revenue, operational and technology levers, not just the cost case | [U/O] | Upside a cost-only frame misses; Bain urges sponsors to move "from full potential diligence to execution on Day 1" [BAIN-GPER-2026; BAIN-DILIGENCE-2025] |
| **Sort each sub-unit:** AI threatens the model, changes it substantially, or mainly helps | [U/O], before any use-case hunt | Which sub-units need reinvention and which need adoption. In Bain's diligence work, under 10% of 300+ companies fell in the most threatened group and about half in "mainly helps" (vendor-published) [BAIN-DILIGENCE-2025] |
| **Prototype the core deliverable with off-the-shelf AI** | [U/O], when exposure is disputed | What a newcomer could copy and where the moat really sits: data, workflow, relationships [BAIN-DILIGENCE-2025] |
| **Ask who owns AI: business, technology or both** (F11) | [U/O] | BCG calls exclusive IT ownership "a key indicator of stagnating companies"; over 40% of its top tier embed shared ownership against 19% of stagnating firms (self-reported) [BCG-GAP-2025] |
| **Ask what will stand in for owner control, incentives and a fixed date** (F13) | [U/O] | A unit leader gets PE's method without its forcing functions: a sponsor, funded targets and a dated review must substitute [ACHARYA-2009] |
| **Ask "would finance accept this number?"** (F5) | Setting measures | Whether the measure is one finance already trusts, not self-reported time saved; 72% of leaders in one survey say they formally measure Gen AI ROI, mostly as productivity gains (self-reported) [WHARTON-2025] |
| **Treat the leader's account as work-as-imagined;** write hypotheses for Map | Leaders, [U/O] | Where practice differs from belief; the cheap wins usually sit in that gap [HOLLNAGEL-2017] |
| **Choose what to map by pain, frequency, impact and readiness,** not by what demos well (F18) | End of Frame | Avoids visible-use-case bias: executives in MIT NANDA's study steered hypothetical budgets to sales and marketing, partly because results there are easier to attribute (interview-based, preliminary) [MIT-NANDA-2025; OPENAI-ACADEMY-2025] |
| **Pair efficiency with a growth or quality measure** | Setting levers | Wharton/GBK report 35% strongly positive ROI among leaders tracking a growth measure against 13% tracking efficiency only (self-reported association) [WHARTON-2026] |
| **State the capacity stance early** (redeploy to backlog, growth or quality; absorb through hiring pace; or reduce) | Before mapping | Whether the case is growth or cost, and how staff will read the project; Wharton/GBK advise rebuilding how work is managed rather than cutting headcount [WHARTON-2026] |

## 6. Common traps

| Trap | Why it happens | How to avoid it |
|---|---|---|
| Efficiency-only ambition | Cost cases are easy to write and defend | Ask F2 and F3; add a quality, risk or growth objective where the remit allows [MCKINSEY-SOAI-2025] |
| Framing around tools | Vendors and licences are visible | Frame around outcomes and workflows; tools come in Reimagine |
| Top-down percentage targets | "Save 20%" is easy to announce | Build value bar by bar from initiatives later; here, set direction and measures only [BDEMERSON-2026] |
| Ignoring pricing in fee-based work | Efficiency looks like pure gain | Ask F6; put pricing on the agenda from Experimenting on [SAGE-2026] |
| Leader's account taken as fact | Leaders see work as imagined | Write it as hypotheses; test in Map [HOLLNAGEL-2017] |
| Technology-only ownership | AI treated as an IT rollout | Name a business owner with decision rights [BCG-GAP-2025] |
| Importing PE levers the unit lacks | The method is described without owner control and incentives | Name substitutes: sponsor, budget, dated review [ACHARYA-2009] |
| Quoting headline statistics bare | Viral numbers hide definitions | Give what was measured, by whom, and provenance [MIT-NANDA-2025] |
| Ambition out of step with the stage | Enthusiasm, or caution | Match measures to the stage (§3); plan the next stage only |
| Headcount targets as the goal | Cost pressure | Frame capacity and roles; rule 1 in `SKILL.md` forbids headcount targets for people |

## 7. Worked example

*Illustrative, continuing the audit-partner profile from `onboarding.md`; module facts from the assurance
controls-testing module.* After F1 the partner says: "Get through the season without burning out the seniors, and stop
getting inspection points on controls testing." The advisor plays it back and asks F2. The partner adds: "And spend more
time on risk, less on ticking." F6: the audits are fixed-fee and fees are under pressure, so saved hours become margin
only if clients do not demand lower fees (F17: "the bigger clients will ask").

*Exposure sort (illustrative).* Controls testing is *changed substantially*: firms are repackaging testing as platform
plus review, clients are moving co-sourced testing in-house, and hourly pricing is under pressure (module §7).
Acceptance and independence is *mainly helped*. The advisor marks this as a judgment to test, not a finding.

*Measures.* Testing hours per key control (KPMG puts the average at 16 hours per control in FY2024, up from 12 in FY2022,
for SOX programmes [K-SOX30]; the practice needs its own figure); review notes per file; inspection points on controls testing;
staff overtime in the season. *Capacity stance:* redeploy freed hours to staff gaps and risk work; no reduction planned;
revisit per opportunity. *Weights:* risk raised to 0.20 and time to value lowered to 0.05, because the practice is
inspection-exposed and willing to take longer to be safe. *What to map:* the key-control testing flow on two
engagements, from evidence request to sign-off. *Hypothesis for Map:* "review is the bottleneck, not testing".

## 8. Output

Write `settings` into the top plan's frontmatter (children inherit them) and a **Frame** section in the body.

```yaml
settings:
  value_levers:
    - { id: capacity, name: "Capacity", metric: "Testing hours per key control", weight: 0.30 }
    - { id: quality, name: "Quality", metric: "Review notes per file; inspection points", weight: 0.30 }
    - { id: risk, name: "Risk", metric: "Exceptions found and resolved before sign-off", weight: 0.20 }
    - { id: experience, name: "Staff experience", metric: "Season overtime hours", weight: 0.20 }
  scoring_weights: { value: 0.30, feasibility: 0.20, data_readiness: 0.15, risk: 0.20, time_to_value: 0.05, strategic_fit: 0.10 }
  guardrails: [ "Independence rules apply to every tool", "No client data in public AI tools", "A person signs off every conclusion and the opinion" ]
  overlay: null
phase_completed: 1
next_phase: 2
```

Lever ids come from the vocabulary: capacity, speed, quality, growth, cost, risk, experience. The **Frame** body
section holds: the ambition sentence; why now; a success-measures table (measure, today's value or source, direction,
who accepts it); the levers and weights with reasons; any scoring-weight change and its reason; guardrails and
boundaries; owner and sponsor; the capacity stance; [U/O] the exposure table (sub-unit, threaten / change / help, why,
confidence) and the value tree; the hypotheses to test; and what Map covers next.
