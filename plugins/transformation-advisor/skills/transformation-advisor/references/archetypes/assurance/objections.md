# Assurance objections

Last researched 2026-10-17. Use this when a practitioner pushes back. Each response gives the evidence and its
provenance; concede what is true. Objections about a single step (for example "our external auditor won't rely on
internal audit's agents") are in the module files' "Common objections" sections. Source keys in brackets resolve in §8
or in [sources.md](sources.md).

## 1. Regulation and inspection

| Objection | Response |
|---|---|
| "The standards don't cover AI, so we can't use it." | No regulator that has spoken prohibits AI. The FRC issued guidance and opened a sandbox; the IAASB chose guidance over a requirement [FRC-NEWS-2026; FRC-SANDBOX-2026; IAASB-ED500-2026]. PCAOB staff recorded firms saying standards were not impediments [PCAOB-GENAI-2024]. The gap is methodology and evidence, not permission. Ask which document they have in mind. |
| "Inspectors will punish us if the AI gets something wrong." | The FRC says its guidance is not a response to identified deficiencies [FRC-NEWS-2026]. What inspectors test is governance and judgement: certification, monitoring, documentation and challenge [IFIAR-TTF-2026]. The one public finding concerned limited input validation and no testing of low-risk-rated items, not AI use as such [CPAB-EVOLVING-2025]. |
| "Documenting AI use will bury the file." | The FRC asks for proportionate documentation, with more held centrally the wider a tool is used, and says outputs checked back to cited sources may need less [FRC-AIAUDIT-2025]. |
| "Human in the loop is enough." | Regulators want it specified: named owners for decision checkpoints and a review of autonomous decisions [IFIAR-TTF-2026]; CPAB says statements that audits stay human-led are not enough without defined involvement at each stage [CPAB-EVOLVING-2025]. |
| "The EU AI Act doesn't touch us." | Possibly true for audit tools: Annex III as published has no audit category. But the AI literacy duty applies to deployers, staffing and performance tools may fall under employment, and clients may be in scope from December 2027 [AIACT-ANNEX3; GIBSONDUNN-AIOMNIBUS-2026]. |

## 2. Quality, reliability and evidence

| Objection | Response |
|---|---|
| "AI isn't reliable enough for audit evidence." | Partly true, and regulators agree it must be controlled: tool output meets the same evidence bar and tools are revalidated when models change [NFRA-CAALLEY-2026; IFIAR-TTF-2026]. Lab results are mixed: a benchmark found models do much staff-level work but sometimes use inappropriate procedures [ARXIV-FAB-2026]. Start where output can be checked against cited evidence, and measure override rates. |
| "Testing 100% of items replaces sampling and judgement." | Whole-population testing does not remove risk assessment; insight on each item may be less conclusive; low-risk classifications should be tested [NFRA-CAALLEY-2026; FRC-GENAI-2026]. CPAB found one tool's low-risk items left untested [CPAB-EVOLVING-2025]. |
| "Our vendor is certified, so we are covered." | A firm cannot rely on vendor terms without validation [NFRA-CAALLEY-2026]. Third-party tools need the same documented rationale, with independent assurance as one route [FRC-AIAUDIT-2025]. A vendor's own certification covers its platform, not your use of it [AIUC-KPMG-2026]. |
| "Review time will eat the savings." | It can. PwC calls savings "not that drastic yet" [CFODIVE-PWC-2026], and 31% of IFIAR members found tools not used as intended [IFIAR-TTF-2025]. Design the review (cited evidence, risk-based re-performance) and budget for parallel running at first. |
| "The vendor numbers prove the case." | Nearly every time-saving figure in this field is vendor-published or a single customer quote [DATASNIPPER-2026; TR-AGENTIC-2025]. No independent accuracy measure exists for any audit AI tool in live use. Ask for a parallel run on one engagement. |

## 3. Economics and pricing

| Objection | Response |
|---|---|
| "AI will collapse audit fees; we must cut prices now." | Audit revenue grew at all four networks in the latest years reported [KPMG-REV-2025; EY-REV-2026; CPAPA-DELREV-2026]. The one AI-linked audit-fee cut is a single small audit, reported second-hand [TFS-KPMGFEE-2026]. Buyer pressure is real (25-30% cuts after competitive reviews, self-reported) but not shown to be caused by AI [FERF-2026]. |
| "AI savings are being passed through to clients." | The documented discounts are PwC consulting work, and PwC said they had plateaued [BBL-PWCPRICE-2025; SFN-PWCPRICE-2025]. |
| "The billable hour is dead." | 76% of 54 small US firms still track billable hours and 13% track value-billed engagements [CPACOM-ATR-2025]. Moves away from the hour are recommendations, not measured behaviour. But hours saved compress revenue unless pricing changes, so put pricing on the table early. |
| "The Big Four have already automated the audit; we can't compete." | The announcements are roll-outs and roadmaps; PwC's own leader says the report "still looks the same" [CFODIVE-PWC-2026]. Most tool use is concentrated in a few audit areas [IFIAR-TTF-2025]. Vendors rent much of the capability [TR-DISCLOSURE-2026; FIELDGUIDE-2026]. |
| "AI-native firms will take our clients." | They exist and are funded, but start with small or private-company audits, report no market share, and carry professional liability like anyone else [REPODO-2026; MODUS-2026]. Watch them; do not assume them. |
| "Private equity will ruin audit quality." | Regulators call it a risk and will inspect for it [CAALLEY-PCAOBPE-2025]; they also name benefits such as technology and succession [FRC-PCLETTER-2024]. No study yet measures an effect on quality or fees. |

## 4. People and skills

| Objection | Response |
|---|---|
| "AI means fewer auditors." | Mixed evidence. Some firms cut graduate intake and partners [OMANOBS-GT-2026; BBL-KPMGPARTNERS-2026]; a peer-reviewed study found AI-adopting audit offices hired more junior and mid-level auditors (authors' summary) [ISCA-LAWSHEN-2025]; BLS projects 5% growth to 2035 [BLS-OOH-2026]. Attribution of cuts to AI is unproven. |
| "We have a talent shortage, so AI can't hurt talent." | The shortage is at the experienced layer, while cuts are at entry level [FRC-ARAQ-2026; OMANOBS-GT-2026]. AI pressure on the entry layer and a shortage of experienced reviewers can coexist, and make each other worse. |
| "Juniors will learn by reviewing AI output." | Firms say so [YAHOO-KOSAR-2025]; the PCAOB's acting chair says new paths will be needed [NYSSCPA-BOTIC-2025]. The only controlled study (software, not audit) found delegation cut learning and engagement preserved it [ARXIV-SKILL-2026]. Treat it as a risk to design for. See [workforce.md](workforce.md) §7. |
| "Our people don't want AI." | 81% of tax and audit professionals say they use AI regularly, more than a third admit using unauthorised tools, and almost a third would consider leaving if AI expectations go unmet (vendor-run) [TRI-FOP-2026]. |
| "Internal audit can cut headcount because AI does the testing." | Budget cuts rose in 2025, but the survey does not attribute them to AI, and strategically aligned functions report better funding [JOFA-IAPULSE-2026]. Only 28% of leaders feel ready to audit AI risks [OPTRO-FOF-2026]. |

## 5. Data and clients

| Objection | Response |
|---|---|
| "Our clients are small and will never give us system access." | 53% of small US firms cite lack of access, yet 39% already automate confirmations and 35% trial-balance import, which need less client cooperation [CPACOM-ATR-2025]. Bank consent flows can bypass the client's files (self-reported case) [CIRCIT-CS-BDONL-2026]. |
| "AI will read whatever the client sends; we don't need integrations." | The IAASB says it may be impossible to evaluate GenAI tool inputs and penalises unstructured inputs whose integrity may not survive extraction [IAASB-ED500-2026]. The FRC's own examples rely on a data-quality gate [FRC-AIAUDIT-2025]. |
| "Forged documents are a bank problem, not an audit one." | Authenticity is already a reliability attribute in the draft evidence standard [IAASB-ED500-2026], and 75% of anti-fraud professionals saw GenAI document forgery rise [ACFE-SAS-2026]. Audit-specific incidence is unmeasured, so this supports a design check, not a prevalence claim. |
| "Our clients aren't asking about AI." | Audit partners say about two-thirds of audit committees have raised it, and rate most clients' AI governance as early or developing [BOARDMEMBER-CAQ-2026]. The demand is for governance and quality evidence, not necessarily for tools. |

## 6. Independence, liability and insurance

| Objection | Response |
|---|---|
| "Licensing an AI tool can't be an independence issue." | It is unresolved. AICPA ethics staff warn that tool-provider terms shifting control, fees or evidence access can create threats in SOC work and may apply more widely [JOFA-SOCETH-2026]; the SEC and PCAOB are reviewing independence rules [TR-HOHL-2026; PCAOB-SSR-PAGE-2026]. Get independence advice before vendor equity, referral or fee-sharing terms. |
| "If the tool is wrong, the vendor is liable." | A law firm expects liability not to shift to the provider and an AI error to be "unlikely, by itself" a defence; it is untested [DAC-AUDITAI-2026]. |
| "Insurance will cover it." | Underwriters say standard cover generally includes AI-related negligence unless excluded, and exclusions may follow claims; this is not audit-specific [BUSINESSINS-AIEO-2026]. Check the wording at renewal. |

## 7. How to respond well

- Concede first what is true: evidence of quality is missing everywhere in this field.
- Separate measured, announced and forecast ([state-of-ai.md](state-of-ai.md) §7).
- Turn the objection into a design requirement: a control point, a metric, a parallel run or an independence review.
- If the objection is a fear for jobs, switch to the approach in [people-and-change.md](../../method/people-and-change.md).
