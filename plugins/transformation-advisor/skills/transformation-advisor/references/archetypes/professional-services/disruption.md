# Professional-services disruption scenarios

Last researched 2026-10-19. Use this at unit or firm scope, in Frame (how exposed is this business) and in Reimagine
lens 4 (reinvent). Each scenario separates what exists, what is forecast (and by whom) and what would have to be true.
Every example names its firm type. No source assigns probabilities; neither does this file. Source keys in brackets
resolve in §10 or in [sources.md](sources.md).

## 1. The short version

| Scenario | Evidence status | Who it hits first | Signal to watch |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Fee model shifts | Large consultancies and IT services report outcome fees; law mostly hourly; client pressure measured in surveys | Hourly law and advisory; repeatable work | Share of fees by model; client AI terms; published fixed or outcome deals |
| 2. AI-native firms and productised services | Launched and authorised; no volume, margin or quality data | Commoditised, document-based work | Client counts, prices, insurance, regulator actions |
| 3. Outside capital | Deals closed in accounting; MSO workarounds in law; state pushback | Mid-market accounting; mid-size law; owner-managed firms | Deal volume; AICPA and state rules; investors' margins |
| 4. Pyramid reshapes | Hiring expectations falling; current leverage rising; one trial on skills | Graduate intake; business services; junior learning | First-year classes; leverage data; junior skill measures |
| 5. Clients insource | Stated intentions in vendor surveys; no measured volume moved | Routine legal work sent to firms | Outside-counsel spend; panel changes |
| 6. Labs and platforms move in | Launches and funding; press-reported deployment arms | Technology consulting; legal tooling vendors; firms' implementation work | Lab services revenue; lab-direct deals with firms and clients |

**Counter-evidence to a collapse story is substantial.** Accenture's FY26 consulting revenue rose 5% in US dollars
[ACCENTURE-Q4FY26]; PwC's FY26 revenue rose 5.7% [AT-PWC-2026]; Am Law 100 worked rates rose almost 10% in Q1 2026
[TRI-LFFI-Q1-2026]. The pressure so far shows in pricing, mix and headcount, not aggregate revenue.

## 2. Three questions leaders mix up

Keep apart **how work gets done** (changing fast), **how it is priced** (changing slowly and unevenly) and **who owns
the firm** (changing through structures that skip the hardest rules). Evidence moves at a different speed for each
(an advisor reading of the sources below). Thomson Reuters offers three illustrative paths, drawn from how respondents
describe their organisations, not market forecasts: **Elevate** (human expertise at the centre), **Scale** (capacity
without proportional headcount) and **Reimagine** (subscription advisory, productised services, AI-native practices)
[TR-FOP-REPORT-2026].

## 3. Scenario 1: the fee model shifts

**The picture.** Hours stop tracking value once AI does much of the effort, so fees move to fixed, hybrid, outcome or
subscription bases.

- **Exists (consulting and IT services).** McKinsey (strategy consulting) says about a quarter of its global fees are
  outcome-based, a rounded self-reported figure; a McKinsey technology leader cited 20% as "probably more recent", and a secondary
  summary says roughly a third of its *work* is tied to performance fees, a different measure [BI-MCKINSEY-2025;
  RESULTSENSE-2026]. TCS (IT services) says about 80% of contracts in one business-services segment use outcome
  measures [REUTERS-ITSERVICES-2026].
- **Exists (law and accounting).** Keypoint Law (Australian law firm) prices fixed plus hourly [LAWYERSWEEKLY-KEYPOINT-2026];
  Kopka Law (US insurance-defence firm) runs a KPI-based retainer [TRI-KOPKA-2026]; Goodwin (law) benchmarks fixed-fee
  quotes against market ranges [GOODWIN-PERSUIT-2025]; KPMG, PwC and RSM (accounting) are reported brainstorming value
  and outcome pricing, and RKL is piloting it [BLOOMBERGTAX-PRICING-2026].
- **Subscription.** PwC's US CEO said some tax and consulting services would become tools clients use "without a PwC
  person in the loop", potentially by annual subscription; PwC One launched with six automated services, with no
  published price [CPAPA-PWC-2026].
- **Still mostly hourly.** About 90% of US legal dollars are hourly [TRI-SOTLM-REPORT-2026]; 86% of solo and 46% of
  enterprise law firms have not changed pricing since adopting AI (vendor) [CLIO-SOLO-2026].
- **Forecasts.** Brynjolfsson and Petropoulos (Stanford): AI pushes consulting toward hybrid and asset-based pricing;
  pure time and materials survives only at the margins (theory-based; the lab discloses a general gift from Accenture)
  [STANFORD-DEL-2026]. Forrester (analyst blog): providers will reprice and put fees at risk [FORRESTER-BLOG-2025]. HFS
  (analyst): 56% of buyers of AI-enabled IT and business services prefer base fee plus outcome kicker, while 54% of
  current engagements are effort-based (adjacent market) [HFS-SAS-2026].
- **What would have to be true.** Clean cost-to-deliver data; outcome baselines and attribution; partner pay not tied
  to hours; fee agreements that explain AI ([guardrails.md](guardrails.md) §5).
- **Counter-view.** Tech Mahindra (IT services) declined to guarantee prices on assumed 70-80% gains and Infosys walked
  away from uneconomic contracts [REUTERS-ITSERVICES-2026]. McKinsey says its outcome pricing did not start because of
  AI [BI-MCKINSEY-2025].
- **For the leader.** Decide where AI savings go before clients decide for you; pilot a fixed or hybrid fee on one
  repeatable matter type with measured cost-to-deliver ([scoping-pricing](modules/scoping-pricing.md)).

## 4. Scenario 2: AI-native firms and productised services

**The picture.** New firms built on agents plus experienced reviewers sell outcomes or documents, not hours.

- **Exists (law).** Garfield.Law (UK law firm) was authorised by the SRA in May 2025 for small-claims debt recovery up
  to £10,000; it is "not autonomous" and named solicitors stay accountable [GLP-GARFIELD-2025]. Crosby (US law firm)
  advertises fixed rates by the document with attorneys reviewing all work [CROSBY-SITE-2026]. Falcon (Belgian law
  firm) offers six contract reviews a month for a flat EUR 1,000 [NONBILLABLE-FALCON-2026]. General Legal (US law firm
  with an MSO structure) reports about 400 clients six months after Y Combinator (self-reported) [LAWSITES-GENERAL-2026].
- **Exists (consulting and accounting).** Queen's Tower Advisory (UK consulting challenger) targets "20 per cent
  humans, 80 per cent AI agents" (a target, press-reported) [RESULTSENSE-2026]. Ascend (PE-backed accounting platform)
  has a 30-person AI team; its CEO says no off-the-shelf AI-native suite exists today [AT-AINATIVE-2026]. Crete
  (Thrive-backed accounting roll-up) planned more than $500m of acquisitions to equip firms with OpenAI-powered tools
  [REUTERS-CRETE-2025].
- **Productised incumbents.** Debevoise (law) offers its STAAR AI-risk platform to clients, reported as a subscription;
  A&O Shearman (law) licenses tools through a revenue-sharing arrangement with Harvey [DEBEVOISE-BLOOMBERG-2026;
  ABAJ-SHARE-2026]. PwC One is the clearest large-firm case [CPAPA-PWC-2026].
- **Not known.** No revenue, volume, margin or error data for any AI-native firm or PwC One.
- **What would have to be true.** Regulators keep authorising; insurers cover the model; experienced reviewers can keep
  pace with agent volume; clients accept a product in place of a relationship.
- **Counter-view.** Most examples are small or targets; Ascend's CEO warns a one-person firm with agents risks being "an
  AI-powered job rather than an AI-powered business" [AT-AINATIVE-2026].
- **For the leader.** Ask which of your services could be sold as a product, and whether you would rather build it or
  compete with it ([firm-finance](modules/firm-finance.md) `#17`).

## 5. Scenario 3: outside capital reshapes ownership

**The picture.** Private equity and other investors fund technology and consolidation, changing who owns the firm and
what returns it must make.

- **Accounting (furthest along).** Crowe (No. 12 US firm) closed a KKR investment in August 2026 for talent, technology
  and innovation, separating attest from non-attest services, then launched CroweONE, a shared ownership plan open to
  all employees [AT-CROWE-KKR-2026; AT-CROWE-2026]. Grant Thornton agreed to buy CBIZ for $5bn with New Mountain Capital
  backing (announced) [BGOV-GTCBIZ-2026]. A law firm's publication says PE holds stakes in roughly a third of the 30
  largest US accounting firms (unverified, and the same document says "top 300" elsewhere) [FOXROTHSCHILD-MSO-2026].
- **Law (structure-driven).** Rule 5.4 bars fee-sharing with non-lawyers in most US states. Investors favour MSOs, which
  finance the back office, including "artificial intelligence improvements", over Arizona's licensed structures
  [BLOOMBERGLAW-AZ-2026]. A reported $700m letter of intent for a stake in Wood Smith Henning & Berman (US law firm) runs
  through an MSO; Colorado enacted a fee-sharing ban in August 2026 [BESTLAW-WSHB-2026]. McDermott Will & Emery (US law
  firm) confirmed it was exploring selling a stake; that is an exploration, not a deal [FOXROTHSCHILD-MSO-2026].
- **Tax advisory (Europe).** European PE committed more than EUR 500m to WTS (press-reported) [RESULTSENSE-2026].
- **Forecast.** An Apax co-CEO called hourly-billing, non-audit bookkeeping-type firms vulnerable (FT via a Dutch outlet)
  [VANMORGEN-SUPERRETURN-2026].
- **Counter-view.** Blake Oliver (commentator) argues PE finds efficiencies within a model but is not designed to
  reinvent it [BLAKEOLIVER-PE-2026]; fewer UK mid-tier firms are considering PE [ICAEW-MIDTIER-2026]; Winston cites UK
  failures such as Slater and Gordon [WINSTON-PE-2026]; an academic argues no state bar has MSO governance standards
  [COLUMBIA-BREYDO-2026]. Audit independence limits are in [assurance](../assurance/ARCHETYPE.md).
- **For the leader.** Know what your structure allows before choosing a business-model path; capital turns AI into a
  financing question ([economics.md](economics.md) §7).

## 6. Scenario 4: the leverage pyramid changes shape

**The picture.** AI does much of the junior work, so firms hire fewer juniors and become diamond- or pod-shaped.

- **Exists.** Large US law-firm graduate hires fell 7.5% in 2025; UK Big Four graduate intakes fell from 2023 to 2024
  [BLOOMBERGLAW-HIRING-2026; SFN-BIG4-2025]. Business-services cuts at Baker McKenzie and Clifford Chance (law) cite AI
  among reasons [LEGALCHEEK-BAKER-2026; CITYAM-CC-2025].
- **But leverage is rising now.** US law-firm non-equity to equity partner hours are at their highest since late 2018
  and associate demand grew in Q2 2026 [TRI-LFFI-Q2-2026].
- **Forecasts.** Citi's survey of 57 large law firms: two-thirds expect fewer first-years in 2028 than 2025, 19% do not
  know [BLOOMBERGLAW-HIRING-2026]. Forrester: diamond or pod structures proposed, pipeline still needed [FORRESTER-BLOG-2025].
- **Skills risk.** In the one randomised trial, lasting gains went to seniors and juniors split (Google-run) [NBER-AUTOR-2026].
- **What would have to be true.** A new way to build judgment; partners who can review agent output at volume.
- **For the leader.** Treat support-layer cuts, intake size and skill formation as three decisions
  ([workforce.md](workforce.md) §6).

## 7. Scenario 5: clients take work in house

- **Stated intentions (vendor surveys, legal).** 68% of 130+ in-house lawyers said more than 10% of outside work could
  be insourced with AI [JURO-INHOUSE-2026]; Axiom (alternative legal services provider) reports "80%+" of departments
  plan to bring significant work in house or to alternative providers within 24 months [AXIOM-GC-2026]; 64% of 657
  in-house respondents expect less reliance on outside counsel [ACCEVERLAW-2025].
- **Not measured.** No source measures work actually moved. Consulting and accounting clients: almost nothing public.
- **For the leader.** Ask which clients could take work back first, and what you would offer instead
  ([client-development](modules/client-development.md)).

## 8. Scenario 6: labs and platforms move into services

- **Exists.** OpenAI launched a majority-owned Deployment Company on 11 May 2026 and agreed to buy an applied-AI
  consultancy; Decrypt reports Anthropic announced a $1.5bn deployment venture days earlier (press-reported)
  [DECRYPT-DEPLOYCO-2026]. Four AI majors launched legal offerings in five months [LAWSITES-MSFT-2026].
- **New demand too.** Financial-services buyers are most likely to hire consultants for risk and AI ethics under
  frameworks such as the EU AI Act [SOURCE-PRECISION-2026].
- **For the leader.** Decide which client work you would not want a lab-backed firm or a software vendor doing, and
  what you offer that they cannot.

## 9. Exposure by kind of work

| Kind of work | Exposure | Why |
|---|---|---|
| High-volume, document-based, repeatable (contract review, standard drafting, routine filings) | High | AI-native firms and per-document pricing target it [CROSBY-SITE-2026; NONBILLABLE-FALCON-2026] |
| Hourly advisory with junior-heavy staffing | High on price | Saved hours cannot be billed; clients ask for savings [TRI-LEGALWEEK-2026] |
| Implementation and technology consulting | Medium to high | Labs and platforms entering; outcome fees spreading [DECRYPT-DEPLOYCO-2026; REUTERS-ITSERVICES-2026] |
| Bet-the-company, novel or relationship-led work | Lower | Judgment and accountability stay human in every source read |
| Regulated attest work | Field-specific | See [assurance](../assurance/disruption.md) and [tax](../tax/disruption.md) |

Exposure ratings are advisor judgments from the evidence cited, not source findings.
