## 1. Roles

| Setting | Roles | Where AI touches them first |
|---|---|---|
| Law firms | Trainees and associates, senior associates, non-equity and equity partners; paralegals; business services (billing, BD, KM, IT, resourcing) | Research, review, first drafts, time narratives; business-services roles re-scoped |
| Consultancies | Analysts and consultants, managers, principals and partners; resource managers; PMO and delivery leads | Analysis, decks and documents, status reporting, staffing proposals |
| Accounting and advisory (firm side) | Associates, seniors, managers, partners; offshore and shared-service teams | First-pass preparation and review moves to AI; review load shifts upward (detail in assurance and tax) |
| Engineering and architecture | Project staff, project managers, principals; proposal teams | Proposals, project reporting, documentation |
| In-house legal and shared services | Lawyers, legal operations, paralegals, contract managers | Intake, first-pass drafting and review, outside-counsel invoice review |
| New hybrid roles | Legal engineers, AI and innovation leads, pricing professionals, knowledge curators, AI engineers | Created by AI work itself |

## 2. The talent model and the pyramid

- **The pyramid pays twice.** Junior work has "always served two purposes: billing and training" [VAULT-PYRAMID-2026].
  If AI absorbs that work, the firm loses both a revenue line and the training ground at once.
- **Current data show leverage rising, not collapsing (US law).** In Q2 2026 associate demand grew 4.3% and non-equity
  partner demand 6.0% while equity partner demand fell 1.2% [TRI-LFFI-COSTS-2026]; the ratio of non-equity to equity
  partner hours is the highest since late 2018 [TRI-LFFI-Q2-2026]. Associate demand leverage hit a record 1.55 in Q4
  2025, then 1.47 [TRI-LFFI-LEVERAGE-2026].
- **Alternative shapes are discussed, not proven.** Forrester notes "the death of the pyramid has been pundit fodder
  for a decade" and that diamond or pod structures are proposed, while consultancies still need a pipeline
  [FORRESTER-BLOG-2025]. In one survey 14% of law firms expect agents to do most work
  ([talent-learning](modules/talent-learning.md) `#20`, grade C).
- **AI-native entrants skip the base.** General Legal's chief executive says an AI-native firm depends on hiring highly
  experienced lawyers rather than supercharging juniors [LAWSITES-GENERAL-2026]; Queen's Tower Advisory targets
  "20 per cent humans, 80 per cent AI agents" (a target) [RESULTSENSE-2026]. Scenarios: [disruption.md](disruption.md) §6.

## 4. How roles are evolving

- **Entry level:** from producing first drafts to reviewing and validating AI output ([talent-learning](modules/talent-learning.md)
  `#07`). PwC's assurance leader expects first-years to work like fourth-years within three years (an expectation)
  [BI-PWC-2025]. One junior accountant warns that giving juniors AI oversight too early is a risk [ICAEW-WORRY-2026].
- **New junior routes:** AI seats and innovation-group roles; Sidley made an AI seat compulsory for London trainees
  [LEGALCHEEK-SIDLEY-2026]; legal engineers [LEGORA-MINTERELLISON-2026].
- **Mid-level:** a training provider that ran a survey suggests mid-level staff become "workflow engineers"
  [LAW360-GA-2026]; engagement leads at one consultancy run teams of people and agents
  ([delivery-management](modules/delivery-management.md) `#14`).
- **Partners:** senior lawyers' mentoring and supervision become critical to teaching verification (one expert's view)
  [TRI-TEACHV-2026]; in one survey, 55% of respondents (74% in law firms)
  named partners the least prepared group [LAW360-GA-2026]. PwC's US CEO said partners who resist AI will have no place at
  the firm (FT interview, as reported) [CPAPA-PWC-2026].
- **Resource managers:** from filling hours to composing teams and owning delivery risk if outcome pricing grows
  [KANTATA-RMI-2026].
- **Pricing, data and AI roles:** pricing professionals model AI-assisted matters [AMBITION-PRICINGMANAGER-2026]; Kirkland reports 180+ AI engineers
  and data scientists [BLOOMBERGLAW-KIRKLAND-2026]; ILTA firms expect added headcount in IT, innovation, security,
  training, KM and data science [ILTA-TECHSURVEY-2026].
- **Business services:** law-firm COOs at a roundtable expected fewer billing administrators and more technology, data
  and AI enablement roles [WESTERNALLIANCE-COO-2026].

## 5. Skills

- **What firms say they now hire for:** Fredrikson & Byron hires and assesses for judgment, learning agility and
  working with AI ([talent-learning](modules/talent-learning.md) `#13`). A TRI series says GPA, school and technical
  aptitude say little about performance when the answer is unclear, and that accounting firms should hire for
  "figure-it-out" ability [TRI-TAAP2030-2026].
- **Verification is the core new skill.** UK legal professionals see junior gaps in reasoning (72%) and verification
  (69%); 2% say AI mainly strengthens learning (vendor survey of 873) [LEXIS-MENTORSHIP-2026].
- **AI-enabled roles pay more:** a 67% wage premium in professional services in PwC's job-ad analysis [PWC-AIJB-PS-2026].
- **Training is widespread but counts activity, not skill:** PwC 330,000+ people trained, Accenture 550,000+
  [AT-PWC-2026; CIODIVE-ACCENTURE-2025]. Law firms requiring training before AI access: 11% (smallest) to 72%
  (largest) [ILTA-TECHSURVEY-2026].

## 6. The apprenticeship question

**The question:** if AI does the work juniors learned from, where do they learn judgment, and who will be a partner in
ten years?

- **The one randomised trial.** 133 patent lawyers: AI raised work quality, but after three months the lasting unaided
  skill gain went to seniors (+0.45 SD); juniors showed no average gain and their scores split. Conducted and funded
  by Google (several authors are Google employees) [NBER-AUTOR-2026].
- **Practitioners are worried.** 48% of 1,816 professionals fear harm to judgment development; 71% say early-career
  roles need structured support from experienced peers. Legal professionals expect the time to trusted judgment to
  lengthen by about 1.7 years; tax professionals expect it to shorten by about 1.0 (vendor survey) [TR-FOP-REPORT-2026].
- **Beliefs about how repetitive work builds judgment are "largely inferential"** [TRI-DESIGNL-2026]; one junior accountant says
  the tasks AI took were not ones he learned from [ICAEW-WORRY-2026].
- **History warns against closing intake.** Firms that cut intake after 2008 reportedly struggled for managers about
  five years later [ICAEW-JUNIOR-2026]. Vault notes no firm has answered where skill comes from if the training ground
  disappears [VAULT-PYRAMID-2026].
- **Billing rules constrain the fix.** Clients cannot be billed for time AI saved or, per a summary of ABA 512, for
  learning tools used regularly for clients [CALBAR-GENAI-2026; CIVILITY-ABA512-2024]; clients resist paying for
  junior-completed tasks [TRI-LDEV-2026].
- **Designs firms use** (no outcome data published for any of them): AI training gates and certification; reviewer
  roles for juniors; billable credit for AI work (Ropes & Gray); AI seats; unaided skill checks modelled on the trial;
  supervisor review of juniors' AI use ([talent-learning](modules/talent-learning.md) `#01`-`#11`).

**For the leader:** decide what a junior must learn without AI, test it, and fund development time explicitly before
cutting the work that used to teach it.

## 7. Capacity, utilisation and hybrid teams

- **Baselines:** services firms' billable utilisation 66.4% in 2025, the lowest in SPI's history (Deltek-sponsored)
  [DELTEK-SPI-2026]; architecture and engineering just under 60%, with staff growth of 1.2% against forecast revenue
  growth of 9.5% and turnover above 13% [DELTEK-CLARITY-2026].
- **Readiness for agents is low:** 52.7% of resource managers are not equipped to manage hybrid human-agent teams and
  4% are well equipped; 7.3% use outcome data in staffing [KANTATA-RMI-2026]. Assignable agents are mostly not on
  the roadmap: 32% say they are not, 25.3% are exploring and 10.7% are piloting [KANTATA-RMI-REPORT-2026].
- **Delegated staffing does not exist yet:** every staffing agent read "proposes" or "recommends"; a randomised test of
  algorithmic assignment of expert work (a public agency) favoured the managers [IPA-PERU-2026].
- **Employment AI rules reach staffing:** EU high-risk obligations apply to AI that allocates tasks or evaluates
  workers from 2 December 2027 [EC-AIACT-2026]; detail in [guardrails.md](guardrails.md) §9.

## 8. Retention and incentives

- **Value-gap attrition:** 24% of professionals who see an AI value gap are considering leaving within two years; the
  replacement cost estimate is $232,000 (vendor survey and estimate) [TR-FOP-REPORT-2026]. Mid-career staff are the
  most mobile [TR-FOP-REPORT-2026].
- **Incentives are moving:** Shoosmiths unlocked a bonus pool at one million Copilot prompts (a usage measure, not
  skill) [LEGALCHEEK-SHOOSMITHS-2025]; KPMG rates staff on AI use [BLOOMBERG-KPMG-2025]; Accenture reportedly links
  leadership promotion to AI adoption [DECRYPT-ACCENTURE-2026].
- **Hours still drive pay.** Partner and staff pay keyed to utilisation and hours is the main internal brake on
  pricing change [TRI-ILTACON-2025]; Wipfli adds cycle time and client satisfaction to staff metrics
  [BLOOMBERGTAX-PRICING-2026].

## 9. In-house teams

- No named in-house talent or learning programme was found; in-house voices appear in surveys only
  ([talent-learning](modules/talent-learning.md) §1).
- One legal-operations respondent expects legal ops to become a strategic design function for how legal work is
  structured [TR-FOP-REPORT-2026].
- In-house teams building their own agents and AI-first drafting defaults change what in-house lawyers do
  ([work-production](modules/work-production.md) `#18`, `#19`).
- Non-legal shared-service teams: no evidence found.
