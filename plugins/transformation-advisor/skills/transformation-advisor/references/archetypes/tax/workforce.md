## 1. Roles

| Setting | Roles | Where AI touches them first |
|---|---|---|
| Firms | Seasonal and staff preparers, seniors, managers, partners; national-office and risk specialists; TP economists; controversy specialists | First-pass preparation, extraction, research and drafting; review load shifts upward |
| Retail and small practices | Seasonal tax pros, enrolled agents, owners | Data entry and organizers; advice in the appointment |
| In-house | Tax analysts, managers, directors, head of tax; indirect tax, TP and provision specialists; tax technologists | Data assembly, reconciliations, research; ownership of automations |
| New hybrids | Tax technology and data roles, AI leads and champions, accountants building vendors' agents | Created by AI and data work |

Credentials matter for who may represent and sign: as of 1 Sep 2026 the IRS listed 886,338 current PTIN holders,
including 209,076 CPAs, 71,966 enrolled agents and 26,256 attorneys (credentials overlap) [IRS-PREPSTATS-2026]; PTIN holders
without a credential cannot represent clients unless they complete the Annual Filing Season Program, which allows
limited representation on returns they prepared and signed [IRS-PREPCRED-2026].

## 2. The pipeline

- **Rising for three years.** Spring 2026 enrollment in US four-year accounting programmes rose 8.9% to 205,180, the
  third straight rise; 2025 had the most first-time CPA Exam candidates since 2018 outside the 2023 spike
  [AICPA-ENROLL-2026].
- **Licensure is loosening.** Nearly all states have added a route that swaps the fifth year of study for experience;
  in Pennsylvania about 48% of 734 new CPAs in 12 months came through the bachelor's route [AT-150HR-2026].
- **Firms pay to speed licensure.** EY and PwC US each doubled CPA exam bonuses to US$10,000 for joiners from 1 June
  2026 [AT-EYBONUS-2026; CFODIVE-PWCBONUS-2026].
- **Federal outlook.** BLS projects accountant and auditor jobs up 5% from 2025 to 2035 and says efficiency gains from
  technologies such as AI are "not expected to reduce overall demand" [BLS-ACCT-2026].
- **Treat with care:** claims of 340,000 fewer accountants since 2019 and a 125-million-hour tax shortfall were not
  traced to a primary source [TRI-JUDGMENT-2026; BLACKORE-2026].

## 3. The pyramid and entry-level hiring

- **Firm side: the base is narrowing.** PwC US reportedly plans to cut entry-level hiring by almost a third over three
  years, with tax and assurance associate hiring down 32% from 2025 to 2028 (secondary reports of an internal
  presentation) [FORTUNE-PWC-2025; MGMTCONS-PWC-2025]. UK Big Four graduate intakes fell between 2023 and 2025 (KPMG's
  scheme 1,399 to 942 in 2024; primary sources not stated) [SFN-BIGFOUR-2025].
- **But Top 100 US firms grew in aggregate**: employees up 8.62% to 556,378, partly through 225 mergers, while
  prioritising lateral hires, which the editors suggest may reflect automation and outsourcing that "reduce the need for
  entry-level staff to do grunt work"
  [AT-TOP100-2026].
- **Independent evidence (not tax-specific).** Employment of 22-25 year-olds in AI-exposed occupations is 19% below
  trend, mainly through reduced hiring; accountants and auditors appear among the paper's most-exposed example
  occupations; the authors call this descriptive,
  not causal [STANFORD-CANARIES-2026]. An MIT doctoral thesis finds firms with one standard deviation more prior
  data integration have 2.7% lower corporate accountant employment and 13% fewer openings after GenAI, concentrated in junior roles and driven by attrition [MIT-DOWNING-2026]. A Stanford
  GSB paper finds about 8.5% of accountant time reallocated from data entry, suggesting augmentation [STANFORD-GSB-2025].
- **Counter-view.** Sala argues models "conflate tax preparation with tax practice" and that AI cannot hold
  culpability, so corporate tax teams will shrink little [SALA-TAXNOTES-2026].

## 4. Seasonal labour and offshoring

- **Seasonality is structural.** H&R Block had about 4,600 regular full-time associates and a peak of about 73,400
  including seasonal staff in fiscal 2026; its risk factors name dependence on seasonal staff and third parties' AI
  reducing demand [HRB-10K-2026].
- **Offshoring is the default at large firms.** 86% of IPA 100 firms employ offshore staff and 68% offshore tax returns;
  the article asks, without answering, how onshore juniors will develop judgment if repeatable work moves offshore
  [IPA-OFFSHORE-2026].
- **The consent frame.** Disclosure to a preparer outside the US needs taxpayer consent, and for 1040 returns SSNs
  cannot go abroad except under defined safeguards [ECFR-7216-2026; IRS-RP2013-14]. A newsletter reading OPR's alert lists offshore
  subprocessor access as "not addressed" [BARMORE-OPR-2026].
- **Corporate sourcing.** In KPMG's 53-organisation indirect tax benchmark, VAT/GST compliance is managed 32% centrally
  in-house, 23% local in-house, 26% co-sourced and 19% outsourced [KPMG-INDIRECT-2026].
- **AI and offshore headcount: no data.** One podcast guest, a vendor-side voice, forecasts less outsourcing
  [CPAPA-TAXGPT-2026]. Treat it as a hypothesis to test with the leader's own numbers.

## 5. How roles are evolving

- **Preparers become reviewers.** Vendors describe "start at review"; a customer says "I'm reviewing, and that's a
  completely different job" (see [compliance-individual-business](modules/compliance-individual-business.md) §7).
- **Review becomes the bottleneck.** TEI attendees describe errors that are "polite, quiet and technical" and hard to
  review at volume [TEI-TAXTECH-2026]; Werfel names control dilution [TAXADVISER-WERFEL-2026].
- **New hybrid roles in postings** (employer-stated, chosen for shape, not volume): Databricks' Tax Data and Technology
  Manager builds pipelines and AI use cases under SOX change control [JOB-DATABRICKS-2026]; Stripe's tax systems
  architect owns tax systems and controls [JOB-STRIPE-2026]; Anthropic's tax systems analyst uses Claude to build tools
  and checks output before anyone relies on it [JOB-ANTHROPIC-2026].
- **Who fills them.** Of departments that hired a technologist for tax alone, 57% hired from technology and 24% from tax;
  55% expect current roles to change within three to five years [TRI-DUALROLES-2025].
- **AI leads and champions.** PwC reports 3,200 AI champions and about 80% of its US people trained [PWC-MEGNA-2025].
- **Partners and advisers.** Deloitte's scenarios move professionals toward oversight, interpretation, policy and
  disputes (a planning framework, not a forecast) [DELOITTE-TTT-2026].

## 6. The apprenticeship question

Routine tasks (tie-outs, workpapers, first drafts, review notes) both produced output and built judgment, and AI
compresses exactly those repetitions [PRIMEGLOBAL-2026; CPAPA-STOPTRAIN-2026].

- **The profession's own worry.** AICPA's Profession Ready work found gaps in technical fundamentals (tax firms report
  more than half of incoming staff need remediation on debits, credits and accruals), professional capabilities and
  judgment about when to challenge AI output; 48% of tax professionals fear AI harms independent judgment (survey cited
  by a vendor-owned institute) [TRI-JUDGMENT-2026].
- **Proposals, none measured.** Simulation first: juniors work many simulated returns before reviewing automated output
  ("how do you know what good looks like if you've never done the work yourself?") [TRI-JUDGMENT-2026]. An
  "apprenticeship ledger" recording, per AI-compressed workflow, what routine work went, what judgment task replaces it
  and who reviews it [CPAPA-LEDGER-2026]. Thomson Reuters lists "define the role of early-career professionals" as a
  priority [TR-FOP-2026].
- **Few firms have redesigned yet.** About 20% of firm leaders in a vendor survey report outsourcing junior roles or
  filling them with AI [KARBON-JUNIOR-2026].
- **For the plan:** name, for each AI workflow, where juniors will practise the judgment it used to teach, and how a
  senior will know a junior can challenge the output. No firm has published results for any model.

## 7. Skills and the training duty

- **Training is a regulatory expectation.** OPR reads section 10.35 as requiring an understanding of the technology and
  its limits, and section 10.36 as requiring firm procedures including staff training [IRS-OPR-2026].
- **Skills demand runs ahead of hiring requirements.** 94% of Deloitte's respondents expect AI skills to be essential in
  four to five years and 45% name them their greatest near-term need [DELOITTE-LU-2025]; EY's respondents prioritise
  hiring data scientists and tax technologists (73%) [EY-TFO-2025]; yet 2% of corporate tax departments make GenAI skills
  a hiring requirement [TR-TENSIONS-2025].
- **Key-person risk.** TEI attendees describe a training programme that "failed spectacularly" and workflows nobody
  could explain after the builder left [TEI-TAXTECH-2026].

## 8. Retention

- More than a quarter of tax and audit professionals would not join a firm lacking professional-grade AI, and nearly one
  in three would consider leaving if AI expectations go unmet within two years (vendor-owned survey) [TR-FOP-2026].
- Staff retention is a top-five expected issue for every firm size except solo practitioners [AICPA-TOPISSUES-2026].
- More than a third admit using unapproved tools, so the issue is governance more than refusal [TR-FOP-2026].

## 9. In-house talent

- **Flat headcount expected, not cuts.** 58% of KPMG Germany's respondents expect stable domestic tax headcount, 72%
  abroad [KPMG-DE-2026]; for VAT/GST teams, 58% expect headcount largely unchanged because of AI, 21% an increase and 21%
  a decrease [KPMG-INDIRECT-2026].
- **Under-resourced and still hiring.** 58% of 288 departments say they are under-resourced and 38% intend to hire more
  tax professionals [TR-CORPTAX-2025].
- **Retirements bite.** 61% of EY's 1,600 leaders expect senior retirements to significantly affect the function; time
  on routine activity is 53% against a desired 21% [EY-TFO-2025].
- **Technology skills are short.** About a third of KPMG's indirect tax respondents have no technology specialists on the
  team [KPMG-INDIRECT-2026].

## 10. The tax authority's workforce

The IRS lost more than a quarter of its staff in 2025, holds headcount near 74,000 and calls itself "AI-first"; its CEO
says AI "is not designed to eliminate jobs" [IRS-AIFIRST-2026]. GAO found too few skilled staff to build AI and no AI
workforce plan [GAO-2026]. For practitioners, that likely means faster automated matching with thinner human follow-up (advisor inference; see
[controversy](modules/controversy.md)).
