# Tax objections

Last researched 2026-10-17. Use this when a practitioner pushes back. Each response gives the evidence and its
provenance; concede what is true. Objections about a single step (for example "our auditors won't accept an
AI-assisted provision") are in the module files' "Common objections" sections. Source keys in brackets resolve in §8
or in [sources.md](sources.md).

## 1. Regulation and professional duties

| Objection | Response |
|---|---|
| "The rules don't cover AI yet; we'll wait for guidance." | OPR, the UK PCRT bodies, Australia's TPB and Germany's BStBK each say existing rules apply now; OPR names firm procedures and training under section 10.36 [IRS-OPR-2026; PCRT-AI-2026; TPB-GS55-2026; BSTBK-KI-2026]. Waiting is not a position any of them offers. |
| "Hallucinated citations are a litigator's problem." | Tax cases include counsel admonished (*Clinco*), IRS expert reports withdrawn (*Riddle Aggregates*), an IRS motion questioned (*Khoja*) and a UK taxpayer's appeal [TAXCT-CLINCO-2026; TAXCT-RIDDLE-2026; BLAW-TAXCT-2026; FTT-HARBER-2023]. Honest limit: no tax-practitioner sanction for AI misuse was found. |
| "We have human review, so we're covered." | Review has to be real and recorded: OPR says verify facts, citations and calculations; TPB expects documented review at each step; TEI attendees describe errors that are "polite, quiet and technical" [IRS-OPR-2026; TPB-GS55-2026; TEI-TAXTECH-2026]. Werfel names control dilution as volume rises [TAXADVISER-WERFEL-2026]. |
| "Our enterprise tool is secure, so section 7216 isn't an issue." | Security is necessary, but the question is use and disclosure. The regulation's consent line turns on whether the service makes a "substantive determination"; there is no IRS guidance on AI and commentators disagree. Decide per workflow, write down the reasoning, and consider consent where the vendor's role is substantive [ECFR-7216-2026; WINTHROP-ABA-2026]. |
| "Telling clients we use AI will frighten them." | No universal US duty to disclose is stated in the sources; PCRT asks members to consider transparency; TPB needs client permission for third-party AI; a broker recommends engagement-letter disclosure with an opt-out [TAXCT-CLINCO-2026; PCRT-AI-2026; TPB-GS55-2026; DIGIN-INSURERS-2026]. |
| "Insurers will make this uninsurable." | In the accounting line insurers are asking about AI policies, premiums have not moved, and Aon saw no substantive AI claims at accounting firms as of Apr 2026. Exclusions reported elsewhere were not verified for accounting forms [DIGIN-INSURERS-2026; INS-EXCLUSIONS-2026]. |

## 2. Quality and reliability

| Objection | Response |
|---|---|
| "AI can't be trusted with tax; models get returns wrong." | Partly true. Unaided 2025 models computed fewer than a third of simplified returns correctly [COLUMN-TAXCALCBENCH-2025], and the best model scored 49% on corporate tax research [VALS-TAXAGENT-2026]. Designs that keep calculation in a deterministic engine target exactly this [JHU-TAXREASON-2025]. Start where output can be checked against source documents, and measure overrides. |
| "The vendors say 65% faster and 99% accurate." | Those are company statements from launch releases without published definitions or denominators [TR-SUTAI-2026; BLACKORE-2026]. Ask for customer-measured baselines and a parallel run on last year's work. |
| "The Big Four have already automated tax; we can't compete." | Most of what is public is announcements and one firm's statement that agents are in production for named steps [EY-TAXLABS-2025; PWC-MEGNA-2025]. A firm that tested five AI preparation vendors found none ready for its season [WCG-2026], and small firms can rent the same products. |
| "Review time will eat the savings." | It can: review is the new bottleneck in every module. Design the review (cited sources, risk-based re-performance) and budget a parallel run first. No firm has published review-time data. |
| "AI that can't show its sources is fine if the answer is right." | OPR says opaque logic may make reliance unreasonable for written advice; a commentator calls untraceable output "paying twice" for verification [IRS-OPR-2026; CPAPA-OPACITY-2026]. |

## 3. Economics and pricing

| Objection | Response |
|---|---|
| "AI will wipe out our tax fees." | Big Four tax lines grew in the latest years reported, small-firm fees are above 2020 levels, and 80% of one survey's firms plan rises [EY-REV-2026; NSA-2023; CPAPA-IGNITION-2025]. No before-and-after fee study on tax work exists, so neither collapse nor immunity is shown. |
| "We bill by the return, so AI only helps us." | Mostly true for small-firm preparation (3% hourly, 37% fixed fee in one customer survey) [IGNITION-2025]. But OPR's fee and disclosure points still apply to work before the IRS, and AI tools add a per-seat or per-return cost [IRS-OPR-2026; WCG-2026]. |
| "We'll move to value pricing and keep the savings." | The AICPA argues for value pricing, but OPR tells practitioners to credit AI cost reductions; the AICPA has asked for clarification and no answer was found [JOFA-FEES-2026; IRS-OPR-2026]. Document the value delivered, the AI cost and the disclosure. |
| "Offshoring already gave us the savings." | Likely true for many: 68% of IPA 100 firms offshore returns [IPA-OFFSHORE-2026]. That makes offshore cost the right baseline for any AI case. No source shows offshore-prepared returns are cheaper than reviewed AI-prepared ones, or the reverse. |
| "We can't afford AI; the big firms will outspend us." | Research AI lists at about US$1,498 a user a year [BLUEJ-PRICING-2026], and capability is rented from vendors. The full cost (data work, review, token costs) is not benchmarked [TEI-TAXTECH-2026]. |
| "Roll-ups and private equity are hype." | They are funded and buying: 10 of the top 20 US firms are or will be PE-owned, and Crete reported over US$300m revenue at Jun 2025 [CFOBREW-2026; CRETE-REUTERS-2025]. Results are company-stated. |

## 4. People and skills

| Objection | Response |
|---|---|
| "There's a CPA shortage; AI just fills the gap, so nobody loses a job." | Both are partly true. Enrollment and first-time candidates are rising [AICPA-ENROLL-2026], while PwC US reportedly plans a one-third cut in entry-level hiring and young workers in exposed jobs are 19% below trend through reduced hiring [FORTUNE-PWC-2025; STANFORD-CANARIES-2026]. No source quantifies the net for tax. |
| "AI will shrink our team." | In-house leaders mostly expect stable headcount (58% domestic in KPMG Germany's survey) [KPMG-DE-2026], and Sala argues culpability keeps people in the loop [SALA-TAXNOTES-2026]. Firm-side effects concentrate on junior hiring [MIT-DOWNING-2026]. Expect a change in composition rather than a uniform cut. |
| "Juniors will learn by reviewing AI output." | Practitioners propose it, but AICPA's Profession Ready lead warns juniors must do the work before supervising AI's version, and more than half of incoming tax-firm staff already need remediation on fundamentals [TRI-JUDGMENT-2026]. No firm has published results. See [workforce.md](workforce.md) §6. |
| "Our people don't want AI." | More than a quarter of tax and audit professionals would not join a firm without professional-grade AI, and more than a third already use unapproved tools (vendor survey) [TR-FOP-2026]. The issue is governance more than refusal. |
| "We can't hire tax technologists." | Employers are hiring them and accept tax-adjacent experience; 57% of departments hiring a tax-only technologist chose a technology background [JOB-DATABRICKS-2026; TRI-DUALROLES-2025]. |

## 5. Data, clients and the tax authority

| Objection | Response |
|---|---|
| "Our data isn't ready, so AI can wait." | Common and partly right: 51% of tax and finance functions are early in data maturity [EY-TFO-2025]. But the data layer is itself where most tax AI evidence sits ([value-chain.md](value-chain.md) §3); start there rather than wait. |
| "The IRS will pre-fill returns, so preparation disappears." | Pre-population is constrained by third-party data timing and the system's structure; no AI-driven return-free system was found live, and the IRS is short of AI skills [ESP-DIRECTFILE-2026; GAO-2026]. |
| "The tax authority isn't using AI on us." | The IRS had 126 use cases in June 2025; HMRC says AI and analytics enabled GBP 10bn of tax protected and recovered in 2025-26 (self-reported) [GAO-2026; HMRC-ARA-2026]. In e-invoicing countries, authorities arrive with data already analysed [TRINST-TEIAUDITOR-2026]. |
| "Our clients aren't asking about AI." | Clients now arrive with chatbot answers of their own, and agentic commerce raises who-collects questions for sellers ([research-advisory](modules/research-advisory.md); [BTAX-AGENTICCOMMERCE-2026]). |

## 6. In-house departments

| Objection | Response |
|---|---|
| "There's no budget case if headcount stays flat." | The case is capacity and risk: under-resourced and reactive departments report less confidence and more penalties [TR-CORPTAX-2025]. Build it from the department's own time data; no cost-per-return benchmark exists [IRS-BTB-2026]. |
| "IT owns AI, not us." | That is the usual position (two of 50+ at TEI said tax owns it) [TEI-TAXTECH-2026], and it is a risk: someone must own tax data, positions and the automations staff build. |
| "We'll wait for our auditor to say what they accept." | No source covers auditing AI-prepared tax balances, so waiting may be long. Agree the evidence and controls with the auditor early (see [provision-pillar-two](modules/provision-pillar-two.md)). |

## 7. How to respond well

- Concede first what is true: independent quality evidence is missing everywhere in tax.
- Separate measured, announced and forecast ([state-of-ai.md](state-of-ai.md)).
- Turn the objection into a design requirement: a review record, a consent decision, a metric, a parallel run or a
  pricing policy.
- If the objection is a fear for jobs, switch to the approach in [people-and-change.md](../../method/people-and-change.md).
