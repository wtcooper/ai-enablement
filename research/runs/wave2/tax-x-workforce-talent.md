# Tax x workforce-talent: staffing, pipeline, pyramid, new roles, in-house talent

- **Archetype:** tax (compliance, provision, indirect tax, transfer pricing, international and Pillar Two, controversy, research and advisory, tax data and technology; firms and in-house departments).
- **Cross-cutting topic:** workforce-talent.
- **Prepared:** 2026-10-17 (wave 2). Sources were read with `curl` (and `r.jina.ai` where blocked) into a private working folder. Refs reuse the landscape's keys where one existed; new refs follow `PUBLISHER-SHORT-YYYY`.
- **Reading convention:** a locator in brackets follows each ref. "Self-reported" means a firm or vendor describing its own results; "survey" figures are as published by the sponsor and are not averaged. Forecasts are labelled with who made them.

## 1. Summary

- **The supply side is turning, but slowly and unevenly.** US four-year accounting undergraduate enrollment rose for the third straight spring (8.9% to 205,180 in spring 2026), and 2025 had the most first-time CPA Exam candidates since 2018 (outside the 2023 spike) [AICPA-ENROLL-2026]. Nearly all states have added a licensure route that substitutes work experience for the fifth year of study, so the "150-hour" barrier is being removed [AT-150HR-2026]. No source shows yet whether these gains reach tax compliance capacity.
- **Demand-side signals point the other way for entry-level hiring.** PwC US reportedly plans to cut entry-level hiring by almost a third over three years, with tax and assurance associate hiring down 32% from 2025 to 2028 (secondary reports of an internal presentation) [FORTUNE-PWC-2025; MGMTCONS-PWC-2025]. UK Big Four graduate intakes fell between 2023 and 2025 [SFN-BIGFOUR-2025]. A Stanford and ADP study finds employment of 22 to 25 year-olds in AI-exposed occupations 19% below trend, driven by reduced hiring (not tax-specific) [STANFORD-CANARIES-2026].
- **The apprenticeship problem is the profession's own stated worry, and the fixes are still proposals.** AICPA's Profession Ready lead says the "by osmosis" learning model is breaking and points to simulation-based training; one firm is known to run a gamified tool [TRI-JUDGMENT-2026]. Only about 20% of firm leaders in a vendor survey report changing organisational design by outsourcing junior roles or filling them with AI [KARBON-JUNIOR-2026]. No source gives measured results for any redesigned training model.
- **In-house tax teams mostly expect flat headcount, not cuts.** 58% of KPMG Germany respondents expect constant domestic tax headcount and 72% abroad [KPMG-DE-2026]; 58% of KPMG's 53 multinationals expect VAT/GST headcount "largely unchanged" because of AI, with 21% expecting an increase and 21% a decrease [KPMG-INDIRECT-2026]. A tax law professor argues AI will not shrink corporate tax teams much because AI cannot hold culpability [SALA-TAXNOTES-2026]. Departments are under-resourced (58%) and still plan to hire [TR-CORPTAX-2025].
- **A new hybrid role is visible in job postings and surveys.** Databricks, Stripe and Anthropic are advertising tax-systems and tax-data roles that mix tax operations, SQL or data engineering, SOX controls and AI tooling [JOB-DATABRICKS-2026; JOB-STRIPE-2026; JOB-ANTHROPIC-2026]. Of departments that hired a technologist for tax alone, 57% hired from a technology background and 24% from tax [TRI-DUALROLES-2025]. Only 2% of corporate tax departments make GenAI skills a hiring requirement [TR-TENSIONS-2025].
- **Offshoring and seasonal labour are the least-evidenced part.** The regulatory frame is clear (consent before disclosure to a preparer outside the US; no consent route for 1040 SSNs outside narrow safeguards) [ECFR-7216], and the IRS OPR alert does not address offshore subprocessors [BARMORE-OPR-2026]. Public data on how AI changes offshore or seasonal headcount is almost entirely commentary: one podcast guest forecasts less outsourcing [CPAPA-TAXGPT-2026]; H&R Block lists seasonal-workforce dependence as a risk and an AI-driven demand reduction by third parties as another [HRB-10K-2026].
- **Retention is now tied to AI provision.** More than a quarter of tax and audit professionals in a Thomson Reuters survey would not accept a job at a firm without professional-grade AI, and nearly one in three would consider leaving if AI expectations are unmet within two years (vendor-owned survey) [TR-FOP-2026]. Staff retention ranks in the top five expected issues for every firm size except solo practitioners [AICPA-TOPISSUES-2026].
- **The tax authority's workforce is a mirror case.** The IRS lost more than a quarter of its staff in 2025, has held headcount flat near 74,000 in 2026, and calls itself "AI-first" [IRS-AIFIRST-2026]; GAO found it has no AI workforce plan [GAO-2026].

## 2. Findings

### 2.1 The CPA, enrolled-agent and preparer pipeline

- **Enrollment and exam volume are rising from a low base.** AICPA, analysing National Student Clearinghouse data, reports spring 2026 enrollment in four-year undergraduate accounting programs up 8.9% to 205,180, following +12.7% in spring 2025 and +4.8% in spring 2024; total undergraduate accounting enrollment (including two-year programs) was 281,992 versus 266,868 a year earlier, up 5.7% against 1.3% across all majors [AICPA-ENROLL-2026; paragraphs 1 to 3]. Aside from a 2023 spike that typically precedes an exam format change, 2025 had the highest first-time CPA Exam candidate count since 2018 and the most candidates passing all four sections since 2017 (AICPA release) [AICPA-ENROLL-2026; JofA paragraph 4]. More than 87,000 individuals sat for the CPA Exam in 2025 [CPAPA-SELLS-2026; paragraph 3].
- **A claim to treat with care:** a Thomson Reuters Institute article says undergraduate accounting enrollment is "up by 25% since 2023" and that the US lost 340,000 accountants (more than one-third of the licensed workforce) since 2019, but gives no primary source for either [TRI-JUDGMENT-2026; paragraph 4]. I could not reproduce the 25% from AICPA's yearly figures (compounding the three four-year-program increases would be my own arithmetic, not a published figure). The Black Ore launch article likewise cites about 300,000 net departures in two years attributed to BLS and a 125-million-hour annual tax shortfall that the article itself says the company did not attribute to an external source [BLACKORE-2026; section "Black Ore and the Accountant Shortage"]. I could not find either figure in the BLS page I read.
- **Federal labour data are modest.** BLS counts 1,595,200 accountants and auditors in 2025 (median pay $83,680, May 2025), projects 5% growth from 2025 to 2035 (79,400 jobs) and about 115,300 openings a year, mostly replacement; it says AI and robotic process automation will likely raise efficiency but that this "is not expected to reduce overall demand" [BLS-ACCT-2026; Quick Facts, Job Outlook, Technological change]. BLS's tax preparer page could not be opened (see Gaps).
- **Licensure is being deliberately loosened.** Accounting Today reports (29 Jun 2026) that nearly all states have adopted extra pathways letting candidates substitute work experience for the fifth year of education, with remaining states expected to change by the end of 2027 [AT-150HR-2026; opening paragraphs]. Ohio's law (signed 8 Jan 2025, effective 1 Jan 2026) offers a bachelor's degree plus two years' experience, or a master's plus one [AT-150HR-2026]. Pennsylvania began licensing on the new route on 30 Jun 2025 and reports 734 new CPAs in 12 months, about 350 (nearly 48%) through the bachelor's pathway; the Pennsylvania Institute of CPAs CEO says it is too early to judge effects on hiring or audit quality [AT-150HR-2026; Pennsylvania paragraphs]. The year-end review described close to half of states as having passed or considering such bills as of 31 Dec 2025 [AT-TOP2025-2025; item 1].
- **Big Four firms are paying to speed CPA completion.** EY doubled its bonus to $10,000 for entry-level hires who pass all four parts in their first full year, for joiners from 1 June 2026, as part of a $1 billion investment announced in 2024 [AT-EYBONUS-2026; paragraphs 1 to 2]. PwC doubled its CPA bonus to $10,000 (effective 1 June, reported late May 2026) for hires who finish before joining or in their first year; CFO Dive reports that dozens of states have changed licensure rules [CFODIVE-PWCBONUS-2026; paragraphs 1 and 5].
- **Preparer population snapshot (IRS).** As of 1 Sep 2026 the IRS lists 886,338 individuals with current 2026 PTINs, including 209,076 CPAs, 71,966 enrolled agents and 26,256 attorneys; credentials overlap, and 72,049 Annual Filing Season Program completions were recorded [IRS-PREPSTATS-2026; table]. PTIN-only preparers have no authority to represent clients before the IRS except on returns they prepared; CPAs, EAs and attorneys have unlimited representation rights; EAs pass a three-part IRS exam and complete 72 hours of continuing education every three years [IRS-PREPCRED-2026]. I found no prior-year IRS snapshot to show a trend (see Gaps).

### 2.2 Seasonal labour and the preparer shortage

- **Seasonality is structural.** H&R Block had about 4,600 regular full-time associates at 30 Jun 2026 and its peak headcount including seasonal associates during fiscal 2026 was about 73,400 [HRB-10K-2026; Item 1 "Human Capital"]. Its risk factors say the business depends on seasonal tax-preparation staff who may be needed "on short notice" during extended seasons, and that the market for such personnel is competitive [HRB-10K-2026; Item 1A, key personnel risk factor]. The same filing says third parties may deploy AI in ways that could reduce demand for its services and products [HRB-10K-2026; Item 1A, AI risk factor].
- **Vendors frame AI as a shortage response; the evidence is self-reported.** Black Ore's April 2026 launch (75 pilot firms; 3,700 on a waitlist; accuracy above 99% and time savings up to 98% on complex filings, all company-stated) was framed around the accountant shortage, and the article says firms rely increasingly on offshore staff and long season hours [BLACKORE-2026; paragraphs 2 to 4 and "Black Ore and the Accountant Shortage"]. Thomson Reuters offers "AI to scale" (more capacity without more headcount, "particularly attractive for managing busy tax seasons and reducing recruitment strain") as one of three strategic paths [TR-FOP-2026; "Finding your strategic path"].
- **Staffing pressure at the authority too.** The IRS missed its hiring goals for this year's filing season, then made more than 4,000 hires in 2026 while headcount stayed at 74,000 start-to-end of year; in February about 1,500 IT and HR staff were put on involuntary details to frontline taxpayer service [IRS-AIFIRST-2026; paragraphs on hiring and details].

### 2.3 The pyramid and entry-level hiring

- **UK Big Four graduate intake fell.** Scottish Financial News reports KPMG's UK scheme down 29% from 1,399 (2023) to 942 (2024), with about 1,000 expected in 2025; Deloitte from 1,700 to 1,400; EY from 1,800 to 1,600; PwC from 1,600 to 1,500 [SFN-BIGFOUR-2025; paragraph 4]. It cites Indeed data showing UK accountancy graduate job adverts 44% lower in 2025 than in 2023, versus 33% for all graduate jobs, and Patrick Morgan data showing UK headcounts down 4% (KPMG, PwC), 2% (Deloitte) and 1% (EY) so far in 2025 after cuts of 3% to 7% in 2024 [SFN-BIGFOUR-2025; paragraphs 5 to 7]. The article does not state the primary sources of the intake figures. KPMG says it recruits "around 1,000 graduates and apprentices a year"; PwC and Deloitte declined comment [SFN-BIGFOUR-2025; closing paragraphs].
- **PwC US.** Fortune, citing Business Insider documents, reports PwC plans to cut US entry-level hiring "by almost a third over the next three years" [FORTUNE-PWC-2025; paragraph on entry-level cuts, 8 Sep 2025]. A secondary summary of the internal presentation says tax and assurance associate hiring falls 32% between 2025 and 2028 and audit new hires 39% [MGMTCONS-PWC-2025; "internal presentation" paragraph; I could not re-open the page, and I did not read the Business Insider original]. Fast Company (12 Feb 2026) repeats that PwC announced it would recruit a third fewer new graduates by 2028 [FASTCO-PWC-2026; paragraph 2]. Separately, PwC US revamped its learning and development programme to balance AI fluency with critical thinking (Bloomberg Tax, lede only; remainder paywalled) [BTAX-PIVOT-2026; first two paragraphs]. PwC UK's head attributed that firm's lower graduate intake mainly to weak productivity and subdued investment and deal-making [FORTUNE-PWC-2025].
- **Top 100 US firms are growing, not shrinking, in aggregate.** The Accounting Today Top 100 databank (US figures, compiled to year-end 2025) shows total employees up 8.62% to 556,378, professionals up 7.32% to 363,161, partners up 6.16% to 34,664 and revenue up 8.58% to $140.8 billion; tax is 24% of fee revenue [AT-TOP100-2026; Databank overview]. The same issue reports 225 mergers in the year and says the firms are prioritising lateral hires as automation and outsourcing "reduce the need for entry-level staff to do grunt work" [AT-TOP100-2026; page 4 and page 12 "Talent"]. Growth includes acquisitions, so it does not isolate organic hiring.
- **Independent evidence on young workers (not tax-specific).** Using ADP payroll data through June 2026, Brynjolfsson, Chandar and Chen find no evidence of widespread economy-wide displacement, but employment of 22 to 25 year-olds in AI-exposed occupations is 19% below where it would be had it kept pace with less-exposed peers; the gap works mainly through reduced hiring, experienced workers show no comparable gap, and the authors call the facts descriptive "canaries", not causal estimates [STANFORD-CANARIES-2026; abstract and introduction]. "Accountants" is listed as the fourth-largest occupation by ADP employment in the highest-exposure quintile (Oct 2022) [STANFORD-CANARIES-2026; Table A.6].
- **Accounting-specific academic evidence.** An MIT master's thesis (May 2026; not peer-reviewed) finds that firms with one-standard-deviation greater prior data-integration investment had 2.7% lower accountant employment and 13% fewer job openings after generative AI, concentrated in junior positions, with manager-level demand stable and the decline driven by attrition not layoffs [MIT-DOWNING-2026; abstract]. A Stanford GSB working paper (May 2025; 277-accountant survey and field data from 79 small and mid-sized firms using one vendor's software) finds about 8.5% of accountant time reallocated from routine data entry to higher-value tasks and concludes the evidence suggests augmentation [STANFORD-GSB-2025; abstract]. Neither study is limited to tax.
- **Counter-view.** Michael Sala (Tax Notes State, 20 Jul 2026) argues that models "conflate tax preparation with tax practice" and that corporate tax functions have not been return-preparation pools for decades [SALA-TAXNOTES-2026; abstract]. Only the abstract was available.
- **Vendor survey on role redesign.** Karbon's 2026 report says 20% of accounting firm leaders have changed organisational design by outsourcing junior roles or filling them with AI, and that firms using AI save on average 60 minutes per employee per day (vendor-owned survey) [KARBON-JUNIOR-2026; "The profession is asking the question out loud"].

### 2.4 The apprenticeship problem

- **What the profession says is being lost.** Routine tasks (reconciliations, tie-outs, workpapers, memo drafts and review notes) both produced output and built judgment; AI compresses exactly those repetitions at the early-career stage [PRIMEGLOBAL-2026; paragraphs 2 to 4; CPAPA-STOPTRAIN-2026; paragraphs 4 to 5]. A tax respondent in a Thomson Reuters Institute and TEI survey said lower-level positions will be replaced by technology and that this "will pose significant challenges in developing talent to fill senior positions" [TRI-DUALROLES-2025; last paragraph].
- **Evidence of a skills gap.** AICPA's Profession Ready research held conversations with more than 1,000 members and found three consistent gaps: technical fundamentals (tax firms report more than half of incoming staff need remediation on debits, credits and accruals), professional capabilities (critical thinking, skepticism), and judgment about when to challenge AI output [TRI-JUDGMENT-2026; "Profession Ready" paragraphs]. In the Thomson Reuters Future of Professionals data cited there, tax professionals expect the time to develop trusted judgment to compress by one year and 48% fear AI harms independent judgment development [TRI-JUDGMENT-2026; paragraph "One of the most consequential findings"]. These are survey and interview findings reported by a vendor-owned institute.
- **Proposed responses (none measured).** AICPA's Carl Mayes advocates simulation: juniors work large numbers of simulated returns before they review automated output ("how do you know what good looks like if you've never done the work yourself?"); he knows of one firm with a gamified internal tool and one large firm that halved technical training time in favour of critical-thinking exercises [TRI-JUDGMENT-2026; Mayes paragraphs]. A CPA Practice Advisor opinion piece proposes an "apprenticeship ledger" recording, for each AI-compressed workflow, what routine work disappeared, what judgment task replaces it, who reviews it and what shows independence [CPAPA-LEDGER-2026; "The practical tool" paragraph]. A vendor CFO's piece recommends source-document literacy, structured mentorship, engagement visibility and reviewing AI exceptions [CPAPA-STOPTRAIN-2026; list of four areas]. Thomson Reuters lists "define the role of early-career professionals" as a universal priority [TR-FOP-2026; "Universal priorities"].
- **Labour-market context cited alongside.** Robert Half research is cited as showing 74% of finance and accounting leaders plan to increase permanent headcount in the second half of 2026 and 75% saw skills shortages delay projects (respondent base leans toward corporate finance, per the article) [CPAPA-STOPTRAIN-2026; paragraph 3].

### 2.5 Offshoring, outsourcing and sourcing models

- **Legal frame.** A US preparer must obtain taxpayer consent before disclosing return information to a preparer located outside the United States [ECFR-7216; 301.7216-3(a)(3)(i)(D)]. For Form 1040 series returns, a US-located preparer may not obtain consent to disclose the SSN to a preparer outside the US, except where it uses an adequate data protection safeguard defined in IRS guidance and verifies it in the consent request [ECFR-7216; 301.7216-3(b)(4)(i) and (ii)].
- **AI and the offshore question is open.** A practitioner newsletter reading IRS OPR Alert 2026-19 lists "offshore subprocessor access and the special SSN rule" as "not addressed" [BARMORE-OPR-2026; "What OPR did NOT resolve"]. The alert itself requires firms to vet outsourced or third-party AI tools and document §10.36 procedures [IRS-OPR-2026; §10.36 section].
- **Where sourcing sits in corporate indirect tax.** In KPMG's 53-organisation benchmark, VAT/GST return preparation is 32% central in-house, 23% local in-house, 26% co-sourced and 19% outsourced; the report says the gap from declining local preparation is being filled by central hubs and external partners [KPMG-INDIRECT-2026; p.10]. In KPMG Germany's survey 40% of respondents expect increased use of shared service centres and 50% expect stable development [KPMG-DE-2026; paragraph "AI boosts efficiency"].
- **Big Four offshore footprint.** Scottish Financial News says all four UK firms expanded operations in Malaysia and India in 2022 and 2023, with EY and PwC also in the Philippines [SFN-BIGFOUR-2025; paragraph 8]. I did not verify any link between PwC US's entry-level cuts and offshore Acceleration Center integration; it appeared only in search summaries of Business Insider reporting I did not read.
- **Practitioner views on AI and offshoring (podcasts).** Two hosts of the Accounting Technology Lab said in June 2024 that Black Ore processing is onshore so the disclosures usually required for offshoring are "minimized", and that firms commonly use temporary staffing, outsourcing or offshoring [CPAPA-BLACKORE-2024; transcript]. In June 2026 the episode description for a tax-research podcast says the guest "predicts firms will increasingly deploy specialized AI agents, reduce reliance on outsourcing" and shift toward advisory and outcome-based pricing (a forecast by the guest, who is a vendor-side voice) [CPAPA-TAXGPT-2026; episode description].
- I found no independent, quantified 2025 to 2026 source on how AI has changed offshore tax headcount. Searches returned mainly provider marketing pages, which I did not rely on (see Gaps).

### 2.6 In-house tax department staffing and talent

- **Under-resourced and still hiring.** The Thomson Reuters Institute 2025 State of the Corporate Tax Department report (288 departments worldwide) finds 58% describe themselves as under-resourced (51% the year before), 38% intend to hire more qualified tax professionals in the next year or two (32% in 2024), and 59% lack confidence they can upgrade tax technology and automation over two years; over half of time is spent on reactive work (vendor-owned survey) [TR-CORPTAX-2025; "Resource strain" and hiring paragraphs]. Departments reporting more than half of time on proactive work were less likely to report penalties (31% versus 56%) [TR-CORPTAX-2025; section on proactive versus reactive work].
- **Headcount expectations.** KPMG Germany: 58% expect constant domestic tax headcount, 72% constant abroad (sample size not stated in the release) [KPMG-DE-2026; key findings]. KPMG indirect tax: 58% largely unchanged, 21% increase, 21% decrease "as a result of AI"; about one third have no technology specialists on the team; teams also own e-invoicing (33%), tax data reporting (26%), customs (16%) and digital services taxes (18%) [KPMG-INDIRECT-2026; p.5 to p.7]. Thomson Reuters-TEI 2026 report: fewer than half of 170 tax leaders received a budget increase last year [TR-CORPTECH-2026; paragraph 5].
- **Retirements and fewer entrants.** In EY's 1,600-leader Tax and Finance Operations Survey (30 jurisdictions), 61% expect senior tax professionals' retirement to significantly affect their function, 66% say fewer new accountants entering the profession will hurt talent needs, and respondents spend 53% of time on routine activities versus a desired 21% [EY-TFO-2025; "Building agile, AI-ready teams"]. Bloomberg Tax (a vendor) says its own survey found three out of four tax departments struggle to attract and retain staff; sample not stated [BTAX-LEVERAGE-2026; paragraph 2].
- **In-house practice at the frontier.** At the April 2026 TEI Tax Technology Seminar, attendees described AI that "fails quietly", tax rarely owning AI implementation, a training programme across dozens of global clients that "failed spectacularly", and Alteryx workflows nobody could explain after the builder left; OpenAI's four-person tax department demonstrated live automation pipelines (recap by a vendor-owned institute, 4 May 2026) [TEI-TAXTECH-2026; paragraphs 2 to 5].

### 2.7 New roles: tax technologists, tax data engineers, product accountants

- **Survey evidence on dual roles.** The TRI and TEI 2025 Corporate Tax Technology Report, as summarised by TRI, finds 17% of departments outsource technology services entirely, 15% used technology consultants in the past year, only 28% rely primarily on shared IT staff, and among departments that hired a technologist solely for tax, 57% hired a primarily technology background and 24% a tax background; 39% (up from 32%) expect new job roles within three to five years and 55% (up from 41%) expect current roles to change [TRI-DUALROLES-2025; paragraphs 4 to 9]. The article headline claims more than half of corporate tax departments have dual tax/technology roles; the body figures above are the ones I can attribute.
- **Job postings (employer-stated, dated).**
  - Databricks, Tax Data & Technology Manager (first published 14 Sep 2026, listing updated 6 Oct 2026): reports to the Senior Director, Tax; builds pipelines on the finance data lake, delivers AI use cases for tax (calculation automation, anomaly detection, natural-language interfaces), enforces Git and CI/CD to satisfy SOX change management; requires 6+ years in tax automation, transformation or data engineering with at least two in tax or a tax-adjacent field; CPA or CFA is "nice to have" [JOB-DATABRICKS-2026].
  - Stripe, Business Systems Architect (Tax) (first published 20 Aug 2026): owns Oracle Fusion Tax and in-house tax solutions, SOX controls and tax-systems roadmap; requires 8 years building tax systems and an engineering, computer science or finance degree [JOB-STRIPE-2026].
  - Anthropic, Finance Systems Analyst, Tax (first published 7 Aug 2026): "an early hire on a small team", "not a software engineering role", runs tax-systems intake, configuration, UAT, SQL reconciliations and SOX/ITGC evidence, and uses Claude to build tools while checking output before anyone relies on it [JOB-ANTHROPIC-2026].
  - Basis (AI accounting agents), Member of Accounting Staff (20 May 2026): accountants who "won't be doing accounting for clients" but build the product with engineers; its Accounting Solutions Consultant posting (6 Jan 2026) asks for 3+ years in accounting, finance, FP&A, banking or private equity and lists pay of $100K to $225K [JOB-BASIS-2026].
  These are four employers selected for being AI or tech-sector tax teams; they show role shape, not hiring volume or prevalence.
- **Skills demand.** In Deloitte's Tax Transformation Trends 2025 survey (via Deloitte Luxembourg), 45% name AI-related skills as their greatest need in one to two years, 94% expect AI skills to be essential in four to five years, 53% are recruiting digitally native talent, and limited AI expertise is a barrier for 36% [DELOITTE-LU-2025; "Topping up the talent pool" and Figure 4]. EY: 73% prioritise hiring data scientists and tax technology experts and 89% invest in upskilling and reskilling [EY-TFO-2025; talent section]. Thomson Reuters: 95% expect GenAI to be central within five years, yet only 24% treat GenAI skills as "nice to have" in new hires and 2% as a requirement [TR-TENSIONS-2025; Tension Point 5].
- **Skills shortage in the roles themselves.** About one third of KPMG's indirect-tax respondents have no technology specialists, and the report says teams are "simply not equipped with enough people with the right technology skill set" [KPMG-INDIRECT-2026; p.7]. KPMG Germany's top hurdles to AI include IT approvals (47%), data protection (41%) and lack of knowledge about AI applications (41%) [KPMG-DE-2026; "AI boosts efficiency"].

### 2.8 Skills, training and the competence duty

- **The regulator frames training as a duty.** IRS OPR Alert 2026-19 says competence under §10.35 includes understanding the technology and its limits, §10.36 requires firm procedures including comprehensive staff training on AI use, and §10.22 requires reviewing AI output, with cited facts, citations and calculations verified [IRS-OPR-2026; Circular 230 sections 1, 3 and 4].
- **Fees and efficiency.** The alert says billing for manual time not actually spent may violate §10.27 depending on facts, and that cost savings should be passed on openly [IRS-OPR-2026; §10.27 section]. This bears on how recovered junior time can be priced.
- **Training failure modes reported by practitioners.** See TEI recap above; the recap also mentions undocumented automation built by departed staff, a key-person risk for new technical roles [TEI-TAXTECH-2026].
- **Unapproved use.** More than one-third of professionals admit using unauthorised AI tools [TR-FOP-2026; "Govern the tools being used"].

### 2.9 Retention and the employee value proposition

- More than one-quarter of tax and audit professionals say they would not accept a job at a firm lacking professional-grade AI, almost one in three say they would consider leaving if AI expectations are unmet within two years, nearly half say work fulfilment is the primary benefit of AI, and nearly half feel pressure to generate financial gains from AI (Thomson Reuters Future of Professionals 2026, tax and audit paper; vendor-owned) [TR-FOP-2026; paragraphs 4 to 5 and "Align AI with your professionals' needs"].
- In AICPA's 629-respondent CPA Firm Top Issues survey (20 Apr to 22 May 2026), staff retention is in the top five expected five-year issues for all firm sizes except solo practitioners; hiring experienced staff ranked first for firms with 11 to 30 employees; staff workload management is in the top four for the four larger size groups [AICPA-TOPISSUES-2026; paragraphs 2 to 5].
- Accounting Today's Top 100 firms describe investments in in-house recruiters, development and compensation, and link client retention to staff retention [AT-TOP100-2026; page 12].

### 2.10 AI-native entrants and the labour model

- Multiplier (raised $27.5M, June 2025) bought Citrine International Tax, a 12-person cross-border tax accounting boutique, applied its AI, and says profit margins more than doubled (company-reported via TechCrunch); investors back similar roll-ups of people-intensive service firms [MULTIPLIER-2025; paragraphs 3 to 6].
- Black Ore says 40% of the 20 largest CPA firms were in its 75-firm pilot (company claim) [BLACKORE-2026; paragraph 2]. Neither source reports staffing changes at the customer firms.

### 2.11 Tax authority workforce (context)

- GAO found IRS staffing reductions left too few skilled employees to support or develop AI tools; one analytics group lost 63 employees who worked full- or part-time on AI; the IRS had not identified AI skills needs or a workforce plan, and agreed to recommendation 1 (update expected fall 2026) [GAO-2026; summary and recommendations table].
- The IRS CEO told staff AI "is not designed to eliminate jobs"; the CHCO said headcount began and will end 2026 at 74,000 despite 4,000+ hires; the CIO said filing season 2027 has twice as many provisions to implement as 2026 [IRS-AIFIRST-2026; paragraphs 2 to 9].

## 3. What it means for an AI transformation conversation

### 3.1 Implications

1. **Frame talent as three linked problems, not one.** Supply (credentialled people), development (how juniors acquire judgment when routine work is automated) and shape (the roles that run tax data and systems). Evidence is strongest for development risk and role shape, weakest for supply recovery [AICPA-ENROLL-2026; TRI-JUDGMENT-2026; TRI-DUALROLES-2025].
2. **Capacity gains do not automatically become headcount cuts in-house.** Surveyed corporate departments largely expect flat headcount and still intend to hire [KPMG-DE-2026; KPMG-INDIRECT-2026; TR-CORPTAX-2025]. The pressure is on the firm side (entry-level intake) [FORTUNE-PWC-2025; SFN-BIGFOUR-2025]. Ask which of the two the leader actually sits in.
3. **The "supervise AI" model assumes judgment that is formed by the work being automated.** If review is the entry job, who teaches review? The profession's own leaders say this is unsolved [TRI-JUDGMENT-2026; PRIMEGLOBAL-2026].
4. **Hiring profile is shifting toward hybrid skills, but requirements lag.** Postings mix tax operations with SQL, ERP and SOX; surveys show GenAI skills rarely required in hiring [JOB-DATABRICKS-2026; JOB-ANTHROPIC-2026; TR-TENSIONS-2025].
5. **Retention now includes the AI toolset.** Professionals link staying to professional-grade AI and fulfilling work, and firms that save time but do not reinvest in coaching risk thinning their bench [TR-FOP-2026; CPAPA-LEDGER-2026].
6. **Compliance rules shape workforce design.** Training, competence, fee transparency and offshore consent all attach to staffing choices; they are design inputs, not afterthoughts [IRS-OPR-2026; ECFR-7216].

### 3.2 Interview probes

1. When AI drafts the first pass, who on your team still prepares a return or a provision from scratch, and how often? How do your seniors know a junior can challenge the output?
2. What did you do with the hours recovered last busy season: capacity for more clients, fewer seasonal hires, coaching time, or lower fees? Did the fee conversation change (OPR Alert 2026-19 on §10.27)?
3. If you are an in-house leader: what are your open requisitions, which of them are tax technologists or data roles, and where do those people report (tax, finance data, IT)? Who covers the work if the one person who built the automation leaves?
4. How much of your first-pass preparation or research is offshore or outsourced, what does your §7216 consent flow say about AI subprocessors, and has AI changed the offshore plan?
5. What does your AI policy say about training duty (§10.36) and which roles have a measured competence check before they sign off on AI-assisted work?
6. Where do you expect your exposure to retirements and to fewer entrants to bite first: partners, managers, or technical specialists?

### 3.3 Objections and evidence-based responses

| Objection | Evidence-based response |
|---|---|
| "There is a CPA shortage; AI just fills the gap, so nobody loses a job." | Both are partly true. Enrollment and first-time exam candidates are rising [AICPA-ENROLL-2026], while PwC US reportedly plans a roughly one-third entry-level cut [FORTUNE-PWC-2025] and young workers in exposed jobs are 19% below trend through reduced hiring [STANFORD-CANARIES-2026]. The gap may close from the supply side while entry-level demand falls. No source quantifies the net for tax. |
| "AI will shrink corporate tax teams." | Surveyed in-house leaders mostly expect stable headcount [KPMG-DE-2026; KPMG-INDIRECT-2026], and one academic argues culpability keeps people in the loop [SALA-TAXNOTES-2026]. But 21% of KPMG indirect-tax respondents expect a decrease, and the Stanford and MIT results show junior-weighted effects at firm level [MIT-DOWNING-2026]. Expect composition change rather than uniform change. |
| "Juniors will learn by reviewing AI output." | Practitioners propose it [CPAPA-STOPTRAIN-2026], but AICPA's lead warns juniors must do the work before supervising AI's version, and notes more than half of incoming tax-firm staff already need fundamentals remediation [TRI-JUDGMENT-2026]. No firm has published results on reviewer-first training. |
| "Offshoring is the real labour story; AI just replaces it." | Plausible, and one guest says so [CPAPA-TAXGPT-2026], but the sourcing data I found is indirect-tax-specific (19% outsourced, 26% co-sourced) [KPMG-INDIRECT-2026] and no source measures AI-driven change in offshore tax staffing. Treat it as a hypothesis to test with the client's own data. |
| "We cannot hire tax technologists; they are too expensive and rare." | Postings show employers are hiring them anyway and accept tax-adjacent experience [JOB-DATABRICKS-2026], and 57% of departments hiring a tax-only technologist chose a technology background [TRI-DUALROLES-2025]. Pay and supply evidence is thin; the listed Basis range ($100K to $225K) is for a product role, not a benchmark. |
| "Staff will resist AI." | In the vendor-owned survey the larger risk to retention is the absence of AI: over a quarter would not join a firm without it [TR-FOP-2026]. The same survey shows over a third using unapproved tools, so the issue is governance more than refusal. |

## 6. Gaps

- **Measured outcomes are absent.** No source reports hiring, utilisation, review-time, error-rate or retention results from a redesigned junior-training model, from AI-assisted offshore replacement, or from the agent-prepared-return workflow. Everything on training design is proposal or opinion [TRI-JUDGMENT-2026; CPAPA-LEDGER-2026].
- **Tax-specific entry-level data.** Stanford, MIT and the UK graduate figures are not limited to tax. The PwC US tax-associate figure (32%) comes through a secondary page that I could not re-open and a Business Insider original I did not read; US Big Four tax intake for KPMG, Deloitte and EY was not found. The Scottish Financial News UK intake figures lack stated primary sources.
- **Preparer and seasonal labour trend.** I found only a single IRS PTIN snapshot (1 Sep 2026); archived earlier snapshots were not reachable, so there is no trend. BLS's tax preparers page returned 403 or 422 and was not read. I found no data on seasonal preparer hiring at firms other than H&R Block, nor on Jackson Hewitt or franchise attrition.
- **Offshoring.** No independent 2025 to 2026 quantification of offshore tax headcount or of AI's effect on it. Web results were provider marketing (for example an uncorroborated "AICPA MAP survey" claim that 25% of firms use offshore teams), which I did not use. Business Insider and Going Concern (India) coverage was not reached.
- **Unverified figures in circulation.** The 340,000 fewer accountants, "25% enrollment rise since 2023" [TRI-JUDGMENT-2026], and the BLS-attributed 300,000 departures and 125-million-hour shortfall [BLACKORE-2026] were not traced to a primary source.
- **Survey limits.** Several headline numbers come from vendor-owned or Big Four surveys with undisclosed sampling (KPMG Germany sample size; Bloomberg Tax "three out of four"; Deloitte survey sample). The EY and Thomson Reuters full reports are gated, so only press releases or summary articles were read.
- **Pay and compensation.** No salary benchmarks for tax technologists or data engineers beyond individual postings; no tax-specific wage trend after AI.
- **Community voice.** Reddit (r/taxpros, r/Accounting) and TaxProTalk were blocked from this environment; the community type is represented by one newsletter only.
- **Not reached:** the AICPA Profession Ready report itself, CPA.com "AI-ready associate" material, ICAEW and ACCA pipeline data, the UK Tax Adviser and CIOT workforce material, Tax Notes full articles, and the TEI 2026 Annual Conference (starts 18 Oct 2026, no recaps yet).
- **Small-practice and enrolled-agent voice.** NAEA or NATP survey data on solo preparers' staffing and AI adoption was not found.
