# Tax: state of AI disruption (cross-cutting)

- **Archetype:** tax (compliance, provision, indirect tax, transfer pricing, international tax and Pillar Two, controversy, research and advisory, tax data and technology; firms of all sizes and in-house departments)
- **Topic:** state-of-ai-disruption: adoption today, AI-native entrants and roll-ups, and four business-model scenarios (agentic preparers, continuous compliance, tax-authority pre-population, outcome-based pricing)
- **Prepared:** 2026-10-17 (wave 2). Method: landscape leads followed with `curl`/`pdftotext`; `r.jina.ai` mirror for GAO, OECD, Axios and EY; six rationed web searches for the scenario topics. 55 sources opened (access: full) across nine source types; 2 more are search-only and flagged.
- **Citation format:** `[REF, locator]`. Locators are the page's heading, section or paragraph position because most sources are web pages without page numbers. "Company claim" means the figure is the vendor's or firm's own statement, not independently measured.

## 1. Summary

- **Usage is broad; measurement is thin.** Four surveys with different samples all say a majority of tax functions use AI in some form: 81% of tax and audit professionals use AI regularly (Thomson Reuters), 71% of German tax departments use AI tools (KPMG Germany), 92% of 176 indirect-tax leaders use AI "in some form" (Fonoa). Against that, only 12.5% of the Fonoa sample have specific AI targets or KPIs, and 57.4% would not be confident defending AI-assisted decisions to a tax authority. Adoption is measured; outcomes mostly are not. [TR-FOP-2026, first paragraph; KPMG-DE-2026, key findings; FONOA-2026, "AI adoption" and "AI mandate" sections]
- **The 2025 and 2026 numbers describe different populations and cannot be tracked as a trend.** The 67% of corporate tax departments "not using GenAI" comes from 288 departments surveyed for TR's 2025 report; the 71% "using AI tools" is a German KPMG quick survey with no stated sample size. [TR-CORPTAX-2025, closing paragraph; KPMG-DE-2026, header and key findings]
- **Agentic products are announced and shipping, but production evidence is mostly vendor-stated, and one adopter's trial of five vendors found none ready.** Thomson Reuters, Black Ore, Basis, Perplexity, Avalara, Sovos, EY, KPMG and PwC all announced or described agentic tax work between June 2025 and May 2026. A 4,500-return CPA firm (WCG) evaluated five AI preparation vendors in 2026 and committed to none for this season. [TR-COCOUNSEL-2025; BLACKORE-2026; WCG-2026, opening and "five vendors" sections]
- **Independent accuracy evidence is scarce and unflattering.** The one public benchmark of end-to-end personal return calculation found frontier models correct on fewer than a third of simplified returns (2025 models). No independent head-to-head of commercial tax preparation or research products was found. [COLUMN-TAXCALCBENCH-2025, abstract]
- **AI-native entrants are real and funded, but their operating results are company statements.** Examples: Multiplier (ex-Stripe founder; says margins more than doubled at one acquired boutique), Crete/Current (Thrive-backed; $500M planned acquisition spend), Black Ore (75 pilot firms). Private equity holds stakes in 11 of the top 30 US firms, so AI roll-ups compete with a larger, older capital wave. [MULTIPLIER-2025; CRETE-REUTERS-2025; JUPID-2026]
- **Outcome-based pricing is being debated, and the first regulator statement cuts against passing AI savings to the firm.** IRS OPR Alert 2026-19 (24 Jun 2026) says billing for manual time AI replaced may violate the unconscionable-fee rule; the AICPA (Sep 2026) is asking the IRS to clarify because the language "doesn't appear to allow" value pricing. [IRS-OPR-2026, Circular 230 item 2; JOFA-FEES-2026, first paragraphs]
- **Tax authorities are the counter-party and are scaling AI while shrinking.** GAO counted 126 IRS AI use cases (June 2025) against 10 in August 2022 and found the skills base eroded; the IRS CEO says an "AI-first IRS" will reach a mid-90s compliance rate in five years (a forecast). Pre-population is a data-availability problem more than an AI problem in the sources found. [GAO-2026, "What GAO Found"; IRS-AIFIRST-2026, paragraphs 4-7; ESP-DIRECTFILE-2026, summary]
- **The scenario that most affects an advisory conversation is the mismatch one.** Deloitte's 2x2 crosses a company's AI adoption with its tax authority's, and calls the "high-adoption, low-authority" quadrant "more realistic for most multinationals". Departments with advanced AI facing authorities at different stages face a patchwork, not a single future. [DELOITTE-TTT-2026, pp. 3, 5]

- **Expectations, labelled as forecasts.** KPMG Germany's respondents expect stable headcount (58% domestic, 72% abroad) and 90% "expect increased use of generative AI" [KPMG-DE-2026, key findings and "AI boosts efficiency"]. - **The firm-side adopter test is the most informative single piece of practice evidence.** WCG (approximately 4,500 1040s in 2026, heavy on K-1s) tested five AI tax-preparation vendors and says "no one is ready"; its objections were no K-1 or consolidated 1099 support in one product, a proprietary-platform "reset" risk when a return hits an exception, the handoff into Lacerte or UltraTax where diagnostics still run, and prices of "$100, $150 or potentially more per return". This is one firm's published opinion (24 Jul 2026), not a study. [WCG-2026, summary bullets and vendor sections; publication date from page metadata]
- **Indirect tax shows the vendor-claim pattern clearly.** Thomson Reuters says ONESOURCE Sales and Use Tax AI cuts routine reporting time "by up to 65%", early customers report "up to 75%" lower audit exposure, and large-enterprise compliance cycles fall from 30 to 11 days; these are company statements in a launch release (15 Jan 2026) [TR-SUTAI-2026, bullets 1-4]. Avalara's Agentic Tax and Compliance platform (launched 30 Sep 2025) has agents that file "upon approval" and that notify a human for "novel situations" and "key decisions" [AVALARA-AGENTIC-2025, paragraphs 4 and 8].
- **Mid-tier firms position AI as platform investment.** RSM US launched myRSM Tax (16 Jun 2025) as an AI-powered ecosystem building on a "$1 billion dollar technology and AI investment strategy" [RSM-MYRSM-2025, paragraphs 1-2]. The release names automation of manual tasks and structured tax-data access and gives no outcome figures [RSM-MYRSM-2025, paragraph 3].

### 2.3 Quality evidence that exists

- **TaxCalcBench (Column Tax, arXiv 22 Jul 2025):** state-of-the-art models "succeed in calculating less than a third of federal income tax returns even on this simplified sample set", misusing tax tables, miscalculating and misdetermining eligibility. The authors work for a company that sells tax infrastructure. The models tested were 2025 models. [COLUMN-TAXCALCBENCH-2025, abstract]
- **A neurosymbolic alternative (Johns Hopkins, arXiv, revised 5 Feb 2026):** combining an LLM that translates statute into logic programs with a symbolic solver can "dramatically improve performance" and bring expected cost "well below real-world averages" on the SARA dataset, with auditability treated as the design requirement. This is a research prototype on a statutory-reasoning dataset, not a deployed product. [JHU-TAXREASON-2025, abstract]
- **Consumer-chatbot test, secondhand:** PYMNTS reports a TaxSlayer test of four chatbots, reported by the New York Times, in which the tools miscalculated refund or amount owed "by an average of more than $2,000" across eight fictional scenarios. The original was not opened. [PERPLEXITY-2026, "From Chat to Execution"]
- **Vendor accuracy claims:** Black Ore says Tax Autopilot achieved "accuracy above 99 percent across tens of thousands of returns during the pilot" and time savings of "up to 98 percent on complex filings" (company statements relayed by Sovereign Magazine; the measure of accuracy is not defined) [BLACKORE-2026, "How Tax Autopilot Works"]. Perplexity's claims for Computer for Taxes are from internal tests, per the landscape; the PYMNTS piece read here gives no accuracy figure [PERPLEXITY-2026, whole article].
- **Professional-liability evidence:** In *Clinco v. Commissioner* (T.C. Memo. 2026-16), Judge Holmes found three of four cases cited on a notice-of-deficiency signature argument were likely "hallucinations"; the court "stopped short of formal sanctions" but warned the tax bar [TAXCT-CLINCO-2026, paragraphs 2-6]. OPR cites a Deloitte Australia report (July 2025) with invented quotes and non-existent references, after which Deloitte reportedly agreed to refund part of its fee [IRS-OPR-2026, "Real-World Consequences"].
- **Silent failure:** TEI Tax Tech attendees described AI that "fails quietly and even politely", including an agent that admitted it could not open an uploaded schedule after producing numbers (an anecdote relayed at the seminar) [TEI-TAXTECH-2026, "A very bad wizard"].
- **Absence, stated plainly:** no source found gives override rates, error rates in production, parallel-run results or review-time measurements for agent-prepared returns outside vendor statements. [Checked across all sources listed; see Gaps.]

### 2.4 AI-native entrants and roll-ups

- **Multiplier Holdings** (founded late 2022 by ex-Stripe executive Noah Pepper) pivoted from selling software to tax accountants to acquiring them: it bought Citrine International Tax (12 people), applied its AI tools, and says profit margins "more than doubled". It raised $27.5M (seed and Series A, Lightspeed lead) on 18 Jun 2025 and says it aims to build an "AI-powered competitor to the Big Four". A later broker blog says Multiplier has raised $62.5M and bought 8 firms (secondary source). [MULTIPLIER-2025, paragraphs 3-9; JUPID-2026, "Who is buying" summary]
- **Lightspeed's investor view** (a forecast-flavoured claim by a funder): the strategy works best buying small firms because "they are more open to changing their existing processes"; a 200-accountant firm "is unlikely to get adopted at a [high] rate". [MULTIPLIER-2025, paragraph 8]
- **Crete Professionals Alliance** (Thrive Capital-backed, now called Current per the Jupid blog) told Reuters (4 Jun 2025) it planned to invest "over $500 million" over two years acquiring US accounting firms and equipping them with OpenAI-powered tools. It reported over $300 million annual revenue, 20+ accounting businesses and 900 employees at that date, taking majority stakes and leaving original owners a minority stake. A Crete-owned firm's president said AI tools in audit testing saved "hundreds of hours every month" (a quote). [CRETE-REUTERS-2025, paragraphs 1-6; JUPID-2026, summary]
- **Private equity is the larger comparator.** A buyer-side blog cites KPMG Corporate Finance: PE stakes in 11 of the top 30 US firms (as of Q3 2025), more than $50 billion invested in CPA firms over six years, and expectation of more than half of the top 30 being PE-backed. The blog is written by a roll-up buyer (Jupid) and attributes its figures to others; it was last reviewed 2 Sep 2026. [JUPID-2026, summary list]
- **Software-first entrants** (Black Ore, Basis, Aiwyn) sell to existing firms rather than buy them. Basis raised $100M at a $1.15B valuation (Series B, Feb 2026); a Black Ore article relays Basis's claim that it is used by 30% of the 25 largest US firms (the Built In NYC article itself does not state this). [BASIS-2026, paragraphs 1-2; BLACKORE-2026, "AI Tax Preparation Funding"]
- **Black Ore's positioning relies on a labour-shortage argument**: it cites Bureau of Labor Statistics departures and a "125 million-hour annual shortfall" it did not attribute to a source. [BLACKORE-2026, "Black Ore and the Accountant Shortage"]
- **Search-only, not verified:** Accrual ($16M), Gelt, Skalar and Limetax rounds appear in the landscape from search extracts; Sifted blocked a direct read. A search summary describes Accrual as General Catalyst-backed. [Landscape AINATIVE-ROLLUPS; web search 2026-10-17, not opened]

### 2.5 Tax authorities as counter-party

- **IRS scale and capacity.** GAO reports 126 active IRS AI use cases in the June 2025 inventory (10 in August 2022), of which 65 were too sensitive or R&D exempt from public reporting and 61% (77) were "in development"; the Research, Applied Analytics and Statistics group said it lost 63 employees who worked on AI; over 25% of use cases lacked a stated benefit. [GAO-2026, "What GAO Found"]
- **Different count, different scope.** The Treasury-wide inventory reported by FedScoop (29 Jan 2026) counts 129 Treasury use cases (54 prior year) with 61 at the IRS (49 prior year), and the only IRS "high-impact" use is a pre-deployment agentic tool for cloud infrastructure code. [TREAS-AIINV-2026, paragraphs 2-5; publication date from page metadata] The two IRS counts (126 and 61) are not comparable without the scoping; do not reconcile them.
- **Agents in IRS legal and dispute functions.** The IRS is rolling out Salesforce Agentforce in the Office of Chief Counsel, Taxpayer Advocate Service and Office of Appeals for case summarization and search; the Salesforce executive says agents are "not allowed to make final decisions". An IRS Chief Counsel technology counsel is quoted saying it "would be negligence" not to use AI tools. [IRS-AGENTFORCE-2025, paragraphs 1-6 (Axios, 21 Nov 2025; read via text mirror)]
- **"AI-first" announcement.** The IRS CEO told staff on Wednesday 30 Sep 2026 that AI "is not designed to eliminate jobs"; headcount began and will end the year at about 74,000 after the 2025 cut of more than a quarter of the workforce. He said "five years from now" he sees "a mid-90s compliance rate", against an IRS estimate of about 85% (a forecast by the IRS CEO). [IRS-AIFIRST-2026, paragraphs 4-9; dated 2 Oct 2026]
- **Audit selection and notices.** Practitioner commentary (opinion, 2 Jul 2026) says AI models support examination selection and third-party matching that generate automated CP2000 notices; some figures in that piece (TIGTA project counts) were not verified against primary reports here. [ACCTODAY-2026, paragraphs 2-9] EisnerAmper (27 Jan 2026) says the IRS issued a January 2024 request for information on AI checks "at their initial filing" and that revenue agents are beginning to use generative AI for drafting exam documents. [EISNER-IRSAI-2026, "fraud" and "examination" paragraphs]
- **OECD tax administrations.** Heads of tax administrations (49 jurisdictions, Cape Town, 18-20 Nov 2025) agreed to "move towards pilots" on real-time automated sharing of secure information; the Tax Administration 2025 series says it covers how AI "is shaping the future of tax administration". The release states the pilots intent, not outcomes. [OECD-FTA-2025, bullet 1 and paragraph 3 after the bullets]
- **HMRC.** Its roadmap (reported by ICAS, 29 Oct 2025) targets at least 90% digital customer interactions by 2030 (about 76% then), and names agentic AI for case management and process automation; HMRC plans principles for third-party AI in software that interacts with it. [ICAS-HMRC-2025, "The strategic approach" and "Specific examples"] HMRC told an FOI requester its R&D compliance team did not use generative AI for taxpayer letters, while the FT reported suggestions of unauthorised use by individual caseworkers (relayed by ICAS). [ICAS-HMRC-2025, "Potential pitfalls"]
- **EU.** A European Papers article argues the EU AI Act's treatment of tax-administration systems is unclear and cites SyRI, eKasa and the Dutch toeslagenaffaire as seminal cases of tax-authority AI harm. [HADWICK-EUAIACT, abstract]

### 2.6 Governance constraints that shape every scenario

- **Existing duties apply.** IRS OPR Alert 2026-19 (24 Jun 2026) applies Circular 230 sections 10.22 (due diligence), 10.27(a) (fees), 10.35 (competence), 10.36 (firm procedures), 10.37 (written advice) and IRC 6713/7216 (confidentiality) to AI use; section 10.36 requires firm procedures, training, vetting of third-party AI and documentation. [IRS-OPR-2026, items 1-6]
- **Opacity as the operative test.** A practitioner commentary argues the alert's section 10.37 language makes untraceable AI output a reliance problem and says a tool that cannot show its sources forces a manual verification layer ("paying twice"). [CPAPA-OPACITY-2026, paragraphs 3-9; dated 19 Sep 2026; opinion]
- **Risk framework and council.** The Werfel framework (20 risks in four categories) and the AICPA-led Council on AI Risk in Tax (announced 23 Sep 2026) exist to refine it; the framework is a risk register (hallucination, drift, explainability, over-trust, policy drift), not a standard. [AICPA-CART-2026, paragraphs 3-4; AICPA-WERFEL-2026, Appendix A, risks 1-5] Publication date conflict: the *Journal of Accountancy* (1 Sep 2026) refers to Werfel's article in "the August issue of The Tax Adviser"; the CART item (23 Sep 2026) says it was published "earlier this month". [TAXCT-CLINCO-2026, paragraph 12; AICPA-CART-2026, quote by Werfel]
- **Transfer pricing.** The IRS LB&I director for treaty and transfer pricing operations said on 29 Jul 2026 that AI "shouldn't replace" legal, economic and factual judgment, that expert witnesses must explain AI inputs, assumptions and methods, and that the IRS will "want to see the formulas". [IRS-LBI-TP-2026, paragraph 4]

### 2.7 Business-model scenarios

Each scenario separates what exists, what is forecast, and what would have to be true. No source found assigns probabilities.

**Scenario A: agentic preparers (agent drafts the return; a professional reviews and signs)**

- *Exists (announced or early adopter):* TR Ready to Review (Nov 2025), Black Ore Tax Autopilot (open to firms 29 Apr 2026), Basis (agent that "autonomously completed a 1065", demonstrated, Feb 2026), Perplexity Computer for Taxes (2 Apr 2026, consumer, $17 per month Pro tier). [TR-COCOUNSEL-2025; BLACKORE-2026; BASIS-2026, paragraph 2; PERPLEXITY-2026, "From Chat to Execution" and "How It Works"]
- *Evidence of readiness:* mixed to negative from the one independent adopter evaluation (WCG, K-1 gaps, software handoff) and from benchmark work (TaxCalcBench). Black Ore says its product routes the finished return into a firm's existing tax software for sign-off, which addresses the handoff concern WCG raises about other vendors; WCG did not name which vendor did what, so no vendor-level inference is made here. [BLACKORE-2026, "How Tax Autopilot Works"; WCG-2026, vendor sections]
- *Forecast (Thomson Reuters Institute):* three strategic paths for firms (elevate, scale, reimagine); a minority are deferring decisions. Vendor-owned. [TR-FOP-2026, "Finding your strategic path"]
- *What would have to be true:* return-level exception handling that does not strand work in proprietary systems; independent error measurement; supervision that preserves junior development (TR names "Define the role of early-career professionals" as a priority). [WCG-2026, vendor 3 paragraph; TR-FOP-2026, "Universal priorities"]
- *Counter-view (academic):* Sala argues AI will not shrink corporate tax teams much, because models "conflate tax preparation with tax practice" and AI "cannot shoulder regulatory, ethical, or financial consequences of culpability". [SALA-TAXNOTES-2026, abstract paragraph]

**Scenario B: continuous compliance (always-on, near-real-time, preventive controls)**

- *Exists:* vendor framing, not yet an outcome. Avalara describes agents as "always on, always ready, 24/7" and an "always-on" model replacing point-in-time processes; Sovos plans proactive issue detection before deadlines; Thomson Reuters brands "touchless compliance … by default, manual by exception". [AVALARA-AGENTIC-2025, paragraphs 2-3; SOVOS-SOVI-2026, bulleted capabilities; TR-SUTAI-2026, Grove quote]
- *Drivers on the authority side:* OECD tax administrations moving to real-time automated information-sharing pilots; HMRC's digital targets. [OECD-FTA-2025; ICAS-HMRC-2025]
- *Scenario text (Deloitte, a firm's planning framework, PDF dated July 2026):* Scenario 4 "Tax transformed" has "continuous or near real-time compliance" and preventive AI-driven controls; Scenario 3 (authority advanced, company not) means "heightened audit and dispute exposure" and a "forced, high-pressure transformation". Deloitte presents these as scenarios, not predictions, and calls Scenario 2 (company advanced, authority not) "more realistic for most multinationals". [DELOITTE-TTT-2026, pp. 3-7; creation date from PDF metadata]
- *Agentic commerce adds a tax-collection question:* AI systems "can't bear tax obligations", so "the company operating the agent may have to collect taxes" under marketplace rules. The McKinsey $3-5 trillion by 2030 figure is a forecast cited by Bloomberg Tax. [BTAX-AGENTICCOMMERCE-2026, paragraphs 1, 4-5]
- *What would have to be true:* upstream data quality and common data models (Deloitte lists data integrity, clear ownership of AI-driven outcomes and audit trails as decisions "that apply across all four" scenarios). Data, not calculation, is called the binding constraint after Pillar Two by a vendor (Kognitos, so interested). [DELOITTE-TTT-2026, p. 8; KOGNITOS-2026, "defining 2026 development" paragraph]

**Scenario C: tax-authority pre-population (authority prepares or pre-fills; taxpayer confirms)**

- *What the sources say about feasibility:* a Treasury-economist NBER simulation (2022, 2019 data) estimated 66-75 million returns (42-48% of all returns) could be accurately pre-populated from current-year information returns and the prior-year return, with accuracy falling as income rises. That is a non-AI estimate. [NBER-PREPOP-2022, abstract]
- *Policy view (May 2026):* a Direct File policy report says expanding pre-population "would end tax filing as we know it", but that within the existing system "we cannot eradicate tax returns altogether", and that it depends on "technical improvements in the timing and processing of third-party information returns". It frames the constraint as data, not AI. [ESP-DIRECTFILE-2026, Summary bullets 1-3]
- *AI link:* the IRS RFI (January 2024) sought AI to identify issues at filing so taxpayers can correct them; the IRS "has also stated" it would use AI to flag credits and deductions not claimed (EisnerAmper's account). [EISNER-IRSAI-2026, "fraud" paragraph]
- *Incentive for the authority:* with headcount flat at about 74,000, the IRS CEO frames AI as raising compliance and reducing wait times. [IRS-AIFIRST-2026, paragraphs 4-6]
- *No source found reports an AI-driven return-free or authority-computed filing system live in any jurisdiction* (search 2026-10-17; sources above are policy and research, not launches). The OECD item is the nearest signal (pilots on real-time information sharing). [OECD-FTA-2025, bullet 1]
- *What would have to be true:* timely, reliable third-party data; authority capacity (GAO found AI skills eroded after staff cuts); governance for authority-side hallucination and drift (Werfel risks 1-5). [GAO-2026, "What GAO Found"; AICPA-WERFEL-2026, Appendix A]

**Scenario D: outcome-based and value pricing**

- *Pressure that exists:* TR reports "nearly half" of respondents feel pressure to generate financial gains from AI and "one-third" say their firms have not adapted commercial models. [TR-FOP-2026, paragraph 4]
- *Stated firm intent (Bloomberg Tax, Mar 2026):* PwC's US tax leader: "Time's becoming less and less of relevance"; KPMG's advisory vice chair says value-based pricing "is, from our view, incredibly important"; RSM's partner frames a 20-hour report cut to 10 hours as "all margin for me or … price reduction for our client". RKL has onboarded "several dozen" clients under a value-based model it is piloting. These are leaders' statements, not published price changes. [BTAX-PRICING-2026, paragraphs 3-4, 9, 14-15 and "New Pricing Options"]
- *The regulatory friction:* OPR says billing for manual time not spent, or double billing for AI-assisted tasks, "may violate" section 10.27 "depending on the facts" and that practitioners should "fairly credit" cost reductions; the AICPA says that implies efficiencies be passed on, "overlooks" AI costs and that services are "increasingly priced based on value delivered". The AICPA CEO called the language non-authoritative and possibly "overstepping a little bit". The reporting does not say whether the IRS has agreed to change anything. [IRS-OPR-2026, item 2; JOFA-FEES-2026, paragraphs 2-4 and CEO paragraph]
- *Cost side:* per-return AI preparation pricing quoted to WCG ("$100, $150 or potentially more") is a variable cost a firm would carry under any pricing model. [WCG-2026, summary bullets]
- *Not found:* any firm publishing contracts priced on outcome (for example accepted-return or audit-result fees). The Bloomberg piece describes value-based pricing as under development.

## 3. What it means for an AI transformation conversation

### Implications

1. **Start from the use-versus-outcome gap.** Most leaders can say they use AI; few can say what it produced. A first diagnostic is whether the function has any workflow-level measure (time saved, notices avoided, review hours) and an audit trail for AI-assisted decisions; the Fonoa checklist asks these five questions, and most respondents answer no to at least three. [FONOA-2026, "How to identify your AI accountability gap"]
2. **Separate the capability question from the ownership question.** The strongest practitioner signal in 2026 is not "does it work" but who owns it, who reviews, and what it costs per run (TEI Tax Tech: ownership vacuum, silent errors, rising token costs). [TEI-TAXTECH-2026, key insights]
3. **Data readiness before agents.** EY finds 51% of tax and finance functions early in data maturity; Deloitte puts data integrity, ownership and explainability in all four scenarios; the first GloBE return exposed XML and data-mapping work (via TR's description of more than 100 data points). [EY-TFO-2025; DELOITTE-TTT-2026, p. 8; TR-CORPAI-2026, GIR paragraph]
4. **Plan against the authority, not only the vendor.** Whether a department's counter-party authority is at "Tax Administration 2.0" or "3.0" changes the urgency of upstream controls (Deloitte Scenario 3). [DELOITTE-TTT-2026, p. 6]
5. **Treat pricing and governance as one design decision.** For firms, the OPR fee language and the AICPA response mean the pricing model, engagement letter and AI-use documentation should be reviewed together. [IRS-OPR-2026, item 2; JOFA-FEES-2026]
6. **Roll-up competition is a strategy question for mid-size and small firms.** Funders say small firms are the targets; the AI-native buyers say they take majority stakes and run the production work. [MULTIPLIER-2025, paragraph 8; CRETE-REUTERS-2025, paragraph 5; JUPID-2026, comparison]

### Interview probes

- "Which tax processes would you say are in production with AI today, which are pilots, and which are only announced? Who signed off each?" (separates the three states)
- "What have you measured (hours, notices, first-time-right, review findings) and what is only a usage count?"
- "If a tax authority asked how an AI-assisted position was reached, what would you hand over?" (57.4% not confident in the Fonoa sample)
- "Who owns tax AI: tax, finance IT, a central AI team? Who pays the model costs?" (TEI poll: two of 50+)
- "Which of your counter-parties (IRS, HMRC, local authorities) are ahead of you on AI, and where does that create an asymmetry?" (Deloitte quadrants)
- For firms: "How do you price work AI has shortened, and has anyone read OPR Alert 2026-19 against your engagement letters?"
- For firms: "Who has approached you about acquisition, and what do you believe they will do to your production work?"
- For junior-development: "If review replaces preparation, how do juniors learn judgment?" (TR priority four)

| Objection | Evidence-based response |

## 6. Gaps

- **No measured outcomes for agent-prepared returns.** No source gives production error rates, override rates, review-time measurements or quality-control results outside company statements (Black Ore, TR, Avalara). The Black Ore pilot figures and the TR "up to" figures lack published definitions. [Sections 2.2, 2.3]
- **No independent head-to-head benchmark** of commercial tax preparation or research products; TaxBench (Columbia) is still in development per the landscape; TaxCalcBench tests 2025 general models, not products. [COLUMN-TAXCALCBENCH-2025]
- **Survey gaps.** Full reports behind the TR, Fonoa, Blue J/CPA.com, Karbon and Wolters Kluwer surveys were not reached (landing pages or articles only). TR-FOP-2026 sample size, KPMG-DE-2026 sample size and the AICPA tax software survey AI figures are unknown. The Wolters Kluwer Future Ready Accountant page returned 403.
- **EY survey figures in the landscape (75%, 80%) were not found in the primary release**; EY's 51% and 44% are used here instead. The landscape's TR "538 subset" and "35% use unapproved tools" were not verified (the article says "more than one-third").
- **AI-native entrants:** Accrual, Gelt, Skalar and Limetax rounds and Black Ore customer shares remain search-extract only (Sifted returned 403). Crete's figures are from June 2025 and may be out of date. The Jupid blog is a buyer's marketing content citing others.
