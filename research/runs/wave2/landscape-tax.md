# Landscape scan: tax

- **Archetype:** tax. Tax services and tax functions: tax compliance and reporting (corporate, partnership, individual and trust returns), tax provision (ASC 740 / IAS 12), indirect tax (VAT/GST, US sales and use), transfer pricing, international tax and the OECD global minimum tax (Pillar Two), tax controversy and disputes, tax research and advisory, and tax data and technology. Delivered by professional-services firms of all sizes (Big Four, mid-tier, small CPA practices) and by in-house corporate tax departments; tax authorities' own AI use is included as context.
- **Proposed modules:** compliance · provision · indirect-tax · transfer-pricing · international-pillar-two · controversy · research-advisory · tax-data-technology
- **Prepared:** 2026-10-17 (wave 2)
- **Method note:** Searches were run first, then pages were opened with `curl` (text extraction, `pdftotext` for PDFs) and, for bot-walled hosts, the `r.jina.ai` text mirror (worked for OECD news, GAO, TEI, Wolters Kluwer's procurement guide; failed for PwC, IMF, WK news and some OECD pages). Figures marked "company claim" or "per search" were not independently verified. Items marked `(unverified)` are single-source or conflicting. ## 1. Summary

- **Agentic tax products shipped in volume between November 2025 and June 2026, but the evidence is mostly vendor-sourced.** Thomson Reuters launched Ready to Review (agentic 1040 preparation) on 5 Nov 2025 and ONESOURCE Sales and Use Tax AI on 15 Jan 2026. Basis raised $100M and showed an agent completing a 1065 (Feb 2026). Perplexity released a general-purpose agent that drafts federal returns (2 Apr 2026). Black Ore opened Tax Autopilot to CPA firms after a pilot with 75 firms (29 Apr 2026). Avalara moved to "AI-executed" compliance (9 Apr 2026) and Sovos added MCP and A2A interoperability (24 Mar 2026). On the firm side, EY announced Tax Labs and a Tax Agent Factory (Nov 2025), PwC reports agents in production for K-1 mapping and diagnostics, and KPMG signed an Anthropic alliance with tax as the first focus (19 May 2026). Independent accuracy evidence is thin and unflattering: TaxCalcBench found frontier models compute fewer than a third of simplified federal returns correctly.
- **Regulators and professional bodies answered with "existing duties apply", not new rules.** UK PCRT bodies issued AI guidance (19 Jan 2026), the IRS Office of Professional Responsibility issued Alert 2026-19 (24 Jun 2026), and Australia's TPB issued final guidance (22 Jul 2026). In the Tax Court, *Clinco* (T.C. Memo. 2026-16) put hallucinated citations on the record, and the judge floated an AI-use certification rule. Danny Werfel's 20-risk AI framework for tax and the AICPA's Council on AI Risk in Tax (September 2026) are the closest thing to a governance standard. OPR's reminder not to bill manual hours that AI saved links governance to the pricing question.
- **Tax authorities are the other half of the story, and they are scaling AI while shrinking.** GAO (24 Mar 2026) counted 126 active IRS AI use cases, up from 10 in 2022, and found the skills base and strategy lagging after 2025 staff cuts. The IRS CEO told staff on Wednesday 30 Sep 2026 (reported 2 Oct) that the agency will be "AI-first", with flat headcount near 74,000 and an aim of a mid-90s compliance rate. HMRC's roadmap targets 90% digital interactions by 2030 with agentic case management. The OECD found 70% of 54 FTA members use AI. The EU AI Act's treatment of tax-administration systems remains unsettled.
- **Adoption is broad but shallow, and data is the bottleneck.** Thomson Reuters reports 81% of tax and audit professionals use AI regularly (2026), yet 67% of corporate tax departments were not using GenAI in its 2025 data and 35% of professionals use unapproved tools. KPMG Germany found 71% of tax departments using AI tools (May 2026). Fonoa found 92% of indirect tax functions use AI but only 12.5% have specific targets. EY found 75% of finance and tax functions in the earliest stages and 80% struggling with foundational data. TEI Tax Tech 2026 attendees named silent hallucinations, no clear owner and rising token costs as the new problems.
- **Pillar Two turned from a policy fight into a data pipeline.** The first GloBE Information Returns fell due on 30 Jun 2026; filers found country-specific XML variants and business-rule validation harder than the tax law. The Side-by-Side package (5 Jan 2026) and a second OECD package with a revised GIR (11 Sep 2026) keep moving the target.
- **Conferences:** TEI (TechTAXcelerate Nov 2025, Midyear Tax Labs Mar 2026, Tax Technology Seminar Apr 2026) is the richest public record of in-house practice. The ABA Tax Section May Meeting 2026 launched an AI task force and hosted an AI transfer pricing panel with an IRS panelist. Bloomberg Tax's June 2026 forum, the D.C. Bar tax conference (Jan 2026) and Oxford CBT (Jun 2026) put OpenAI, Big Four and Treasury speakers on stage. IFA Lisbon had only a side seminar, and TCPI and NYU showed no AI content. TEI's 2026 annual conference starts on or within a day of this scan.
- **Business-model pressure is visible:** firms are exploring value-based pricing as hours shrink (Bloomberg Tax, Mar 2026), AI-native roll-ups (Multiplier, Accrual, Skalar, Limetax reported) are buying practices, and TR finds one third of tax and audit firms have not adapted their commercial models.
- **Evidence density:** compliance high, indirect-tax high, research-advisory high, controversy high (if tax-authority AI is in scope), transfer-pricing medium, international-pillar-two medium, tax-data-technology medium, provision low. See section 10 for proposed module changes (combine provision with Pillar Two; split compliance; add tax-authority AI; treat professional responsibility as cross-cutting).

## 10. Module check

**Proposed list:** compliance · provision · indirect-tax · transfer-pricing · international-pillar-two · controversy · research-advisory · tax-data-technology.

The list matches how large firms and corporate tax departments divide the work: Big Four tax practices sell compliance, tax accounting (provision), indirect tax, transfer pricing, international tax and controversy; the technology layer sits across them. It under-serves three things the evidence shows: the small-firm and individual-return market, tax-authority AI, and professional-conduct governance. Evidence density is uneven, so the changes below mostly redistribute effort.

**Evidence density per module (judged by how much specific public material exists):**

| Module | Density | Why |
|---|---|---|
| compliance | **High** | Dense vendor and firm launches (TR Ready to Review, Black Ore, Basis, myRSM Tax, Perplexity, WK Axcess Scan), benchmarks (TaxCalcBench, TaxBench), TEI labs, PwC K-1 agents. Weak on measured outcomes. |
| provision (ASC 740 / IAS 12) | **Low** | Almost nothing provision-specific and independent: product pages (Bloomberg Tax, Corptax roadmap), a Bloomberg forum panel mention, vendor blogs, ASU 2023-09 as a workload driver. No practitioner-conference session titled on AI in provision was found. |
| indirect-tax | **High** | Avalara, Sovos, Vertex, TR launches in 2025–2026; Fonoa survey; ViDA timeline; agentic-commerce tax debate. Mostly vendor-sourced. |
| transfer-pricing | **Medium** | ABA §482 AI panel with IRS, IRS LB&I remarks (Jul 2026), KPMG, IBFD journal article, Aibidia and Exactera, OECD intragroup-services consultation. No survey data and few named deployments. |
| international-pillar-two | **Medium** | Rich on rules, deadlines and the XML filing pipeline (OECD packages of Jan and Sep 2026, first GIR season, WK/Lucanet/FGS). AI-specific evidence is thin (TR/Orbitax, Deloitte "Pillar Two Agent" unverified, KPMG 2024 survey). |
| controversy | **High** (if scoped to include tax-authority AI use and AI-related sanctions); **Medium** for practitioner-side dispute tooling | IRS GAO, TIGTA, Treasury inventory, "AI-first IRS", Clinco, OPR alert, EU AI Act commentary, AI audit-selection fairness. Practitioner controversy tools (notice triage, Appeals) are thin. |
| research-advisory | **High** | Blue J, CoCounsel, Bloomberg, CCH; hallucination and sanctions record; PCRT, OPR, TPB; TEI research labs; arXiv work. No independent benchmark of commercial tools. |
| tax-data-technology | **Medium** | Good surveys (EY, KPMG, Deloitte, TR, Fonoa) and TEI tech curriculum; the OECD digital-administration agenda; GIR XML. Few case studies of in-house data architecture. |

**Recommended changes:**

1. **Combine provision with international-pillar-two for one deep dive** ("income tax accounting and the global minimum tax"). Provision is low-density on its own, and Pillar Two's ASC 740/IAS 12 accounting and the GIR data pipeline are where provision teams are actually busy. This follows the brief's rule that low-density modules get one combined dive.
2. **Split compliance into two lenses, or at least two sub-sections:** (a) *individual, trust and small-business return preparation by CPA firms* (1040/1065/1120-S; Black Ore, Basis, Ready to Review, CCH Scan, H&R Block, TaxCalcBench) and (b) *corporate direct-tax compliance in house and at large firms* (ONESOURCE, Corptax, Form 6765 Section G, R&D credit automation, Neo.Tax). The vendors, buyers, risks and evidence are different. The first is where agentic launches cluster; the second is where TEI and large-company practice live.
3. **Scope controversy to include "tax authority AI and its governance"**, or add a short context module "tax-authority-ai". IRS, HMRC, ATO, OECD FTA, GAO/TIGTA and EU AI Act material drive taxpayer-side risk (audit selection, notices, chatbots) and the AI arms race (what a tax authority can see). It is under-documented in the brief's list yet is the best-evidenced theme in this scan.
4. **Treat professional responsibility and AI governance as a cross-cutting module** rather than leaving it to each module. The same documents apply everywhere: IRS OPR Alert 2026-19 (due diligence, competence, fees, §7216), PCRT AI guidance (UK), TPB (Australia), the Werfel risk framework and CART, Clinco. Module deep dives should cite it, not re-research it.
5. **Keep tax-data-technology, but define it** as in-house tax operations and data architecture (ERP-to-tax data flows, automation sustainment, e-invoicing/continuous transaction controls and real-time reporting, GIR XML, MCP/A2A interoperability). Otherwise it overlaps indirect-tax and Pillar Two.
6. **Optional additions if capacity allows:** (a) *tax credits and incentives* (R&D credit automation, Form 6765; TEI Tax Lab, Neo.Tax) as a sub-topic of compliance; (b) *state and local tax* (CDTFA chatbot, state audit-selection bias, SALT in TEI) which the proposed list omits; (c) *tax policy of AI* (nexus, PE, value creation, intragroup services) as a short frontier note since D.C. Bar, ABA, Oxford and OECD events all addressed it; (d) customs/trade classification where tariffs and AI classification meet (TR Global Classification AI), which falls outside this archetype's description but touches indirect tax.
7. **Retain research-advisory as one module** but flag two distinct buyers: tax advisers at firms (Blue J, CoCounsel, Bloomberg, CCH Advisor) and in-house tax counsel (TEI research labs; general legal AI such as Harvey showed no tax-specific launch in this scan).
