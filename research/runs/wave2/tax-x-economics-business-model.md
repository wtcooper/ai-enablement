# Tax x economics and business model: how tax practices and departments make and spend money

- **Archetype:** tax (firms of all sizes, AI-native entrants, and in-house corporate tax departments)
- **Topic:** economics-business-model (pricing, revenue and margin benchmarks, cost structure, offshoring, private equity and roll-ups, AI pricing pressure, in-house budgets)
- **Prepared:** 2026-10-17. Source refs follow `PUBLISHER-SHORT-YYYY`; refs shared with `landscape-tax.md` reuse its keys.
- **Reading convention:** each factual sentence ends with `[REF, locator]`. "Self-reported" means a survey respondent or the company said it. "Vendor" means a seller of the product being described. Forecasts are labelled with who made them. Where a figure has an unusual base, the base is stated in the sentence.

---

## 1. Summary

- **Pricing is already moving off the clock, but unevenly.** In a July 2025 survey of 219 US firms that use the Ignition billing platform, only 3% charge hourly for tax preparation; fixed fee (37%) and "minimum fee plus complexity" (23%) lead, and value pricing is 19% [IGNITION-2025, tax prep pricing section]. Bloomberg Tax, citing a 2025 AICPA survey of more than 1,000 firms (it does not name the survey; the AICPA's 2025 MAP survey, median-based with 81% of responses from firms with revenue of $5M or less, is the likely match), gives a median net hourly billing rate of $170, up nearly 7% in two years, and says the report recommended accelerating the shift away from hourly [BTAX-PRICING-2026, opening, AICPA survey paragraph; AICPA-MAP-2025, opening paragraphs]. The Big Four are "exploring" value and outcome pricing; nothing published shows them having switched [BTAX-PRICING-2026, opening paragraphs; New Pricing Options (PwC mix of models)].
- **The Big Four's tax revenue is still growing; there is no visible AI revenue collapse yet.** EY tax revenue was $12.7B in FY25 (+5.2% USD) and $13.8B in FY26 (+6.0% local currency); PwC tax and legal was $12.7B in FY25 (+1.1% USD); KPMG tax and legal grew 7.8% USD in FY25; Deloitte tax and legal grew 5.8% local currency in FY26 [CPAPA-EYREV-2025, service-line paragraph; EY-REV-2026, service-line paragraph; CPAPA-PWCREV-2025, service-line list; CPAPA-KPMGREV-2025, service-line list; DELOITTE-REV-2026, Tax & Legal].
- **Private equity has reached the top of the US profession and the deals are accelerating.** Crowe (sold a majority stake to KKR, reported at nearly $3B, announced 11 Jun 2026) and Eide Bailly (Reverence Capital, announced 23 Jun 2026) both split attest from tax and advisory through alternative practice structures, and PE money goes into the non-attest side [CPAPA-CROWE-2026; EIDE-2026; CFOBREW-2026]. Grant Thornton Advisors, backed by New Mountain, agreed on 29 Jul 2026 to acquire the listed firm CBIZ at a $5.0B enterprise value [GT-CBIZ-2026; CBIZ-Q2-2026]. The AICPA ethics committee is rewriting independence guidance in response [PEEC-2025; PEEC-2026].
- **Offshoring is now the default labor model at large firms, and tax return preparation is its anchor.** 86% of the IPA 100 firms employ offshore FTEs (up from 83%), offshore staff average 8.2% of headcount, and 68% offshore tax returns; every one of the 25 largest firms plans to increase it [IPA-OFFSHORE-2026]. Taxpayer-consent rules for disclosure to a preparer outside the United States are set in Rev. Proc. 2013-14, effective 14 Jan 2013 and superseding Rev. Proc. 2008-35 [IRS-RP2013-14, sec. 5.04(e), sec. 8, sec. 9].
- **Pricing pressure from AI is documented, but mostly as anecdote and regulator guidance, not as measured fee decline in tax.** KPMG International obtained a 14% lower fee from its own auditor by arguing AI should cut costs (an audit, not a tax, example) [IRISHTIMES-KPMG-2026]. IRS Office of Professional Responsibility Alert 2026-19 says billing for manual time not actually spent may violate the Circular 230 fee rule, depending on the facts [IRS-OPR-2026, item 2]. Thomson Reuters reports one third of tax and audit professionals say their firm has yet to adapt its commercial model [TR-FOP-2026, pressure paragraph].
- **In-house tax budgets are flat and thin.** Only about 39% of 170 US corporate tax technology decision-makers (Nov-Dec 2025) reported a budget increase in the past year, although 69% expect increases over 3-5 years [TR-CORPTECHRPT-2026, Budgets and capabilities]. A separate 2025 survey of 288 departments puts average technology spend at about 15.4% of the tax department budget [TR-CORPTAX-2025, Budget realities]. The median external tax spend in a 251-respondent 2024 survey was $450,000 [TR-SOTCD-2024, Figure 14].
- **A cost-per-return benchmark for in-house departments is not published.** The closest public price points are practitioner fee surveys (NSA 2023: $279 to $392 for a 1040 with state; $1,004 for a 1120) [NSA-2023, Average Fees table] and a vendor-run pricing survey [IGNITION-2025]. The IRS is running a Business Taxpayer Burden Survey on time and money cost for tax year 2025, but results are not published [IRS-BTB-2026].
- **Capital is flowing to AI-native alternatives, with unaudited claims of margin gains.** Multiplier says AI more than doubled the profit margin of an acquired 12-person tax boutique [MULTIPLIER-2025, article body]. Crete (Thrive-backed) has said it plans to invest over $500M in acquisitions [REUTERS-CRETE-2025, opening paragraph]. Accrual (a software vendor, not a roll-up) launched with $75M and claims preparation time down more than 85% [CPAPA-ACCRUAL-2026]. None of these margin or time figures is independently verified.

## 2. Findings

### 2.1 Fee and pricing models

**Per-return and fixed fees (small and mid-size practices)**
- The Ignition survey (219 US accounting customers, July 2025; sample skews to Ignition customers, which the report says may overstate pricing confidence) found fixed fee the most common tax-prep model at 37% (35% in 2024), "minimum fee plus complexity" at 23% (30% in 2024), "set fee for each form and schedule" at 5% (12% in 2024), value pricing at 19% and hourly at 3% [IGNITION-2025, tax prep pricing section; method note].
- In the same survey, the most common fee band for a "basic individual tax return" (mostly pre-filled data, some deductions, no rental or capital gains) was $400-$599 (27% of firms), then $600-$799 (22%); 10% charged under $400 and 5% over $1,500 [IGNITION-2025, individual returns question]. For business tax returns, $1,000-$1,499 was the most common band (29%), then $1,500-$1,999 (26%) [IGNITION-2025, business returns question].
- The National Society of Accountants' 2023 Income and Fees report lists average fees by form for 2023: Form 1040 itemized with state $392 and not itemized with state $279; Form 1041 $608; Form 1065 $815; Form 1120 $1,004; Form 1120S $970; Form 706 $1,533 [NSA-2023, Average Fees for Federal Tax Returns table]. Every line shown is above the 2020 column (for example 1040 itemized $323 to $392, Form 1120 $913 to $1,004) [NSA-2023, same table]. The NSA report's survey sample size was not stated in the text I read.
- The two fee surveys are not comparable: Ignition asks for the firm's "average fee currently charged" for a defined basic return, and NSA reports an average by form across member practices [IGNITION-2025, individual returns question; NSA-2023, same table].
- Ignition reports that 80% of firms plan to raise fees for 2026, most by 5-10%; 18% cite improving profit margin (12% a year earlier) and about 9% cite revenue growth (5% a year earlier) as the reason [CPAPA-IGNITION-2025, pricing-plans paragraph]. Two-thirds of respondents that raised prices in the past year said they lost no clients or lost some but kept profitability stable [CPAPA-IGNITION-2025, client-loss paragraph].
- A Thomson Reuters 2025 survey of 639 tax, audit and accounting firm respondents (Q1 2025) found that 75% said tax-return preparation (business and individual) is their most profitable service [TR-SOTP-2025, key findings]. Respondents on average estimated 11.35% of firm revenue is at risk from do-it-yourself filing, and 13.59% for firms of one to three people [TR-SOTP-2025, Figure 8 text].

**Hourly rates and the move away from them**
- The AICPA 2025 MAP survey (fiscal 2024 results, 1,073 completed responses) reports a median 6.7% increase in net client fees against 9.1% in the 2023 survey, and says there is a slow but steady increase in value and fixed pricing offset by a decline in hourly billing [AICPA-MAP-2025, revenue-growth paragraph; value and fixed pricing paragraph; methodology paragraph].
- Bloomberg Tax (5 Mar 2026), citing a 2025 AICPA survey of more than 1,000 firms "most of which have net client fees below $5 million" (survey not named; plausibly the MAP survey), gives a median net hourly billing rate of $170 (up nearly 7% over two years) and says the report recommended firms consider accelerating the shift away from hourly billing [BTAX-PRICING-2026, opening, AICPA survey paragraph].

**Value-based and outcome-based pricing at larger firms**
- Bloomberg Tax reports that top US firms are "developing" future pricing models for corporate clients and that leaders are "brainstorming" ways to charge on value and outcomes [BTAX-PRICING-2026, opening paragraphs]. PwC US Tax Leader Krishnan Chandrasekhar said time is "becoming less and less" relevant and described mixing time-based pricing for early brainstorming with value-based pricing later in an engagement [BTAX-PRICING-2026, opening quote; New Pricing Options (PwC mix of models)].
- RSM US partner Sergio de la Fe gave a worked example: a tax advisory report that takes 20 hours could take 10 with generative AI, and the firm has to decide whether the saving is margin or a price cut; RSM uses multiple pricing models and is "increasingly" billing on value [BTAX-PRICING-2026, Pricing Dilemma, RSM].
- RKL LLP (a tax partner is quoted) has onboarded "several dozen" clients under a value-based pilot model created by the consultancy Fore LLC [BTAX-PRICING-2026, New Pricing Options, RKL].
- KPMG US Vice Chair of Advisory Rob Fisher said AI investment is cost that often cannot be attributed to individual projects, which is a reason for value-based pricing [BTAX-PRICING-2026, Pricing Dilemma, Fisher quote].
- Thomson Reuters's 2026 Future of Professionals report summary says nearly half of tax and audit respondents feel pressure to generate financial gains from AI and one-third say their firms have yet to adapt commercial models; it describes a path in which firms use AI to "elevate" and "command premium fees based on expertise rather than volume" (a Thomson Reuters framing, not a measured result) [TR-FOP-2026, pressure paragraph; strategic paths list].

**Contingent and outcome-based fees: the legal limit**
- Circular 230 section 10.27 bars contingent fees for services in matters before the IRS, with exceptions for examinations of or challenges to an original return, certain amended returns or refund claims filed within 120 days of a written examination notice, claims solely about statutory interest or penalties, and judicial proceedings under the Internal Revenue Code [ECFR-10.27, (b)(1)-(4)]. A contingent fee includes a fee based on a percentage of a refund [ECFR-10.27, (c)(1)]. I did not find an authoritative source on typical contingency percentages in R&D credit or similar work; the figures in vendor and promoter pages I saw (for example 15-25% for Employee Retention Credit promoters) are not used here.

**Subscription, managed services and software-priced offerings**
- KPMG's tax managed services page (12 May 2026, vendor) says that, per its 2026 Managed Services Outlook Survey, tax is the number-one function where managed services are deployed at scale, 40% of companies have adopted managed services at scale in the tax function, and a KPMG practice leader said the tax managed services market is "approaching $1B" (his experience, not a measured market size) [KPMG-MS-2026, stat block and quote].
- EY reports that 54% of tax leaders in its 2024 Tax and Finance Operations Survey are rethinking operating models, and that co-sourcing is the most important change under consideration [EY-TAXNEWS-2025, item text].
- AI research tools are priced per seat. Blue J lists $1,498 per year per user for its individual plan and "contact for pricing" for team plans [BLUEJ-PRICING-2026]. Thomson Reuters's CoCounsel Tax page says its research module is an add-on priced per user, with no figure shown [TR-COCOUNSEL-PRICING-2026].
- Thomson Reuters said the Tax, Audit & Accounting Professionals segment grew 8% organically in Q2 2026, with recurring revenue up 9% driven by tax and audit products including GoSystem and CoCounsel, and an adjusted EBITDA margin of 38.7% [TR-Q2-2026, segment section]. This segment is "most seasonal", with about 60% of full-year revenue in the first and fourth quarters [TR-Q2-2026, same section].

### 2.2 Revenue and margin benchmarks

**Big Four tax lines**
- EY: tax services $12.7B in FY25, up 5.2% in US dollars [CPAPA-EYREV-2025, service-line paragraph]; $13.8B in FY26, up 6.0% in local currency per EY and 8.6% in US dollars per CPA Practice Advisor [EY-REV-2026, service-line paragraph; CPAPA-EYREV-2026, service-line paragraph].
- PwC: tax and legal services $12.7B in FY25 (year to 30 Jun 2025), up 1.1% in US dollars, against advisory up 4.6% and assurance up 1.9%; total revenue $56.9B (+2.9% USD) [CPAPA-PWCREV-2025, service-line list].
- KPMG: tax and legal services grew 7.8% in US dollars in FY25, which KPMG attributes to demand for AI-enabled managed services and help navigating global tax reform (company statement); total revenue $39.8B [CPAPA-KPMGREV-2025, service-line list].
- Deloitte: Tax & Legal revenue grew 5.8% in local currency in FY26 (year to 31 May 2026); total revenue $74.5B, up 3.8% in local currency [DELOITTE-REV-2026, opening paragraph and Tax & Legal].
- These are firm-reported global figures. Tax lines are not stated on the same basis across firms (US dollars versus local currency, and "tax and legal" versus "tax"), so they should not be ranked against each other.

**US firms below the Big Four**
- The AICPA MAP survey (median values, fiscal 2024) reports net remaining per partner or owner of $252,663, up 11.9% from $225,725 in fiscal 2022; "net remaining" is net client fees minus expenses before partner compensation [AICPA-MAP-2025, net remaining per partner paragraph].
- INSIDE Public Accounting reports revenue per equity partner across its firms rose from $1.33M in 2008 to $2.58M in 2025, and that "more recent data suggests momentum may be moderating, particularly among larger firms" [IPA-RPP-2026, article body].
- Accounting Today's editor reported on the 2026 Top 100: the Top 100 grew about 8.6%; tax is about 25% of revenue for firms above $1B and roughly 40% for the firms below that; advisory is 44% of revenue at billion-dollar firms, 21% at $100M-$1B firms and 13% below $100M; Top 100 firms reported 225 deals against a usual 110 to 120 per year (all as spoken in a podcast) [ATODAY-TOP100-2026, M&A and advisory-mix sections].
- Thomson Reuters's 2025 survey (639 respondents) found 65% said revenue increased in the prior 12 months, with a mean increase of 21.3% among those respondents (not across all respondents), and 45% said profit increased with a mean increase of 25.6% among those [TR-SOTP-2025, Figure 10]. Treat as self-reported and skewed to growers.
- CBIZ (listed; accounting, tax, advisory, benefits and insurance, so not a tax-only margin) reported adjusted EBITDA of $347M on revenue of $1,531M for the first half of 2026 (22.7% margin; 23.7% in the prior-year half) and $103M on $682M in Q2 2026 (15.1%; 17.6% a year earlier) [CBIZ-Q2-2026, results tables].
- Consumer tax preparation at scale: H&R Block fiscal 2026 (year to 30 Jun 2026) revenue was $3,945.4M, compensation and benefits $1,564.0M, occupancy $457.2M, marketing $277.8M, and income from continuing operations before income taxes $853.9M [HRB-FY2026, income statement]. Computed from those lines, compensation and benefits are about 39.6% of revenue (field wages $996.7M, about 25.3%) and pre-tax income about 21.6% [derived from HRB-FY2026, income statement]. H&R Block attributed the revenue increase to a higher net average charge and company-owned volume in US assisted preparation [HRB-FY2026, revenue paragraph].
- UK: Business & Accountancy Daily's Top 75 (30 Mar 2026) reports fee income of £24.13bn, up 5%, with mid-tier PE-backed firms growing fastest and Big Four growth "flatlining" [UKAD-TOP75-2026, opening paragraphs]. An operations-software vendor's blog says 17 of the UK Top 75 are PE-backed with collective fee income up nearly 20% against a market average of 5% (vendor claim, not traced to a primary table) [ENATE-2026, opening paragraphs].

### 2.3 Cost structure

- **Labor is the dominant cost.** H&R Block's compensation and benefits are about 40% of revenue (see 2.2) and it says opex growth was driven mainly by field wages [HRB-FY2026, operating expenses paragraph]. The AICPA reports median new-graduate salary for a bachelor's degree rose almost 11% in two years to $60,834 and for a master's nearly 17% to $67,750 [AICPA-MAP-2025, salary paragraph].
- **Capacity, not just cost, is the constraint the vendors sell against.** Black Ore cited about 300,000 net departures from the profession in two years (US Bureau of Labor Statistics, as relayed by the article) and an estimated 125-million-hour annual shortfall in US tax services worth about $25B, a figure the article says the company did not attribute to an external source [BLACKORE-2026, shortage paragraphs].
- **Staffing model changing.** PwC's global headcount fell by 5,600 in the 12 months to 30 Jun 2025 to 364,000, and the firm dropped a 2021 pledge to add 100,000 people [CPAPA-PWCREV-2025, headcount paragraphs]. Accounting Today notes Top 100 firms are thinking more about lateral hires than entry-level hires, hiring non-accountants, and about automation, AI and offshoring [ATODAY-TOP100-2026, staffing discussion in the second half]. Thomson Reuters notes 63% of large-firm respondents are reallocating administrative and bookkeeping tasks to staff without accounting qualifications [TR-SOTP-2025, key findings].
- **Technology and AI spending.** RSM announced a $1B investment in technology and AI and launched myRSM Tax as the first expression of it (16 Jun 2025) [RSM-MYRSM-2025, release opening]. Deloitte states a $3B investment through 2030 to modernize delivery and operations [DELOITTE-REV-2026, investment paragraph]. Grant Thornton Advisors has announced a $1B AI and technology investment [GT-CBIZ-2026, AI paragraph]. EY says it invests more than $1B a year in AI-first platforms [CPAPA-EYREV-2025, final paragraph]. More than 90% of firms planned to raise technology spending by up to 20%, according to the AICPA as quoted by Bloomberg Tax [BTAX-PRICING-2026, Pricing Dilemma, AICPA technology-spend sentence].
- **The cost of the AI itself is an open question.** TEI Tax Technology Seminar attendees (April 2026) discussed "rising token costs that could entirely change the economic case for automation"; the recap gives no figures [TEI-TAXTECH-2026, key insights].
- **In-house cost structure.** See 2.7.

### 2.4 Offshoring

- INSIDE Public Accounting (data dive updated 12 Oct 2026): 86% of IPA 100 firms employ offshore FTEs (83% the year before); offshore staff average 8.2% of total headcount; 97% of firms with programs plan to maintain or expand; all of the 25 largest plan to increase; 68% of IPA 100 firms report offshoring tax returns; options include third-party providers, captive operations or combinations [IPA-OFFSHORE-2026, article body]. The article raises, without answering, how onshore early-career staff will develop judgment if repeatable work moves offshore [IPA-OFFSHORE-2026, closing paragraphs].
- CBIZ completed an acquisition of BINDZ, adding 250+ India-based professionals, and describes it as unlocking "durable margin expansion levers" (company statement) [CBIZ-Q2-2026, highlights].
- Crete says it has operations in Asia (company statement via Reuters) [REUTERS-CRETE-2025, company-profile paragraph].
- Thomson Reuters's 2026 corporate tax technology survey found 8% of 170 US corporate tax departments plan to "outsource more work" as their main way of dealing with capacity constraints over the next 12 months, against 32% who plan process optimization without hiring or technology spend and 28% who prioritize technology [TR-CORPTECHRPT-2026, capacity-constraints figure]. KPMG Germany's 2026 survey found 40% of respondents expect increased use of shared service centers and 50% expect stable development [KPMG-DE-2026, efficiency paragraph].
- The consent rule for foreign preparation: Revenue Procedure 2013-14 (effective 14 Jan 2013) requires consent language stating that disclosure "may result in your tax return information being disclosed to a tax return preparer located outside the United States" when the Social Security number is masked or absent, and sets different requirements where the disclosure includes an unmasked Social Security number [IRS-RP2013-14, sec. 5.04(e); sec. 5.07; sec. 9]. A 2003 Accounting Today column shows firms outsourcing 1040s to India already, for "anticipated lower cost per return" and relief on staffing, so the practice is old and the change is scale [ATODAY-2003-OFFSHORE, opening paragraphs].
- Not found: any published cost-per-return or wage-arbitrage figure from a named firm. Vendor-blog numbers on offshore wages circulate but could not be traced to a primary source and are not used.

### 2.5 Private equity and roll-ups

**Scale and recent deals**
- CFO Brew (26 Jun 2026) reported that 10 of the top 20 US firms are, or will soon be, owned by private equity; Crowe and Eide Bailly both adopt alternative practice structures that put tax and consulting lines in the investor-backed entity [CFOBREW-2026, article body].
- Crowe: Wall Street Journal-reported majority sale to KKR and co-investors at "nearly $3 billion"; Crowe had U.S. revenue of nearly $1.28B per INSIDE Public Accounting; the new Crowe Advisory LLC provides all tax, advisory and other non-attest services and Crowe LLP keeps attest; the deal was announced 11 Jun 2026 and expected to close in Q3 2026 [CPAPA-CROWE-2026, article body (deal, structure, revenue, timing)]. A Crowe release dated 7 Aug 2026 announces the close; I saw this only as a search-result snippet (the page is bot-blocked), so treat it as unconfirmed here.
- Eide Bailly: announced 23 Jun 2026; over $800M in annual revenue and 3,500 employees; Eide Bailly Advisory LLC (non-CPA) will provide business advisory and non-attest services; expected to close in Q3 2026 [EIDE-2026, release body].
- Grant Thornton Advisors / CBIZ: all-cash acquisition at an enterprise value of $5.0B ($55 per share, about a 54% premium to the 30-day volume-weighted average price); expected close Q4 2026, subject to CBIZ shareholder approval and regulatory approvals; the combined US business would have annual domestic revenue above $5B; described by the parties as the largest transaction of its kind in more than 25 years [GT-CBIZ-2026, release body; CBIZ-Q2-2026, transaction paragraph].
- Accounting Today's editor said every new entrant to the 2026 Top 100 is PE-backed in some way, and that M&A not involving PE is "significantly impacted" by PE deals in expectations on multiples and cash [ATODAY-TOP100-2026, first takeaway].
- Thomson Reuters's research article (5 Jan 2026) says roughly half of the top 25 firms have completed or are pursuing PE transactions and that Citrin Cooperman acquired more than 20 firms in four years under New Mountain [TR-PE-2026, PE push section]. The Thomson Reuters Tax Firm Growth white paper (Sep 2025) adds that retiring partners in PE deals receive cash payouts often two to three times higher than traditional internal buyouts [TR-TFG-2025, private equity push section].
- A buyer-run guide (Jupid, which runs a hybrid roll-up) attributes to KPMG Corporate Finance that PE had taken stakes in 11 of the 30 largest US firms by Q3 2025 and over $50B had flowed into CPA firms in six years, and that KPMG Corporate Finance expects more than half of the top 30 to be PE-backed [JUPID-2026, key facts list]. That is second-hand and from an interested party; the CFO Brew count is the better-sourced one.

**Valuation, multiples and the "flip" market**
- Accounting Today (30 Mar 2026), quoting M&A advisers: a "good" multiple of EBITDA is now around 5-7x, "very good" 7-8x, "outstanding" 9-10x or even 11x, and since PE arrived multiples have risen "essentially one point per year"; advisers also warn that headline values can hide earn-outs, clawbacks and offset rights [ATODAY-WORTH-2026, multiples paragraph and fine-print quotes].
- A secondary account reports that New Mountain bought Citrin Cooperman at about 11x EBITDA and sold to Blackstone in 2025 at a $2B valuation (about 15x), "the first known PE-to-PE sale of a U.S. accounting firm" [FINSTORY-2026, Citrin Cooperman paragraph]. The sources conflict on the year of the first Citrin deal: 2021 per Thomson Reuters and the secondary account, 2022 per Accounting Today [TR-PE-2026, PE push section (Citrin Cooperman); ATODAY-WORTH-2026, second paragraph]. The same secondary account, citing the Financial Times, says Exponent sought about £1bn (15-17x EBITDA) for UK roll-up Xeinadin and could not find a buyer at that price [FINSTORY-2026, Xeinadin paragraphs]; I could not open the Financial Times original.
- CPA Trendlines (2 Jan 2026) reports that PE investors are paying higher prices for CPA firms that use AI to expand capacity "rather than cut headcount" [CPATL-PEVAL-2026, opening paragraph].

**Why PE wants tax**
- The investment thesis stated by an operations-software vendor is predictable recurring revenue, consolidation potential and an advisory runway, with value creation through lower cost per unit of delivery, technology and platform scale [ENATE-2026, investment thesis paragraphs]. Thomson Reuters's article makes the capital argument: partnerships struggle to fund multimillion-dollar technology buildouts [TR-PE-2026, capital argument paragraph]. Both are advocacy-leaning sources.

**The independence rulebook is being rewritten**
- On 19 Dec 2025 the AICPA Professional Ethics Executive Committee voted to issue an exposure draft on alternative practice structures, distinguishing investor "significant influence" from "control" and revising the "network firm" definition; comments closed 30 Apr 2026; if adopted it would take effect one year after adoption [PEEC-2025, article body]. In August 2026 the committee agreed to continue discussion at an interim October meeting on a second exposure draft, and also discussed a new interpretation prohibiting attest-client investment in firms [PEEC-2026, article body].

**AI-native roll-ups and platforms**
- Multiplier (seed and Series A, $27.5M, 18 Jun 2025) bought Citrine International Tax, a 12-person cross-border tax accounting boutique, and says AI "more than double[d]" its profit margins; the lead investor's partner said the strategy works best on small firms because a 200-accountant firm is unlikely to adopt at a high rate (investor view) [MULTIPLIER-2025, article body].
- Crete Professionals Alliance (Thrive Capital-backed): over $300M annual revenue, 20+ accounting businesses, 900 employees; plans to invest over $500M in two years in acquisitions; takes majority stakes with local owners keeping a minority; says a Tampa audit firm's AI tools saved "hundreds of hours every month" (a firm head's statement) [REUTERS-CRETE-2025, article body].
- Accrual (launched 5 Feb 2026, $75M led by General Catalyst) is an AI-native tax preparation and review platform for accounting firms, not an acquirer of firms (the landscape file groups it with roll-ups; that grouping is incorrect). It claims preparation time down more than 85% and review time down up to 60%, and that every 50 complex returns adds the capacity of one accountant (all company claims) [CPAPA-ACCRUAL-2026, platform and claims paragraphs].
- Black Ore opened Tax Autopilot to CPA firms on 29 Apr 2026; it says 75 pilot firms included 40% of the 20 largest CPA firms, and claims accuracy above 99% and time savings up to 98% on complex filings (company claims); about 3,700 firms were on a waiting list [BLACKORE-2026, opening and pilot-statistics paragraphs]. The article gives no pricing.
- Thomson Reuters's survey found 57% of tax, audit and accounting professionals said PE is not on their radar and 30% said they are not interested even if approached; 87% of those surveyed are not actively seeking PE funding (wording as published in the paper's takeaway) [TR-TFG-2025, private equity push section; executive summary].
- The PE counter-argument from the same research: well-capitalized competitors are pulling ahead on technology and talent, and independent firms face growing pressure to compete [TR-PE-2026, "Competitive pressure" bullet; TR-TFG-2025, key takeaways].

### 2.6 Pricing pressure from AI

- **Client-driven pressure (adjacent, audit).** The Irish Times (6 Feb 2026) reported KPMG International pressed its auditor Grant Thornton UK to pass on AI cost savings and threatened to change auditor; Grant Thornton acceded, and KPMG International's audit fee fell to $357,000 for 2025 from $416,000 in 2024 (14% in dollar terms; the fee was paid in sterling) [IRISHTIMES-KPMG-2026, article body]. In the same article, Ideagen Audit Analytics data showed average European audit fees rose in nearly every country the prior year [IRISHTIMES-KPMG-2026, article body (audit-fee data paragraph)]. This is a single, small, audit fee and is not evidence of the tax market.
- **Regulator-driven pressure on the fee itself.** IRS OPR Alert 2026-19 (24 Jun 2026) says billing clients for manual labor or time not actually spent, or double billing for AI-assisted tasks, may violate the Circular 230 section 10.27 fee rule "depending on the facts"; it says cost savings should be passed on openly and practitioners should disclose AI activities performed and credit the client's account for cost reductions [IRS-OPR-2026, item 2].
- **Pricing as a response by the sellers.** Bloomberg Tax: "Is that all margin for me or is that all price reduction for our client?" (RSM's de la Fe) [BTAX-PRICING-2026, Pricing Dilemma, de la Fe quote]. Fore LLC's CEO argued that without a worth-based approach firms "will have to add more clients" to fill the revenue gap [BTAX-PRICING-2026, opening, Fore LLC (River) quote].
- **Capacity-selling instead of price-cutting.** Black Ore and Accrual frame the benefit as serving more clients with the same headcount (vendor positioning) [BLACKORE-2026, FAQ section; CPAPA-ACCRUAL-2026, claims paragraph]. Thomson Reuters's "elevate / scale / reimagine" framing is similar, with "scale" meaning more capacity without more headcount [TR-FOP-2026, strategic paths list].
- **Evidence of productivity from adjacent accounting work.** A Stanford GSB working paper (Choi and Xie, 7 May 2025; 277 accountants surveyed, plus field data from 79 small and mid-sized firms using one AI accounting product) reports a 55% increase in weekly client support among adopters, about 8.5% of accountant time reallocated from data entry to higher-value tasks, and a 7.5-day reduction in monthly close time [STANFORD-CHOIXIE-2025, abstract]. This is bookkeeping and close work, not tax, so it supports the productivity premise only by analogy.
- **Counter-view on headcount.** Tax Notes State (20 Jul 2026), Michael Sala argues AI will not shrink corporate tax teams much, because models conflate tax preparation (data into forms, already automated) with tax practice (dealing with authorities, auditors and executives) and AI cannot hold culpability [SALA-TAXNOTES-2026, abstract].
- **No measured fee decline in tax.** The sources read show list prices and fee-increase plans rising (Ignition: 80% of firms plan increases; NSA: fees above 2020 levels) and Big Four tax revenue growing [CPAPA-IGNITION-2025, pricing-plans paragraph; NSA-2023, Average Fees table; EY-REV-2026, service-line paragraph]. I found no published before-and-after of fees on the same tax work after AI adoption.

### 2.7 In-house tax department budgets and cost

**Resourcing and budget direction**
- The Thomson Reuters Institute and Tax Executives Institute 2026 Corporate Tax Department Technology Report (survey Nov-Dec 2025; 170 US-based corporate tax professionals at companies with at least $100M revenue, mostly above $1B): about 39% reported a department budget increase in the past year, with most others seeing cuts or no change; 69% expect budgets to keep increasing over 3-5 years, and 92% expect capabilities to improve; half said their company bought no new tax technology in the past 12 months; dissatisfaction with the tech stack rose to 56% from 34% [TR-CORPTECHRPT-2026, executive summary and Budgets and capabilities]. The respondents were equally optimistic the year before, and most "never received the expected budget boost" [TR-CORPTECHRPT-2026, Budgets and capabilities]. A Thomson Reuters blog (23 Jul 2026) adds that only 27% said they could secure the budget and resources when needed and only 9% called themselves active AI users [TR-UNDERSTAFF-2026, data paragraphs].
- The 2025 State of the Corporate Tax Department survey (288 senior decision-makers, July-August 2025) found 58% say their departments are under-resourced (51% a year earlier), 52% expect at least some additional budget for new technology, and 38% intend to hire more qualified tax professionals (32% in 2024) [TR-CORPTAX-2025, highlights and budget section].
- The same survey links resourcing to outcomes: 43% of well-resourced departments versus 26% of under-resourced ones are very confident of timely forecasting, and 37% versus 24% of avoiding penalties; 31% of respondents spending more than half their time on proactive work incurred penalties versus 56% of mostly reactive departments (self-reported) [TR-CORPTAX-2025, confidence-gap section].

**Where the money goes**
- Technology: in the 2025 survey, "less than 20%" of the typical department budget goes to tax technology and software, with an average of about 15.4% [TR-CORPTAX-2025, Budget realities]. In the 2024 survey (251 respondents, August 2024): 32% spend under 10%, 48% spend 10-25%, 20% spend over 25%; departments at companies with over $6B revenue spend around 10%, and smaller companies (under $50M) spend more as a share [TR-SOTCD-2024, Figure 13 and text].
- External support (2024 survey, spend in the last fiscal year): median external tax spend $450,000 overall (the prior-year column in the same figure shows $780,000); 21% spend under $100,000, 31% $100,000-$500,000, 11% $500,000-$1M, 25% $1M-$5M and 13% $5M or more; the median for companies with over $6B revenue was $1,000,000 (small sample) [TR-SOTCD-2024, Figure 14]. The share spending $1M or more fell more than 10 points from the prior year's 49% to 38% [TR-SOTCD-2024, text after Figure 14].
- People: average home-country headcount was 9 tax professionals, 1 analyst and 1 administrative staffer, with an average of 36 tax professionals at companies with over $6B revenue [TR-SOTCD-2024, staffing section].
- KPMG Germany's May 2026 quick survey (sample size not stated): 71% of tax departments use AI tools; 58% expect domestic tax headcount to stay constant and 72% expect the same abroad [KPMG-DE-2026, key findings].

**Make, buy, or manage**
- 32% of respondents in the 2026 survey plan to deal with capacity constraints by process optimization with no new hiring or technology, 28% by technology, 12% by both equally, 8% by outsourcing and 8% by hiring [TR-CORPTECHRPT-2026, capacity-constraints figure].
- Spending on outside advisers is higher where departments are under-resourced [TR-SOTCD-2024, text after Figure 14]. KPMG and EY both promote co-sourcing and managed services for compliance (see 2.1) [KPMG-MS-2026; EY-TAXNEWS-2025].
- A Thomson Reuters blog argues the cost of running a tax department lean appears on other lines (audit fees, restatement costs, missed planning) rather than in the tax budget (Thomson Reuters, a vendor, advocacy) [TR-UNDERSTAFF-2026, body].

**Cost per return**
- No source I read publishes an in-house cost per return, per provision or per jurisdiction filing. The IRS is surveying businesses on time and money spent to prepare federal income tax returns for tax year 2025; no results are posted on the page [IRS-BTB-2026, survey description]. Outside prices for comparison are in 2.1 (NSA 2023: Form 1120 $1,004; 1120S $970; 1065 $815).

## 3. What it means for an AI transformation conversation

### Implications

1. **The unit of sale is the lever, not the tool.** AI changes the hours behind a return, not the price on the engagement letter. Practices that bill by the return or by fixed fee keep the saving; practices on hourly rates give it away or have to explain it. Fixed-fee prevalence in small-firm tax prep (37%) and the AICPA's push away from hourly suggest the starting position is better than the "billable-hour crisis" headlines imply for compliance, and worse for advisory work [IGNITION-2025, tax prep pricing section; AICPA-MAP-2025, value and fixed pricing paragraph; BTAX-PRICING-2026].
2. **Who keeps the saving is now a regulatory question as well as a commercial one.** OPR Alert 2026-19 ties AI use to the Circular 230 fee rule, so a pricing policy for AI-assisted work (what is passed on, what is disclosed) is a governance document, not only a pricing memo [IRS-OPR-2026, item 2].
3. **Capital and scale are changing who the competitors are.** PE-backed and AI-native platforms are buying capacity, offshore delivery and software at a scale partnerships find hard to match [TR-PE-2026, capital argument paragraph; CBIZ-Q2-2026, highlights; REUTERS-CRETE-2025]. A small or mid-size practice leader's AI plan is partly a defensive answer to that, and the valuation of the practice itself now depends on EBITDA and perceived AI capability [ATODAY-WORTH-2026; CPATL-PEVAL-2026].
4. **Offshoring and AI are substitutes and complements for the same pool of routine work.** With 68% of large firms already offshoring returns, the marginal gain from AI is measured against an already cheaper baseline, and the early-career training pipeline is the shared casualty [IPA-OFFSHORE-2026, article body]. An AI business case that ignores the offshore baseline will overstate savings.
5. **In-house, the constraint is budget and ownership more than model quality.** Flat budgets, no cost-per-return baseline and 8% planning to outsource more suggest the transformation case has to be built from the department's own time data, and the funding comes from finance, not tax [TR-CORPTECHRPT-2026; TR-UNDERSTAFF-2026]. Rising token costs are a new cost line nobody has benchmarked [TEI-TAXTECH-2026].
6. **Tax-software vendors are capturing growth now.** Thomson Reuters's tax and audit segment grew 8% organically with a 38.7% EBITDA margin in Q2 2026; firms are paying per-seat for research AI and for platform AI [TR-Q2-2026; BLUEJ-PRICING-2026]. Part of the productivity saving is passed to software vendors.

### Interview probes (for a leader in a tax practice or department)

1. For your three largest recurring tax services, what is the unit of billing today (per return, fixed annual fee, hourly, value), and what happened to the effective rate per hour on those services in the last tax season?
2. When AI shortens a task your staff used to bill or budget for, what is your written policy on who keeps the saving, and have you read your engagement letters against OPR Alert 2026-19?
3. What share of your return preparation capacity is offshore or outsourced today, what is the cost gap per return, and would an AI preparation tool be measured against the offshore cost or the onshore cost?
4. (In-house) What do you spend each year on tax technology and on external advisers, and could you produce a cost per return, per provision and per jurisdiction filing from your own data? Who in finance would have to agree to move that budget?
5. (Practice owner) If a PE-backed or AI-native firm bought your biggest local competitor, what would you change first, and what would a buyer see if they valued your firm on EBITDA today?

### Objections and evidence-based responses

| Objection | What the evidence supports | What it does not support |
|---|---|---|
| "AI will wipe out our tax fees." | Big Four tax revenue grew in FY25 and FY26 [EY-REV-2026; DELOITTE-REV-2026; CPAPA-PWCREV-2025]; small-firm fee surveys show higher fees than in 2020 and 80% planning increases [NSA-2023; CPAPA-IGNITION-2025]; one audit client extracted a 14% cut by arguing AI [IRISHTIMES-KPMG-2026]. | There is no before-and-after fee study on tax work, so neither a collapse nor immunity is demonstrated. |
| "We bill by the return, so AI only helps us." | Fixed fee is the most common tax-prep model (37% in a customer survey) and hourly is 3% [IGNITION-2025]. | The survey sample is Ignition customers; and OPR notes disclosure and fee-fairness duties still apply where the engagement is before the IRS [IRS-OPR-2026, item 2]. |
| "Private equity is a fad; our clients want a local CPA." | Over half of tax professionals say PE is not on their radar, and 87% of those surveyed are not actively seeking it [TR-TFG-2025]. | Half of the top 20 US firms are or will be PE-owned, and PE now sets valuation norms even for non-sellers [CFOBREW-2026; ATODAY-WORTH-2026]. The independence rules are being rewritten [PEEC-2026]. |
| "We can't afford AI; the vendors and Big Four will outspend us." | Per-seat tax research AI lists at about $1,498 per user per year [BLUEJ-PRICING-2026]; smaller departments spend a larger share of budget on technology than the largest [TR-SOTCD-2024, Figure 13]. | Per-seat research cost is not the full cost: data work, review time and token costs are not benchmarked [TEI-TAXTECH-2026]. |
| "Offshoring already gave us the savings." | 86% of large firms offshore and 68% offshore returns, so the cost baseline is already low [IPA-OFFSHORE-2026]. | No source shows that offshore-prepared returns are cheaper than AI-prepared returns with review; the evidence for AI preparation is vendor-claimed [BLACKORE-2026; CPAPA-ACCRUAL-2026]. |
| "In-house tax won't shrink, so there's no budget case." | The Tax Notes argument and KPMG Germany's survey (58% expect stable headcount) agree [SALA-TAXNOTES-2026; KPMG-DE-2026]. | The case in a flat-headcount department is capacity and risk (under-resourced departments report more penalties and less forecasting confidence) rather than savings [TR-CORPTAX-2025, confidence-gap section]. |

## 6. Gaps

- **In-house cost per return and cost per provision.** No public benchmark found for what it costs a corporate tax department to produce a return, provision or jurisdictional filing. The only price proxies are outside-practice fee surveys (NSA, Ignition) and the TR/TEI budget shares. The IRS Business Taxpayer Burden Survey for tax year 2025 is in the field and its results are not available. Paid benchmarking (for example Hackett, large-firm tax function surveys, TEI's own surveys) was not reachable.
- **Tax-specific margins.** Margins are available for CBIZ (mixed lines), H&R Block (consumer) and Thomson Reuters's tax software segment. No source gave a tax-practice-level margin for a Big Four or mid-tier firm, and PE-backed firms do not publish margins. The "net remaining per partner" and "revenue per partner" figures are not margins.
- **Deloitte, KPMG and PwC FY26 tax lines in dollars.** Deloitte FY26 and KPMG FY25 give growth rates but I did not retrieve a dollar tax figure for them; PwC's FY26 results were not located.
- **Measured fee effects of AI in tax.** There is no before-and-after study of fees or realization on tax work after AI adoption. The one hard data point on AI-driven fee reduction is an audit fee (KPMG International and Grant Thornton UK).
- **Outcome-based and contingent pricing in tax.** Beyond the Circular 230 rule, I found no authoritative data on how widespread outcome-based pricing is in tax advisory or how contingent fees are set in credit studies. Bloomberg Tax (Mar 2026) reports intent, and the article is paywalled after the Employee Incentives section.
- **Offshoring economics.** The IPA data give prevalence (86%, 68%, 8.2%) but not savings per return, provider versus captive mix, or quality outcomes. Big Four India headcounts found only in career-site blogs and were not used. Tax Notes, the Financial Times and Wall Street Journal originals on PE valuations could not be opened.
- **PE outcomes.** There is no independent evidence on what PE ownership does to fees, quality or tax-client retention at tax firms. Valuation multiples come from advisers quoted by Accounting Today and a secondary article; deal values for Crowe and Eide Bailly come from press reports of unnamed sources. The Citrin Cooperman first-deal year conflicts (2021 vs 2022).
- **AI-native roll-up performance.** Multiplier's margin claim, Crete's hours saved and Accrual's and Black Ore's time and accuracy figures are self-reported by the companies or their executives and unverified.
- **Non-US markets.** UK coverage is limited to a fee-income headline and a vendor blog; no Europe, India or Australia pricing or margin data beyond that.
- **Sample caveats to carry forward.** Ignition's respondents are its own customers; the AICPA MAP sample is 81% firms with revenue of $5M or less; Thomson Reuters's surveys are vendor-owned and the corporate department samples are 170 to 288 respondents; the KPMG Germany survey's sample size was not stated.
