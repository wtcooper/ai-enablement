# Source landscape: mid-market extensions to the assurance and tax archetypes

Researched 2026-10-20. Scope: AI in assurance and tax work at firms serving private, middle-market companies, owners and families. Read first: the assurance and tax `ARCHETYPE.md` module lists and their `sources.md` (existing keys reused where the same source applies).

**Read column.** `V` = page or PDF opened and read. `M` = opened through the r.jina.ai mirror (site blocked direct curl). `S` = known only from a search-result summary; the page was not opened, so treat details as `(unverified)`. Where only part of a page could be read, the row says so.

## 1. Summary

- **Where the evidence is.** Public AI material for mid-market firms is concentrated in four places: (1) audit-platform launches by mid-tier firms and vendors (Grant Thornton gtap, RSM Ask Luca, EisnerAmper audit agent, Caseware Verity, Citrin Cooperman's Caseware DAS suite); (2) agentic tax-preparation start-ups aimed at CPA firms (Basis, Black Ore, Accrual, Thomson Reuters Ready to Review); (3) AICPA guidance, led by the 13 Oct 2026 practice aid on AI for financial statement auditors and the 19 Oct 2026 AICPA and AIUC assurance offering for AI systems; (4) state tax agencies adopting AI for audit selection.
- **Where it is thin.** There is almost no AI-specific public material for SSARS engagements, benefit plan audits, single audits, fiduciary and estate returns, or Form 990 work. The few items found are CPE session titles, one vendor course, and vendor product pages. No AI finding from AICPA peer review, ARSC, DOL EBSA or the Governmental Audit Quality Center was found.
- **Peer-review data gives the best practitioner taxonomy.** Peer-review reports split work into audits (single audit, other Government Auditing Standards, ERISA, FDICIA, other), reviews, compilations and preparations (with and without disclosures), SOC, and agreed-upon procedures [TSCPA-PEERREVIEW-2025]. That, not the proposed eight-module list, is how reviewers and the AICPA's "must-select" training divide the work [AICPA-PEERCONF-2026].
- **Module verdict (detail in section 10).** Merge `private-company-audit` and `reviews-compilations` into one `private-company-assurance` module. Keep `benefit-plan-audits` and `nonprofit-government` but treat benefit plans as low-evidence. Do not create `pass-through-owners`; widen `compliance-individual-business` and `research-advisory`. Keep `multistate-private`, scoped to state income, franchise and payroll nexus plus state-agency AI. Fold `nonprofit-tax` into `nonprofit-government` (design question: it crosses the assurance/tax archetype line). Treat `trusts-estates-gifts` as a thin module with a combined deep dive.
- **Size effect.** Larger and mid-sized tax firms report being further along than small firms (63% of large versus 35% of small firms proactive or optimized in technology) [TR-SOTP-2026]. Only 21% of firms surveyed by Karbon have an AI policy or strategy [KARBON-AI-2026]. Firms with fewer than ~30 professionals are where the Curious and Experimenting stages dominate.
- **Source base.** 93 source rows across eight types (a few rows group several items); 70 were opened and read, the rest are known from search summaries only (section 15).
