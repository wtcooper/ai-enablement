# Topic research: AI in acquisitions and integration

Prepared 2026-10-20 for the transformation-advisor (acquisition path) and ai-portfolio (roll-in playbook) skills.
Public sources only. Refs follow PUBLISHER-SHORT-YYYY. Locators are sections, headings or named passages because most sources are web pages without page numbers. Where a figure comes from a survey, the sample is stated. "Self-reported" means the named organization reports its own result, "vendor-published" means a firm that sells related services published it, and "independent" means a party with no stake measured it.

Tags used below: **[sourced]** the point is in the cited source; **[synthesis]** my combination of sourced points; **[proposed]** a working rule that no source states, offered as a starting point.

## 1. Summary

- **The acquisition thesis is now openly an AI thesis, but the proof is thin.** Platforms in accounting (Current/Crete, Aprio, Baker Tilly, Grant Thornton Advisors, Citrin Cooperman) and venture-backed roll-up vehicles (General Catalyst, Thrive Holdings) all describe AI as a reason to buy and a way to create value. The measured results published so far are company-reported, with no disclosed method (REUTERS-CRETE-2025; ACCTODAY-CURRENT-2026; GC-LONGLAKE-2026).
- **Diligence frameworks converge.** RSM, KPMG, PwC and Baker Tilly all assess some version of strategy, data, technology, talent, governance and adoption, then tie the findings to price and a day-one plan. No source offers an independent validation of any of them (RSM-AIDD-2025; KPMG-AIDD-2025; PWC-AIDD-ND; BAKERTILLY-AIDD-2024).
- **Repeatable capability means one stack, embedded builders and trained people.** The platforms' stated pattern is a shared technology stack, engineers working next to practitioners, and capital for training. The unifying idea is that work can then move between firms (WK-PE-2025; OPENAI-THRIVE-2025; CPAPA-CURRENT-2026).
- **Independence and client data decide what can be shared.** In the US, proposed AICPA rules (exposure draft released 2025-12-29) would restrict attest services to a sponsor's portfolio companies and tighten who counts as a covered member. Tax-return data and file transfers need consent steps that predate AI (WINSTON-APS-2026; TAXADVISER-7216-2024; CAMICO-MERGE-2022).
- **Integration is mostly a people problem, and M&A is the slowest and least efficient value lever.** In FTI's 2026 survey of 555 PE leaders, 35% called their M&A implementation efficient, the lowest of any lever, and only 25% saw M&A results within 12 months (FTI-VCI-2026). Bain advises sequencing: stabilize first, transform where the thesis needs it (BAIN-OUTLOOK-2026).
- **Personal productivity is not P&L.** Baker Tilly's CEO calls individual AI productivity "table stakes" and expects the P&L change to come from rebuilt workflows (CFOBREW-BAKERTILLY-2026). MIT NANDA's preliminary study says the same of general tools (MITNANDA-GENAI-2025).
- **Regulators and bodies are watching PE and AI together.** The PCAOB says its inspectors will look at firms that have taken PE and at AI use in 2026. The FRC and ICAEW ask firms to engage early (ATODAY-PCAOB-2025; BLOOMBERGTAX-FRC-2025; ICAEW-PE-2025).
- **Evidence from other roll-ups is a warning, not a forecast.** Anesthesia and gastroenterology roll-ups show price rises with no quality gain. That is health care, with different pricing power, and it is not evidence about AI (NBER-ANESTHESIA-2025; JAMA-GI-2025).

## 2. Core concepts in plain language

1. **Platform versus umbrella.** A PE-backed platform firm can buy and fully merge other firms, or sit above firms that keep their own brand and leadership and take shared services (NJCPA-PE-2026, "platform strategy" and "umbrella organizations"). Current describes the second model and says partner firms keep leadership, brand and culture (CPAPA-CURRENT-2026). *For AI:* the model decides whether you roll out one stack and one workflow set, or offer tools and let each firm adopt. *For knowledge work:* partners who keep their clients and brand need evidence, not mandates.

2. **Alternative practice structure (APS).** Only licensed CPA firms can provide attest services. Investors therefore own a separate non-attest entity, and the attest firm stays independent. Aprio and Baker Tilly describe exactly this split (CPAPA-APRIO-PE-2024; BAKERTILLY-AIDD-2024 footer). *For AI:* data, tools and people shared across the line can create independence threats. A shared AI platform is a shared service that has to be assessed. [synthesis]

3. **Diligence as a five-to-ten-dimension maturity read, priced.** Consultancies assess AI strategy, data, infrastructure, talent, governance, adoption and value, then link findings to EBITDA, price and the first 100 days (KPMG-AIDD-2025; RSM-AIDD-2025). *For AI:* the point is to find what must be fixed before AI can deliver and put a cost on it, not to give the target a score.

4. **Repeatable capability.** What the platform rolls into each acquired firm: tools, standard workflows, training, a shared data platform, and a small central team. Wolters Kluwer's example is shifting return preparation from a short-staffed office to one with capacity, which requires common tools and workflows (WK-PE-2025). *For AI:* a capability is only repeatable if prerequisites such as data access, consent and independence checks are also repeatable.

5. **Workflow-native versus personal-productivity AI.** Baker Tilly's CEO separates AI that raises individual productivity from AI embedded in rebuilt tax and audit workflows, and says only the latter should move the P&L (CFOBREW-BAKERTILLY-2026). *For knowledge work:* what changes is the step in the flow, not the tool on the desk.

6. **Integration thesis and sequencing.** Bain says winners focus integration on a short list of value drivers and set an order of operations: where to stabilize and integrate, and where and when to transform (BAIN-OUTLOOK-2026, "Make big bets pay off"). *For AI:* AI should not be the first wave of change in a firm whose billing, time and data are not yet on common systems. [synthesis]

7. **The proof gap.** Grant Thornton's 2026 survey finds PE leaders confident about AI strategy but short of measurable results and of the governance to prove them (GT-AIIMPACT-2026). *For AI:* write the baseline and the business case into the underwriting so post-close claims can be checked.

8. **How platforms pitch AI to targets.** The pitch combines independence and local control, capital, shared technology and upskilling, and a talent argument. Current's pitch is that AI lets local firms compete with large incumbents and that its holding period is "effectively forever" (CPAPA-CURRENT-2026). Thomson Reuters' survey backs the talent argument: more than one-quarter of professionals would not accept a job at a firm lacking professional-grade AI (TR-FOP-2026). *For the advisor:* separate the pitch from the evidence behind it.

## 3. Expert moves

Format: move — when to use it — what it reveals — source ref.

1. **Ask for a usage report by named tool for the last full month (active users, not licences), and ask what staff use outside approved tools** — diligence, first management call — real adoption versus shelfware and shadow use — WK-PE-2025 (more than 40% of firms not fully using current technology investments); TR-FOP-2026 (a search summary of the full report cited 35% of professionals using AI their organization has not approved; that figure is not on the summary page I read, so treat it as unconfirmed) [synthesis].
2. **Score the target on six prerequisites (data, infrastructure, skilled talent, governance and security guardrails, cultural openness, aligned use cases) and attach a post-close remediation cost to each red item** — diligence — the work and spend between close and any AI benefit — RSM-AIDD-2025.
3. **Insist on a line of sight from each claimed AI use to EBITDA within six months** — screening AI claims in the thesis — which claims are validated use cases and which are aspiration — RSM-AIDD-2025 (operating-partner framing).
4. **Ask how the target measured any claimed savings, and ask to see the underlying data before accepting the number** — diligence — overstated savings; KPMG's SaaS case found projected AI savings overstated and hidden data-licensing and vendor lock-in risk — KPMG-AIDD-2025.
5. **Run an outside-in cost-base map from public sources before bidding (workforce structure, spend profile)** — pre-LOI — a first estimate of cost synergy that can be tested in diligence; Bain reports one media acquirer's post-close actuals landed within 90% of the forecast — BAIN-CAPABILITY-2026 (anonymous, non-accounting case; the phrase "within 90% of actuals" is the source's wording).
6. **List the target's attest clients against the sponsor's whole portfolio and upstream entities (fund, general partner, adviser) before signing** — diligence — client conflicts that can reach across the sponsor's fund family under the proposed AICPA rules — WINSTON-APS-2026; ICAEW-PE-2025; ACCOUNTANCYEUROPE-TPO-2025.
7. **Check engagement letters and consents for tax-return information: does anything allow processing by an AI tool, an affiliate or a central team?** — diligence and day-1 planning — consent gaps under Sec. 7216; disclosures to affiliates are on the list of disclosures that can need consent — TAXADVISER-7216-2024. No source gives an authoritative reading of AI tools under 7216 (see Gaps).
8. **Limit what a buyer's team sees in the data room: confidentiality agreement, and for tax clients a written agreement limiting use of return information to the purchase** — pre-signing — client-data exposure during diligence — CPAI-SUCCESSION-ND; JOFA-MA-2017.
9. **Decide early whether selling partners become equity or non-equity partners, because it changes the client-notice duty for file transfer** — structuring — whether written client consent (with the 90-day negative-consent option) is needed before files move — CAMICO-MERGE-2022.
10. **Pilot on one high-volume workflow with a stated baseline and a returns or case count** — first 100 days — a credible, comparable number; Current's reported Tax AI pilot gave a time-saving figure over a stated 7,000 returns — ACCTODAY-CURRENT-2026; CPAPA-CURRENT-2026.
11. **Embed builders with practitioners rather than shipping tools** — scaling past the first firm — fit to the work and faster feedback; the OpenAI–Thrive Holdings announcement describes embedded research, product and engineering teams — OPENAI-THRIVE-2025; TECHCRUNCH-AIROLLUP-2025 (General Catalyst's view that applied AI engineers are the scarce piece).
12. **Standardize the technology stack and workflow across acquired firms before expecting shared capacity** — post-close — whether work can move between offices; Wolters Kluwer gives a preparation-shifting example and an onboarding example — WK-PE-2025 (vendor perspective).
13. **Make the AI workflow-native and do not use tool-usage counts as a performance measure** — design of the adoption plan — Baker Tilly's CEO called usage measurement in reviews a "red herring" — CFOBREW-BAKERTILLY-2026.
14. **Write an integration thesis that names where to stabilize and where to transform, and hold AI to the second list** — before close — focus on a short list of value drivers — BAIN-OUTLOOK-2026.
15. **Use AI inside integration planning (draft integration thesis, team charters, work plans, decision lists) but leave time for people to absorb change** — integration planning — Bain's European bank case saved time; Bain also says people still need time to process even with good information — BAIN-CAPABILITY-2026 (anonymous, non-accounting).
16. **Budget training time and migration cost explicitly** — integration plan — plans commonly understate both; the author estimates bringing a firm onto a new IT platform can cost as much as $10,000 per FTE (an author's estimate, not a measurement) — JOFA-CULTURE-2018.
17. **Keep local brand and leadership where the thesis depends on partners staying engaged, and say so in writing** — pitch and offer — what incoming partners fear most; Current says partner firms keep leadership, brand and culture, and Reuters reports local owners keep a minority stake — CPAPA-CURRENT-2026; REUTERS-CRETE-2025.
18. **Show incoming partners evidence from a firm like theirs, on a timeline that works for them, instead of mandating a deployment** — day 1 to day 100 — resistance and priorities — GT-AIIMPACT-2026 (operating-partner quote).
19. **Lead the AI pitch with the talent argument, not the cost argument** — recruiting targets and incoming staff — what the people at the target care about; more than one-quarter of professionals say they would not accept a job at a firm lacking professional-grade AI — TR-FOP-2026.
20. **Keep a portfolio AI inventory per acquired entity: what runs, who owns it, what stage, what governance** — from day one — the proof an investment committee, lender or buyer will ask for — GT-AIIMPACT-2026.
21. **Write the AI business case at underwriting and track "exceeded / met / missed"** — post-close — FTI's high performers were more likely to report exceeding their AI business case (19% versus 5% of others) — FTI-VCI-2026 [synthesis on the tracking practice].
22. **Make the first-wave post-close priorities policy, data centralization and training, then use cases** — first 100 days — RSM says weak governance and data need first-wave attention — RSM-AIDD-2025.
23. **Keep named human review on high-stakes outputs and size reviewer capacity into the margin case** — design and sizing — AI output that looks finished still needs checking; Deloitte's 2026 study says human review remains the top requirement and TechCrunch relays "workslop" survey evidence — DELOITTE-PULSE-2026; TECHCRUNCH-AIROLLUP-2025.
24. **Engage the regulator and professional body early on any attest entity** — structuring — UK FRC and ICAEW urge early engagement, and the PCAOB says its inspections will look at firms that have taken PE — BLOOMBERGTAX-FRC-2025; ICAEW-PE-2025; ATODAY-PCAOB-2025.
25. **Ask employees (not only leaders) what worries them about AI and how leadership has explained its purpose** — diligence, culture read — whether change management is needed early — RSM-AIDD-2025 ("cultural openness").
26. **Ask for pricing, realization and client-attrition trends for the platform's previous acquisitions** — diligence on the acquirer when you are advising a target, or on the platform's record — whether margin gains came from pricing rather than work change; the health-care roll-up studies found price rises after add-on acquisitions — NBER-ANESTHESIA-2025; JAMA-GI-2025 [synthesis; adjacent evidence].
27. **Ask the acquirer for references from two previously acquired firms about what actually changed in the first 100 days** — target-side diligence — integration reality versus pitch — A&M reports fewer than one-quarter of respondents embed value-creation planning before the letter of intent, so references are a proxy for plan quality — AM-VALUECREATION-2026 [synthesis].
28. **Ask what the reported scale numbers measure (firms, offices, employees, revenue, as of when)** — reading any platform's public claims — comparability; Current's own and Accounting Today's published figures differ in basis — REUTERS-CRETE-2025; CPAPA-CURRENT-2026; ACCTODAY-CURRENT-2026.

## 4. Question bank

Scope tags: **[T]** team, **[U]** unit (practice line, office, acquired firm), **[O]** organization (platform or sponsor). Journey stages: **pre-LOI, diligence, sign-to-close, day 1-100, scale, steady state**.

### Onboard and frame (who is asking, what is the deal thesis)
- Is the thesis about capacity, margin, growth, or capability acquisition? Which AI claims are in the base case and which are upside? [O] pre-LOI
- Is this a fully merged platform, an umbrella of independent firms, or a capability buy? [O] pre-LOI — NJCPA-PE-2026
- Is there an attest entity, and which side of the APS line does each tool, data set and team sit on? [O] diligence — CPAPA-APRIO-PE-2024
- What does the sponsor's hold period and exit path imply for investment in training and tools? [O] pre-LOI — ACCOUNTANCYEUROPE-TPO-2025 (PE horizons 3-7 years)
- Who in the target decides whether to accept shared tools, and who loses control? [U] diligence

### Map (what is the work and the data)
- Which three workflows consume the most staff hours (tax prep, audit testing, bookkeeping, advisory deliverables)? What was last month's actual volume, not a typical month? [U/T] diligence
- Where does client data live, who can see it, and which systems would a central team need to reach? [U] diligence
- Which clients are also portfolio companies or related parties of the sponsor? [O] diligence — WINSTON-APS-2026
- What share of the target's engagement letters allow processing of client data by third parties or affiliates? [U] diligence — TAXADVISER-7216-2024
- Which tools are on the approved list, and which others do staff use? [T] diligence [proposed; the shadow-use question follows move 1]

### Diagnose (AI and data maturity of the target)
- Data: how often do you meet missing or wrong data, and how easily can you combine data across departments? [U] diligence — RSM-AIDD-2025
- Infrastructure: legacy on-premise systems, manual pipelines, ad hoc integrations? [U] diligence — RSM-AIDD-2025
- Talent: who owns AI or analytics, how many roles, how much of the work depends on outside parties, who is trained and how often? [U] diligence — RSM-AIDD-2025
- Governance: written AI policy, human-review rule for high-stakes outputs, data-privacy practice, who approves new tools? [O/U] diligence — RSM-AIDD-2025
- Culture: what concerns have staff raised (job security, accuracy, disruption) and how has leadership explained AI's purpose? [U/T] diligence — RSM-AIDD-2025
- Use cases: which are tied to measurable goals, and which exist "for AI's sake"? [U] diligence — RSM-AIDD-2025
- Value: how were claimed savings measured, over what baseline, and has anyone outside the team checked them? [U] diligence — KPMG-AIDD-2025
- Vendor and data risk: licensing terms, data ownership, portability, lock-in? [O/U] diligence — KPMG-AIDD-2025
- Do management's roadmaps match delivery pace (interview diagnostics, pipeline testing)? [U] diligence — PWC-AIDD-ND
- Is the technology stack consistent across offices, or does each office run its own tools? [U] diligence — WK-PE-2025
- Partners: how many are within five years of retirement, and what do they intend to do with their client relationships? [U] diligence [proposed; succession is the main driver of PE interest in NJCPA-PE-2026]

### Reimagine (four lenses applied to acquired firms)
- Improve the step: which step in tax preparation, audit testing or review gets faster with a shared assistant or shared team skills? [T] day 1-100
- Automate the flow: which end-to-end flow (data mapping, memo drafting, client onboarding) can a workflow automation take on across firms? [U] day 1-100 — REUTERS-CRETE-2025; WK-PE-2025
- Delegate the outcome: which outcome could a background agent or managed agent runtime own with human review (reconciliations, first-draft returns)? [U/O] scale — BAKERTILLY: workflow-native rebuild (CFOBREW-BAKERTILLY-2026)
- Reinvent the work: what advisory offer becomes viable when compliance capacity is freed? [O] scale — CPAPA-CAPSTONE-2026; TR-FOP-2026 ("elevate, scale, reimagine")

### Prioritize and make the case
- What is the value-creation plan per firm, and what share of it depends on AI? [U/O] sign-to-close — AM-VALUECREATION-2026
- What is the time to first measurable benefit, and does it fit inside the hold period? [O] pre-LOI — FTI-VCI-2026 (66% report AI benefits within 12 months)
- Can the CFO of each acquired firm prove ROI today? If not, what measurement must be built first? [U] day 1-100 — GT-AIIMPACT-2026
- What is the downside case if AI benefits arrive late (price or retention impact)? [O] pre-LOI [proposed]

### Plan (sequencing, change, data and independence)
- Which 30/60/100-day items are policy, data, access, training? Which are use cases? [U] sign-to-close — RSM-AIDD-2025
- What is the client notice and consent plan for file transfer and tool access? [U] sign-to-close — CAMICO-MERGE-2022
- Which sponsor-side people become covered members, and what is the monitoring system for portfolio changes? [O] sign-to-close — WINSTON-APS-2026; ACCOUNTANCYEUROPE-TPO-2025
- What do incoming partners get in writing about brand, leadership and compensation, and what do they give up? [U] sign-to-close
- How will junior staff build skills if AI takes the entry-level work? [O/U] scale — ATODAY-PCAOB-2025
- Who owns the AI inventory, and how often is it reviewed? [O] steady state — GT-AIIMPACT-2026

## 5. Common traps

| Trap | Why it happens | Evidence | How to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Underwriting AI savings from the target's or vendor's claims | Claims are not tied to a baseline or method | KPMG's SaaS case found savings overstated (KPMG-AIDD-2025; vendor-published, anonymous, software target). GC and Current figures are self-reported (TECHCRUNCH-AIROLLUP-2025; ACCTODAY-CURRENT-2026) | Ask for method and data; put AI upside outside the base case until measured [proposed] |
| Treating AI as a standalone project | Seen as a tools rollout | FTI: high performers are not adopting at materially higher rates but get results by embedding AI in core levers (FTI-VCI-2026) | Tie each use to a value lever and an owner |
| Mistaking personal productivity for P&L impact | Usage is easy to count | Baker Tilly's CEO expects no material P&L change from personal productivity (CFOBREW-BAKERTILLY-2026). NANDA: general tools mainly lift individual productivity (MITNANDA-GENAI-2025, Executive Summary) | Rebuild the workflow; measure the step, not the tool |
| Mandating tools in a partner-led firm | Platform wants speed | Operating-partner view: you cannot mandate a deployment; evidence from a similar business moves management teams (GT-AIIMPACT-2026) | Show a comparable firm's result and a timeline that suits them |
| Finding independence conflicts after signing | The sponsor's wider portfolio is checked late | The draft AICPA rules could restrict attest work for portfolio companies across the sponsor's fund family (WINSTON-APS-2026). Portfolios change often, so conflicts are hard to track (ACCOUNTANCYEUROPE-TPO-2025) | Screen the sponsor's holdings in diligence; build monitoring |
| Client data and consent gaps | Consent rules were written before AI and before platforms | Sec. 7216 consent can be needed for disclosures to affiliates and for uses beyond preparation (TAXADVISER-7216-2024). ET 1.400.205 sets written-consent steps for file transfer (CAMICO-MERGE-2022) | Map data flows and consents before day 1; take counsel's view on AI tools |
| Under-budgeting training and migration | Integration plans stay optimistic | Author's estimate of up to $10,000 per FTE to move a firm to a new IT platform (JOFA-CULTURE-2018, not measured) | Budget time and cash explicitly [synthesis] |
| Culture and partner retention | PE and partnership norms differ | 80% of independent UK firms cited cultural and ethical alignment as a reason not to pursue PE (ICAEW report as cited in ACCOUNTANCYEUROPE-TPO-2025). Academic evidence on partner turnover after PE is still being gathered (UVA-FAR-2026) | Treat retention as a measured outcome; track departures and client moves |
| Review burden ("workslop") eats the margin | AI output looks finished | Survey of 1,150 workers reported by TechCrunch: 40% say they take on extra work from low-quality AI output (TECHCRUNCH-AIROLLUP-2025; cross-industry) | Size reviewer capacity into the model |
| Skill erosion and the junior pipeline | AI takes entry-level tasks | PCAOB acting chair warned of reliance eroding skepticism and said new paths for junior auditors will be needed (ATODAY-PCAOB-2025) | Plan development paths in the operating model |
| Profit pressure on audit quality | Sponsor returns and exit horizon | PCAOB official: PE can reduce staffing and specialists and threaten independence and culture (ATODAY-PCAOB-2025). Accountancy Europe lists quality, independence and exit-strategy risks (ACCOUNTANCYEUROPE-TPO-2025) | Keep attest governance separate; expect inspection scrutiny |
| Price-led gains in roll-ups | Market power after add-ons | Anesthesia roll-ups: prices up 18% within six months of add-on deals and 25-30% eventually, no quality gain found (NBER-ANESTHESIA-2025). GI practices: +4.5% price, no quality difference detected (JAMA-GI-2025). Both are health care | Do not assume pricing power; test client attrition [synthesis] |
| Over-integrating early | Wanting uniformity quickly | Bain: winners do not get caught up resolving internal complexity in the first wave (BAIN-OUTLOOK-2026) | Sequence; transform only where the thesis needs it |
| Comparing platform numbers with different bases | Firms report firms, offices, employees and revenue differently | Current: Reuters (2025) and the company (2026) versus Accounting Today's Top 100 entry (ACCTODAY-CURRENT-2026) | Ask for definitions and dates |
| Generalizing from a young category | Few roll-ups have a full cycle | Newcomer: too early to assess roll-up performance as a category (NEWCOMER-ROLLUP-2025) | Present AI roll-up results as claims, not benchmarks |

## 7. Worked examples

### A. Current (formerly Crete Professionals Alliance): AI as the platform thesis
- **Thesis and structure.** Reuters reported in June 2025 that Crete planned to invest over $500 million over two years to buy US accounting firms and equip them with OpenAI-powered tools. It takes majority stakes, local owners keep a minority stake, and it supports firms with recruiting and administration (REUTERS-CRETE-2025). Thrive Holdings and OpenAI announced in December 2025 that OpenAI would embed research, product and engineering teams in Thrive Holdings' companies, starting with accounting and IT services (OPENAI-THRIVE-2025).
- **What it deployed.** In June 2026 Current said an embedded team of AI engineers and researchers builds products with accountants, and that its first Tax AI pilot ran in tax year 2025 (CPAPA-CURRENT-2026).
- **Reported result.** Participating firms saw average 31% savings in tax preparation time and up to 98% accuracy across 7,000 returns (company-reported; baseline and accuracy measure not published).
- **What an advisor should take from it.** Pilot design, embedded builders and a named count are the transferable parts. The headline numbers are claims. The pitch to targets is local control plus capital and tools; the CEO also says the roll-up playbook is often "cost extraction on a deadline" and that Current is different (CPAPA-CURRENT-2026).
- **Limits.** Scale figures differ by source and date (E18). No independent check of the pilot was found.

### B. Baker Tilly after Moss Adams, and Grant Thornton's CBIZ deal: scale as the funding mechanism for AI
- **Baker Tilly.** Sold a stake to Hellman & Friedman and Valeas Capital Partners in 2024 and closed the Moss Adams deal in June 2025. Its CEO says the sponsor sharpened its technology thinking, that Baker Tilly is among the few portfolio companies prioritized for the Ode with Anthropic venture, and that scale lets it invest in technology "at a rate that we never would have done as independent firms." It plans to rebuild workflows such as tax and audit with agentic AI and calls personal-productivity AI "table stakes." Two further acquisitions were announced after Moss Adams (CFOBREW-BAKERTILLY-2026; BLACKSTONE-ODE-2026 for the venture's announcement).
- **Grant Thornton Advisors.** Announced a $5 billion cash acquisition of CBIZ (reported 2026-07-30), backed by additional equity from New Mountain Capital, and ties it to AI: the CEO says scale gives flexibility to invest in tools, training and adoption, and describes introducing as many as seven tools each quarter. The deal was pending at the date of the cited articles (IAB-GTCBIZ-2026; BLOOMBERGTAX-GT-2026).
- **What an advisor should take from it.** Platforms frame size as the way to fund AI and training. As an advisor, ask what the acquired firm's share of the shared stack is and when it arrives.
- **Limits.** These are leader statements and announcements. No published outcome measure was found for either firm's integration.

### C. Diligence on AI claims: the KPMG software-target case with the RSM prerequisites
- **Case.** A client sought to buy a mid-market SaaS provider whose pitch rested on AI-driven operations. The diligence team assessed data ownership, model performance, vendor dependencies and integration readiness, and benchmarked maturity. It found projected savings overstated and risks in data licensing and vendor lock-in. The client renegotiated and set Day 1 priorities (KPMG-AIDD-2025).
- **How to apply it to a professional-services target.** Use RSM's six prerequisites as the interview spine and attach a remediation cost to each red item: if data are siloed, expect time and money centralizing and governing them before benefits show (RSM-AIDD-2025).
- **Limits.** The KPMG case is a vendor-published, anonymous software acquisition (adjacent, grade cap B). Neither source says how often diligence findings changed price.

### D. Limits from another roll-up industry: anesthesia practices
- **Evidence.** In NBER working paper 33217, the first acquisition in a market was followed by no price change, but add-on acquisitions were followed by about an 18% price rise within six months and 25-30% eventually. The authors found no evidence of higher quality (NBER-ANESTHESIA-2025). A gastroenterology study found a smaller price rise and no detected quality effect (JAMA-GI-2025).
- **Use.** A reminder that roll-up margin can come from market power rather than better work, and that regulators track it. Ask what share of a platform's reported margin gain comes from pricing.
- **Limits.** Health care, insurer-negotiated prices, and no AI element. Do not read these as findings on accounting.

## 8. Mapping to steps

### 8.1 transformation-advisor: acquisition path (assess a target or newly acquired firm's AI maturity, with a subagent brief)

**Intake fields.** Entity and structure (platform, umbrella, standalone; attest entity yes/no); deal stage (pre-LOI, diligence, sign-to-close, day 1-100, scale); thesis (capacity, margin, growth, capability); AI claims in the base case; who is asking (acquirer, target, or operating partner); public sources available; client-data and independence constraints known. [proposed]

**Assessment template (one page per target).** Sections follow the six RSM prerequisites, extended where other sources add dimensions: 1 Data; 2 Infrastructure and tool stack; 3 Talent and training; 4 Governance and security (policy, human-review rule, approved tools, shadow use); 5 Culture and change readiness; 6 Use cases tied to measurable goals; 7 Client-data and consent posture; 8 Independence and structure (attest, sponsor portfolio); 9 Vendor and licence dependencies; 10 Evidence of measured results. Dimensions 1-6 from RSM-AIDD-2025, 4 and 9 and 10 echo KPMG-AIDD-2025 and PWC-AIDD-ND, 7 and 8 from TAXADVISER-7216-2024, CAMICO-MERGE-2022 and WINSTON-APS-2026. Per dimension record: evidence seen, source of evidence (document, interview, public), rating (red, amber, green), remediation cost and time, owner.

**Red flags** (from sources).
- Claimed savings with no method or baseline (KPMG-AIDD-2025).
- Siloed data, no governance, no cataloguing (RSM-AIDD-2025).
- Legacy on-premise systems, manual data pipelines, ad hoc integrations (RSM-AIDD-2025).
- Heavy dependence on outside parties and no staff upskilling (RSM-AIDD-2025).
- Vague or missing AI policy; no human-review rule for high-stakes outputs (RSM-AIDD-2025).
- Each office on different tools (WK-PE-2025).
- Attest clients that are sponsor portfolio companies or their related parties (WINSTON-APS-2026).
- Engagement letters that do not allow the intended processing of client data (TAXADVISER-7216-2024).
- Unclear roadmap delivery, promises beyond demonstrated pace (PWC-AIDD-ND).

**Decision rules and thresholds.**
- Sourced: require a line of sight to EBITDA within six months for a use to count toward the base case (RSM-AIDD-2025); expect AI benefits within 12 months as the survey norm for PE (FTI-VCI-2026: 66%); effective date of the proposed AICPA rules is one year after adoption (NYSSCPA-APS-2025); the file-transfer notice allows a 90-day negative-consent window (CAMICO-MERGE-2022).
- Proposed (no source gives a threshold): treat AI synergy as upside, not base case, when three or more of the six prerequisites are red; make the independence screen and the client-consent map conditions for signing; report each rating with its evidence type so the reader can see how much rests on management statements.

**Subagent brief (template).** Objective: produce a one-page maturity read on [entity] from public sources. Inputs: entity name, structure, public filings, press releases, website, job postings, trade-press coverage. Method: apply the ten dimensions; label each finding as stated by the entity, reported by a third party, or inferred. Output: rating table with sources and dates, red-flag list, ten questions for management, a short list of assumptions to test. Guardrails: public sources only, no client-confidential information, no vendor recommendations (describe tools by runtime class: personal assistant, shared team skills, workflow automation, background agent, managed agent runtime), quote under 25 words, mark dates, keep "announced", "piloting" and "in production" distinct. [proposed; guardrails follow the module precision rules]

### 8.2 ai-portfolio: a playbook for rolling repeatable capabilities into acquired firms

**Capability catalogue entry (fields).** Capability; runtime class; prerequisites (data access, consent, independence assessment, stack); workflow step it changes; training needed; owner at platform and at the acquired firm; baseline and KPI; time to first measurement; rollout wave; evidence status (claimed, piloted, in production, measured).

**Rollout waves (shape).** 
- Wave 0, sign-to-close: client-notice plan, consent map, independence screen, clean-team rules for data access. [CAMICO-MERGE-2022; WINSTON-APS-2026]
- Wave 1, day 1-100: AI policy, approved-tools list, training, data centralization and governance, personal-assistant tier. [RSM-AIDD-2025]
- Wave 2: shared team skills and workflow automation on one or two high-volume flows with baselines and counts. [ACCTODAY-CURRENT-2026 for pilot design; WK-PE-2025 for stack standardization]
- Wave 3: workflow-native rebuilds and background or managed agents with human review; advisory offers once capacity is freed. [CFOBREW-BAKERTILLY-2026; TR-FOP-2026]
Sequencing follows Bain: stabilize and integrate first, transform where the thesis needs it (BAIN-OUTLOOK-2026). The wave structure itself is [synthesis].

**Per-firm variation rules.** Platform model: umbrella firms get an offer and evidence; merged firms get a mandate with a timeline. [synthesis from NJCPA-PE-2026 and GT-AIIMPACT-2026] Attest entities: no shared tool or data flow without an independence assessment. [synthesis]

**Measurement.** Record the business case at underwriting; track met, exceeded, missed (FTI-VCI-2026 definitions); keep an AI inventory per acquired entity with owner and governance status (GT-AIIMPACT-2026); report both time savings and what the freed time was used for. [proposed]

**Gates (proposed, no source gives numbers).** Move a capability from pilot to roll-out only when: baseline and method are documented; consent and independence checks are complete; named reviewers exist; one firm other than the pilot firm has run it. Pause roll-out when reviewer workload or client complaints rise (TECHCRUNCH-AIROLLUP-2025 on review burden).

- **No independent measurement of AI value at a platform.** Current's pilot, Long Lake's figures and General Catalyst's task-automation claims are all self-reported or investor-published. The AI-specific survey figures are from consultancies that sell the services they survey.
- **McKinsey primary material was not reached.** McKinsey pages returned 403 through curl and the text mirror. The McKinsey figures are second-hand through CFO Dive.
