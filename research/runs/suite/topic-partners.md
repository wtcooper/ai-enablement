# Topic: Leading AI change in partner-led firms

Skill and steps fed: transformation-advisor (people and change, leadership summary) and ai-enablement (partners and skeptics): stakeholder mapping, partner briefings, objection handling, leader expectations.
Prepared 2026-10-20. Public sources only. All pages were read via `curl` or the r.jina.ai text mirror between the first and last fetch of this run; "access" in section 9 says how much of each was read.

How to read the labels used below:

- **self-reported**: the organization or an executive describes its own results, usually in a talk, interview or press statement.
- **vendor-published**: a vendor or vendor-owned research arm published the figure.
- **independent**: a third party (academic, regulator, professional body, survey firm) ran the study.
- **secondary**: the figure reached me through a news article or blog describing someone else's study. I did not read the primary.
- **opinion**: commentary with no data behind the claim.
- **[inference]** on an expert move means the move is my synthesis of the cited material, not something a source states as a practice.
- Under the Grade A rule (2026-10-17), nothing in this file is graded A. Where a named organization reports a figure as measured, I say so; recollections and rough estimates are marked as such.

## 1. Summary

- **Partners are owners, not employees, so the change lead has influence rather than authority.** Empson's study of three professional firms, as reviewed by Flood, describes partnership as limiting the use of formal power and leadership as "a nebulous and fragile phenomenon" (JOTWELL-EMPSON-2013). Mandates alone are weak levers; the sources that report success describe peer influence, visible use by senior people, and a business-problem framing (LAW360-2025, CIPD-2026, POLSINELLI-2026).
- **The billable hour is the most-cited barrier, but the evidence on how AI changes hours is thin and mixed.** Almost 60% of in-house counsel in an ACC/Everlaw survey reported "no noticeable savings yet" from outside counsel's AI use (BLOOMBERG-ACC-2025). A Georgetown law professor argues AI could reinforce the hourly model through rates, write-offs and expenses, not only hours (LF-PERLIN-2025). Both are arguments or client-side perceptions, not measured firm outcomes.
- **Leaders are often the least comfortable users.** In the ICAEW/Chartered Accountants Worldwide survey, 47% of senior leaders said they were comfortable using AI, and 53% of C-suite respondents did not feel prepared for AI's impact on their role (ICAEW-CAW-2025). BCG's cross-industry survey found the share of employees feeling positive about GenAI rose from 15% to 55% with strong leadership support, and only about a quarter of frontline employees said they received it (BCG-ATWORK-2025).
- **Visible, specific wins and peer champions have the best qualitative support, and the case write-ups are self-reported.** A&O Shearman, Michael Best, Lightfoot Franklin and a CIPD-studied firm all used named practitioners, bite-sized training, mapped influencers or champion networks (PAPERJAM-2025, LAW360-2025, CIPD-2026). None publishes a controlled comparison.
- **Skeptic objections are often legitimate.** Independent studies show real error rates in legal AI research tools (17% to 33% hallucination in a preregistered study, ARXIV-MAGESH-2024), a task where AI made consultants less likely to be correct (HBS-DELLACQUA-2023), and a government report with fabricated references (PEOPLEMATTERS-2025). The advisor should concede these and show the control rather than argue.
- **Accountability is shifting from exhortation to expectation.** KPMG says staff AI objectives enter performance reviews from 2026 and describes tracking as not disciplinary (IAB-KPMG-2025). PwC's US CEO was reported as saying partners who resist AI would not last (CPAPA-PWC-2026). Polsinelli's chair says there is no billing credit for AI training (POLSINELLI-2026). No source reports outcomes of these expectations yet.
- **Honest role communication matters because the pyramid and partner pipeline are visibly under pressure.** Big Four partner promotions were reported at 179 for the 2025 cycle versus 276 in 2022 (ACCTAGE-2025). Several law firms have announced business-services cuts citing AI (LEGALCHEEK-BAKER-2026). Statements that avoid either denial or overstatement ("some roles phased out, others evolve") are the pattern in public statements.
- **Measurement is the weak point.** Only 18% of respondents in the Thomson Reuters Institute 2026 survey knew their organization tracked AI ROI (TRI-AIPS-2026). Partner conversations stall without a baseline.

## 2. Core concepts in plain language

1. **Influence without authority.** In a partnership the people whose behavior must change co-own the firm. A managing partner or head of AI usually cannot order a rainmaker to change how they practice. Empson's research (via JOTWELL-EMPSON-2013) describes a "leadership constellation" of the senior pair, practice heads, business-services heads and key influencers. For AI work: map and recruit the constellation; do not rely on a sponsor's title.

2. **Partner economics set the real incentives.** Partners are paid from profit, and profit depends on hours, rates, leverage (junior staff per partner) and realization. Greenwood and Empson argue partnership has strong incentive systems for knowledge workers under particular conditions (ACCT-GREENWOOD-2003). Perlin lists four variables AI can move (hours, rate, write-offs, expenses), which is a useful checklist for any practice's economics (LF-PERLIN-2025). For AI: ask what happens to a partner's numbers when they spend time learning, and when a task takes less time.

3. **Leverage and the pipeline.** The pyramid trains tomorrow's partners through junior work. Commentators and Empson (quoted in ACCTAGE-2025) note firms struggle to forecast partner income streams under AI, and promotion intakes were cut. For AI: automating junior tasks is also a talent-pipeline decision, which partners care about for succession reasons.

4. **Resistance as information.** LexisNexis calls consultant resistance "rational" because teams protect expertise and reputation (LEXIS-CONSULT-2025). Practitioner sources list time pressure, fear of looking incompetent, and billing metrics as the usual objections (MANDS-2026, PATTERN-2025; both opinion). For AI: treat each objection as a hypothesis about a missing condition (time, trust, proof, incentive), then test it.

5. **Diffusion is social.** Gawande, citing Rogers, writes that diffusion is "essentially a social process" in which people follow those they know and trust (GAWANDE-2013). For AI: repeated, hands-on contact from respected peers beats broadcast emails.

6. **The jagged frontier.** AI helps on some tasks and hurts on others, even within one workflow. In the BCG/HBS experiment, consultants using AI did more tasks and faster on tasks inside the frontier, but were 19% less likely to be correct on one task outside it (HBS-DELLACQUA-2023). For AI: skeptical seniors are partly right; the advisor's job is to find which tasks are inside the frontier for that practice.

7. **Accountability stays with the professional.** Regulators and professional bodies say responsibility cannot be delegated to a tool, and managerial lawyers must set policy, train and monitor (LAWNEXT-2024 on ABA Formal Opinion 512; LAWSOC-GENAI; VINCI-2026 on the SRA warning). For AI: partner accountability is a professional-duty fact, not a change-management preference.

8. **Usage is not value.** Usage rose (40% organization-wide in 2026 vs 22% in 2025 in the TRI survey) while few organizations track ROI (TRI-AIPS-2026). For AI: agree what "success" means before briefing partners.

## 3. Expert moves

Format: move, when to use it, what it reveals, source. Where a move is my synthesis, it carries [inference].

1. **Map the informal power structure, not the org chart: list the senior pair, practice heads, business-services heads and the rainmakers or "eminences grises" whose view others wait for.** When stakeholder mapping in a partnership. Reveals who can actually move peers and who can quietly block. JOTWELL-EMPSON-2013 (review, section on "Leadership Constellation").
2. **Find influencers with data, not nominations: combine staff survey results and IT service-desk call patterns with practice-group personas.** When choosing champions. Reveals vocal people open to improving things, who need not be tech-savvy. LAW360-2025 (Michael Best section).
3. **Pick champions for peer credibility and voice, accept that early adopters are hard to find in a low-risk-tolerance profession.** When forming the first coalition. Reveals whether the firm has enough credible volunteers or must lean on trusted staff. LAW360-2025.
4. **Put respected business-minded partners (not only technologists) in charge of the AI program and frame it as a business problem.** When an earlier effort produced "plenty of activity" without progress. Reveals whether the program has legitimacy with partners. POLSINELLI-2026 (chair's account; self-reported).
5. **Show a tool in use on a named workflow with a measured baseline instead of a tool demo.** When briefing a partner group. Reveals whether partners judge the workflow or the technology. [inference] built on A&O's "Can we take Harvey away?" question (PAPERJAM-2025) and the TRI finding that few track ROI (TRI-AIPS-2026).
6. **Ask pilot users the "take it away" question, then report the answer at the partner meeting.** After a pilot has run long enough for habits to form. Reveals dependence, which is stronger evidence for peers than satisfaction scores. PAPERJAM-2025 (a partner's recollection six months after rollout; self-reported, not a tracked metric).
7. **Do the economics for one practice with partners: ask what changes in hours, rate, write-offs, expenses and staffing, using Perlin's four variables.** When the objection is "this kills our billings." Reveals whether offsets are plausible or wishful, and which partners are exposed. LF-PERLIN-2025 (argument; no measured firm outcomes).
8. **Ask what clients are saying: outside counsel guidelines, requests to use or not use AI, and expectations of savings.** When framing urgency. Reveals external pressure and conflicting instructions. TRI-AIPS-2026 (40% of firm respondents had received both "use AI" and "don't use AI" instructions); BLOOMBERG-ACC-2025 (more guidelines asking AI time savings be reflected in billing).
9. **Respond to "I tried it, it doesn't work" with "which task, and we will come back to you," then route the failure to the innovation team.** When a skeptic reports a bad trial. Reveals whether the task is outside the frontier or the use was poor. POLSINELLI-2026 (chair's wording, paraphrased); HBS-DELLACQUA-2023.
10. **Concede documented failure modes, then show the verification control.** When a senior expert cites hallucinations or the Deloitte report. Reveals whether the objection is about risk (answerable with controls) or identity. ARXIV-MAGESH-2024 (17% to 33% hallucination in three legal research tools); PEOPLEMATTERS-2025; CIPD-2026 ("verify, verify, verify" rule).
11. **Have senior people use the tool themselves and share what worked and what did not.** When leader credibility is in doubt. Reveals the gap between leader statements and use. CIPD-2026 (LegalCo: senior lawyers used the tool and shared learnings); ICAEW-CAW-2025 (47% of senior leaders comfortable).
12. **Pair a respected peer with a skeptic for repeated, personal sessions rather than broadcast communication.** When broadcast training stalled. Reveals trust and habit barriers. [inference] from GAWANDE-2013 (Rogers' "people talking to people"; the cited example is health care, not a firm).
13. **Establish a cross-functional steering group that meets on a fixed rhythm and includes senior legal and business-services leaders.** At rollout. Reveals conflicts between practice and operations early. CIPD-2026 (fortnightly group).
14. **Make training a condition of access and keep it short and varied.** At rollout. Reveals whether access is gated by capability. CIPD-2026 (mandatory training before use); LAW360-2025 (lunch-and-learns, short videos, one-page handouts); BCG-ATWORK-2025 (regular use sharply higher with at least five hours of training; only one-third say they were properly trained).
15. **Decide and say out loud whether learning time is credited, and record the rule.** When partners ask "what about my hours?" Reveals whether stated priorities match compensation. POLSINELLI-2026 (no billable credit for AI training); LAWNEXT-2024 (ABA 512: a lawyer may not bill a client for time spent gaining competence in AI unless the client requests it); MANDS-2026 (opinion: unchanged incentives mean unchanged behavior).
16. **Separate usage tracking from policing and say which it is.** When setting leader expectations. Reveals fear of surveillance. IAB-KPMG-2025 (KPMG describes monitoring as not intended as discipline; AI objectives in 2026 reviews).
17. **Ask which services could be priced or packaged apart from hours.** When partners say AI shrinks the fee base. Reveals appetite for new revenue streams. AO-AGENT-2025 and ARTLAW-2025 (A&O Shearman announced agents to be sold and revenue shared); CPAPA-PWC-2026 (PwC said it would offer alternatives to hourly billing and subscription-style tools).
18. **Ask about the partner-to-junior ratio the plan assumes and who trains the next generation.** When a plan automates junior work. Reveals pipeline and leverage risk. ACCTAGE-2025 (Empson on forecasting partner income); BGOV-ODOWD-2026 (opinion).
19. **Segment the partner group by exposure and horizon: rainmakers, practice heads, partners near retirement, leverage-dependent partners.** Before drafting the briefing. Reveals different objections and incentives per group. [inference]; BGOV-ODOWD-2026 and MANDS-2026 are opinion sources that make similar distinctions.
20. **Run a shadow-AI diagnostic: ask where people already use personal tools.** At diagnosis. Reveals real demand and unsanctioned risk. MIT-NANDA-2025 (workers from over 90% of surveyed companies reported regular personal AI use; weak methodology, see section 6); BCG-ATWORK-2025 (more than half would find alternatives if tools are missing).
21. **Tie partner accountability to the professional-duty floor: policy, training, monitoring, supervision.** When a partner says AI is optional. Reveals which obligations apply regardless of preference. LAWNEXT-2024 (ABA 512, Rules 5.1 and 5.3); VINCI-2026 (SRA: those responsible for supervision may face consequences if inadequate supervision lets false citations through).
22. **Collect and communicate short-term wins early and often.** After the first pilot. Reveals what peers will believe. KOTTER (step "Generate Short-Term Wins"); LAW360-2025 (Lightfoot Franklin's newsletter).
23. **Run an ADKAR check by partner subgroup: which of awareness, desire, knowledge, ability, reinforcement is missing?** When adoption stalls after training. Reveals whether to brief, persuade, train or change incentives. PROSCI-ADKAR.
24. **Give people a safe place to ask basic questions: office hours with specialists and recognition for experimenters.** For senior people who do not want to look unskilled. Reveals hidden knowledge gaps. LEXIS-CONSULT-2025 (vendor blog); EDMONDSON-1999 (team psychological safety is associated with learning behavior in work teams; study in a manufacturer).
25. **Agree the proof before the pilot: ask each skeptic what result would change their mind.** When designing a pilot. Reveals whether the objection can be settled by evidence. [inference]; TRI-AIPS-2026 (few organizations track ROI).
26. **Use real client-facing outcomes as the lead narrative for partners and capacity arguments for associates.** When drafting briefings. Reveals which message each audience uses. MANDS-2026 and CUMBERLAND-2026 (opinion); LF-PERLIN-2025 (clients pay for outcomes).
27. **State role changes in the firm's actual terms: which roles are being phased out, which will evolve, and what consultation applies.** When communicating about roles. Reveals trust; vague reassurance reads as denial. LEGALCHEEK-BAKER-2026 (firm spokesperson wording); NONBILLABLE-AO-2026 (A&O statement: "limited and localized headcount reductions" where necessary).
28. **Check claimed time savings for how they were measured before repeating them.** When a champion cites savings. Reveals whether the figure is a lawyer's recollection or tracked data. PAPERJAM-2025 (lawyers "report saving two to three hours a week"); LEXIS-CONSULT-2025 (56% of consultants using AI report 3–4 hours a day, vendor survey).

## 4. Question bank

Tags: scope = team / unit / organization; stage = exploring (E), piloting (P), scaling (S).

**Onboard**
- Who else has to say yes before this plan can start, and who can say no without being asked? (unit, organization; E)
- Is this firm a partnership, a corporate with partner titles, or a hybrid, and who votes on investment? (organization; E)
- What has been tried before, and what did partners conclude from it? (unit, organization; E, P)
- What would you want me never to propose to your partners? (team, unit; E)

**Frame**
- Which metric does the partner group watch most (profit per equity partner, realization, utilization, revenue)? (organization; E, P, S)
- What would count as success in twelve months, and how will it be measured today? (all; E, P, S) [TRI: 18% know of ROI tracking]
- Where is the pressure coming from: clients, competitors, recruits, the board of partners, or the leader's own view? (organization; E)
- What is the leaders' own use of AI today, in practice? (unit, organization; E, P) [ICAEW 47%]

**Map**
- Who are the five partners others check with before backing a change? (unit, organization; E)
- Which practice groups are open to change and which are exposed to client pushback? (unit; E, P) [LAW360 Michael Best mapping]
- Who are the likely champions, and what are they known for besides technology? (team, unit; E, P)
- Where do leaders hear about AI from, and is that view filtered? (organization; E) [CUMBERLAND-2026, opinion]
- Which partners are near retirement, and who holds their clients? (organization; E, S)

**Diagnose**
- Show me the last matter or engagement for this work: how many hours, how many people, how much rework? (team; E, P)
- What do clients say about AI use on this work? (unit; E, P, S) [TRI-AIPS-2026]
- What happens to a partner's numbers if this task takes half as long? (unit; E, P, S)
- Where are people already using personal AI tools? (team, unit; E, P) [NANDA, BCG]
- What are the ways AI use here could go badly wrong, and who signs off today? (team, unit; E, P)
- What do you currently charge for this work and on what basis (hours, fixed fee, subscription)? (unit; E, P, S)

**Reimagine**
- Which part of this workflow would a skeptical senior expert agree is routine? (team; E) [inference]
- Which outputs could be sold or priced without hours? (unit, organization; P, S) [AO-AGENT-2025, CPAPA-PWC-2026]
- If juniors no longer do this task, how do they learn judgment? (unit, organization; P, S) [BGOV-ODOWD-2026, opinion]
- Which tasks do you want a human to still own, and why? (team; E, P)

**Prioritize**
- Which candidate has a visible win within one quarter that a partner will recognize? (unit; E, P) [KOTTER short-term wins]
- Which candidates threaten a specific partner's practice? (unit, organization; E, P, S)
- Which depend on partners changing behavior versus staff changing behavior? (unit; E, P)

**Make the case**
- What proof would change each skeptic's mind? (team, unit; E, P) [inference]
- Which data can we show from our own work, not the vendor's? (unit; P, S)
- What are the three objections we expect, and what is the honest answer to each? (unit, organization; E, P, S)
- What will this cost in partner time and in non-billable time? (unit, organization; E, P, S)
- What does the pricing model look like in three scenarios: hours fall, hours hold with higher rates, hours replaced by fixed or subscription fees? (organization; P, S) [LF-PERLIN-2025]

**Plan (leader expectations and accountability)**
- What are the expectations for partners, in writing, and what happens if they are not met? (organization; P, S) [IAB-KPMG-2025, POLSINELLI-2026]
- Is training time credited, and who decided? (unit, organization; P, S)
- Who owns policy, training and monitoring under the professional rules? (organization; P, S) [LAWNEXT-2024, VINCI-2026]
- What will you say about roles, and when? (organization; P, S) [LEGALCHEEK-BAKER-2026]
- Who reviews partner-level AI use, and in what forum? (unit, organization; S)

**Roll-up (unit to organization)**
- Which units' objections are the same objection? (organization; S)
- Which unit leaders have credibility with the others? (organization; S)
- Where do unit plans assume the same scarce partner time? (organization; S)

## 5. Common traps

| Trap | Why it happens | Evidence | How to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Buying licences and expecting use | Technology treated as the whole change | Law360 reports firms spending heavily on licences that go unused (LAW360-2025, introductory paragraph; no figure). BCG: only one-third say they were properly trained (BCG-ATWORK-2025). | Pair access with training, a named workflow and a champion. |
| Broadcast mandate to autonomous partners | Partners can decline without penalty and clients follow them | Practitioner opinion (CUMBERLAND-2026, MANDS-2026). Empson: partnership limits formal power (JOTWELL-EMPSON-2013). No outcome data on mandates. | Work through the leadership constellation; use peers and visible wins. |
| Assuming hours will fall (or will not) | Both sides argue from the model | ACC/Everlaw: nearly 60% of in-house respondents saw no noticeable savings; over 60% said it is too early (BLOOMBERG-ACC-2025). Perlin argues hours could be offset (LF-PERLIN-2025). Furlong's 2023 essay predicts a "treadmill" (FURLONG-2023, opinion). | Model several scenarios for the practice; measure before claiming. |
| Leaders asking others to use what they do not use | Seniors less comfortable; fear of looking unskilled | 47% of senior leaders comfortable (ICAEW-CAW-2025). BCG: leaders and managers report more regular use than frontline (BCG-ATWORK-2025). | Make leader use visible; offer private skill-building. |
| No baseline or ROI measure | Usage counts are easy; value is hard | 18% know ROI is tracked (TRI-AIPS-2026). | Agree measures first; separate usage from value. |
| Incentives that contradict the message | Billing metrics unchanged | Polsinelli gives no billing credit for AI training (POLSINELLI-2026). ABA 512 bars billing clients for competence time (LAWNEXT-2024). | Decide the rule explicitly; recognize contributions outside billing. |
| Overpromising accuracy | Vendor claims of "hallucination-free" | 17% to 33% hallucination in three legal research tools (ARXIV-MAGESH-2024). Deloitte Australia report with fabricated references (PEOPLEMATTERS-2025). | State limits; require verification; show controls. |
| Dismissing resistance as irrational | Advocates see only benefits | LexisNexis calls it rational (LEXIS-CONSULT-2025). Jagged frontier shows real loss on some tasks (HBS-DELLACQUA-2023). | Convert objections into tests. |
| Vague or false reassurance on roles | Fear of reaction | Firms publicly say some roles will be phased out (LEGALCHEEK-BAKER-2026). A CIPD case firm's leaders said AI would not be used to cut support roles, which worked there under their own circumstances (CIPD-2026). | Say only what you control; give timing and consultation. |
| Ignoring the pipeline | Efficiency framing hides succession | Promotion intake cuts (ACCTAGE-2025); opinion on apprenticeship loss (BGOV-ODOWD-2026). | Include career path and training design in the plan. |
| Trusting self-reported adoption figures | Executives quote usage with no definition | Polsinelli chair's "90% have used" and "mid-60s% daily" are verbal statements (POLSINELLI-2026). Definitions of "use" differ across surveys (compare rows 1, 6 and 18 in section 6). | Ask how "use" is defined and measured. |
| Threat-first accountability | Pressure to move fast | PwC CEO quoted as saying resisters will not last (CPAPA-PWC-2026, reporting of an FT interview); no source reports effects. | Pair expectations with support and clear criteria; do not copy without evidence. |

## 7. Worked examples

### A. A&O Shearman: lawyer-led enterprise rollout, later productized
- **Source:** PAPERJAM-2025, AO-AGENT-2025, ARTLAW-2025.
- **What happened:** A partner in the Markets Innovation Group says the firm deployed Harvey enterprise-wide from late 2022 and describes asking lawyers six months later whether it could be taken away, with the answer "Absolutely not" (her recollection). She reports lawyers say they save two to three hours a week; separately she describes the principle for the firm's contract-review tool as keeping lawyers in the process to validate output (PAPERJAM-2025). In April 2025 the firm announced agents built with Harvey to be rolled out internally and sold to clients and other law firms, with the firm sharing software revenue (AO-AGENT-2025; ARTLAW-2025).
- **What it teaches the advisor:** Lawyer-partners lead the program (not IT); the lead argument is dependence and keeping lawyers in the loop; the firm later opened a revenue line that is not billed by the hour. Limits: all figures are self-reported, the firm is unusually well resourced, and the "revenue share" announcement is a plan, not an outcome. In May 2026 the firm was reported to be making limited business-services reductions (NONBILLABLE-AO-2026), which shows enthusiasm for AI and role changes coexist.

### B. Michael Best: stakeholder mapping, champions and Kotter steps
- **Source:** LAW360-2025.
- **What happened:** The chief innovation and technology officer used Kotter's eight steps, started with the "Three Whys" for staff, built a coalition of attorney champions, and found influencers by mapping stakeholders (personas, practice groups, surveys, IT service-desk data). He looked for vocal people regardless of topic.
- **Lesson:** A concrete method for partner stakeholder mapping that uses data; no outcome figures are published, and the account is the officer's own.

### C. Polsinelli: a business problem, a no-credit rule, and honest skeptic handling
- **Source:** POLSINELLI-2026 (podcast transcript).
- **What happened:** After about a year of "plenty of activity" and little progress, the chair says he gave AI to respected senior people rather than technologists and treated it as a business problem. He says skeptics who said "it doesn't work" were told the firm would get back to them. He says some people use it little. He refuses special billable credit for AI training. He reports 90% of lawyers used a platform and the "mid-60s" percent use it daily (verbal, self-reported).
- **Lesson:** A leadership expectation stated without incentives, matched by capacity to respond to failed trials. The chair's statements are not tracked metrics. No evidence is given on whether the no-credit rule helps or hinders adoption.

### D. Deloitte Australia: accountability when an AI error reaches a client
- **Source:** PEOPLEMATTERS-2025, REGION-2025, CYBERDAILY-2025.
- **What happened:** A government report worth about A$440,000, published on the department's website on 14 August 2025 (per the department's statement quoted by Region Canberra), was found by a university researcher to contain fabricated references and a made-up quote. A corrected version disclosed use of Azure OpenAI GPT-4o. Deloitte said the errors did not affect the report's substantive content and agreed to repay the final instalment. A senator said Deloitte had "a human intelligence problem."
- **Lesson for skeptics:** Their risk concern has a public precedent. The advisor can use it to argue for verification roles and named sign-off, not against AI use. Articles do not report partner-level consequences.

## 8. Mapping to steps

Thresholds below are working heuristics proposed by the analyst from the sources; none is a published standard unless a source is named.

### Onboard
- **Checklist:** Confirm governance form (partnership, hybrid, corporate), who decides investment, and who the user can actually bring along (JOTWELL-EMPSON-2013). Ask what has been tried before.
- **Template fields:** Firm type; decision forum; sponsor and their standing with peers; prior initiatives and verdicts; named partners the user does not want pushed.
- **Decision rule:** If the sponsor has no standing with practice heads, add an influence-building step before any partner briefing.

### Frame
- **Checklist:** State what "success" means in measures the partners use (profit per partner, realization, client retention) and in AI measures (usage, time, quality). Note that only 18% know of ROI tracking (TRI-AIPS-2026).
- **Template fields:** Partner metric; baseline; target; measurement owner; client pressure (instructions both ways); leader baseline use.
- **Decision rule:** No partner briefing without a baseline for at least one workflow.

### Map (stakeholder mapping)
- **Checklist:** Leadership constellation (senior pair, practice heads, business-services heads, key influencers) (JOTWELL-EMPSON-2013); personas by practice (LAW360-2025); partner segments (rainmaker, practice head, near-retirement, leverage-dependent).
- **Template fields (stakeholder row):** Name or role; formal power; informal influence; economic exposure (hours, rate, leverage); stated stance; stance basis (evidence, identity, incentive, time); ADKAR gap (PROSCI-ADKAR); who they listen to; ask of them.
- **Decision rule:** Recruit champions among vocal, trusted people, not only the most technical (LAW360-2025). Treat unknown stance as unmapped, not neutral.

### Diagnose
- **Checklist:** Last month's actual volume, hours, rework per workflow; client AI instructions; shadow use (MIT-NANDA-2025, BCG-ATWORK-2025); leader comfort (ICAEW-CAW-2025); error risk and who signs off (ARXIV-MAGESH-2024, PEOPLEMATTERS-2025); supervisory duties (LAWNEXT-2024, VINCI-2026).
- **Template fields:** Workflow; hours per matter; fee basis; realization or write-off history; AI use today (sanctioned and not); verification step; accountable person.
- **Decision rule:** If the fee basis is hourly and the task is highly routine, run the four-variable economics (hours, rate, write-offs, expenses) before proposing it (LF-PERLIN-2025).

### Reimagine
- **Checklist:** For each lens (improve, automate, delegate, reinvent) ask what happens to partner economics and to the junior pipeline. Ask which outputs could be priced apart from hours (AO-AGENT-2025, CPAPA-PWC-2026).
- **Template fields:** Lens; step changed; hours effect; rate or price effect; staffing effect; training effect; client communication.
- **Decision rule:** Flag any idea that removes junior work without a replacement learning route (opinion support only: BGOV-ODOWD-2026).

### Prioritize
- **Checklist:** Visible win in one quarter; partner exposure; dependency on partner behavior versus staff behavior; verification burden.
- **Template fields:** Candidate; named win; owner partner; exposed partners; evidence quality of savings (measured, self-reported, vendor).
- **Decision rule:** Prefer candidates whose win a named partner can describe in client terms. Down-rank candidates whose benefit rests only on self-reported time saved (PAPERJAM-2025, LEXIS-CONSULT-2025).

### Make the case (partner briefing)
- **Checklist (briefing structure, analyst-proposed):** (1) the client and market facts (TRI-AIPS-2026 instructions; BLOOMBERG-ACC-2025 client expectations); (2) a workflow from this firm with a baseline and the honest result; (3) the economics under three scenarios; (4) the risks and the control (ARXIV-MAGESH-2024, PEOPLEMATTERS-2025); (5) the ask of partners and what it costs in their time; (6) the expectation and accountability model (IAB-KPMG-2025, LAWNEXT-2024); (7) the plan for roles stated in the firm's own terms (LEGALCHEEK-BAKER-2026).
- **Template fields:** Audience segment; their likely objection; the honest answer; the evidence; the ask; the decision date.
- **Decision rule:** Each objection must be answered with evidence or an explicit test, never with a slogan. If the objection is about incentives, change the incentive or say it will not change (POLSINELLI-2026 as an example of saying it will not).

### Plan (leader expectations and accountability)
- **Checklist:** Written leader expectations; usage tracking versus policing stated (IAB-KPMG-2025); training rule and credit decision (POLSINELLI-2026, LAWNEXT-2024); steering rhythm and membership (CIPD-2026); champions network (CIPD-2026, LAW360-2025); wins communication (KOTTER); role communication (LEGALCHEEK-BAKER-2026).
- **Template fields:** Expectation; owner; measure; review forum and date; support offered; consequence stated.
- **Decision rule:** Only set consequences the firm will actually apply. A working training floor to start from: BCG found regular use sharply higher at five or more hours of training (BCG-ATWORK-2025; cross-industry, not a partner standard).

### Roll-up (unit to organization)
- **Checklist:** Merge unit objection lists; find shared champions; check for competing demands on the same partners.
- **Decision rule:** If three or more units list the same objection, resolve it at organization level (incentive, policy or client position) rather than unit by unit. (Working heuristic, analyst-proposed.)

### ai-enablement: partners and skeptics
- **Checklist:** Segment partners; pair skeptics with respected peers for repeated hands-on sessions [inference from GAWANDE-2013]; give a safe place for basic questions (LEXIS-CONSULT-2025); concede documented failure modes and show controls (ARXIV-MAGESH-2024); never promise outcomes you do not control.
- **Template fields:** Objection; type (risk, time, identity, incentive, trust); test; owner; result.

## 10. Gaps

- **No measured partner-level adoption data.** All adoption figures are for firms, professions or cross-industry samples, or are executives' verbal statements. I found no survey that isolates partners.
- **No outcome evidence for accountability mandates.** PwC's reported stance, KPMG's performance-review change and Polsinelli's no-credit rule are described, with no data on effect.
- **Partner compensation formulas and any AI credit are not public.** Searches found Polsinelli's refusal of billing credit and no examples of firms crediting AI contribution in partner pay. Big Four promotion and pay figures are from trade press with no named data origin.
- **Primary documents I could not read:** SSRN papers (Perlin, Choi et al. full text, Dell'Acqua full text), ABA Formal Opinion 512, the SRA warning notice, McKinsey's State of AI, the Financial Times PwC interview, the Harvard Law Center on the Legal Profession piece on law-firm business models (known only through a blog pointer), Empson's book and 2013 report, Thomson Reuters Institute 2025 reports (URL guesses failed).
- **Foundations I could only reach through summaries or not at all:** Maister, Susskind, Heifetz (the one fetched page returned an error), Garicano-style leverage models. I did not rely on them.
- **Champion-network evidence is qualitative and self-reported** (CIPD-2026 is an anonymized case; Law360 accounts are the officers' own). No comparison group exists.
- **Time-saved figures** are lawyers' or consultants' own reports (PAPERJAM-2025, LEXIS-CONSULT-2025); I found no independently measured time savings in partner-led firms in these sources. The randomized student studies measure speed under test conditions, not practice.
- **Law Society page is undated;** the SRA material is a training vendor's summary.
- **MIT NANDA's 95% figure** rests on a small, non-random sample, and the report says its numbers are directional; do not use it as a prevalence estimate.
- **The 95%/41% example in BGOV-ODOWD-2026** (staff versus leadership AI use) is posed as a hypothetical in the article, so I did not use it as evidence.
- **Source types missing:** conference sessions from the last 12 to 18 months (ILTACON and similar) beyond what Law360 summarizes, and unions or staff-side views on role communication.
