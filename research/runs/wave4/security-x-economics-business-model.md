# Security x economics and business model: how security is funded and sold, and what AI does to it

Archetype: security (in-house CISO organizations and security service providers). Topic: economics-business-model. Compiled 2026-10-17. Sources were retrieved with `curl` (some through the `r.jina.ai` text mirror) and `pdftotext`; 41 distinct sources were read, plus 5 search-result-only items that are labelled as such. Refs follow `PUBLISHER-SHORT-YYYY` and reuse the landscape's refs where one exists (`IBM-CODB-2026`, `SANS-SOC-2026`, `ISC2-WORKFORCE-2025`, `FORRESTER-RSAC-2026`, `ARCTICWOLF-AURORA-2026`, `ARXIV-2610.10608`). Every figure is stated with what it measures and who measured it. Vendor-authored figures are self-reported and flagged `[vendor]`.

---

## 1. Summary

- **Security budgets are flat in aggregate while AI money moves around security, not into it.** IANS and Artico Search's survey of more than 500 security executives found average security budget growth of 5% in 2026 (4% in 2025), with 45% of organizations flat and 10% cut; security's share of IT spend is back to 9.9%, the same as 2021. At 38% of organizations AI security spending is funded outside the security budget. (`IANS-BUDGET-2026`) Broad surveys that ask about intent look rosier: 78% of 3,887 executives in PwC's survey expect budgets to rise. (`PWC-DTI-2026`) The gap is a sampling and wording difference, not a contradiction to resolve.
- **Spending forecasts and vendor results show a market still growing faster than buyers' own budgets.** Gartner's forecast (search-result only) has worldwide end-user information security spending at $244B in 2026. (`GARTNER-FORECAST-2026`) The large platforms report fast growth: Palo Alto's next-generation security ARR was $9.10B, up 63%, and CrowdStrike's ARR was $5.84B, up 25%. (`PANW-FY26Q4-2026`, `CRWD-FY27Q2-2026`) Both are vendor-published.
- **Consolidation is real, but it is mostly capital and bundling, not proof of buyer savings.** Disclosed M&A counts are at record pace (Momentum Cyber via an advisory firm: 219 deals in H1 2026). Acquirers include platforms, a services giant (Accenture) and private equity (Thoma Bravo's Proofpoint buying Varonis for about $6.3B, announced 2026-10-15). Forrester warns that "consolidation doesn't eliminate tools, skills, or complexity" and that post-deal pricing and packaging are unsettled. (`WINDSORDRAKE-MA-2026`, `ACCENTURE-DRAGOS-2026`, `PROOFPOINT-VARONIS-2026`, `FORRESTER-RSAC-2026`, `FORRESTER-PV-2026`)
- **Managed security is priced on units of coverage (endpoint, identity, data source, user licence), and AI is introducing two new units: investigations and compute.** Published list prices exist for small-business MDR (Huntress, CrowdStrike Falcon); enterprise MDR is mostly quote-only. AI SOC vendors price by investigation capacity (Dropzone) or compute units (Microsoft Security Copilot SCUs, 400 per 1,000 E5/E7 licences included). Arctic Wolf sells "unlimited investigations" at "predictable pricing" and criticises metered AI pricing. (`HUNTRESS-PRICING-2026`, `CROWDSTRIKE-PRICING-2026`, `DROPZONE-PRICING-2026`, `MSLEARN-SCU-2026`, `ARCTICWOLF-AURORA-2026`)
- **The pricing-pressure question is open and contested.** Per a vendor's summary of a Gartner note, MDR providers will keep AI efficiency gains rather than lower prices, and buyers may insource. AI SOC vendors claim providers can reach 70-85% gross margins against 25-40% for headcount-based models (vendor claim, no method published). Public-market investors briefly priced in disruption: CrowdStrike fell 11.6% and Zscaler 11.3% on the day Claude Code Security launched (2026-02-20, press report). (`INTEZER-GARTNERMDR-2026`, `D3-AGENTICMDR-2026`, `YAHOO-CCS-2026`)
- **Cyber insurance is in a soft market and is not yet an AI-driven profit story.** Marsh's index shows cyber rates down 4% globally in Q2 2026, the twelfth straight quarterly fall; Swiss Re projects $16.4B global premium for 2026; the NAIC recorded the first-ever fall in US direct written premium (data year 2024). New AI exclusions found so far are in general liability and management liability forms, not cyber forms. (`MARSH-GIMI-2026`, `SWISSRE-CYBER-2026`, `NAIC-CYBER-2025`, `INSJOURNAL-AIEXCL-2026`)
- **Independent evidence that AI changes cost is thin and cuts both ways.** IBM/Ponemon: breaches at organizations making extensive use of security AI and automation averaged $4.00M against $5.93M with none, with 36% of organizations in the extensive group. An academic benchmark found every tested LLM triage approach missed at least 40.4% of attack-related alerts. Vendors' own cost claims (for example "12 times more cost-effective") have no published method. (`IBM-CODB-2026`, `ARXIV-2610.10608`, `ARCTICWOLF-AURORA-2026`)

## 2. Findings

### 2.1 How much security spends: budgets, share of IT, share of revenue

**Market-level forecasts (Gartner, search-result only).**
- Gartner projected worldwide end-user spending on information security at $213B in 2025, up from $193B in 2024, and estimated $240B for 2026 in a press release dated 2025-07-29. (`GARTNER-FORECAST-2026`, press-release snippet in search results; page returned 403)
- A later Gartner forecast document (4Q25 edition) puts 2026 at $244B, with 11.6% constant-currency growth. (`GARTNER-FORECAST-2026`, document-listing snippet, doc 7291530; not opened)
- Forecasts are Gartner's, not measurements. Secondary sites quote other 2026 figures ($247.5B, $248.9B) for later quarterly updates; none could be verified against Gartner itself, so they are not used.

**What organizations actually report (IANS and Artico Search, 2026, more than 500 security executives; seventh year of the study).**
- Average security budget growth was 5% in 2026 and 4% in 2025. In 2026, 45% of organizations reported flat budgets and 10% a decrease. Average growth peaked at 17% in 2022. (`IANS-BUDGET-2026`, press release 2026-09-15, paras 1-2; landing-page FAQ citing report pp. 3-4)
- The landing page headline says median growth was 0% for the second straight year, and that 55% of security leaders saw flat or shrinking budgets while 45% won an increase, the lowest share since 2021. (`IANS-BUDGET-2026`, landing-page header and stats)
- Security's share of IT spend is 9.9%, the same as 2021, because AI-driven IT budgets grew faster than security's. (`IANS-BUDGET-2026`, landing-page FAQ citing pp. 5-6, 13-14)
- Venture- and private-equity-backed companies and well-funded public firms continued to grow security budgets aggressively, so the average hides a split. (`IANS-BUDGET-2026`, press release para 2)
- Software, including AI tooling, is 35% of the average security budget, two points behind staff and compensation, the closest gap on record; AI security tooling alone is 3% of the average budget. (`IANS-BUDGET-2026`, press release key findings)
- Drivers of budget increases among CISOs with growing budgets: business or operational risk (48%), board focus (largest average increase, 23%), new regulation (22%). Only 3% cited a major industry breach, and that driver produced the smallest average increase (9%). Of cuts, 78% trace to enterprise-wide cost cutting. (`IANS-BUDGET-2026`, press release; landing-page FAQ citing p. 12)
- 64% of CISOs expect an increase in 2027 and 8% a cut; 64% asked for an increase in 2026 but 45% received one. (`IANS-BUDGET-2026`, press release; landing page)

**Other surveys, measured differently.**
- PwC's 2026 Global Digital Trust Insights (3,887 business and technology executives in 72 countries): 78% say their cyber budget will increase over the next year, and 32% of those expect a 6-10% rise. AI (36%) is the top budget priority, ahead of cloud security (34%). (`PWC-DTI-2026`, Thailand press release 2025-11-14, "AI emerges as top-of-mind" section) This measures expected direction among executives, not realized growth.
- Wiz's 2026 CISO Budget Benchmark (300+ security leaders; Wiz is a vendor, now part of Google): 85% of organizations increased budgets in the year, and nearly nine in ten expect to grow them again in 2026. People are the largest line at about one quarter of spend. (`WIZ-CISOBUDGET-2026`, "Bigger Budgets, Smaller Confidence" and "Cloud and People" facts; page undated) Compare with IANS's 45%: the samples differ, and one is vendor-run.
- ISC2 (16,029 individuals responsible for cybersecurity, surveyed July-August 2025): organizations reporting budget cuts were 30% in 2023, 37% in 2024 and 36% in 2025; hiring freezes 39%; layoffs 24%. Enterprises of 10,000+ staff reported layoffs at 32% against 17-18% for small and mid-sized organizations. (`ISC2-WORKFORCE-2025`, "Economic pressure" section and Methodology)
- Forrester's RSAC 2026 recap (2026-03-31) says budgets "were coming under scrutiny as companies worry about the global economy and their spending on AI." (`FORRESTER-RSAC-2026`, "Topics not getting the attention they deserve")

**Share of revenue.** No current, openly readable benchmark was found. A Deloitte-sponsored item in the Wall Street Journal (search-result snippet only, page blocked) said financial institutions' cybersecurity spending fell as a share of revenue in banking, capital markets and insurance from 0.72% in 2021 to 0.54% in 2023, and rose in investment management from 0.40% to 0.49%. (`DELOITTE-FSISAC-2024`, search-only). Several SEO "benchmark" sites publish blended percentages that cite no primary data and are not used here.

**AI and the budget line.**
- 69% of CISOs name AI for security the top priority for net-new dollars; 76% of organizations have no distinct AI budget line; AI funding rose at about 70% of organizations that track it as a line item against 42% where it is embedded in the security budget and 31% where it is funded in IT, data or innovation budgets. (`IANS-BUDGET-2026`, press release key findings; landing-page FAQ citing pp. 14, 23)
- 91% of CISOs expect AI to make security teams more productive in the next 12 months, 81% expect new roles and skills demand, and 69% do not expect AI to reduce existing headcount. (`IANS-BUDGET-2026`, press release para 3) These are expectations.
- The IBM/Ponemon follow-on study (May 2026; 456 of the 602 breached organizations responded) found 85% of the breached organizations aware of frontier-AI cyber capabilities planning to increase security spending, against 64% that planned an increase after experiencing their own breach. (`IBM-CODB-2026`, press release 2026-07-29, "Organizations are starting to act on future risk"; PDF "Security investments" section) This is planned spend, self-reported.

### 2.2 What it costs: SOC cost structure and the in-house versus provider choice

- **Labour dominates.** In IANS's data staff and compensation is the largest budget category (about 37%, since software at 35% is "two points behind"). (`IANS-BUDGET-2026`, press release key findings) Wiz's survey puts people at about one quarter of spend. (`WIZ-CISOBUDGET-2026`, "Cloud and People Dominate the Spend") The two use different category definitions.
- **24x7 coverage multiplies headcount.** Illustrative arithmetic by this analyst, not from a source: 8,760 hours in a year divided by roughly 2,080 paid hours per full-time employee is about 4.2 people per continuously staffed seat before leave and training. An AI SOC vendor, D3 Security, estimates that covering one 2,000-alert-per-day account at one-third staffing takes 50-60 analyst FTEs, about $6-7.2M a year at a $120,000 fully loaded cost. This is a vendor estimate. (`D3-AGENTICMDR-2026`, "Run the P&L both ways")
- **Staffing and funding gaps are the main barriers to SOC AI.** SANS's 10th annual SOC report (444 practitioners and 69 leaders, per search result; sponsors include Dropzone AI, Elastic, Fortinet, Google, Palo Alto, Swimlane and Wiz) states that staffing and funding gaps remain the biggest barriers, and that most organizations use AI without governance, validation or a defined workflow. (`SANS-SOC-2026`, report landing page summary and sponsor list). The landscape file records the headline numbers (79% use AI/ML tools, 36% embed them in a defined workflow); those were not re-verified here.
- **Cost of failure sets the value ceiling.** IBM/Ponemon's 2026 study of 602 organizations (breaches March 2025 to February 2026) reports a global average breach cost of $4.99M, up 12%. Breaches at organizations that used security AI and automation extensively averaged $4.00M against $5.93M for those that did not, a $1.93M difference, and took 215 days to identify and contain against 280. Extensive use was 36% of organizations (32% the year before). (`IBM-CODB-2026`, PDF "Security AI and automation" section, Figures 34-35) This is a correlation across breached organizations, not a controlled test. Ponemon conducted it and IBM sponsored and analysed it.
- **AI-enabled attacks raise defender costs.** The same study says one in four malicious breaches were AI-enabled (up 56%) and averaged about $6M. (`IBM-CODB-2026`, press release lead paragraph)
- **Labour market.** Forrester reports that staffing shortages "have been eclipsed by a shortage of open positions", with vendors still hiring early-career practitioners "in fewer numbers" (`FORRESTER-RSAC-2026`, "Staffing shortages" bullet). ISC2 says AI is the top skills need and roles such as SOC analyst are "evolving" (`ISC2-WORKFORCE-2025`, summary section). Hard data on junior hiring was not found.

### 2.3 How security is sold: MSSP and MDR pricing models

**Published list prices (vendor-published, fetched 2026-10-17).**
- Huntress: Managed EDR with a 24/7 SOC has an MSRP of $8.99 per endpoint per month and an example price of $7.99 at 100 endpoints; Managed ITDR $4.80 per licensed identity per month ($3.60 at 100); Managed SIEM $4.00 per data source per month ($3.50 at 100), billed per source with pooled storage and "no overages"; Managed security awareness training $2.08 per learner ($1.75 at 100). (`HUNTRESS-PRICING-2026`, product tiles)
- CrowdStrike publishes self-serve Falcon prices: Go, Pro and Enterprise at $7.99, $14.99 and $19.99 per device billed monthly (at 100 units, falling with volume), or $59.99, $99.99 and $184.99 per device billed annually. The fully managed "Complete" tier shows "Contact sales". (`CROWDSTRIKE-PRICING-2026`, pricing table)
- Secondary price guides cite per-endpoint ranges for enterprise MDR (for example $119-$440+ per endpoint per year, or $72K-$144K a year for 500 endpoints at Expel). These come from vendor-adjacent blogs with unclear derivation (search-result summaries only) and are not used as findings.

**Units of pricing in the market.**
- Per endpoint, per identity and per data source are the units in published managed-service prices. (`HUNTRESS-PRICING-2026`)
- Per investigation capacity: Dropzone AI sells "up to 4,000 full investigations per year per AI analyst", which the vendor says matches the average output of a human tier-1 analyst; the fetched page shows no list price, offers enterprise and MSSP tiers by quote, and the MSSP tier pools AI analysts across customers. (`DROPZONE-PRICING-2026`, pricing page)
- Per compute unit: Microsoft Security Copilot is metered in Security Compute Units. Provisioned capacity is billed hourly (minimum one SCU) with optional overage, and eligible Microsoft 365 E5 and E7 customers receive 400 SCUs a month per 1,000 paid licences, up to 10,000, at no additional cost. Rollout began 2025-11-18 for existing Security Copilot customers on E5 and is phased for others. (`MSLEARN-SCU-2026`, "capacity" and "inclusion" articles) Inclusion in a licence bundle is a pricing move that pressures standalone AI SOC tools.
- Flat or unlimited: Arctic Wolf (2026-08-03) says "AI security increasingly comes with metered, consumption-based pricing" which can "climb unpredictably", and that its Aurora customers get "predictable pricing, unlimited data ingestion, and unlimited investigations". It also claims the platform is "roughly 12 times more cost-effective than building and maintaining an agentic SOC internally", with no method given. (`ARCTICWOLF-AURORA-2026`, section "Making Enterprise-Scale, AI-Native Security Operations Accessible and Predictable") `[vendor]`

**Outcome-based pricing: warranties are the main form found.**
- Arctic Wolf's cyber resilience bundle (2026-08-03) includes eligibility for up to $3M under its Security Operations Warranty, subject to terms, conditions and coverage requirements; the company says it complements cyber insurance. (`MSSPALERT-AW-2026`, warranty section)
- Other MDR warranties (Rapid7 up to $1M; ESET via Cysurance $500K or $1M) were seen only in search results, and a vendor blog argues most are marketing instruments. (`MDR-WARRANTIES-2026`, search-only) No source found describes a contract where the fee itself varies with a measured security outcome.
- Arctic Wolf also introduced a metric, "Mean Time to Trusted Action", defined by the vendor. (`MSSPALERT-AW-2026`) Gartner is reported (via Intezer) to advise buyers to refactor service metrics to outcomes rather than volume of AI-processed alerts. (`INTEZER-GARTNERMDR-2026`)

**Vendor-claimed provider economics.**
- D3 Security says headcount-bound MDR/MSSP gross margins are typically 25-40% and that AI-autonomous operation can reach 70-85% at scale, and asserts analyst tenure at providers under 18 months and annual turnover above 30%. These are claims by an AI SOC vendor selling to providers. (`D3-AGENTICMDR-2026`, opening and "Run the P&L both ways")
- Arctic Wolf says its agents autonomously resolve more than 60% of case volume as high-confidence closures and that about one-third of complex cases receive final human verification. (`ARCTICWOLF-AURORA-2026`, "Closing the Trust Gap") Self-reported; no external audit.
- Valuation evidence for provider businesses (M&A advisor synthesis, ranges "appear" rather than are measured): MDR/XDR specialists at platform scale about 12-16x adjusted EBITDA or 2-4x recurring revenue, lower-middle-market MSSPs 8-11x, with MDR commanding roughly 2-3 turns over monitoring-only peers; Sophos bought Secureworks for $859M in cash (announced October 2024, closed 2025-02-03), about 2.3x trailing revenue. (`CTACQ-MSSPMULT-2026`, Executive Summary)

### 2.4 Consolidation of security platforms

- **Buyer sentiment.** PwC's survey: 47% of executives list cyber tool consolidation among their responses to talent shortages, alongside AI/ML tools (53%) and automation (48%). (`PWC-DTI-2026`, "Skills gaps" section) Wiz's survey: 58% of organizations run more than 25 security tools, and larger enterprises often 50 or more. (`WIZ-CISOBUDGET-2026`, Fact 3; vendor-run)
- **Vendor results.** CrowdStrike: ending ARR from accounts using its Falcon Flex licensing exceeded $2.29B, up 101% year over year; 51% of subscription customers use six or more modules (35% seven or more, 26% eight or more) as of 2026-07-31; Q2 FY27 revenue $1.47B, ARR $5.84B up 25%. (`CRWD-FY27Q2-2026`, headline bullets and "Recent Highlights") Palo Alto Networks: Q4 FY26 revenue $3.41B (+34%), next-generation security ARR $9.10B (+63%), a $20B FY30 ARR target, and a GAAP net loss of $282M for the quarter against net income of $254M a year earlier, alongside non-GAAP net income of $853M. (`PANW-FY26Q4-2026`, highlights and CEO quote) Both are company-published and include non-GAAP measures.
- **Deal flow.** An advisory firm summarizing Momentum Cyber reports 219 cybersecurity M&A deals in H1 2026 with $9.1B in disclosed value excluding previously announced megadeals, "the fastest half on record"; Google closed its $32B Wiz purchase in March 2026 and Palo Alto completed $25B for CyberArk in February. (`WINDSORDRAKE-MA-2026`, Executive Summary; an M&A intermediary, secondary) Full-year 2025 totals differ by source: more than $84B (Windsor Drake) and 398 transactions and $102B (Momentum Cyber via SiliconANGLE, as quoted by CT Acquisitions). (`WINDSORDRAKE-MA-2026`, `CTACQ-MSSPMULT-2026`) Treat tallies as provider-specific.
- **Consolidation of the AI SOC startup layer.** Cribl acquired technology assets of Radiant Security's AI SOC product on 2026-08-19 to run on its telemetry platform; terms were not in the release. (`CRIBL-RADIANT-2026`) LevelBlue, which calls itself the largest pure-play managed security services provider, completed its acquisition of Cybereason on 2025-11-25. (`LEVELBLUE-CYBEREASON-2025`)
- **Services firms moving into software.** Accenture announced on 2026-06-18 a majority stake in Dragos and 100% of runZero and NetRise at a combined enterprise value of about $4.175B; the three together had about $208M ARR as of June 2026, up 53%; closings ran from 2026-07-31 to 2026-09-17. Accenture says its cybersecurity business had $10B of revenue in fiscal 2025, up from $700M in 2016. (`ACCENTURE-DRAGOS-2026`, opening paragraphs and "Transaction Builds on...") Self-reported.
- **Analyst caution.** Forrester told RSAC attendees to buy the platform narrative only if a vendor shows "more than a bundled suite" (unified UI, shared data model, real integrations), and warned that ignoring long-term cost, roadmap alignment and exit risk makes platform decisions "harder and more expensive over time". (`FORRESTER-RSAC-2026`, "Attendees worry about the need for multiple security platforms") On the Proofpoint-Varonis deal Forrester advises favouring shorter terms or contractual protection in multiyear renewals and notes that stable operation before close "says nothing about the product decisions and subsequent pricing and packaging that will follow." (`FORRESTER-PV-2026`, final recommendations)

### 2.5 Private-equity ownership of providers

- **Thoma Bravo and Proofpoint.** Proofpoint, "a portfolio company of Thoma Bravo", agreed on 2026-10-15 to buy Varonis in an all-cash deal valued at about $6.3B ($49.75 a share): a 52% premium to the close on 2026-06-22, before sale rumours, and 17% over the close on 2026-09-01; 8.4x last-twelve-months revenue and 53x last-twelve-months free cash flow. Close is expected in the first half of 2027, subject to shareholder and regulatory approval. Thoma Bravo provides committed equity and has debt financing commitments; Ontario Teachers' Pension Plan will invest in Proofpoint. (`PROOFPOINT-VARONIS-2026`, paras 1-3, 5 and footnote 1) Announced, not completed. Varonis's boilerplate says its platform is backed by 24x7 managed detection and response.
- **Serial acquirer.** Forrester lists Proofpoint's acquisitions as Tessian, Normalyze, Dathena, Hornetsecurity, Acuvity and now Varonis, calls its integration record "uneven", and notes that a recent email consolidation shows what sustained integration work can deliver. (`FORRESTER-PV-2026`, "Judge By What Proofpoint Ships")
- **PE-backed budgets.** IANS observes that venture- and PE-backed companies continue to grow security budgets aggressively, so PE-owned customers may behave differently from the average. (`IANS-BUDGET-2026`, press release para 2)
- **Provider ownership examples.** Globes (via search result) says LevelBlue, spun out of AT&T, is controlled by WillJam Ventures with about $1B in annual revenue per PitchBook (secondary; not verified at source). LevelBlue's own release names SoftBank Corp., SoftBank Vision Fund 2 and Liberty Strategic Capital as investors on completion of the Cybereason deal. (`LEVELBLUE-CYBEREASON-2025`) A Bloomberg-sourced search summary says KKR-backed Optiv (a security services and reseller firm) carries about $1B of debt, more than half due in 2026, with confidential talks with second-lien lenders in November 2025 and an S&P CCC rating (`OPTIV-DEBT-2025`, search-only, unverified). The possible limit to leverage-funded services roll-ups is worth probing, but this one item is not enough to generalize.
- **Valuation context.** Advisors report PE and strategic buyers paying services-and-MDR multiples in the ranges in 2.3. A newsletter author notes "so many of the cyber companies are PE-owned" (`VENTUREINSEC-MA-2026`, opinion). No source gave a count of PE-owned security providers; that count is a gap.

### 2.6 Cyber insurance economics

- **Rates.** Marsh's Global Insurance Market Index (2026-07-23): cyber insurance rates fell 4% globally in Q2 2026, the twelfth consecutive quarterly decline, after a 5% decline in Q1; the largest cyber decline was in India, Middle East and Africa (14%), then Latin America and Caribbean (10%), and the US was 2%. Insurers are competing on broader coverage, expanded terms and lower deductibles. (`MARSH-GIMI-2026`, "Other findings" and the Donnelly quote) Swiss Re puts the global rate change at about -13% in 2025 and -5% in 2026, the fourth consecutive year of decline, with pricing stabilizing in the US and Europe still discounting. (`SWISSRE-CYBER-2026`, "Rate declines ongoing") Howden's headline (search-only) says global cyber rates are down 27% since mid-2022. (`HOWDEN-RATES-2026`, search-only)
- **Premium.** Swiss Re projects $16.4B of global premium in 2026 and $17.1B in 2027, premium growth of 5% a year since 2022, North America about $10.7B (two thirds) and Europe $3.42B. Micro-SME and SME penetration is only 5-10% and 10-20%; mid-market 40-50%; large corporates 60-70%. (`SWISSRE-CYBER-2026`, premium and penetration sections) Munich Re estimates the market at nearly $15B in 2025 and expects about $28B by 2030 (a forecast), noting that growth has slowed over three years. (`MUNICHRE-SURVEY-2026`, survey summary) The NAIC reports about $15B globally for 2024 (+7%) but the first-ever reduction in US direct written premium, about $9.14B, down 7% from $9.84B (including alien surplus lines). (`NAIC-CYBER-2025`, executive summary, published 2025-11-10) The estimates differ by method and year; none is a measurement of the same quantity.
- **Loss experience.** Coalition (own claims book, full-year 2025, published 2026-03-05): initial ransom demands up 47%; global claims frequency up 3% and severity down 19% to an average loss of $116,000; ransomware the costliest claim type at an average $269,000; business email compromise and funds transfer fraud 58% of incidents; and 64% of closed claims resolved with no out-of-pocket loss for the policyholder. (`COALITION-CLAIMS-2026`, bullets) Munich Re's claims data shows first-party claims at 62% of managed claims, driven by business interruption, privacy liability and incident response, with most claims affecting micro-companies and SMEs. (`MUNICHRE-TRENDS-2026`, "Cyber claims insights")
- **Insurance as a security vendor.** Coalition describes "Active Insurance" that combines the policy with prevention and response services, which blurs the line between insurer and security provider. (`COALITION-CLAIMS-2026`, last bullet and boilerplate)
- **AI in coverage.** ISO (Verisk) generative-AI exclusion endorsements for commercial general liability (CG 40 47, CG 40 48, CG 35 08) took effect in January 2026, and Berkley introduced an absolute AI exclusion for D&O, E&O and fiduciary products in 2025. A Lathrop GPM attorney expects "rapid adoption of some version of an AI exclusion in most lines", while a Verisk executive said he did not know how many insurers have adopted them. (`INSJOURNAL-AIEXCL-2026`, 2026-08-17) These are not cyber policies. Swiss Re's view (search summary, page read for rates only) is that AI mostly amplifies known cyber risks and AI claims are still limited. (`SWISSRE-CYBER-2026`)
- **Gap in evidence.** None of the insurance sources read ties premium discounts to a specific security service such as MDR, or to AI use by the insured.

### 2.7 Pricing pressure from AI on security vendors and providers

- **Provider-side view (Gartner note via Intezer, an AI SOC vendor).** Gartner's research note "The Impact of AI on MDR Services" reportedly says cost savings leadership expects from AI in the SOC "will largely go unrealized" because MDR providers will absorb efficiency gains, that more organizations will consider insourcing MDR as AI tools mature, and that AI SOC agents are "increasing the security team's internal competition for traditional MDR services". For some uses, such as after-hours coverage with no remediation, it suggests an AI tool may suffice. (`INTEZER-GARTNERMDR-2026`, "What Gartner is really saying") Secondhand; Gartner's text was paywalled. A Help Net Security headline (search-only, 2026-09-09) reports another Gartner prediction that by 2028, 70% of large SOCs will pilot AI agents but only 15% will see measurable results without structured evaluation. (`HELPNET-GARTNER-2026`, search-only)
- **Vendor-side view.** Forrester says most SecOps vendors now offer an "agentic SOC", with differences hard to see "at an architectural level (what models are being used, how cost is optimized, etc.)", and recommends prioritizing utility, trust and cost. (`FORRESTER-RSAC-2026`, "The agentic SOC is ascendant")
- **Public markets.** On 2026-02-20, when Anthropic launched Claude Code Security, CrowdStrike fell 11.6%, Zscaler 11.3% and Palo Alto 3.2%, per Yahoo Finance; Wedbush called it an overreaction (search-result summary adds a JP Morgan "indiscriminate" comment and a 4.9% fall in a cybersecurity ETF per Bloomberg, both unread). (`YAHOO-CCS-2026`) CrowdStrike's CEO later described a "Mythos moment" that produced "mass-market acceptance that AI adoption needs security", and Palo Alto's CEO said recent AI advances are "elevating cybersecurity to the top of the CIO priority list". (`CRWD-FY27Q2-2026`, `PANW-FY26Q4-2026`; executive quotes in vendor releases) The two reactions run in opposite directions: fear of substitution, then vendor claims of demand.
- **Bundling.** Microsoft includes Security Copilot capacity in E5 and E7 licences. (`MSLEARN-SCU-2026`) Cribl adds an AI SOC to its data platform. (`CRIBL-RADIANT-2026`) Both turn a standalone product into a feature, a standard route to price compression, although no source measures the effect.
- **Quality limits the substitution case.** An arXiv preprint (2026-10-07) found that across 1,247 alerts from a multi-stage attack scenario, each of five LLM triage approaches missed at least 40.4% of attack-related alerts; the authors' own multi-agent design cut the false-negative rate to 3.1% while escalating 18.4% of alerts to analysts. (`ARXIV-2610.10608`, abstract) Preprint, authors' own benchmark. Arctic Wolf cites SANS's survey that nearly two-thirds of security professionals received AI guidance later found incorrect. (`ARCTICWOLF-AURORA-2026`, "Closing the Trust Gap")
- **Labour effect expectations.** IANS: 69% of CISOs do not expect AI to reduce existing headcount, and an IANS partner says AI in workflows "has not led to massive layoffs, but it has led to the repurposing of team members". (`IANS-BUDGET-2026`, press release)

## 3. What it means for an AI transformation conversation

### 3.1 Implications

1. **Funding is the first constraint, and AI funding often sits outside the CISO's control.** With budgets nearly flat and 38% of AI security spend funded elsewhere, a CISO who treats AI as a security-budget item will compete with other priorities, while one who ties it to the business's AI programme may reach more money. (`IANS-BUDGET-2026`)
2. **Make AI spend visible.** Organizations with a distinct AI budget line report increases about 70% of the time against 42% when embedded and 31% when funded elsewhere. This is correlation (IANS), but it is a cheap governance step. (`IANS-BUDGET-2026`)
3. **Value cases should rest on business risk and board language, not breach headlines.** Only 3% of growing budgets cite a major breach; board attention (23%) and regulation (22%) produced the largest average increases. (`IANS-BUDGET-2026`)
4. **The make-versus-buy line is moving.** Pricing units are shifting from coverage to investigations and compute, and Gartner (via Intezer) expects insourcing pressure. A leader should map which SOC tiers are bought as a service, which as software, and which are staffed, and compare each on cost per outcome, not per endpoint. (`DROPZONE-PRICING-2026`, `MSLEARN-SCU-2026`, `INTEZER-GARTNERMDR-2026`)
5. **For providers: the margin story and the price story may not match.** If providers keep efficiency gains, buyers will push for outcome metrics and warranties; if competitors pass savings through, unit prices fall. Providers need a position on both before renewal season. (`INTEZER-GARTNERMDR-2026`, `MSSPALERT-AW-2026`)
6. **Consolidation shifts negotiating power.** Multi-module licensing is growing quickly at the platform vendors, and Forrester advises shorter terms and exit planning. (`CRWD-FY27Q2-2026`, `FORRESTER-PV-2026`)
7. **Insurance is a lever on the buyer side, not yet on the AI side.** Soft pricing gives buyers room to negotiate coverage terms, while AI exclusions are appearing in other liability lines. Review cyber, E&O and D&O wordings for AI language. (`MARSH-GIMI-2026`, `INSJOURNAL-AIEXCL-2026`)

### 3.2 Interview probes

1. "Where does the money for AI in security come from today: your budget, IT, a data or innovation budget? Do you have a separate line for it?" (`IANS-BUDGET-2026`)
2. "What do you pay for security operations today, in which units (endpoints, identities, data sources, investigations, compute), and which of those would change if an agent did the first-tier work?"
3. "At your last MDR or MSSP renewal, did the provider mention AI? Was the price, the scope or the SLA different? Did you ask for outcome metrics or a warranty?" (`INTEZER-GARTNERMDR-2026`, `MSSPALERT-AW-2026`)
4. "How many security vendors do you have, and what is your consolidation plan? What would you need to see before signing a multiyear platform deal after an acquisition?" (`WIZ-CISOBUDGET-2026`, `FORRESTER-PV-2026`)
5. (Providers) "How much of your delivery cost is analyst time per client, and what happens to margin and price if agents handle tier-1? Who in your firm owns that decision?" (`D3-AGENTICMDR-2026`)
6. "Which of your insurance policies mention AI? What do insurers ask about your security controls at renewal?" (`INSJOURNAL-AIEXCL-2026`)

### 3.3 Objections and evidence-based responses

| Objection | Response from the evidence |
|---|---|
| "AI will cut our security headcount and budget." | CISOs surveyed by IANS mostly do not expect headcount cuts (69%), expect AI to create new role demand (81%), and report AI mostly raising software spend (software 35% of budget). Expectations, not outcomes. (`IANS-BUDGET-2026`) |
| "Our MDR will pass AI savings on to us." | Per a vendor summary of a Gartner note, providers are expected to keep the gains. Ask for outcome metrics. (`INTEZER-GARTNERMDR-2026`) |
| "AI SOC is cheaper than analysts, so replace the provider." | Vendor price units exist (4,000 investigations per AI analyst per year) but no independent cost-per-investigation measurement was found; a benchmark preprint shows high miss rates for generic LLM triage. (`DROPZONE-PRICING-2026`, `ARXIV-2610.10608`) |
| "Consolidation will save us money." | Buyers report tool sprawl and consolidation intent, but Forrester says consolidation does not remove tools, skills or complexity and warns about later pricing. No source measured savings. (`WIZ-CISOBUDGET-2026`, `FORRESTER-RSAC-2026`) |
| "AI security tooling is a cost with no payoff." | Across IBM/Ponemon's breached organizations, extensive security AI and automation use coincided with $1.93M lower average breach cost; correlation, in organizations that were breached. (`IBM-CODB-2026`) |
| "Insurance will cover AI mistakes." | Newly filed exclusions target general liability and management liability, and rates are falling in cyber; whether cyber forms cover AI incidents depends on wording. Check each policy. (`INSJOURNAL-AIEXCL-2026`, `MARSH-GIMI-2026`) |
| "A warranty makes the provider accountable." | Arctic Wolf's warranty is up to $3M with terms and coverage requirements, and is described as complementing insurance. Read the terms. (`MSSPALERT-AW-2026`) |

## 6. Gaps

- **Budget as a share of revenue.** No current readable benchmark; only a 2021-2023 financial-sector snippet (search-only). Industry-by-industry IT-spend and revenue shares were not retrievable. Gartner's CISO spending and budget-share research is paywalled.
- **Gartner primary text.** Gartner's newsroom and documents returned 403; the 2026 spending forecast, the MDR note and the SOC-agent prediction are known only through snippets or vendor summaries.
- **Provider economics.** No audited MSSP or MDR margins, pricing by contract, or customer renewal pricing changes after AI. All margin and cost-per-investigation numbers found are vendor claims. Arctic Wolf, eSentire, Expel and ReliaQuest financials and ARR were not found.
- **Outcome-based contracts.** Only warranties found; no source documents fees that vary with measured outcomes. Warranty fine print was not read (Rapid7 and others blocked).
- **PE ownership.** No census of PE-owned security providers, and no data on PE hold-period effects on service quality or pricing. The Optiv debt item is search-only. Varonis's deal body at PE Hub was behind a consent wall, so the primary press release was used.
- **Insurance.** The link between security controls (including MDR, EDR or AI use) and premiums or retentions was not found in any readable source. Howden's report and the Marsh cyber-specific section beyond one bullet were not read. NAIC loss ratios for 2024 were not extracted. No AI exclusions in cyber policy forms were confirmed.
- **Independent AI-in-SOC cost evidence.** Apart from IBM/Ponemon (correlational) and one preprint, there is no independent measurement of cost per investigation, analyst hours saved or false-negative rates in production.
- **Labour data.** BLS wage data was blocked; no hard data on junior hiring.
- **Date uncertainty.** The Wiz benchmark and Intezer blog are undated in the fetched text; the Yahoo article date is inferred from search-result context; the Zscaler/Red Canary completion release was fetched but not used because its date was not captured.
- **Conflicting tallies.** M&A totals for 2025 differ between providers ($84B, $96B, $102B) and could not be reconciled; Gartner's later 2026 quarterly updates were quoted at different values by secondary sites.
- **Terminology anomaly noted in the landscape** (CISA and NIST pages showing "Super Intelligence") was not encountered in any source used here.
