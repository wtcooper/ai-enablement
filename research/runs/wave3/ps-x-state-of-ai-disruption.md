# State of AI disruption in professional services (cross-cutting)

Archetype: professional-services. Topic: AI adoption across consulting, legal and accounting firms; AI-native firms and "service as software"; private equity and outside capital; client in-sourcing; business-model scenarios for the billable hour and the leverage pyramid.

Prepared 2026-10-19. Method: `curl`, `pdftotext` and the r.jina.ai mirror for 50+ pages; 13 web searches used only to find leads, with every cited page then opened. Public sources only. Refs reuse the landscape's keys where the landscape had one; new refs follow `PUBLISHER-SHORT-YYYY`. Source-quality labels: **independent** (academic, regulator, market data), **self-reported** (firm or survey respondents describing themselves), **vendor-published** (vendor or vendor-commissioned), **press-reported** (journalist's account, not checked against a primary).

Conventions: "announced", "piloting" and "in production" are kept distinct. In-house legal or shared-service examples are tagged **[in-house]**. Practice-specific substantive work (audit procedures, tax technical work) is left out. Forecasts are labelled with who made them.

## 1. Summary

- **Adoption is near-universal in law; the maturity spread and the value gap are the story.** 94% of 508 ILTA-survey law firms are "engaged with GenAI" (up 14 points; engagement is a broad measure, and more than half issue Copilot licences only on request) (ILTA-TECHSURVEY-2026, p. 3). Only 38% of 896 architecture and engineering firms report measurable positive business impact from AI (DELTEK-CLARITY-2026, Key Findings, A&E). In a Thomson Reuters survey of 1,816 professionals, 34% use AI their organisation has not sanctioned and 35% in firms with a named AI strategy say practice does not match it (TR-FOP-2026, key findings).
- **Measured evidence of savings is thin; announced intent is abundant.** Firm representatives at Legalweek 2026 said "we're still gathering the data" when clients asked what AI saves (TRI-LEGALWEEK-2026). SPI Research's headline is that 84.8% of firms say AI saves them time, while the share saying it generates revenue is behind the paywall (SPI-AI-2026, "Part III The Leak"). Only 25% of stand-out lawyers strongly agree their firm has a plan for monetising AI use (TRI-STANDOUT-2026, section on strategy-to-practice gap).
- **The client side is the pressure point and is starting to in-source.** 78% of corporate clients say AI-enabled quality improvement is very important or essential, and 6% say most or all providers deliver it; 32% have reconsidered or plan to reconsider relationships within 12 months (TR-FOP-2026, "AI capability is now a procurement criterion"). In a vendor survey of 130+ **[in-house]** lawyers, 68% said more than 10% of work sent to law firms could be insourced using AI (JURO-INHOUSE-2026, press release). Counter-signal: Am Law 100 worked rates rose almost 10% in Q1 2026 and demand is strong (TRI-LFFI-Q1-2026).
- **The billable hour is under measured pressure but is not yet replaced.** McKinsey is reported to take about a quarter of global fees as outcome-based (press-reported via a Stanford paper and AI Weekly; the landscape's "one third" is a lower-quality secondary) (STANFORD-DEL-2026, p. 14; AIWEEKLY-MCK-2025). In HFS's 2026 buyer data on AI-enabled IT and business services, 54% of current engagements are effort/input-based and 20% outcome-based, and 56% of surveyed buyers prefer a base fee plus outcome kicker (HFS-SAS-2026, slide 13). In Clio's data hourly billing remains the most common model among firms (CLIO-BILLABLE-2026).
- **The leverage pyramid is being squeezed at the base and the support layer, not yet visibly collapsed.** Large-firm first-year hiring fell 7.5% in 2025 (NALP, via Bloomberg Law) and two-thirds of 57 surveyed large firms expect fewer first-years in 2028 than 2025 (Citi, via Bloomberg Law) (BLOOMBERGLAW-HIRING-2026). Meanwhile non-equity partner hours relative to equity partner hours are at their highest since late 2018 (TRI-LFFI-Q2-2026). Cuts announced so far are mostly in business services (Baker McKenzie, Clifford Chance, A&O Shearman) (LEGALCHEEK-BAKER-2026; CITYAM-CC-2025; LEGALCHEEK-AO-2026).
- **Outside capital is arriving through structures that avoid ownership rules, with AI as a stated use of funds.** In accounting, PE holds roughly a third of the 30 largest US firms (press-reported by a law firm), Crowe closed a KKR investment in August 2026, and Grant Thornton agreed a $5B CBIZ acquisition backed by New Mountain (AT-CROWE-2026; FOXROTHSCHILD-MSO-2026; BGOV-GTCBIZ-2026). In law, investors favour management services organisations (MSOs) over Arizona alternative business structures, while California, Illinois and Colorado moved to restrict (BLOOMBERGLAW-AZ-2026; FOXROTHSCHILD-MSO-2026).
- **"Service as software" is real as a product shape but small in scale so far.** Examples: PwC One with six automated services (CPAPA-PWC-2026), Crosby's fixed price per document law firm (CROSBY-SITE-2026), Garfield.Law's SRA authorisation for small-claims debt recovery (GLP-GARFIELD-2025), and an accounting roll-up, Crete, buying firms to equip with OpenAI-powered tools (REUTERS-CRETE-2025). Frontier labs are now moving into implementation services, which is the consulting firms' territory (DECRYPT-DEPLOYCO-2026).
- **Counter-evidence to a "collapse" narrative is substantial.** Accenture FY26 revenue rose 6% in US dollars to $74.18B and consulting revenue rose 5% (3% in local currency) (ACCENTURE-Q4FY26, FY26 revenues). Source Global Research projects technology consulting spend to grow 7.5% in 2026 (SOURCE-INSIGHTS-2026). PwC FY26 global revenue rose 5.7% (AT-PWC-2026). The Thomson Reuters Future of Professionals foreword argues AI is "more likely to accelerate demand" for professional services (TR-FOP-2026, foreword; a forecast by a vendor with an interest in the answer).

## 2. Findings

### 2.1 Adoption surveys and the maturity spread

**Law firms (broad adoption, gated deployment).**
- ILTA's 2026 Technology Survey executive summary reports that 94% of responding firms are "engaged with GenAI", up 14 points year on year, and that one in five firms reported no GenAI engagement twelve months earlier (ILTA-TECHSURVEY-2026, pp. 3-4). Sample: 508 firms (landscape figure; the summary page itself does not restate the sample). Provenance: self-reported by firm technology leaders.
- Deployments remain "deliberately gated": more than half of respondents issue Copilot licences only on request (ILTA-TECHSURVEY-2026, p. 3). Legal-specific tools such as Harvey, Legora and Relativity aiR ramp up sharply among mid-size to larger firms (ILTA-TECHSURVEY-2026, p. 3).
- Governance spread by firm size is wide: requiring specific training before use is 11% in the smallest-size band and 72% in the largest band of the policy table (ILTA-TECHSURVEY-2026, GenAI policy table, p. 5). Marketing of GenAI tools to clients ranges from 2% to 52% across the same bands (same table).
- Most GenAI-engaged firms now use generative tools for drafting, legal research, document summarisation and contract review (ILTA-TECHSURVEY-2026, p. 4).
- One survey respondent is quoted saying GenAI "will compress much of the document review, research, and drafting work that has historically powered the associate pyramid" (ILTA-TECHSURVEY-2026, p. 4). That is a respondent opinion, not a measurement.
- A Thomson Reuters Institute survey of 116 law firm leaders and 2,527 client-nominated stand-out lawyers found over three-quarters agree the firm has an AI strategy but fewer than half are confident in their practice area's ability to succeed as AI integrates (TRI-STANDOUT-2026, key takeaways). Only 36% strongly agree the firm has a clear AI strategy, 25% strongly agree there is a plan for monetising AI usage, and just over 10% strongly agree they are confident in their practice area's ability to succeed (TRI-STANDOUT-2026, strategy-to-practice section). Embedded daily-user partners are nine times more likely than minimal users to report significant efficiency and quality impact (TRI-STANDOUT-2026, partner-gap section). Published 6 Aug 2026.
- At Legalweek 2026 a Thomson Reuters speaker said firms with a GenAI strategy are 3.9 times more likely to achieve ROI, and only 22% of firms have one (TRI-LEGALWEEK-2026, closing section; a speaker's statement, source study not named on the page). The State of the US Legal Market piece restates "almost four times" from earlier Thomson Reuters research (TRI-SOTLM-2026).

**Cross-profession, including in-house.**
- Thomson Reuters' Future of Professionals 2026 (1,816 professionals in law, tax, audit, accounting, compliance, risk and trade across 62 countries; fielded March-April 2026; includes in-house and government departments) reports that 74% use AI several times a week and 44% rely on it multiple times a day (TR-FOP-2026, "AI adoption is widespread"). 34% use unsanctioned tools; 41% of AI users lack access to "fiduciary-grade" tools; among respondents with a named strategy, 66% say AI meets or exceeds expectations while 35% say practice does not match the strategy (TR-FOP-2026, key findings). Provenance: vendor-published (Thomson Reuters sells professional AI tools) and self-reported by respondents. Publication date not shown on the page.
- More than nine in ten respondents experience some AI value gap, and one in four of those is considering leaving within two years; the report estimates $232,000 per replacement (TR-FOP-2026, mid-career section).

**Consulting and project-based firms.**
- SPI Research's 2026 study (245 professional services organisations and embedded service organisations, surveyed July-September 2026) reports that 43.6% of projects now use AI, up from 23.1% in 2025 (SPI-AI-2026, headline box and "What's in the report"). The page says profit "only goes to firms that have built the right foundations" (four foundations, not public) and that 84.8% say AI saves time versus a gated figure for generating revenue (SPI-AI-2026, Parts II-III). Provenance: vendor-published (research firm sells the report; participants self-reported). The landscape dated the report October 2026; the page shows a 14 Jul 2026 first publication, an "Oct 2026 published" label and a 12 Oct 2026 modification.
- SPI's 2025 edition (146 firms, 2 Dec 2025) describes "a market adopting AI fast but struggling to turn its pilots into performance" (SPI-AI-2025, summary). The landscape's "about one in ten attempting truly transformational AI" was not found on the opened page and is not repeated here.
- Deltek Clarity A&E (896 firms; released 12 May 2026): AI adoption rose from 53% to 70% and GenAI use from 64% to 78% year on year, but only 38% report measurable positive business impact tied to AI; 39% say it is too early or they are still piloting (DELTEK-CLARITY-2026, Key Findings, A&E). Top challenges: where and how to apply the technology (62%), integration (56%), data and cybersecurity (52%) (same). Utilisation fell to just under 60% and operating profit to 16.7% (same). Provenance: vendor-published survey.
- Kantata/Resource Management Institute (survey of 44 organisations; 9 Apr 2026): 39% in "traditional staffing" mode, 30% exploring and experimenting, 31% beyond experimentation; 52.7% not equipped to manage hybrid human-agent teams and only 4% well equipped (KANTATA-RMI-2026, press release). Provenance: vendor-commissioned, small sample (the landscape omitted the sample size and the 30% band).
- MCA Member Survey 2026 (1,060 UK consultants): nearly four in five firms say digital and AI will be their biggest growth drivers and three-quarters say they will invest in those areas (MCA-SURVEY-2026, foreword). 20 Jan 2026. Provenance: trade body, self-reported.
- Introhive/Thinktiv (123 partners, 13 Oct 2026; 42 law, 42 accounting, 39 other): 57% of partners place their firm at business-development maturity stage 1 or 2, 84% expect stage 3 or 4 within 18-24 months, and only 22% say their firm has a fully developed AI strategy (INTROHIVE-LAWSITES-2026). Provenance: vendor-commissioned.

**Measured versus announced: where independent measurement exists.**
- NBER working paper 35720 (pre-registered randomised trial, 133 patent lawyers at 11 US firms): AI access raised blinded-scored quality by 0.34 SD at 10 days and 0.38 SD at 90 days, with larger gains for juniors. After three months, performance without AI was 0.32 SD higher than controls overall, but the advantage was concentrated in seniors (0.45 SD); juniors showed no average gain and their scores bifurcated (NBER-AUTOR-2026, abstract). Provenance: independent. Abstract only; full text was not opened.
- Cal Bar's guidance, Vals, and Stanford hallucination studies sit with other analysts' quality-review dives and are not repeated here.
- No independent, firm-level measurement of realised margin or realisation change attributable to AI was found. The nearest proxies are market-wide: Am Law 100 revenue and profit per FTE grew strongly in Q2 2026 and the Index rose sharply, but the report attributes the climb mainly to worked-rate growth and does not isolate AI (TRI-LFFI-Q2-2026). The State of the US Legal Market reports profit per lawyer 8.4% and fees worked per lawyer 16.8% above pre-2022 levels by end-2025, "most of that growth" from rate hikes (TRI-SOTLM-2026, section on pricing power).

### 2.2 What named firms are doing (announced, piloting, in production)

- **PwC (announced and launched).** In March 2026 PwC's US CEO told the FT that some tax and consulting services would become AI-powered tools clients could use "without a PwC person in the loop", potentially on annual subscription, and that partners resisting AI would not last (CPAPA-PWC-2026, paragraphs 1-5). PwC One had launched with six automated services, with more promised "in the coming months" (same). The firm's FY26 results (reported 19 Oct 2026) cite $3B invested across the network in AI and other capabilities, the initial rollout of PwC One, an AI-native assurance platform, and 330,000+ people trained on AI (AT-PWC-2026). Revenue was $60.2B, up 5.7% in US dollars and 3.5% at constant currency; advisory grew 3.0% and assurance 2.6% (AT-PWC-2026). Whether any PwC One service is priced as a subscription is not stated in the sources (the Stanford paper says PwC's own announcement stops short of pricing specifics, STANFORD-DEL-2026, p. 15).
- **McKinsey (press-reported).** McKinsey's UK managing partner said in November 2025 the firm is doing more performance-based arrangements, and McKinsey is reported to take roughly a quarter of global fees on outcome-based pricing (STANFORD-DEL-2026, p. 14, citing Business Insider; AIWEEKLY-MCK-2025, paragraph 2). Resultsense, summarising the FT in May 2026, says "roughly a third" of work is tied to performance-based fees (RESULTSENSE-2026, paragraph 4). The two figures conflict, and neither was checked at McKinsey. Secondary reports also say McKinsey cited about 25,000 AI agents alongside roughly 40,000 people (FANATICAL-MCK-2026, summarising an HBR interview and Business Insider); treat as press-reported and unverified at source.
- **Accenture (reported results).** FY26 revenue $74.18B (+6% USD, +5% local); consulting revenue $36.88B (+5% USD, +3% local); consulting new bookings $40.86B with a book-to-bill of 1.1; Q4 GAAP operating margin 15.3% (ACCENTURE-Q4FY26, FY26 revenues; bookings; operating margin). Accenture's release reports no AI-specific revenue figure.
- **AI-native challengers in UK consulting (press-reported).** Queen's Tower Advisory targets teams "20 per cent humans, 80 per cent AI agents"; European PE has committed more than €500M to tax advisory firm WTS, which aims to hire 100 partners within five years; KPMG UK set up an "air-gapped" initiative, Project Watts, to bypass normal approvals when trialling AI with clients (RESULTSENSE-2026, paragraphs 4-6; summarising FT and City AM). Also reported there: PwC's global headcount down 5,600 and BCG claiming 40% of revenue from AI and tech work. These are claims from a secondary summary and were not verified at source.
- **Hogan Lovells Cadwalader (pilot, then firmwide rollout announced).** After a pilot of 1,300+ lawyers across 18 practice teams, the 3,300-lawyer firm announced a firmwide Legora rollout on 16 Oct 2026 and a design-partner role for research and monitoring tools (LEGORA-HLC-2026). Provenance: vendor-published; no outcome figures disclosed.
- **Harvey customers (announced).** Harvey's newsroom lists firmwide adoption by Drakopoulos (15 Oct 2026) and Ogier (12 Oct 2026) and a deployment across GE Aerospace's legal and compliance organisation **[in-house]**; it also announces a $550M raise at a $15.5B valuation (HARVEY-NEWSROOM-2026, newsroom list; headline only for the in-house item).
- **Law firm support-layer cuts (announced, AI cited as one factor).** Baker McKenzie told Legal Cheek it was rethinking business professional functions "including through our use of AI"; Legal Cheek understands under 10% (roughly 600 roles) of global business services are affected, a number the firm has not confirmed (LEGALCHEEK-BAKER-2026, 6 Feb 2026). Clifford Chance told staff greater AI use and reduced demand for some services required cuts to its London business services team of about 550 (about 10% per the FT) (CITYAM-CC-2025, 21 Nov 2025). A&O Shearman confirmed "limited and localised" London cuts and pointed to investment in central teams, technology and data, not AI by name (LEGALCHEEK-AO-2026, 8 May 2026).
- **Graduate intake (announced or reported).** PwC UK's entry-level numbers are lower this year (1,300 versus 1,500) and its UK head wrote that "graduate intakes everywhere are under pressure" (FORTUNE-PWC-2025, 8 Sep 2025). A trade site summarising the Guardian reports Big Four graduate postings down 44% in 2025; the "diamond model" label is attributed to unnamed firms (PEOPLEMATTERS-BIG4-2025). Both predate the 12-month window; use for direction, not scale.
- **Accounting AI-native stance (practitioner view).** Ascend's CEO (PE-backed platform with a 30-person AI team) says there is no off-the-shelf AI-native product suite today, that "mix and match" tooling is not a path to AI-native, and that a one-person firm with agents risks being an "AI-powered job rather than an AI-powered business" (AT-AINATIVE-2026, transcript, 19 Oct 2026). Opinion of a platform operator with an interest.

### 2.3 AI-native firms and productised "service as software"

- **Crosby (law, in production per its own site).** Describes itself as a PLLC with barred attorneys who review all AI work, carrying malpractice insurance, with "fixed rates by the document" and a "Don't buy hours. Buy outcomes." tagline (CROSBY-SITE-2026, home page). Customer quotes on the page are testimonials, not measurements. Funding figures (a March 2026 Series B of about $60M) came only from search results and are not used as findings.
- **Garfield.Law (authorised May 2025).** The SRA authorised a firm providing regulated services through AI for small-claims debt recovery up to £10,000; it is "not autonomous", takes a step only with client approval, will not propose case law, and named regulated solicitors remain accountable (GLP-GARFIELD-2025, 6 May 2025). Authorisation is the evidence; no volume, outcome or revenue data was found.
- **PwC One** is the clearest large-firm productisation (CPAPA-PWC-2026). A PwC principal described it as "like a front door into the firm" (quoted from a 2026 Taft interview in STANFORD-DEL-2026, p. 15).
- **Salesforce Agentforce and Palantir** are cited by the Stanford paper as software-side comparators whose pricing moved to subscription plus usage plus outcome components; the paper uses them to illustrate the framework (STANFORD-DEL-2026, section 6). Not professional services firms.
- **Accounting roll-ups with AI as the thesis (announced, press-reported).** Crete Professionals Alliance, backed by Thrive Capital, told Reuters in June 2025 it planned to invest more than $500M over two years buying US accounting firms and equipping them with OpenAI-powered tools; it reported $300M+ revenue, 20+ firms and 900 employees; one Crete firm's partner said AI saved "hundreds of hours every month" in audit testing (self-reported, an assertion in a press interview) (REUTERS-CRETE-2025). Audit-testing detail is noted only as a claim; it belongs to the assurance archetype.
- **Frontier labs moving up the stack.** OpenAI launched a majority-owned Deployment Company on 11 May 2026 with $4B+ initial investment and 19 backers, and agreed to acquire UK applied-AI consultancy Tomoro (about 150 specialists), per Decrypt; Decrypt lists McKinsey and Capgemini among backers and says Anthropic announced a $1.5B venture with Blackstone, Hellman & Friedman and Goldman Sachs days earlier (DECRYPT-DEPLOYCO-2026). Press-reported; Decrypt's framing is that the labs are "becoming a version of" consulting rather than partnering with it. AI Weekly separately reports Bain announced multi-year work with OpenAI and an investment in the Deployment Company (AIWEEKLY-MCK-2025, paragraph 4; press-reported).
- **Platform convergence in legal.** Anthropic (May), Google (August), OpenAI (September) and Microsoft (15 Oct 2026) each launched a legal offering, per LawSites (LAWSITES-MSFT-2026). Microsoft's Copilot for Legal offers 20+ skills in Word and a new agentic workspace and plugins from two dozen legal technology partners (same).
- **Buyer-side shape of demand.** Source Global Research's H2 2026 survey says financial-services buyers are most likely to hire consultants primarily for risk and AI ethics under frameworks like the EU AI Act, and that 60% of enterprise tech buyers cite poor data quality and governance as the largest bottleneck to scaling AI (SOURCE-INSIGHTS-2026, 28 Sep 2026). Provenance: independent research firm, paywalled underlying report.

### 2.4 Private equity and outside capital

**Accounting (most advanced).**
- Crowe closed a KKR investment in August 2026 (announced June) with capital earmarked for "talent, technology and innovation", and launched CroweONE, a broad-based ownership plan for 5,600 employees and 539 partners; the first version of the story wrongly called it an ESOP (AT-CROWE-2026, 19 Oct 2026). Crowe is No. 12 on the Top 100 with $1.4B revenue (same).
- Grant Thornton agreed to acquire CBIZ for $5B in cash with financial backing from New Mountain Capital, expected to close in Q4 2026; Bloomberg Law describes firms "bracing for AI technologies to disrupt their core businesses" while PE funnels billions into the sector (BGOV-GTCBIZ-2026, 29 Jul 2026; excerpt visible, rest paywalled).
- Fox Rothschild states PE now holds a stake in roughly one-third of the 30 largest US accounting firms (also "one-third of the top 300" in another paragraph), and describes Baker Tilly's 2024 H&F/Valeas investment leading to a $7B merger with Moss Adams in 2025 (FOXROTHSCHILD-MSO-2026, "Accounting" and "Rise of Private Capital"). Law-firm marketing document; figures are not independently verified. An earlier Global Finance piece counted PE stakes in five of the top 26 US firms in July 2024 (GFMAG-2024).

**Law (structure-driven).**
- Direct non-lawyer ownership is prohibited in almost all US jurisdictions under the Rule 5.4 fee-sharing ban; Arizona, Utah, D.C. and Puerto Rico (49% non-lawyer equity, effective 1 Jan 2026) are exceptions (FOXROTHSCHILD-MSO-2026, "Ownership Restrictions").
- Arizona has 171 non-lawyer-owned firms, including KPMG Law US, LegalZoom and Rocket Lawyer, but its ABS committee voted in March 2026 to require firms to provide legal services rather than only referrals and to serve Arizona residents; investors are choosing MSOs, and 5 of 42 applications since August 2025 intended outside financing (BLOOMBERGLAW-AZ-2026, 16 Sep 2026). MSOs let investors finance back offices, "to take investment to finance artificial intelligence improvements" while attorneys keep legal ownership (same).
- California (AB 2305, 68-0 on 6 Apr 2026), Illinois and Colorado advanced restrictions in April-May 2026 (FOXROTHSCHILD-MSO-2026, "Ownership Restrictions"; BLOOMBERGLAW-AZ-2026 on California 2025 and Colorado and Illinois 2026 fee-sharing limits). The two sources differ slightly on what each state enacted; confirm with the statutes.
- BigLaw signals: McDermott Will & Emery confirmed in November 2025 it was exploring selling a stake; Quinn Emanuel's founder said he was "open" to outside investors; Morgan & Morgan retained JPMorgan to explore outside capital (FOXROTHSCHILD-MSO-2026, "Rise of Private Capital"). All are statements of exploration, not completed deals.
- Winston Taylor's analysis (19 Mar 2026) calls law "one of the last major professional services segments without meaningful PE penetration" and lists cautions including UK experience (Slater and Gordon wrote off more than AUD 1B of goodwill) and PE health-care bankruptcies (WINSTON-PE-2026). Law-firm commentary, not independent.
- The ILTACON 2026 G200 programme included a keynote on private equity, alternative ownership, pricing models and AI commercialisation (landscape; ILTA-ILTACON-2026, not re-opened this run).

### 2.5 Client in-sourcing and client pressure

- **[in-house]** Juro's "State of In-house 2026" (130+ senior lawyers across Europe and the US, 9 Feb 2026): 44% were somewhat or very confident they could halve outside counsel spend and still manage risk; 68% said more than 10% of work sent out could be insourced with AI, and 20% said more than 25%; among respondents who knew their firms used AI, 73% believed the firm keeps all or most of the savings (JURO-INHOUSE-2026). Provenance: vendor-published (a contract automation vendor, with an interest in the insourcing story); small sample.
- **[in-house]** Axiom's 2026 GC Survey Report (10 Feb 2026) says "80%+" of legal departments plan to bring significant law firm work in-house or to alternative providers within 24 months, 66% of departments received budget increases averaging 12%, and 90% still face efficiency pressure (AXIOM-GC-2026, summary page). Provenance: vendor-published (Axiom sells alternative legal services); sample size not on the page.
- **[in-house]** Thomson Reuters' 2024 State of the Corporate Law Department (reported 25 Nov 2024): 68% planned to bring more work back in-house to save costs and 80% said willingness to offer alternative fee arrangements was a key selection factor (GLOBALLEGALPOST-TR-2024). Outside the 12-18 month window; shows the trend predates GenAI-specific pressure.
- At Legalweek 2026, panellists from GSK (client), Greenberg Traurig, Hogan Lovells, Ropes & Gray and DLA Piper described clients asking AI-specific questions about specific matters, some requesting line-item AI transparency on invoices, others issuing guidelines that they will not pay for certain services if performed by AI (TRI-LEGALWEEK-2026). The conference account also records DLA Piper's view that firms should "lead the conversation" on AI ROI (same).
- Legal departments carry their own constraints: Thomson Reuters interviews with general counsel found tool proliferation without governance, limited legal operations infrastructure, security constraints and team resistance **[in-house]** (TRI-STANDOUT-2026, client-pressure section). One GC is quoted wondering whether partner rates are rising to offset associate hours lost to AI (same).
- Only about one-third of embedded-AI partners have discussed AI with 60% or more of their clients; more than one-third of stand-out lawyers have discussed AI with under 20% of clients (TRI-STANDOUT-2026, client-conversation section).
- Business-development readiness is a constraint on the commercial response: 78% of partners say relationship data is hard to act on, and 68% of AI users for BD use only general-purpose tools (INTROHIVE-LAWSITES-2026).

### 2.6 Business-model scenarios for the billable hour and the leverage pyramid

**Facts about the current economics.**
- Am Law 100 worked rates grew almost 10% in Q1 2026 (above 12% for the largest firms) while demand grew 2.7%; the Law Firm Financial Index was flat at 55, its long-run average (TRI-LFFI-Q1-2026). Midsize firms slowed rate growth for the first time since 2021 and their productivity is contracting (same; TRI-LFFI-Q2-2026).
- In Q2 2026 the Index rose sharply; associate and non-equity partner demand grew while equity partner demand slipped; the ratio of non-equity partner to equity partner hours is at its highest since late 2018 (TRI-LFFI-Q2-2026). The authors read this as "fewer equity partners, a larger and more heavily worked group underneath them". This is the opposite of a pyramid collapse in the current data.
- Law firm technology and knowledge-management spend rose about 11% and 10% in 2025; technology spend is up 39.3% over 2021-2025 (TRI-SOTLM-2026). The report warns of exposure for firms "using AI mainly to justify higher billing" if an AI-investment bubble were to burst (same). That is the report's forecast-style warning, not an observed event.
- Clio's 2025 Legal Trends data (vendor) put average realisation at 88% and estimated up to 74% of billable work could be automated; among firms with wider AI adoption, 69% had seen revenue increase (CLIO-BILLABLE-2026, "How AI threatens hourly billing revenue"). Vendor-published; the blog article is from 21 Feb 2026 and refers to the 2025 edition.
- SPI says time saved is not being turned into revenue: 84.8% say AI saves time (SPI-AI-2026, Part III). Cal Bar guidance states hourly billing must reflect time actually spent, flat fees may build AI savings into pricing, and matter-specific AI costs may be billed only with disclosure and no markup without informed written consent (CALBAR-GENAI-2026, billing row, p. 8). PDF metadata dates the document 15 May 2026.

**Scenarios and forecasts published by others (each labelled by author).**
- **Brynjolfsson and Petropoulos (Stanford Digital Economy Lab, 23 Aug 2026) - theory-based forecast:** internal AI augmentation initially lowers input-cost observability, pushing contracts away from pure time-based pricing toward access or hybrid structures; externally deployed agents generate outcome telemetry and push toward outcome-based hybrids. Their four rules: match contract power to signal informativeness; let the AI deployment context set the direction; invest in measurement before outcome pricing, and check whether attribution is the binding constraint; treat hybrid as the equilibrium (STANFORD-DEL-2026, section 7, pp. 20-22). They say pure time-and-materials survives only at the margins (same, p. 14). Note the lab discloses a general gift from Accenture and interviews with Accenture executives (STANFORD-DEL-2026, acknowledgements).
- **Forrester (blog, 15 Sep 2025) - analyst forecast:** providers will reprice, change staffing structures, and put fees at risk; "the death of the pyramid has been pundit fodder for a decade", with diamond-shaped or pod-like structures proposed; consultancies still need a talent pipeline (FORRESTER-BLOG-2025, positions 1, 4 and 5). The landscape's Wave claim that all 10 providers will put fees at risk comes from a search snippet and was not opened.
- **HFS Research (webinar deck, 21 Jul 2026) - analyst view:** 75% of 605 Global 2000 executives said they plan to renegotiate BPO contracts; current commercial model for AI-enabled IT and business services is 54% effort/input, 26% transaction, 20% outcome (n=510); preferred is 56% base fee plus outcome kicker, 51% outcome-based, 42% gain-share, 18% time and materials (n=101) (HFS-SAS-2026, slides 6 and 13). The thesis is "services-as-software": "Billable hours and FTE-based pricing measure effort, not outcomes." Adjacent to professional services (IT and BPO) so it caps the grade for law and accounting.
- **Thomson Reuters Institute (Future of Professionals 2026) - illustrative scenarios:** Elevate (human expertise at the centre), Scale (capacity without proportional headcount) and Reimagine (subscription advisory, productised services, AI-native practices); legal professionals expect the time to trusted judgment to lengthen by 1.7 years while tax professionals expect it to shorten by 1.0 (TR-FOP-2026, "three illustrative paths"; "timeline to trusted judgment"). Scenarios are drawn from how respondents describe their organisations' direction; not forecasts of market share.
- **Citi law-firm advisory group survey (57 large firms, summer 2026) - firm expectations:** two-thirds expect fewer first-year associates in 2028 than in 2025 and nearly 60% fewer second-years; 19% said they do not know their 2028 first-year needs (BLOOMBERGLAW-HIRING-2026). NALP reported large-firm hiring of 6,588 new graduates in 2025, down 538 (7.5%) from the 2024 peak, and a historical model suggests deeper declines (same). Bloomberg's own historical-model extrapolation is the columnist's, not NALP's.
- **Kantata/RMI (April 2026):** 1 in 2 respondents are unsure their organisation will meaningfully adopt outcome-based pricing, and only 7.3% say outcome data is routinely used in staffing (KANTATA-RMI-2026).
- **TRI (Legalweek): single line-item "services rendered" bill** floated by one panellist as an answer to AI compressing hours into minutes; he asked whether clients would accept it (TRI-LEGALWEEK-2026).

**Demand-side counter-scenario.** The Thomson Reuters foreword argues AI will increase demand through new business formation, M&A, restructurings, litigation and contracting (TR-FOP-2026, foreword; forecast by a vendor). Source Global Research projects technology consulting spend growth of 7.5% in 2026 (SOURCE-INSIGHTS-2026). Observed legal demand is the strongest since 2021 if sustained (TRI-LFFI-Q2-2026).

### 2.7 Governance and structural constraints on the scenarios

- Cal Bar says supervision must increase with system autonomy and that agentic systems must not be configured to send client data outside without safeguards (CALBAR-GENAI-2026, pp. 3 and 6). It replaced the November 2023 version and was revised at the California Supreme Court's request (CALBAR-GENAI-2026, executive summary).
- The Law Society of England and Wales published "The future of agentic AI in legal practice" (14 Apr 2026) and has an AI strategy built on innovation, impact and integrity (LAWSOC-AI-2026, hub listing; article not opened).
- A report in which Deloitte Australia partially refunded a government department after AI-generated errors is the reference incident for disclosure and review in consulting (CTV-DELOITTE-2025; the opened CTV page was a navigation shell, so details are held at the landscape's snippet level).

## 3. What it means for an AI transformation conversation

*This section is synthesis, not source content. Sourced facts are cited; judgments are mine and labelled.*

### 3.1 Implications

1. **Separate the three questions a leader is mixing: how work gets done, how it is priced, who owns the firm.** The evidence on each moves at a different speed. Work production is changing fast (ILTA-TECHSURVEY-2026, p. 4). Pricing is changing slowly and unevenly (HFS-SAS-2026, slide 13; CLIO-BILLABLE-2026). Ownership is changing through structures that skip the hardest rules (BLOOMBERGLAW-AZ-2026).
2. **"Hours saved" is the weakest unit of proof.** Surveys show hours saved (SPI-AI-2026) but clients are asking for matter-level proof (TRI-LEGALWEEK-2026) and only 38% of A&E firms can show measurable impact (DELTEK-CLARITY-2026). A leader's first practical job is a baseline: realisation, write-offs, utilisation and fixed-fee margin by matter type, before and after AI.
3. **The pyramid question splits into three different problems.** (a) Support-layer cuts, which are happening now (LEGALCHEEK-BAKER-2026). (b) Entry-level intake, which firms expect to shrink but cannot size (19% "don't know", BLOOMBERGLAW-HIRING-2026). (c) Skill formation, where the one randomised trial finds AI gives durable skill to seniors and splits juniors (NBER-AUTOR-2026). Current profit data show leverage rising, not falling (TRI-LFFI-Q2-2026). Do not treat these as one "pyramid collapse".
4. **Pricing change is gated by measurement and attribution, not by willingness.** The Stanford rules say invest in measurement first and test whether attribution is the binding constraint (STANFORD-DEL-2026, p. 21). Most firms lack the data: 7.3% have routine outcome data in staffing (KANTATA-RMI-2026).
5. **Partners are the transmission mechanism.** Strategy that stays a leadership document does not change client experience (TRI-STANDOUT-2026). Only about a third of embedded-AI partners have discussed AI with most clients (same). Treat partner AI fluency and partner incentives as design variables (PwC's CEO tied AI posture to partner standing, CPAPA-PWC-2026).
6. **Clients are heterogeneous.** The same panel hears "show me the savings" and "we will not pay for AI-done work" (TRI-LEGALWEEK-2026). **[in-house]** teams are building capability to insource part of the work (JURO-INHOUSE-2026) and are themselves constrained (TRI-STANDOUT-2026).
7. **Outside capital turns AI into a financing story.** Capital is earmarked for "talent, technology and innovation" (AT-CROWE-2026) and MSO investors explicitly fund AI improvements (BLOOMBERGLAW-AZ-2026). A leader should know what their capital structure allows before choosing a business-model path.

### 3.2 Interview probes (for a leader in this field)

- Which matters or engagements have you re-priced because of AI? What was the margin before and after, and who captured the saving: firm, client or both?
- If a client asked you today "what did AI save on my matter", what could you show? Who owns that number?
- What share of your work is time-based, fixed-fee, subscription or outcome-linked, and what would have to be true to move 10 points?
- Where in your pyramid has AI already changed the number of people you hire or the work juniors do? What do you do for juniors who no longer get first-draft work?
- How many partners could explain the AI strategy to a client in two minutes? What are partners paid or promoted for?
- Which of your clients could take work back in-house within two years, and what would they take first? **[in-house]** If you run a legal or shared-service team: which outside spend are you actually moving, and what stops you?
- Is anyone offering your service as a product (subscription, fixed price per document) in your market? What would you not want a software company or a lab-backed deployment firm to be doing with your clients?
- What does your ownership and capital structure allow if the next three years need a large technology or acquisition investment?

### 3.3 Objections and evidence-based responses

| Objection | What the evidence says |
|---|---|
| "AI will not touch our billable-hour economics; rates are rising." | Rates and demand are strong (Am Law 100 worked rates +~10% in Q1 2026, TRI-LFFI-Q1-2026) but the same publisher warns that growth came mostly from rate hikes and that firms using AI to justify higher billing are exposed (TRI-SOTLM-2026). Clients report suspecting rate increases offset AI savings (TRI-STANDOUT-2026). The question is timing, not direction of client pressure. |
| "Clients will pay for AI-enabled quality, so there is no discount pressure." | 78% of corporate clients rate AI-enabled quality as important or essential, but 6% say most providers deliver it, and 32% are reconsidering relationships (TR-FOP-2026). Some clients also refuse to pay for AI-done work (TRI-LEGALWEEK-2026). Both postures coexist. |
| "We cannot price on outcomes; we cannot measure them." | The Stanford paper agrees measurement is a prerequisite and that attribution can be the binding constraint, and recommends hybrids as the typical equilibrium (STANFORD-DEL-2026, pp. 21-22). HFS data show surveyed buyers most often prefer a hybrid base fee plus outcome kicker (56%; outcome-based 51%; multiple answers allowed), while 20% of current engagements are outcome-based (HFS-SAS-2026, slide 13). |
| "The pyramid is collapsing, so stop hiring juniors." | Current law-firm data show leverage rising and associate demand up (TRI-LFFI-Q2-2026); hiring forecasts are expectations with 19% undecided (BLOOMBERGLAW-HIRING-2026); the only randomised evidence says juniors' durable skill gain is mixed (NBER-AUTOR-2026). 71% of surveyed professionals say early-career roles need structured support from experienced peers (TR-FOP-2026, mid-career section). |
| "AI-native competitors are tiny." | True so far: Garfield.Law handles small-claims debts up to £10,000 (GLP-GARFIELD-2025); Crosby publishes no scale data on the opened page (CROSBY-SITE-2026); the one-person accounting firm example is anecdotal (AT-AINATIVE-2026). But the labs are moving into legal and implementation services (LAWSITES-MSFT-2026; DECRYPT-DEPLOYCO-2026), and PE-backed platforms are funded to scale (AT-CROWE-2026; REUTERS-CRETE-2025). |
| "PE will not come to law." | Direct ownership is blocked in most states, but investors are using MSOs, BigLaw firms have explored stakes, and accounting is the precedent (FOXROTHSCHILD-MSO-2026; BLOOMBERGLAW-AZ-2026). Regulators in California, Illinois and Colorado are pushing back (same). |
| "Our AI strategy is in place." | In TRI's study over three-quarters of stand-out lawyers say the firm has a strategy but only 25% strongly agree there is a plan to monetise AI use (TRI-STANDOUT-2026). Among TR-FOP respondents with a strategy, 35% say practice does not match it (TR-FOP-2026). |
| "Consulting demand is collapsing." | Not in the numbers: Accenture consulting revenue +5% in USD (ACCENTURE-Q4FY26), Source forecasts +7.5% technology consulting spend (SOURCE-INSIGHTS-2026), PwC advisory +3.0% (AT-PWC-2026). The pressure shows in pricing, headcount and mix, not in aggregate revenue yet. |
