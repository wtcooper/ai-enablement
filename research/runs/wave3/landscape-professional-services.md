Items marked **(snippet)** were seen only in search results or a headline, not opened in full. Items marked **(unverified)** were not confirmed this run. Quotations are under 25 words. ## 1. Summary

**What the evidence says.** Professional services is the field where the AI business-model question is most visible. Legal has the densest public record (conferences, surveys, vendors, regulators). Consulting and accounting have strong press and analyst signals but thin primary data. Architecture and engineering have one good annual benchmark (Deltek) and little else.

- **Adoption is near-universal; value is not.** ILTA's 2026 tech survey (508 firms) reports 94% of firms engaged with GenAI, up 14 points in a year (ILTA-TECHSURVEY-2026). The SPI 2026 study (245 firms) reports 43.6% of projects now use AI, up from 23.1% (SPI-AI-2026). Deltek reports 70% AI adoption among A&E firms but only 38% with measurable benefit (DELTEK-CLARITY-2026). Thomson Reuters finds 91% of professionals think their organisation is not realising AI's potential (TR-FOP-2026).
- **The client is now the pressure point.** 78% of corporate clients call AI-enabled quality improvement very important or essential, but 6% say most providers deliver it. 32% will reconsider provider relationships within 12 months (TR-FOP-2026). At Legalweek 2026, clients asked for matter-level proof of AI savings while some refused to pay for AI-assisted work (TRI-LEGALWEEK-2026).
- **Pricing and leverage are the live business-model questions.** PwC's US CEO said some services become subscription tools "without a PwC person in the loop" (CPAPA-PWC-2026, via FT). Forrester says all 10 providers in its Q2 2026 AI-consulting Wave will put fees at risk (FORRESTER-WAVE-2026). Brynjolfsson and Petropoulos argue AI pushes consulting toward hybrid and asset-based pricing (STANFORD-DEL-2026). Law firm worked rates still grew about 10% in Q1 2026 while LFFI profit signals stayed flat (TRI-LFFI-Q1-2026).
- **The pyramid is under measured pressure.** An NBER randomised trial of 133 patent lawyers found AI raised work quality, but the durable skill gain went to senior lawyers; junior outcomes split (NBER-AUTOR-2026). Large-firm first-year hiring is projected to fall (BLOOMBERGLAW-HIRING-2026, snippet).
- **Agentic AI is the 2026 vocabulary.** EDRM counted at least 18 of 107 Legalweek 2026 sessions touching agentic AI (EDRM-LEGALWEEK-2026). California's State Bar rewrote its guidance at the Supreme Court's request to cover agentic AI (CALBAR-GENAI-2026). The UK Bar Standards Board advised "absolute caution" (LAW360-BSB-2026, headline only).
- **Platform convergence.** Four frontier AI labs and Microsoft entered legal verticals within five months (LAWSITES-MSFT-2026). Harvey raised $550M at a $15.5B valuation (HARVEY-NEWSROOM-2026). PSA vendors (Certinia, Kantata) now ship named agents for estimate, SOW, staffing and project management (CERTINIA-VEDA-2026).

**Most reusable finds for deep dives:** TR Future of Professionals 2026; ILTA Tech Survey executive summary; Cal Bar 2026 guidance; NBER 35720; Stanford pricing paper; SPI 2026; Kantata/RMI staffing study; Certinia Veda agent catalogue; Harvey Legal Work Orchestration post; Legora-Laurel time-capture release.

**Main gaps:** consulting-firm primary documents (McKinsey, BCG, Bain, Deloitte, EY, KPMG pages were blocked or not opened), SRA and ABA texts (blocked), session-level programmes for CLOC and AICPA ENGAGE (JavaScript-rendered), paywalled benchmarks (Source, SPI, Gartner, Forrester full text), and in-house shared-service teams.

## 10. Module check

**Verdict:** the eight proposed modules match how vendors and practitioners divide firm operations (Certinia's Sell/Plan/Deliver/Manage agents, Intapp's Compliance/Profitability/Growth groupings, TRI's COO-CFO and CMBDO forums, and CLOC's twelve legal-ops functions are all close to it). They leave four gaps and two overlaps.

**Evidence density for the proposed modules**

| Module | Density | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| pursuit-proposals | Medium | Loopio 1,533-person survey (vendor), Certinia RFP Responder, Introhive partner survey, Source buyer data. Few firm case studies |
| acceptance-risk | Low | Intapp product map, Cal Bar duties, TRI agentic premortem (conflict checks as tacit knowledge). Almost no public firm detail. Give it one combined deep dive, paired with quality-review's risk side |
| scoping-pricing | High | TRI pricing series, TRI rates report, Stanford pricing paper, Forrester, PwC One, McKinsey outcome fees, SPI "leak" |
| staffing-resourcing | Medium | Kantata/RMI, SPI, Certinia agents, Deltek utilisation, Legalweek ROI sessions. Little independent data |
| delivery-management | Medium | PSA vendors, Harvey orchestration, Deltek, PMI. Practitioner case studies thin |
| quality-review | High | Cal Bar, Deloitte Australia, Vals, Stanford hallucination study, NBER, TRI "Trust by Verify", ILTACON validation session |
| billing-collections | Medium | Laurel/Legora, TRI realisation and rates, client AI-billing demands, Cal Bar billing rules, Intapp Time |
| knowledge-management | Medium | iManage benchmark, APQC, Legalweek KM sessions, McKinsey Lilli (secondary), TRI KM spend |

**Proposed changes**

1. **Add `talent-learning` (high evidence).** NBER 35720, Citi/BCG/Vault hiring data, Big Four graduate cuts, MCA training concern, Karbon junior-learning pieces, ENGAGE talent sessions, Kantata's hybrid-team readiness gap all point to the same decision area: apprenticeship when AI does entry-level work. It is neither staffing (matching people to work) nor knowledge management. This is where the leverage pyramid problem lands.
2. **Add `firm-finance-performance` (high).** Rate setting, realisation, partner economics, AI investment ROI, PE and outside capital (Crowe-KKR, ILTACON G200 keynote), consumption-based AI pricing. TRI's LFFI, rates report and COO/CFO forum, and SPI's four foundations treat this as its own discipline. Keep `billing-collections` narrow (time capture to cash) and move AI ROI measurement here.
3. **Add `client-relationship-development` (medium).** TRI's Stand-out Lawyers finding (partners not discussing AI with clients), the CMBDO Forum, the Introhive survey and the client reconsideration data in TR-FOP-2026 show a distinct task: key-account management, AI value conversations, client feedback. It overlaps `pursuit-proposals`; either widen that module to "pursuit and client development" or split it. Splitting is cleaner because RFP response (document work) and relationship intelligence (data work) use different tools.
4. **Treat substantive work production as a layer, not a module in this base.** Legal research, drafting and review, consulting analysis, and tax or audit procedures hold the largest share of AI product activity (Harvey, Legora, CoCounsel, Lilli, Vals tasks). Recommend a thin shared `work-production` module here (what is common: first-draft generation, synthesis, review against playbooks, data extraction), with practice-specific detail left to extensions (assurance, tax, legal, consulting). Without it, `delivery-management` absorbs content it was not designed for.
5. **Fold `legal operations` (in-house shared-service) into existing modules with a flag, not a new module.** CLOC, TRI-LDOR-2026 and Legalweek in-house sessions describe the same eight functions with a client-side twist (intake, outside-counsel spend, matter management). Tag sources `in-house` instead of adding a module.
6. **Cross-cutting, not modules:** AI governance and shadow AI (34% unsanctioned use, TR-FOP-2026; ILTA policy gap by firm size), client AI terms, and productised services or "service as software" (PwC One). Cover these inside `acceptance-risk`, `quality-review` and `scoping-pricing`, but give each a named thread in deep dives.

**Overlaps to clarify:** `staffing-resourcing` vs `talent-learning` (allocation vs development); `scoping-pricing` vs `firm-finance-performance` (deal-level vs firm-level); `knowledge-management` vs `work-production` (reuse of precedent vs generation of drafts).

## 11. Journey-stage markers

Observable markers for this field, drawn from the surveys above.

| Stage | Markers |
|---|---|
| **Curious** | Individual lawyers or consultants use consumer chatbots outside policy (34% unsanctioned use, TR-FOP-2026); no formal AI policy or only a "do not upload client data" memo (policy and training requirements are lowest at small firms, ILTA-TECHSURVEY-2026); partners ask about AI at conferences but do not discuss it with clients (TRI-STANDOUT-2026) |
| **Experimenting** | Copilot or one legal AI tool licensed on request, pilots of 20-1,300 users (for example the Hogan Lovells Cadwalader pilot, LEGORA-HLC-2026); a named innovation lead and an AI steering group; savings reported anecdotally ("still gathering the data", TRI-LEGALWEEK-2026); weighing hours saved against write-downs |
| **Adopting** | Firmwide licences for a legal or PSA AI platform; mandatory training before access; matter-level use of drafting, review and research agents with human sign-off; client questions about AI use answered with some data; PSA agents drafting estimates or SOWs (CERTINIA-VEDA-2026). Roughly where the 43.6%-of-projects firms sit (SPI-AI-2026) |
| **Scaling** | Workflows redesigned around review gates (HARVEY-ORCH-2026); AI use tracked in time entries and matter economics (LEGORA-LAUREL-2026); alternative fee or fixed-fee offers priced on AI-reduced effort; changes to first-year intake or training; AI value measured against utilisation, margin and revenue per head (SPI foundations); partner incentives tied to AI adoption (PwC) |
| **AI-native** | Delivery by a small human team directing agents (Queen's Tower Advisory targets 20% human, RESULTSENSE-2026; Ascend's 30-person AI team, AT-AINATIVE-2026); productised or subscription offerings clients use without a professional in the loop (PwC One); outcome-based or hybrid pricing is the default for a large share of work (McKinsey figure disputed: one quarter vs one third); hybrid human-agent resource management and outcome data feeding staffing (KANTATA-RMI-2026 shows only 4% feel well equipped) |
