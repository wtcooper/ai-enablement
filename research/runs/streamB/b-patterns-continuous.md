# Agentic patterns, continuous family: `full-population-testing` and `monitor-diagnose-act`

Stream B, shared backbone library. Research run 2026-10-20. Cross-industry evidence and design knowledge for two patterns defined in `library/index.md` section 3. Archetype use cases are cited by id and grade only (section 3); their content is not re-researched.

Conventions. Refs are `PUBLISHER-SHORT-YYYY` (section 8). A locator follows each ref (`§`, `¶`, `p.`). "Advisor inference" marks a design recommendation that I derived from several sources or from standard practice and that no single source states; it is not evidence. "Illustrative arithmetic" marks my own calculation. Where a source is an abstract only (full text paywalled or blocked), the claim is limited to the abstract and section 8 says so.

---

## 1. Summary

- **The two patterns are one family with one failure shape.** Both run a model over a large stream (every item, or every signal over time), surface a small set for people, and stand or fall on the precision of what they surface. Where the stream is large and true exceptions are rare, volume of false alerts, not missed detection, is what breaks the work: Danske Bank's earlier rules-based fraud system produced, in the vendor's words, false positives "at times reaching 99.5% of all transactions" (the source's wording; presumably of alerts), against about one fraud case in 100,000 [FINTECHFUTURES-DANSKE-2017 ¶ citing Teradata]; Google SRE sets the design goal of "very low noise" for anything that pages a human [GOOGLE-SREBOOK-2017 ch.6 intro].
- **The best-measured deployments publish precision, not just volume.** DWP compares the model's confirmed-fraud rate against a stratified random control sample that includes items the model scored low (2.5 times in 2025-26; "around 3 times" in 2024-25), and reviewers are blind to the model's rating [DWP-ATRS-2025 §2 performance text; DWP-FAIRNESS-2025 ¶8, ¶10]. HSBC reports alerts down 60% and two to four times as much suspicious activity identified, over 12 months after go-live against the preceding 12 months [GOOGLE-HSBC-2023 results, fn 1]. Few other organizations published a baseline-controlled figure.
- **Independent quality evidence is thin and mostly negative or mixed.** An external validation of a widely implemented sepsis alert found an AUC of 0.63, 67% of sepsis cases not flagged and alerts on 18% of hospitalized patients [WONG-ESM-2021 abstract]; a regulator found that none of six large audit firms formally measured the audit-quality impact of its automated tools [FRC-ATTREVIEW-2025 §3 p.20]; a state review found 93% of the automated fraud findings it examined were wrong [MICHIGANPUBLIC-MIDAS-2016 ¶2]. No controlled trial of either pattern was found.
- **Alert acceptance is the metric that links the two patterns to outcomes.** In one sepsis early-warning deployment, 89% of alerts were evaluated by a clinician and 38% of those were confirmed; the outcome gain was associated with confirmation within 3 hours, not with alert volume [HENRY-TREWS-2022 abstract; ADAMS-TREWS-2022 abstract]. Systematic review of clinical drug-alert overrides finds average override rates of 46.2% to 96.2% across studies [POLY-OVERRIDE-2020 abstract]. Model-risk guidance already tells banks to analyze overrides as a performance signal [FED-SR117-2011 §V "Ongoing monitoring"].
- **Autonomy is earned by bounding the action, not by trusting the model.** The one production control example with design detail starts in recommendation mode, goes live only when an operator flips a switch, falls back automatically on constraint violation, and can be disabled manually [DEEPMIND-BCOOLER-2022 App. A.3]. The counter-example is Knight Capital: no control to halt its router, 45 minutes, a $460 million loss on positions [SEC-KNIGHT-2013 ¶1, ¶17, ¶21].
- **Governance bites hardest where the pattern touches people's benefits or health.** Fraud detection in government services is presumed "high-impact" under US OMB M-25-21 [OMB-M2521-2025 §6 item m]; the EU AI Act lists benefit-eligibility systems in Annex III but excludes financial-fraud detection from the credit-scoring category [EU-AIACT-2024 Annex III 5(a), 5(b)]; the US FDA treats a sepsis alert as a device function [FDA-CDS-2026 example 11]. EU high-risk obligations now apply from 2 December 2027 (Annex III) after the 2026 Omnibus [FPF-OMNIBUS-2026].
- **Failure cases are all "automated adverse action with no human check or no sampled control".** MiDAS (Michigan), Robodebt (Australia, rules-based, not AI) and the Dutch childcare risk model each combined full-population screening with consequences for individuals and no effective check on the flagged set [MICHIGANPUBLIC-MIDAS-2016; CONVERSATION-ROBODEBT-2023; AMNESTY-DUTCH-2021].
- **Evidence gaps to say plainly.** No retail example and no named predictive-maintenance example with a measured outcome could be verified; manufacturing evidence is a press statement without figures [BMW-DEBRECEN-2025]. Gartner forecasts that over 40% of agentic AI projects will be canceled by end of 2027 (a forecast, not a measurement) [GARTNER-AGENTIC-2025].

## 2. Item profiles

### 2.1 `full-population-testing`

**What it does and where it fits.**
- Tests every item in a population (or a defined stratum) against criteria or a model instead of a sample, and routes exceptions to people who investigate and conclude [library/index.md §3]. Auditing standards already recognize "selecting all items (100 percent examination)", including where "the audit procedure can be automated effectively and applied to the entire population" [PCAOB-AS1105-2026 ¶.24]. Testing specific items is not sampling and its results "cannot be projected to the entire population" [PCAOB-AS1105-2026 ¶.27]; this matters when an advisor hears "we test everything now": the claim covers only the items and criteria the tool actually evaluates.
- Work types: `review-assurance`, `transaction-processing`, `data-reporting` (index §2). Cross-industry forms found: payments integrity and fraud screening (public sector) [TREASURY-OPI-2024; DWP-ATRS-2025], transaction monitoring for money laundering (financial services) [GOOGLE-HSBC-2023], pre-payment claims analytics (healthcare, public payer) [HHSOIG-FPS-2015], static analysis on code changes (technology) [ACM-TRICORDER-2018], in-line and end-of-line inspection (manufacturing) [BMW-DEBRECEN-2025], journal-entry anomaly testing (audit) [FRC-AIAUDIT-2025 pp.8-11].
- Cues in how people describe their work (advisor inference): "we only have time to check 5% / a sample"; "we find it after the money has gone / the release has shipped"; "rules fire thousands of alerts and most are noise"; "random spot checks each quarter"; "a backlog of items waiting for a check".
- It is not necessarily AI. The FRC's worked example combines binary rules-based routines with an AI score and notes that "in most cases" the combined tool flagged similar or smaller numbers of high-risk transactions than the firm's earlier approach [FRC-AIAUDIT-2025 p.10]. Advisor inference: ask whether rules over the full population already solve the problem before proposing a model.

**Ladder range and runtime class.** Library working definition: usual rung `automate` [index §3]. Advisor inference on the rungs, with archetype examples named by id and grade: `assist` (a person runs a one-off analysis over the full extract; `personal-assistant`); `augment` (a tool scores everything and a person reviews each flagged item; `shared-team-skills` or `workflow-automation`; see `assurance-substantive-testing-01` B); `automate` (scheduled runs, exceptions routed to a queue, nobody on the clean path; `workflow-automation` or `background-agent`; see `assurance-controls-testing-08` A); `delegate` (an agent runs tests, investigates exceptions across systems and drafts the conclusion for sign-off; `managed-agent-runtime`; see `assurance-substantive-testing-24` B and `security-ai-security-17` A). In the sources, the human decides adverse outcomes even where screening is automatic: DWP states "there is always a human intervention and decision, with no automated decision making by the model" [DWP-ATRS-2025 §3.2; DWP-FAIRNESS-2025 ¶11].

**How it is built.**
- Inputs: a complete population extract, with its completeness and accuracy tested. The standard asks auditors to "test the accuracy and completeness" of information the company produces [PCAOB-AS1105-2026 ¶.10]. The FRC example makes the tool mandatory only where ledger data is "complete and accurate" with date, time, poster, description, value and account fields present [FRC-AIAUDIT-2025 p.11].
- The AI's role: score or rank items (unsupervised anomaly detection or a trained model), explain why an item was flagged, and sometimes draft the exception note. The FRC example gives a design reason for explanation: users "should be able to understand why a transaction has been identified as unusual" so they can design follow-up procedures responsive to the feature that triggered it; it describes SHAP and LIME-type techniques for opaque models [FRC-AIAUDIT-2025 p.9].
- Thresholds: the FRC example calls for "significant professional judgement" to set each routine's weight and the cut-off above which an item "must be followed up", and calibration "required both theory and experimentation with data" [FRC-AIAUDIT-2025 p.10]. Google treats a code-review check as acceptable only if it "should produce less than 10% effective false positives" (defined as findings where developers took no positive action), and disables an analyzer when its "Not useful" to "Please fix" click ratio exceeds 10% [ACM-TRICORDER-2018 "Criteria for code-review checks"; "Iterate on feedback from users"]. Both are the organizations' own criteria, not a general benchmark.
- Sampling inside a full-population design: DWP sends a random sample of advances the model rated low-risk, plus a random sample of high-risk ones, to human decision makers as one stratified random sample, and the reviewer "does not receive a risk rating" [DWP-ATRS-2025 performance text and §3.2; DWP-FAIRNESS-2025 ¶10]. This yields a measured false-negative signal and a lift figure, and it limits confirmation bias.
- Review budget: an October 2026 preprint argues that evaluating journal-entry detectors on the full population with ROC-AUC ignores the review budget and anomaly types; on four client ledgers three detectors each placed on average 98 anomalies in the first 100 postings but at least 95.8 belonged to one type [GRONEWALD-ARXIV-2026 abstract]. Preprint; not peer reviewed.
- Human checkpoints: a named person investigates and concludes on each exception and documents the rationale [FRC-AIAUDIT-2025 p.11]; adverse decisions about individuals are made by a human and can be appealed [DWP-ATRS-2025 §3.2, "no distinct process for the model"].
- Logging and audit trail: version control so that only the latest approved tool version can run; model frozen before deployment so it "no longer continues to learn" on live work [FRC-AIAUDIT-2025 pp.10-11]; automatic event logs for high-risk systems under EU AI Act Art. 12, which deployers keep for at least six months [EU-AIACT-2024 Art. 12; Art. 26(6)].

**Prerequisites.**
- Data: complete, accurate population with the needed fields (above). Access: read access to the system of record at item level (advisor inference).
- Policy: a defined human decision for any adverse outcome; an appeals route (DWP, OMB) [OMB-M2521-2025 §4(b) item vi].
- Capacity: a staffed exceptions queue sized to the flagged volume (advisor inference; HSBC reports the alert reduction freed investigators to focus on "genuinely suspicious cases" [GOOGLE-HSBC-2023 results]).
- Skills: data science to calibrate and test; domain experts to set criteria. Firms in the FRC review certified tools centrally before use, with some processes "less mature and not supported by documented policies" [FRC-ATTREVIEW-2025 p.2 summary].
- Acceptance by reviewers of the work: in two experiments, experienced external reviewers rated data-and-analytics audit procedures as lower quality than traditional procedures at the same assurance level because they appeared less effortful; a theory-based intervention reduced that effect [EMETT-CAR-2023 abstract]. Advisor inference: plan the conversation with whoever reviews or inspects the work.

**Failure modes and the controls that catch them.**
- False positives swamp reviewers. Illustrative arithmetic (not a measurement): among 1,000,000 items with 0.1% truly exceptional, a test with 90% sensitivity and 99% specificity flags 900 true and 9,990 false exceptions, so about 8% of flagged items are real; with about one case per 100,000 [FINTECHFUTURES-DANSKE-2017] the same test flags 9 true among about 10,000 flags. Controls: track precision (share of flagged items confirmed), disable or retune noisy checks [ACM-TRICORDER-2018], raise thresholds with a measured control sample [DWP-ATRS-2025].
- False negatives and blind spots. An outlier detector only finds items "unusual relative to the population", so items consistently posted to unusual accounts may not be flagged; the FRC example runs rules alongside the model for this reason [FRC-AIAUDIT-2025 p.10]. DWP samples low-risk predictions [DWP-ATRS-2025].
- Adverse action at scale with no effective human check. MiDAS: a state review of some of 53,633 fraud flags found 93% wrong [MICHIGANPUBLIC-MIDAS-2016]; a 2024 class settlement of $20 million followed [UMICH-MIDAS-2024 p.1]. Robodebt: debts were estimated by averaging annual income across fortnights; the Commonwealth later admitted the calculations "did not have a proper legal basis", and a commentator states the scheme was unlawful because the Act requires income actually received in the fortnight [CONVERSATION-ROBODEBT-2023]; the Royal Commission recommended a monitoring and audit body for automated decision-making [ROBODEBTRC-REPORT-2023 Rec. 17.2]. Controls: human decision on adverse outcomes, appeal, pre-deployment testing, and ongoing monitoring [OMB-M2521-2025 §4(b)].
- Disparate impact. DWP's published fairness assessment reports that for some age groups and non-UK nationals "an increased likelihood of being referred" was "inconsistent with the reduced likelihood of those referrals being correct", and the model is to be retrained [DWP-FAIRNESS-2025 ¶23-¶24]. Amnesty reports that nationality was used as a parameter in the Dutch childcare risk model and describes the outcome as racial profiling [AMNESTY-DUTCH-2021 introduction]. Controls: cohort-level precision (referral rate against confirmed rate), exclusion of proxy variables, retraining and re-measurement [DWP-FAIRNESS-2025 ¶14-¶17].
- Attribution of benefit. Even for a long-running system, independent auditors discounted savings: for CY2014 the HHS OIG certified $133.2 million "adjusted" savings (ROI $2.84) against $454.0 million "unadjusted" savings identified, because several sources lead to an administrative action and attribution is not generally feasible [HHSOIG-FPS-2015 summary; p.1]. Control: agree the savings definition with an independent party before reporting.
- Tool drift and change. Firms identified recertification triggers "informally" [FRC-ATTREVIEW-2025 p.21]. Control: defined recertification triggers and version control.

**Value levers and metrics.**
- `risk`, `cost`, `quality`, `capacity`, `speed` (index §5). Metrics shown in sources: confirmed share of flagged items and lift versus a random control sample (DWP 2.5x, 2025-26) [DWP-ATRS-2025]; alerts and detected activity before and after (HSBC: alerts down 60%; two to four times as much suspicious activity; suspicious accounts detected "down to eight days after the first alert") [GOOGLE-HSBC-2023]; adjusted versus unadjusted savings and return on cost (HHS OIG) [HHSOIG-FPS-2015]; fixes applied per day and not-useful clicks per day (Tricorder: more than 5,000 "Please fix" clicks and about 250 "Not useful" clicks per day in January 2018, about 50,000 changes analyzed per day) [ACM-TRICORDER-2018 "Scale of Tricorder"]; amounts prevented and recovered (Treasury: over $4 billion in FY2024, up from $652.7 million in FY2023) [TREASURY-OPI-2024].
- Quality metrics an advisor should require: precision by cohort, override or dismiss rate, false-negative rate from a control sample, reviewer time per exception, share of exceptions concluded as true findings.

**Governance points.**
- NIST AI RMF 1.0: MEASURE 2.4 (system behavior "monitored when in production"), MANAGE 2.4 (mechanisms to "supersede, disengage, or deactivate"), MANAGE 4.1 (post-deployment monitoring including appeal and override), MAP 3.5 (human oversight processes defined) [NIST-AIRMF-2023 Core tables]. NIST AI 600-1 flags automation bias as a risk of human-AI configuration [NIST-AI600-2024 §2.7].
- EU AI Act: Annex III 5(a) covers systems used by or for public authorities to evaluate eligibility for essential public benefits and to "grant, reduce, revoke, or reclaim" them; 5(b) covers creditworthiness evaluation "with the exception of AI systems used for the purpose of detecting financial fraud" [EU-AIACT-2024 Annex III]. Art. 6(3) allows a documented non-high-risk assessment where a system performs a narrow procedural task or detects deviations from prior human decisions "without proper human review", but profiling always keeps high-risk status [EU-AIACT-2024 Art. 6(3)]. Art. 14 requires oversight measures including awareness of automation bias [EU-AIACT-2024 Art. 14(4)(b)]. Art. 27 requires a fundamental rights impact assessment from public-body deployers and for points 5(b) and (c) systems [EU-AIACT-2024 Art. 27(1)]. Application dates for Annex III high-risk obligations moved to 2 December 2027 under the AI Omnibus, per a law-and-policy think tank's timeline (not the Official Journal text) [FPF-OMNIBUS-2026].
- ISO/IEC 42001: management-system standard; I did not open the standard itself (section 8 marks search-only), so no clause-level claim is made.
- US federal: OMB M-25-21 presumes "detection of fraudulent use or attempted use of government services" high-impact, requires pre-deployment testing, ongoing monitoring, human oversight and "timely human review" or appeal for high-impact AI [OMB-M2521-2025 §6 item m; §4(b) items i, iii, v, vi].
- Audit: PCAOB AS 1105 (100% examination, completeness and accuracy of information) [PCAOB-AS1105-2026]; FRC guidance and thematic review (UK) [FRC-AIAUDIT-2025; FRC-ATTREVIEW-2025].
- Sector rules not opened: BSA/AML rules, GDPR Art. 22, healthcare claims rules (section 7).

**How it evolves up the ladder.** Advisor inference, anchored in the sources named: assist to augment adds item-level scoring across the population (Tricorder moved from a dashboard few visited to results inside code review, after which developers acted on them) [ACM-TRICORDER-2018 "Most developers will not go out of their way"]; augment to automate removes the person from the clean path and makes the control sample and cohort-level precision mandatory (DWP design) [DWP-ATRS-2025]; automate to delegate lets an agent investigate exceptions, which raises the audit-trail and sign-off burden (FRC "combination" and documentation expectations) [FRC-AIAUDIT-2025]. No source measured the delegate rung for this pattern outside the archetype entries.

**Anti-patterns (where not to use it).**
- Automatic adverse decisions on individuals with no human review and no control sample (MiDAS, Robodebt) [MICHIGANPUBLIC-MIDAS-2016; CONVERSATION-ROBODEBT-2023].
- Using protected or proxy attributes such as nationality as model inputs for fraud risk (Dutch childcare) [AMNESTY-DUTCH-2021].
- Testing a population whose data is incomplete or unreliable and reporting it as "100% tested" [PCAOB-AS1105-2026 ¶.10; FRC-AIAUDIT-2025 p.11].
- A flagged volume that exceeds review capacity (queue becomes a rubber stamp; advisor inference, see Tricorder trust lessons [ACM-TRICORDER-2018 "Lessons"]).
- Claiming savings that count unrecoverable amounts (HHS OIG adjusted versus unadjusted) [HHSOIG-FPS-2015].
- Judgment-heavy work where the "exception" is a matter of opinion rather than a rule or measurable deviation (advisor inference).

### 2.2 `monitor-diagnose-act`

**What it does and where it fits.**
- Watches signals continuously, explains anomalies and proposes or takes actions [library/index.md §3]. The cross-industry forms found: incident detection and root-cause ranking in cloud operations (technology) [MSFT-RCACOPILOT-2024; META-RCA-2024], clinical early warning (healthcare) [HENRY-TREWS-2022; ADAMS-TREWS-2022; WONG-ESM-2021], supervisory setpoint control of building cooling plants (facilities, industrial) [DEEPMIND-BCOOLER-2022], real-time fraud monitoring (financial services) [FINTECHFUTURES-DANSKE-2017], security operations (see archetype ids, section 3).
- Work types: `coordination-scheduling`, `data-reporting`, `physical-field` (index §2).
- Cues (advisor inference): "someone watches a dashboard", "we find out from the customer", "alerts go to a shared inbox that nobody reads", "on-call spends the first hour gathering context", "we re-plan weekly because conditions change", "the same triage steps every time".
- The three verbs are separable and often delivered at different rungs: detect (rules or model), diagnose (rank causes, assemble context), act (recommend or execute). Meta's system diagnoses only: it retrieves candidate code changes then ranks them with a fine-tuned model to a top five [META-RCA-2024 "Our approach to root cause isolation"]. RCACopilot collects diagnostic information, predicts a root-cause category and writes an explanation [MSFT-RCACOPILOT-2024 abstract].

**Ladder range and runtime class.** Library working definition: `automate` to `delegate` [index §3]. The sources show lower rungs in practice: Meta and Microsoft sit at `assist` or `augment` (an on-call engineer verifies) [META-RCA-2024 "The future"; MSFT-RCACOPILOT-2024 §5.5]. Advisor inference on matching runtime class: `assist` or `augment` diagnosis fits `workflow-automation` or `shared-team-skills` triggered by an alert; `automate` with a closed action set fits `background-agent`; `delegate` (agent owns detect-diagnose-fix across systems, with checkpoints) fits `managed-agent-runtime`. BCOOLER is the documented bounded-automation design: it sends supervisory recommendations; the plant accepts them only after an operator "flips a switch", and control reverts to the existing controller if a constraint is violated by more than a tolerable amount or the agent fails to generate actions [DEEPMIND-BCOOLER-2022 App. A.3]. Archetype examples at higher rungs: `security-operations-19` C (actions only via a closed set of templates), `security-vulnerability-exposure-22` C (agent acts only through a human-approval harness).

**How it is built.**
- Inputs: telemetry (metrics, logs, traces, alerts), asset and ownership data, change history, runbooks. Meta's retriever uses code and directory ownership and the runtime code graph to cut thousands of changes to a few hundred before an LLM ranks them [META-RCA-2024]. RCACopilot matches incoming incidents to team-built "incident handlers" by alert type, which collect diagnostic data; creating a new handler takes manual effort, which the authors say engineers find convenient to reduce by reusing and modifying existing handler actions [MSFT-RCACOPILOT-2024 abstract; §5.4-5.5].
- The AI's role: summarize and rank, not decide. Meta fine-tuned Llama 2 (7B) on about 5,000 instruction-tuning examples built from historical investigations with known root causes [META-RCA-2024 "Training"]. A separate Microsoft study evaluates a ReAct agent with retrieval tools on a dataset of production incidents and a case study in which it was given access to a team's diagnostic services [MSFT-ROY-2024 abstract].
- Alert design is the first control. Google's SRE guidance: page a human only for conditions that are urgent, actionable and affecting users; asking "Can I take action in response to this alert? ... Could the action be safely automated?" [GOOGLE-SREBOOK-2017 ch.6 "Philosophy"]; rules that page "need to be very simple and robust" [same, ch.6].
- Thresholds and confidence: Meta uses "confidence measurement methodologies to detect low confidence answers and avoid recommending them", "sacrificing reach in favor of precision" [META-RCA-2024 "The future"]. BCOOLER enforces action constraints in the policy and predicted observation constraints before an action is chosen; the BMS applies its own safety checks and can modify the action [DEEPMIND-BCOOLER-2022 §3 (problem formulation), §4.2, App. A.3].
- Human checkpoints: explainability and closed feedback loops so responders can "independently reproduce" results [META-RCA-2024]; the clinician confirms or dismisses each alert and indicates source of infection and organ dysfunction on an evaluation page [HENRY-TREWS-2022 extended data fig.]; operator switch and manual disable [DEEPMIND-BCOOLER-2022 App. A.3].
- Logging and audit trail: feedback captured in incident notification emails [MSFT-RCACOPILOT-2024 §5.5]; EU Art. 12 logging and Art. 26 monitoring by deployers (below); NIST MANAGE 4.1 monitoring plans with "appeal and override" mechanisms [NIST-AIRMF-2023].

**Prerequisites.**
- Data and access: telemetry with consistent labels; historical incidents with known root causes for tuning and backtests (Meta backtests against historical investigations "with the information available at their start") [META-RCA-2024]; one year of incidents manually labeled by experienced on-call engineers as ground truth (Microsoft) [MSFT-RCACOPILOT-2024 §5.1].
- Policy: written rules for which actions may run unattended, who can switch the agent off, and what triggers automatic fallback (BCOOLER; MANAGE 2.4). Knight's order records that it lacked supervisory procedures for incident response and written deployment procedures [SEC-KNIGHT-2013 ¶26-¶27].
- Skills: someone who owns detection quality (a Google SRE team of 10 to 12 members typically has "one or sometimes two members whose primary assignment is to build and maintain monitoring systems") [GOOGLE-SREBOOK-2017 ch.6 intro].
- Safety engineering where physical or clinical consequences exist: setting the constraint specification took "significant time" working with the building-systems provider [DEEPMIND-BCOOLER-2022 §6 item 5; §8.5].

**Failure modes and the controls that catch them.**
- Alert fatigue and ignored alerts. In SRE practice "employees second-guess, skim, or even ignore incoming alerts" when pages are too frequent [GOOGLE-SREBOOK-2017 ch.6 intro]. In clinical medicine the overall alert override rates in 23 included studies ranged from 46.2% to 96.2%, and 29.4% to 100% of overrides were judged appropriate, varying by alert type [POLY-OVERRIDE-2020 abstract]. In the Epic Sepsis Model external validation, alerts fired for 18% of hospitalized patients while 67% of sepsis patients were not identified, which the authors call a "large burden of alert fatigue" [WONG-ESM-2021 abstract]. A security vendor's page reports an average of 2,992 alerts per day with 63% unaddressed (Vectra 2026 data), figures it publishes about its own survey and not independently verified [VECTRA-ALERTFATIGUE-2026]. Controls: page only on urgent actionable conditions; track alert-to-action rate and override reasons; analyze overrides as a model-performance signal [FED-SR117-2011 §V].
- Automation bias and complacency. A systematic review of 74 papers found over-reliance on decision support is mediated by user, task and environment factors (workload, time pressure) and mitigated by training, accountability and design factors such as showing confidence [GODDARD-AB-2012 abstract]. FDA's guidance says automation bias "increases" in situations requiring urgent action because users lack time to consider other information [FDA-CDS-2026 pp.15-16]. Controls: training, accountability, confidence display, independent-reproduction design (Meta).
- Wrong diagnosis presented confidently. Meta's backtested top-five accuracy was 42%, so most investigations were not solved by the suggestion; Microsoft's best micro-F1 was 0.766 and macro-F1 0.533 on its test set (the macro figure shows weaker performance on rarer root-cause categories; reading by the advisor) [META-RCA-2024; MSFT-RCACOPILOT-2024 §5.2]. A 2026 benchmark of ten LLM agents on 754 cloud fault cases reports a best final-answer accuracy of 0.76 and 0.68 on two workloads but evidence closure rates of only 0.38 and 0.15, which the authors read as final-answer accuracy "substantially" overestimating evidence-grounded diagnosis [CLOUDOPSBENCH-2026 abstract]. Controls: require cited evidence; measure the evidence process, not just the answer.
- Unsafe automatic action. Knight Capital: new code left an old function active on one of eight servers; the router sent millions of child orders in 45 minutes (4 million executions, 154 stocks, over 397 million shares), with no control to compare orders leaving the system with those entering, no way to halt it, and automated emails flagging the error that staff "generally did not review"; the firm realized a $460 million loss and paid a $12 million penalty [SEC-KNIGHT-2013 ¶1, ¶17, ¶19, ¶21, ¶25 and penalty section]. This is an automated trading router, not AI; it shows the control set the pattern needs. A practitioner post (April 2026) alleges an AI agent deleted a production database through an API call; the account is unverified (the linked post was not read), and commenters in the thread focus on credential scoping, the lack of any confirmation step in the API, and backups stored with the volume [HN-DBDELETE-2026]. Controls: closed action set, scoped credentials, pre-authorized human override, automatic fallback, a tested "stop" [EU-AIACT-2024 Art. 14(4)(e); NIST-AIRMF-2023 MANAGE 2.4].
- Poor external validity. A deployed vendor model performed poorly at an academic medical center despite wide adoption [WONG-ESM-2021 abstract]. Control: local validation before reliance and periodic re-validation [NIST-AIRMF-2023 MEASURE 2.5, MEASURE 2.4].
- Association mistaken for effect. The TREWS mortality finding compares patients whose alert was confirmed within 3 hours against those whose was not, adjusted for presentation and severity: adjusted absolute reduction 3.3% (CI 1.7 to 5.1) and relative 18.7%, in 6,877 patients with sepsis identified before antibiotics, among 590,736 monitored [ADAMS-TREWS-2022 abstract]. It is an observational cohort study, not a randomized comparison. Control: say "associated with" and prefer a stepped rollout or a control group where feasible (advisor inference).

**Value levers and metrics.**
- `speed`, `risk`, `quality`, `cost`, `capacity`, `experience` (index §5). Metrics shown: share of root causes in the model's top five (Meta, 42% in backtesting, web monorepo investigations at creation time) [META-RCA-2024]; micro and macro F1 for category prediction (Microsoft) [MSFT-RCACOPILOT-2024 §5.2]; alerts evaluated (89%) and confirmed (38% of evaluated), sensitivity 82% of 9,805 retrospectively identified sepsis cases, 1.85-hour (95% CI 1.66-2.00) median reduction in time to first antibiotic order when confirmed within 3 hours [HENRY-TREWS-2022 abstract]; energy savings of 9% and 13% at two live sites against the existing controller in alternating-day tests [DEEPMIND-BCOOLER-2022 §5.1-5.2]; false-positive reduction (Danske, 50%, bank executive statement) [FINTECHFUTURES-DANSKE-2017].
- Quality metrics to require: alert-to-action rate, override rate with reasons, share of alerts that were true, evidence-closure rate for diagnoses, rate of automatic fallback or manual disable, count of actions run unattended and reversed.

**Governance points.**
- NIST AI RMF 1.0: MEASURE 2.4, MANAGE 2.4, MANAGE 4.1, GOVERN 1.5 (ongoing monitoring and review planned, with roles) [NIST-AIRMF-2023]. NIST AI 600-1 §2.2 (confabulation) and §2.7 (automation bias) apply where generative models explain alerts [NIST-AI600-2024].
- EU AI Act: Annex III 2 lists AI systems "intended to be used as safety components in the management and operation of critical digital infrastructure, road traffic, or in the supply of water, gas, heating or electricity" [EU-AIACT-2024 Annex III]; deployers must assign oversight to people with "competence, training and authority", monitor operation and keep logs for at least six months [EU-AIACT-2024 Art. 26(2), (5), (6)]; providers run post-market monitoring [EU-AIACT-2024 Art. 72]; Art. 14(4)(e) requires the ability to "interrupt the system through a 'stop' button or a similar procedure that allows the system to come to a halt in a safe state". Annex III obligations now apply from 2 December 2027 [FPF-OMNIBUS-2026].
- US federal: OMB M-25-21 minimum practices include ongoing monitoring designed to detect "changes to an AI system after deployment", training for operators, human oversight and, "when practicable", a fail-safe; its footnote allows that an AI-enabled safety mechanism may require immediate automated action, with oversight and accountability still determined [OMB-M2521-2025 §4(b) items iii-v; fn 31].
- Health: FDA's January 2026 clinical decision support guidance gives as an example that software which analyzes patient-specific information to detect a life-threatening condition such as sepsis and alerts a clinician "is a device function" [FDA-CDS-2026 example 11].
- Finance: US model-risk guidance expects ongoing monitoring including "analysis of overrides with appropriate documentation", noting a high override rate is often a sign the model needs revision [FED-SR117-2011 §V]. SEC Rule 15c3-5 market-access controls were found violated in Knight [SEC-KNIGHT-2013 ¶38-¶44].
- ISO/IEC 42001: not opened (search-only); no clause-level claim.

**How it evolves up the ladder.** Advisor inference anchored in sources: start with diagnosis support where a person verifies (Meta, Microsoft); add "act" only through a closed, pre-authorized set with automatic fallback (BCOOLER); widen autonomy as measured accuracy and override data accumulate, which requires the monitoring of the monitor (Tricorder-style not-useful tracking [ACM-TRICORDER-2018]; model-risk override analysis [FED-SR117-2011]). The sources describe plans, not results, for higher autonomy: Meta "envisions" systems that "autonomously execute full workflows" [META-RCA-2024 "The future"].

**Anti-patterns (where not to use it).**
- Automatic actions that are hard to reverse with no closed action set, no halt control and no tested incident procedure (Knight) [SEC-KNIGHT-2013].
- Paging humans on every anomaly [GOOGLE-SREBOOK-2017].
- Time-critical clinical alerts without local validation and a clinician response design [WONG-ESM-2021; HENRY-TREWS-2022; FDA-CDS-2026].
- Treating a high headline accuracy as proof of diagnosis (evidence closure gap) [CLOUDOPSBENCH-2026].
- "Agentic" relabeling of work that needs only a rule or a report: Gartner states "Many use cases positioned as agentic today don't require agentic implementations" and forecasts cancellations of over 40% of agentic AI projects by end of 2027 (forecast) [GARTNER-AGENTIC-2025].

## 4. Expert moves and probes

### 4.1 `full-population-testing`

1. **"What fraction of items do you check today, and how were they chosen?"** Reveals whether sampling is risk-based or random, and whether the real constraint is time, cost or a standard. Anchor: sampling versus "selecting all items" versus "specific items" are different things with different conclusions [PCAOB-AS1105-2026 ¶.24-.28].
2. **"If we tested everything and flagged 1% of items, who would look at them, and how long does one take?"** Reveals the capacity limit on the exceptions queue; flagged volume above it turns into a rubber stamp. (Advisor inference; see Tricorder trust lesson [ACM-TRICORDER-2018].)
3. **"What share of what your current alerts flag turns out to be real?"** Reveals the baseline precision, the number a new tool has to beat. Danske's pre-AI figure and HSBC's alert reduction show how large the gap can be [FINTECHFUTURES-DANSKE-2017; GOOGLE-HSBC-2023].
4. **"How will you find what the tool misses?"** Reveals whether there is a plan for a random control sample, as DWP runs, or a parallel rules-based routine, as in the FRC example [DWP-ATRS-2025; FRC-AIAUDIT-2025 p.10].
5. **"What happens to the person or the item when it is flagged, and who decides?"** Reveals whether there is adverse action without a human decision. The failure cases all had that gap [MICHIGANPUBLIC-MIDAS-2016; CONVERSATION-ROBODEBT-2023]; the supervisory model is a human decision with appeal [DWP-ATRS-2025 §3.2; OMB-M2521-2025 §4(b)].
6. **"Is the data complete, and who has tested that?"** Reveals the quality of the population. "100% tested" is meaningless if the extract is incomplete [PCAOB-AS1105-2026 ¶.10; FRC-AIAUDIT-2025 p.11].
7. **"Which groups are flagged more often, and are the flags right more often there too?"** Reveals cohort precision. DWP's assessment pairs referral rate with confirmed-fraud rate by cohort [DWP-FAIRNESS-2025 ¶14-¶17].
8. **"Who reviews or inspects this work, and will they accept evidence from the tool?"** Reveals reviewer-acceptance risk [EMETT-CAR-2023 abstract].
9. **"How will the savings or findings be counted, and who agrees the definition?"** Reveals attribution risk: adjusted versus unadjusted [HHSOIG-FPS-2015].
10. **"Does this need a model, or would a rule over everything do?"** Reveals whether the pattern is being over-engineered [FRC-AIAUDIT-2025 p.10].

### 4.2 `monitor-diagnose-act`

1. **"When a signal fires today, what is the first thing a person does?"** Reveals the repetitive diagnostic steps worth automating (Microsoft's handlers collect the same data per alert type) [MSFT-RCACOPILOT-2024 abstract].
2. **"What share of your alerts lead to action, and what do people do with the rest?"** Reveals alert-to-action and override rates, the leading indicator of fatigue [POLY-OVERRIDE-2020; GOOGLE-SREBOOK-2017; FED-SR117-2011].
3. **"Which actions could the system take on its own, and which could not be reversed?"** Reveals the closed action set; irreversible actions stay human-approved. Anchor: OMB fail-safe wording and fn 31; Knight [OMB-M2521-2025 §4(b) item v; SEC-KNIGHT-2013].
4. **"Who can switch it off, how fast, and when did you last test that?"** Reveals the stop control the EU AI Act and NIST expect [EU-AIACT-2024 Art. 14(4)(e); NIST-AIRMF-2023 MANAGE 2.4].
5. **"What do you do when the suggestion is wrong, and how would you know?"** Reveals whether answers carry evidence a person can reproduce and whether accuracy is measured on the evidence process, not only the answer [META-RCA-2024; CLOUDOPSBENCH-2026].
6. **"Do you have historical incidents with known causes?"** Reveals whether backtesting is possible; both Meta and Microsoft used labeled history [META-RCA-2024; MSFT-RCACOPILOT-2024 §5.1].
7. **"Has this been validated on your own data, not the supplier's?"** Reveals external-validity risk [WONG-ESM-2021].
8. **"Are the people responding trained on what the system can get wrong, and is time pressure part of the setting?"** Reveals automation-bias exposure [GODDARD-AB-2012; FDA-CDS-2026 pp.15-16].
9. **"What is the cheapest version: only detect, only diagnose, or also act?"** Reveals the right first rung and keeps `delegate` out of Adopting teams (index §1).
10. **"Does an outcome improve only when people respond in time?"** Reveals the response-design dependency: TREWS associations were with confirmation within 3 hours [ADAMS-TREWS-2022 abstract].

## 5. Evidence on what works and what fails

**Measured results (provenance in brackets).**
- Treasury: over $4 billion prevented and recovered in FY2024 versus $652.7 million in FY2023 [self-reported] [TREASURY-OPI-2024].
- HSBC: alerts down 60%, two to four times as much suspicious activity, 12-month before-and-after comparison [self-reported] [GOOGLE-HSBC-2023 fn 1].
- DWP: 2.5 times (2025-26) and about 3 times (2024-25) more effective than a randomized control sample; fairness inconsistencies for some age groups and non-UK nationals [self-reported, published] [DWP-ATRS-2025; DWP-FAIRNESS-2025].
- CMS FPS: certified adjusted savings $133.2 million versus $454.0 million unadjusted, CY2014, ROI $2.84 [independent] [HHSOIG-FPS-2015].
- Tricorder: scale and click ratios; 10% effective-false-positive criterion [self-reported] [ACM-TRICORDER-2018].
- TREWS: 590,736 monitored; 89% of alerts evaluated, 38% confirmed; mortality and antibiotic-time associations (observational) [self-reported, peer-reviewed] [HENRY-TREWS-2022; ADAMS-TREWS-2022].
- BCOOLER: about 9% and 13% energy savings, alternating-day A/B [vendor-published] [DEEPMIND-BCOOLER-2022].
- RCACopilot: micro-F1 0.766, macro-F1 0.533 on 653 incidents; over 30 teams, over four years [self-reported] [MSFT-RCACOPILOT-2024].
- Meta: 42% top-five backtest accuracy [self-reported] [META-RCA-2024].
- Danske: 50% fewer false positives (executive statement) [self-reported/vendor] [FINTECHFUTURES-DANSKE-2017].

**Failure analyses and independent measurements.**
- Epic Sepsis Model external validation: AUC 0.63; 67% of sepsis cases not flagged; alerts on 18% of hospitalized patients [independent] [WONG-ESM-2021].
- MiDAS: 93% wrong in a state review of some flagged cases; $20 million settlement [press; explainer] [MICHIGANPUBLIC-MIDAS-2016; UMICH-MIDAS-2024].
- Robodebt: income averaging lacked legal basis; 433,000 recipients per the court; Royal Commission recommended an automated-decision monitoring body [independent, relayed] [CONVERSATION-ROBODEBT-2023; ROBODEBTRC-REPORT-2023].
- Dutch childcare: nationality as a model parameter; racial profiling per Amnesty [independent NGO] [AMNESTY-DUTCH-2021].
- Knight Capital: 45 minutes, $460 million loss, no halt control [independent regulator] [SEC-KNIGHT-2013].
- FRC thematic review of six large firms: no formal monitoring of the audit-quality impact of automated tools; recertification triggers informal [independent regulator] [FRC-ATTREVIEW-2025 pp.20-21].
- Alert overrides in clinical order entry: average override 46.2% to 96.2%; 29.4% to 100% of overrides appropriate [independent systematic review of 23 studies] [POLY-OVERRIDE-2020].
- Automation bias: 74-paper systematic review; mitigators include training, accountability, confidence display [independent] [GODDARD-AB-2012].
- LLM agents for cloud root-cause analysis: best answer accuracy 0.76 and 0.68 on two workloads, evidence closure 0.38 and 0.15 [benchmark authors] [CLOUDOPSBENCH-2026].
- Journal-entry detectors evaluated without a review budget can look strong while covering one anomaly type [preprint, October 2026] [GRONEWALD-ARXIV-2026].
- Reviewers rate data-and-analytics audit procedures lower for lower perceived effort, at constant assurance [independent experiments] [EMETT-CAR-2023]. Interviews with 44 auditors, regulators and AI providers suggest automating "low-level" tasks may remove sensemaking that builds early-career judgment [independent] [SAMIOLO-CAR-2023].
- Industry perception: eight AML specialists interviewed (480 minutes) identify high false-positive rates in rules-based monitoring and want explainability [independent, small] [OZTAS-FGCS-2024].
- Forecast: over 40% of agentic AI projects canceled by end of 2027, due to costs, unclear value or "inadequate risk controls" [analyst forecast] [GARTNER-AGENTIC-2025].

**What is absent.** No controlled trial of full-population testing or of monitor-diagnose-act that randomizes at the work-unit level was found. No source reports a parallel run against a human-only process with error rates for either pattern; the closest are DWP's random control sample and the CMS FPS inspector-general certification. Override and escalation rates are published for clinical alerts and implied by TREWS acceptance data, but not found for audit, payments or operations deployments. FRC found no formal audit-quality impact monitoring at six firms [FRC-ATTREVIEW-2025 p.20].
