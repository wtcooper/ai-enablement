# Cross-cutting topic: data and evidence acquisition (assurance)

- **Archetype:** assurance. External audit and other assurance services (financial statement and ICFR audits, SOC, sustainability and other attest), performed by firms of all sizes, plus overlapping in-house internal audit.
- **Topic:** data-evidence-acquisition. Client data and evidence acquisition as the enabling layer for AI in assurance.
- **Prepared:** 2026-10-16. **Landscape read first:** `research/runs/wave1/landscape-assurance.md` (source keys reused where the landscape has one; new keys follow `PUBLISHER-SHORT-YYYY`).
- **Method note.** Pages were opened with `curl` (and `pdftotext` for PDFs). Where a host returned 403, the `r.jina.ai` text mirror was used and is marked in the Sources table. Two web searches were used, only to confirm SAS No. 150 and to test for independent audit sources on AI-forged evidence. Vendor and firm figures are labelled `vendor-published` or `self-reported`; none of the figures about time saved or accuracy in section 2 is independent. Quotations are under 25 words.

## 1. Summary

- **Data access is the binding constraint for small and mid-size US firms, and it bites hardest among the most advanced.** In the CPA.com/AICPA benchmark (54 US firms with complete data, June to August 2025, not representative of all firms), "poor quality client data" was a top blocker for 60% and "lack of access to client information and systems" for 53% (CPACOM-ATR-2025, p. 7). The report says firms further along in transformation are more likely to name client-data access as their biggest obstacle (CPACOM-ATR-2025, p. 7).
- **The most automated audit steps are the data-in steps.** Confirmations (39%), foot-and-proof (39%) and trial-balance taxonomy and import (35%) lead; data ingestion is 19% and substantive analytics 11% (CPACOM-ATR-2025, p. 9). The same report says even firms using substantive analytics cite difficulty gaining access to client data (CPACOM-ATR-2025, p. 19).
- **Standards moved first on confirmations, the one place where "direct from source" is now written into rules.** PCAOB AS 2310 (fiscal years ending on or after 15 Jun 2025) lets an auditor satisfy the cash and receivables requirement by directly accessing information held by a knowledgeable external source, with the auditor in control (PCAOB-AS2310-2023, para .24). AICPA SAS No. 150, published 13 Jul 2026 and effective for periods ending on or after 15 Dec 2028, adds a similar recognition and a new cash-confirmation expectation (JOFA-SAS150-2026).
- **Regulators treat data reliability as a precondition, not something AI removes.** AS 1105.10A requires auditors to evaluate the reliability of external information the company provides in electronic form (PCAOB-TAA-2024; PCAOB-STAFF-1105-2025). The IAASB's August 2026 ED-500 lists extraction, transfer integrity and form of information as factors in how much evaluation is needed, and says GenAI may make it impossible to evaluate a tool's inputs (IAASB-ED500-2026, App. 3 and App. 4, paras 67-68).
- **A vendor layer is forming between client systems and audit AI.** Named examples: Circit (bank and counterparty data), Validis (ledger and trial-balance extraction), Suralink (PBC requests with agents that screen uploads), Inflo, DataSnipper's Alwin (connects to file stores and ERPs), and the Big Four platforms. Every outcome figure found for these is vendor-published or customer-quoted; none is independent.
- **Evidence authenticity has become a stated risk, with thin audit-specific measurement.** ACFE/SAS (713 anti-fraud professionals, March 2026) report 75% saw an increase in GenAI document fraud or forgery over two years (ACFE-SAS-2026). The IAASB's ED-500 already asks auditors to consider authenticity where it matters and says auditors are not expected to be authentication experts (IAASB-ED500-2026, A46-A50). No source found measures how often AI-forged documents reach an audit file.
- **The measured data on what extraction and matching tools save is all vendor or self-reported.** Examples: a sole practitioner using Thomson Reuters Audit Intelligence Test reports about 16 hours down to five or six per engagement (TR-AGENTIC-2025); Moore Polska reports up to 20 hours per audit (CIRCIT-CS-MOORE-2026). Treat both as anecdotes.
- **The AI transformation conversation should start with the client-data pipeline, not the model.** KPMG's own SOX 2030 paper says continuous automated testing "cannot be achieved without continuous evidence collection" and calls manual PBC processes incompatible with AI-enabled testing (KPMG-SOX-2026).

## 2. Findings

### 2.1 What the barrier looks like, with measured data

- **US small and mid-size firms.** In the CPA.com/AICPA Audit Benchmark Survey, 74 firms registered and 54 with complete data were analysed; the survey was open June to August 2025, marketed through campaigns and conferences, and the report calls it not representative of all firms (CPACOM-ATR-2025, Introduction; Appendix I).
  - Top-ranked perceived blockers to transformation: staff not trained 66%, poor quality client data 60%, lack of access to client information and systems 53%, budget constraints 53%, current methodology 30% (CPACOM-ATR-2025, p. 7). The chart does not state its denominator; the methodology gives 54 firms.
  - The report says firms further along are more likely to name client-data access as their biggest obstacle, while firms early in the process more often blame methodology (CPACOM-ATR-2025, p. 7).
  - Automated solutions in place: workflow management 39%, foot-and-proof 39%, confirmations 39%, trial-balance taxonomy and import 35%, engagement letters 26%, risk assessment 22%, data ingestion 19%, substantive analytics 11%, none 22% (CPACOM-ATR-2025, p. 9).
  - 89% do not currently use technology to enable substantive analytics; the most common data-analytics tool is Excel/Power BI (CPACOM-ATR-2025, p. 19).
  - The report's recommended step is to treat client data management as a priority because analytics success depends on access to data (CPACOM-ATR-2025, p. 20, "Step 4").
- **Internal audit.** In Gartner's survey of 119 chief audit executives (August 2025, released 27 Jan 2026), 83% of audit functions were piloting or using AI. Gartner's research chief says progress with GenAI is often limited by poor data quality, technical skills and access, and that analytics outcomes are hampered by data quality (GARTNER-2026).
- **UK small audits.** Inflo, a vendor, reports that at its customer roundtable auditors said many SMEs lack mature financial systems or controls, so analytics and AI use is "often constrained by client-side limitations" such as disorganised records and lack of integration (INFLO-FRCSME-2025). This is a vendor's account of its roundtable, published 22 Jul 2025, about the FRC's SME market study; the FRC page itself says the study found the SME audit market broadly functioning well (FRC-SME-2025).
- **Vendor framing of the same problem.** Suralink says engagement teams are "reduced to manual request managers" because each client upload must be opened and checked for match and completeness (SURALINK-CTEST-2026). On its Client Readiness Gap page it states that over 30% of engagements fail to be delivered on time and on budget because of firm-client misalignment; the page gives no source for that figure (SURALINK-GAP-2026). `vendor-published`, unsourced.
- **Data-quality gates in regulator examples.** The FRC's hypothetical firm ("XYZ LLP") mandates its journal-entry AI tool on engagements where data quality is sufficient, defined as general ledger data that is complete and accurate and contains date, time, poster, description, value and account references (FRC-AIAUDIT-2025, Illustrative example, "Use of the tool"). This is an illustrative case, not a reported deployment.

### 2.2 Extracting data from client ERPs and systems; trial-balance and ledger import

- **Standards.** BS ISO 21378:2019, "Audit data collection" (published 30 Nov 2019), defines common accounting data elements and file formats across purchase, sales, inventory, fixed assets and financial reporting modules, aiming to resolve the problems auditors face when requesting data (BSI-ISO21378-2019). No adoption data was found. The AICPA's Audit Data Standards page could not be opened (see Gaps).
- **ED-500 on extraction.** The proposed ISA 500 (Revised) says the ability to extract information in a usable form may decide whether an auditor can use tools, including format conversion and system limits on large volumes (IAASB-ED500-2026, App. 4 para 1). It also lists "interoperability of information" and "degree of digitization" as factors in when tools are appropriate (IAASB-ED500-2026, App. 3 para 2). It notes privacy law may bar use of sensitive client information in some tools without consent or safeguards (IAASB-ED500-2026, App. 4 para 3).
- **Extraction vendors.**
  - **Validis** says it extracts and standardises transaction-level data from "any accounting system", covering cloud ERPs, on-premises ERPs and uploaded reports, delivered by portal, API or an MCP server; its audit pitch is "GL & TB drill-down. Saving 8+ hours per audit" (VALIDIS-2026). `vendor-published`, no method stated. Its customer logos include Deloitte, Grant Thornton and Withum.
  - **Inflo** says it extracts and standardises client data from any accounting system and supports full-population testing (INFLO-ADA-2026). Three UK firms on an Inflo-hosted panel (15 Sep 2026) describe analytics starting with journal testing, and one says journal-risk assessment varied across engagements before it standardised (INFLO-ANALYTICS-2026). `vendor-published`.
  - **DataSnipper** announced Alwin (7 Oct 2026): cloud agents that "connect to the systems you already use, from file storage platforms to ERPs" and pull in hundreds to thousands of files, with more than 50 pre-built agents and each result linked back to source evidence (DATASNIPPER-ALWIN-2026). An earlier product, AI Extractions, structures data from documents such as bank statements into Excel (DATASNIPPER-AI-2026). The Alwin page names FGMK as a design partner; no outcome figures were given.
  - **Caseware** describes Verity retrieving the trial balance, checking the trial-balance date against the engagement date, and in a CwX 2026 demonstration surfacing a $118,000 variance between the trial balance and board minutes (CASEWARE-VERITY-2026, both blog pages). A Document Intelligence Agent that extracts source-document information into workpapers was in beta (CASEWARE-VERITY-2026, CwX page). A demonstration, not a reported result.
- **Big Four.** EY says its Canvas platform processes more than 1.4 trillion lines of journal-entry data a year and that its multi-agent framework is embedded across all audit phases now, with all end-to-end activities expected supported by 2028 (EY-CANVAS-2026, 7 Apr 2026). Deloitte says Omnia agents execute preliminary procedures including data extraction and evidence analysis (DELOITTE-OMNIA-2026, 24 Jun 2026). KPMG's April 2025 release names expense vouching and unrecorded-liabilities search as agent tasks (KPMG-CLARA-2025). None of these releases describes how client data enters the platform.
- **Research on test data.** A single-author preprint (arXiv, 23 Jun 2026) proposes masking and synthetic provisioning of ERP data for control testing because production copies expose sensitive records; it states its single dataset is synthetic and results show "internal consistency rather than production validation" (ARXIV-ERP-2026). Low evidentiary weight; relevant to the privacy barrier.

### 2.3 PBC and request portals; document intake

- **Suralink** markets a request-to-review platform. Its Document Screener Agent checks each upload against the request; its Sample Testing Agent recognises an uploaded sample list, configures requests and runs tests "the moment each client document arrives", logging every step and exporting to Excel (SURALINK-CTEST-2026; SURALINK-AGENTS-2026). It lists more than 50 API endpoints and integrations including DataSnipper, Materia, and a connector for querying engagement data from Claude (SURALINK-INTEG-2026). Pages are undated. `vendor-published`.
- **Circit's PBC module** was adopted by Clark Schaefer Hackett (a US top-100 firm) after trials from August 2024; the firm rolled it across hundreds of engagements within a month after training in December 2024, and the case study says audit quality remained consistent while speed, accountability and client satisfaction improved (CIRCIT-CS-CSH-2026, metadata date 19 May 2026). The same case study describes the previous state: static Excel request lists, email, and files whose relation to the request was unclear. Self-reported.
- **Thomson Reuters** announced CoCounsel Document Analysis and Audit Intelligence Test on 5 Nov 2025. Early adopters of Document Analysis reported up to 70% faster preparation and workpaper review (TR-AGENTIC-2025). Audit Intelligence Test, developed with Valid8 Financial, matches samples to evidence and traces transactions to bank activity; a sole practitioner in Kentucky is quoted as going from about 16 hours to five or six per engagement (TR-AGENTIC-2025). `vendor-published`; one customer quote.
- **PwC** (28 Oct 2025 briefing) describes Evidence Match as an agent-led module that extracts, matches and validates evidence against documents for cash, receivables and payables, with an evidence trail, and says controls-testing automation is expected to follow the same pattern (PWC-AT-2025). It said end-to-end AI-driven automation should be expected within calendar 2026.
- **Optro** (14 Oct 2026) launched "Agentic Stakeholder Management" to reduce evidence follow-up by helping preparers clarify requests and fix gaps before submission; automated evidence collection is described as upcoming. It cites Reddit's internal audit team automating 175 controls (over 40% of SOX scope) in three months, with 60% average reduction in testing time per control (OPTRO-CRX-2026). `vendor-published`, internal-audit context.
- **Fieldguide** says its agents draft requests, tie out workpapers and surface exceptions (FIELDGUIDE-HOME-2026, undated marketing page). Funding is covered in the landscape (FIELDGUIDE-2026) and was not re-read here.

### 2.4 Confirmation platforms and direct bank data

- **Rule changes.**
  - PCAOB AS 2310 (adopted in Release 2023-008; effective for fiscal years ending on or after 15 Jun 2025): the auditor selects items, sends requests and receives responses directly (paras .15-.16). Intermediaries may be used for direct electronic transmission, but the auditor must understand the intermediary's controls against interception and alteration, test their design and operation, and assess whether the company can override them; if not satisfied the auditor should not use the intermediary (para .17; App. B paras B1-B2).
  - For cash and receivables, the auditor should confirm "or otherwise obtain relevant and reliable audit evidence by directly accessing information maintained by a knowledgeable external source" (para .24). Para .18 lists signs a response may have been intercepted or altered, such as an unexpected return address or no signature.
  - AICPA SAS No. 150 (approved May 2026, published 13 Jul 2026, effective for periods ending on or after 15 Dec 2028, early adoption permitted) requires confirmation procedures for third-party-held cash unless certain conditions exist, recognises direct access to a knowledgeable external source, updates guidance on intermediaries and sets conditions for negative confirmations (JOFA-SAS150-2026; DART-SAS150-2026).
  - ED-500 includes confirmation through an electronic platform as an example of a substantive tool use (IAASB-ED500-2026, App. 3 para 3).
- **Circit.** In a 2 Feb 2026 post on its $22 million growth investment, Circit says that in the previous 12 months auditors used it to confirm assets and liabilities for 150,000 corporate entities and verify on average more than 100 million transactions a month; that more than 400 audit firms use it, including every one of the top 20 global networks; and that it connects over 30,000 banks, fund administrators and counterparties (CIRCIT-2026). A 9 Sep 2026 post gives different counts (over 6,500 connections, 53.8 million transactions verified in 2026, over 100,000 audit engagements to date), so the figures are not comparable across pages (CIRCIT-AS2310-2026). All `vendor-published`.
  - On 9 Sep 2026 it announced direct bank connections for its US network, saying all confirmation types are now on one platform (CIRCIT-DIRECT-2026). It argues that AS 2310 and SAS 150 make direct source access "the new benchmark" (CIRCIT-AS2310-2026); that reading is Circit's. The standard text requires auditor control and intermediary due diligence (PCAOB-AS2310-2023, App. B).
  - Its Verify product describes consented access to balances and transactions from banks "using regulated APIs, industry schemes or direct bank partnerships", with AI-enriched transaction data and 2-way and 3-way reconciliations (CIRCIT-VERIFY-2026). The API page says data flows are encrypted and carry a digital certificate (CIRCIT-API-2026).
  - Claimed effects: "up to 80% faster" confirmations and about 20 hours per audit redirected (CIRCIT-AS2310-2026, vendor); a partner at an unnamed firm quoted at 4 hours saved per client and bank-confirmation turnaround down 80% (CIRCIT-HOME-2026, customer quote); Moore Polska reports up to 20 hours saved per audit (CIRCIT-CS-MOORE-2026); Beever Struthers says 80 to 90% of its confirmation requests run through Circit (CIRCIT-CS-BS-2026).
- **BDO Netherlands and Rabobank.** The case study (metadata date 21 May 2026) says BDO NL already ran automated tests and dashboards, but data acquisition still depended on clients downloading bank files and uploading them to the firm's portal; a Rabobank consent flow now lets customers share bank data directly with the auditor via Circit, and the firm's ambition is to reduce reliance on sampling and test 100%, with a longer-term "Digital Twin Auditing" vision (CIRCIT-CS-BDONL-2026). Self-reported by a vendor case study; no outcome figures.
- **Thomson Reuters Confirmation** says it validates the business details of both auditors and bankers before they can use the platform, logs all user activity, and is used by "1.5 million professionals" in over 170 countries (TR-CONFIRM-2026, undated). `vendor-published`.
- **How the PCAOB treats automated bank feeds inside the client.** In October 2025 staff guidance, an example where the company receives cash receipts through an automated bank feed converted into its ERP concludes the auditor must either test the information against the bank statement or test controls over receiving and processing it (ITGCs and automated application controls) (PCAOB-STAFF-1105-2025, Example 2). Another example (many small invoices, lower risk, unmodified PDFs) concludes there is no more than a remote possibility of unreliable modification, and under the Board's policy statement (Release 2025-004, 18 Sep 2025) staff will not treat the absence of separate testing as noncompliance (PCAOB-STAFF-1105-2025, Example 1). This is staff guidance, not Board rules.

### 2.5 Reliability of data an AI tool consumes

- **AS 1105.10A.** The PCAOB's technology-assisted-analysis amendments (Release 2024-007) add that the auditor should evaluate whether external information provided by the company in electronic form and used as evidence is reliable, by understanding the source and the company's process (including modifications) and then testing whether the information was modified or testing controls over receipt, maintenance and processing (PCAOB-TAA-2024, Federal Register 89 FR 54922, discussion of final para .10A). Examples include bank statements, customer order and cash-receipts information and third-party carrier shipping data. Effective for audits of fiscal years beginning on or after 15 Dec 2025 (PCAOB-TAA-2024, "Effective Date"), so applicable to calendar-2026 audits.
- **ED-500 on tool inputs.** Factors that increase the extent of evaluation: volume, complexity, less-structured information (narratives, video) and a higher possibility that integrity was not maintained "during its collection, extraction or transfer" (IAASB-ED500-2026, App. 3 para 6). It names automation bias risk (App. 3 paras 7-8). The explanatory memorandum records that the IAASB considered a conditional requirement on tool inputs, operation and outputs and decided against it, because GenAI-enabled tools may make it impossible to evaluate inputs or confirm operation, preferring guidance (IAASB-ED500-2026, EM paras 67-69). Exposure draft dated August 2026; comments due 15 Dec 2026.
- **FRC.** In its March 2026 contract-review example (an illustration), the LLM extracts risk indicators from uploaded contracts to target the sample; the guidance lists risks that the extraction prompt is not fit for purpose or that the LLM fails to extract all and only the specified content, and notes the firm adjusts for observed extraction performance, "which will not be perfect" (FRC-GENAI-2026, Illustrative example 2). It does not address who guarantees that the uploaded contracts are the complete population in the passage read; the example's documentation lists the contracts selected.
- **IFIAR.** Of IFIAR members answering the 2024 survey, 88% frequently saw automated tools in journal-entry testing (48 respondents), 55% in risk assessment and in substantive procedures, 43% in internal-controls testing and 39% in IT applications and security configurations (IFIAR-TTF-2025, p. 5). 31% reported findings that a tool was not used as intended, with misread results, thin outlier testing and weak ITGC testing among common findings (IFIAR-TTF-2025, p. 5). The report does not discuss data extraction; the link between journal-entry tool use and ledger extraction is an inference.
- **AICPA.** The 13 Oct 2026 practice aid says that for a client's own AI use, the quantity, quality, accuracy, completeness, relevance and diversity of data can significantly affect an AI system's reliability (AICPA-PA-2026). It says more guidance on evaluating audit evidence when a company uses AI will be added later (AICPA-PA-2026; JOFA-AIPA-2026).

### 2.6 Evidence authenticity and AI-forged documents

- **What the standards say today.** ED-500 treats authenticity as a reliability attribute (source authorised, information not altered). It says authenticity is of significance depending on judgment; relevant indicators include indirectly obtained photocopies or scans, unusual last-minute changes and unusual form; procedures may include testing authorisation and access controls, understanding digital signatures and confirming directly with a third party; and the auditor "is neither trained as, nor expected to be, an expert in the authentication of records" (IAASB-ED500-2026, A46-A50). It also says screenshots from IT applications may be insufficient if the auditor cannot tell when they were taken (App. 4 para 1). The text does not mention generative AI in the authenticity paragraphs.
- **Measured prevalence in fraud generally.** The ACFE/SAS 2026 Anti-Fraud Technology Benchmarking Report (713 respondents in eight regions; 25 Mar 2026 release): 75% report an increase in generative-AI document fraud or forgery over the past two years, and 55% expect deepfake social engineering and GenAI document forgery to increase significantly over the next 24 months; only 7% say their organisations are more than moderately prepared for AI-fueled fraud (ACFE-SAS-2026). The respondents are mostly anti-fraud professionals in public sector (26%) and banking (23%), not auditors.
- **Regulator and forecast context (not audit-specific).** FinCEN's alert of 13 Nov 2024 says criminals used GenAI to alter or create identity documents to defeat verification at financial institutions (FINCEN-DEEPFAKE-2024). Deloitte's Center for Financial Services predicted (29 May 2024) US gen-AI-enabled fraud losses could reach $40 billion by 2027 from $12.3 billion in 2023; this is Deloitte's forecast, built from scenario scoring of FBI IC3 fraud types (DELOITTE-FRAUD-2024).
- **Practitioner and vendor discourse.** An ISACA blog by an internal-audit lead describes receiving an AI-generated document with synthetic signatures and fabricated metadata, not malicious in that case, and argues for a "zero trust" stance (ISACA-EVIDENCE-2026); a single anecdote. Wolters Kluwer (6 May 2026) argues the same, recommending metadata and provenance checks, out-of-band verification and treating detection tools as a supplement (WK-AUTH-2026). The search for independent audit-firm or regulator sources on this returned only this vendor piece.
- **Detection tools.** AppZen describes "digital fingerprinting" of metadata in AI-generated invoices and receipts, and quotes "95% fraud detection accuracy" on its marketing page (APPZEN-FRAUD-2026); Inscribe says "1 in 16 documents show signs of fraud" and "AI-generated fraud grew 5x last year" (INSCRIBE-2026). Both are `vendor-published`, unsourced on the page, and aimed at expense, AP and lending rather than audit.
- **Provenance standards.** The C2PA coalition develops open technical specifications for content provenance (C2PA-ABOUT-2026). No source found shows audit firms or client ERPs using them.
- **Early research warning.** A 2019 paper showed adversarial autoencoders could generate fraudulent journal entries designed to evade computer-assisted audit techniques (ARXIV-DEEPFAKE-ACCT-2019). It predates modern GenAI.
- **SOC evidence integrity.** An anonymous post alleged that compliance-automation startup Delve produced fabricated evidence of board meetings and tests for customers and used rubber-stamping audit firms; Delve called the claims misleading (TECHCRUNCH-DELVE-2026, 21 Mar 2026). Unproven allegations; relevant because they concern evidence generated by the same platform that collects it. The AICPA warned on SOC tool-provider arrangements in April 2026 (JOFA-SOCETH-2026, from the landscape, not re-opened).
- **Confirmations as an authenticity control.** PCAOB para .18 and Circit's own argument both rest on the idea that evidence passing through the client's hands can be altered; Circit says its network verifies counterparties before a confirmation reaches them, which removes the callback step (CIRCIT-AS2310-2026; vendor claim).

### 2.7 How AI changes evidence gathering

- **From sampling to whole populations, if the data is complete.** ED-520/ED-500 application material shows tools analysing entire populations, for example agreeing all sales invoices from an online retailer's records (IAASB-ED500-2026, A-material on technological tools; the landscape also reads ED-520). Inflo's customers describe sample caps disappearing as tools arrive, so teams faced testing hundreds of items instead of dozens (INFLO-ANALYTICS-2026).
- **From the auditor chasing the client to the agent triggering on upload.** Suralink's agent runs the moment a document arrives (SURALINK-CTEST-2026); Optro's tool coaches the preparer before submission (OPTRO-CRX-2026).
- **From client-prepared files to source-held data.** Circit/BDO NL (bank data by consent flow) and Validis (ledger extracts) replace client downloads (CIRCIT-CS-BDONL-2026; VALIDIS-2026).
- **Toward continuous evidence.** KPMG's SOX 2030 paper (24 Sep 2026) says organisations best positioned for AI-enabled assurance embed control evidence in process workflows, store it in enterprise systems or standard repositories that tools can analyse without intervention, and calls "provided by client" processes incompatible with AI-enabled testing (KPMG-SOX-2026, "Automating evidence collection"). It also describes synthetic data for testing controls in new ERP environments before real data is loaded (KPMG-SOX-2026). A forecast by KPMG, not a reported practice.
- **New access protocols.** MindBridge (22 Sep 2026) previewed an MCP server and an agent that evaluates data suitability for an analysis (MINDBRIDGE-ARA-2026); Validis and Suralink also offer MCP or Claude connectors (VALIDIS-2026; SURALINK-INTEG-2026). Vendors name MCP as an access layer; no audit firm adoption was found.
- **Quality of what the tools do with the evidence.** The ICAEW's Sep 2025 test found chatbots did not perform ISA 315 risk assessment as a human would (ICAEW-PIERPOINT-2025, from the landscape; not re-read here). IFIAR's finding that 31% of members reported tools not used as intended (IFIAR-TTF-2025, p. 5) applies equally to extraction-and-match tools. No independent accuracy measurement for any document-matching agent was found.

## 3. What it means for an AI transformation conversation

### 3.1 Implications

1. **Frame the readiness question around the data pipeline.** In the best available US small-firm data, the data steps are both the most automated (confirmations, trial-balance import, foot-and-proof) and the most cited blockers (client data quality and system access). A firm that proposes an "AI audit assistant" without a client-data acquisition plan is probably solving the second problem first.
2. **Distinguish three acquisition channels with different reliability.** (a) Client-supplied files and screenshots (weakest on authenticity under ED-500 and AS 1105.10A); (b) client-system extracts through an extraction layer (reliability depends on completeness, field mapping and ITGCs); (c) source-held data obtained directly from banks and counterparties (strongest under AS 2310 and SAS 150, but only if the intermediary's controls and independence from the client are assessed).
3. **Third-party platforms become part of the evidence chain.** AS 2310 App. B requires intermediary due diligence for confirmations, including whether the company can override controls. The same logic is likely to be asked about any platform that sits between a client and the audit file (an inference, not a stated rule).
4. **Authenticity is shifting from a fraud-brainstorm topic to a workflow design topic.** The evidence for rising GenAI document forgery is from anti-fraud practitioners, not audit data, but the standards already ask for authenticity procedures. The practical question is where the firm's workflow depends on a PDF or screenshot that nobody independently confirms.
5. **Small-firm economics may favour buying the layer rather than building it.** The CPA.com data shows smaller practices struggle with budget (53%) and training (66%) as well as data. Vendors in this layer are selling per-engagement time savings, none independently verified.
6. **Independence and concentration.** If platforms are paid for or controlled by the client, or if one vendor sits between many firms and many banks, the AS 2310 intermediary test and SAS 150 intermediary updates become diligence items. Circit says its network includes every top-20 global network (vendor claim).

### 3.2 Interview probes

- Walk me through how the trial balance and general ledger get from the client into your file. Who extracts it, in what format, and how do you know it is complete?
- Of your last ten engagements, how many did you get direct system access to, and how many got Excel exports by email? What decided it?
- What happens when a client uploads a document: who checks it matches the request, and how long does that take?
- Which of your confirmations still go by paper, email or client-held portal? Which counterparties refuse to use a platform?
- Do you test for authenticity of client-supplied PDFs, and does the answer change for documents your AI tools read? Has anyone on the team ever received a document they suspected was generated?
- Who in the firm assessed the controls of the confirmation or data platform you use, and when was it last reviewed?
- Where does client data sit when an AI tool processes it, and have clients objected? (ED-500 flags privacy law limits on sensitive data in tools.)
- If your largest client switched to an agentic close process, how would that change what evidence exists and when?

### 3.3 Objections and evidence-based responses

| Objection | Response from the evidence |
|---|---|
| "Our clients are small and will never give us system access." | The CPA.com survey shows 53% of firms cite lack of access, but it also shows 39% already automate confirmations and 35% trial-balance import, which need less client cooperation (CPACOM-ATR-2025, pp. 7, 9). Source-held channels (bank consent flows) reduce reliance on the client, per the BDO NL case (self-reported). |
| "AI will read whatever the client sends; we don't need integrations." | The IAASB says it may be impossible to evaluate the appropriateness of GenAI tool inputs, and its reliability factors penalise unstructured inputs and data whose integrity may not survive extraction (IAASB-ED500-2026, EM para 68; App. 3 para 6). The FRC's own examples rely on a data-quality gate and a documented extraction-performance check. |
| "Direct bank feeds make confirmations obsolete." | AS 2310 recognises direct access as one way to satisfy the cash and receivables requirement, with auditor control and intermediary due diligence (PCAOB-AS2310-2023, paras .16-.17, .24; App. B). It does not remove the requirement. SAS 150 is not effective until periods ending on or after 15 Dec 2028. |
