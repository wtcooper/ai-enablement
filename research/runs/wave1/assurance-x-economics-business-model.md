# Assurance x economics and business model

- **Archetype:** assurance. External audit and other assurance services (financial statement and ICFR audits, SOC, sustainability and other attest), performed by firms of all sizes; overlapping in-house internal audit.
- **Topic:** economics-business-model. Audit firm fee levels and pressure, pricing models, margins and realization, the leverage pyramid and partner economics, private-equity investment and consolidation, AI investment, AI-native entrants, and implications for in-house internal audit budgets.
- **Prepared:** 2026-10-16. **Landscape:** `research/runs/wave1/landscape-assurance.md`.

**How to read the evidence.**
- Source refs use `PUBLISHER-SHORT-YYYY`. Refs from the landscape are reused where the same document was opened here. New refs are added in Section 5.
- A locator follows each ref in square brackets, for example `[EY-REV-2026, "Assurance" paragraph]`.
- Access is `full` (page or PDF opened and read), `full-mirror` (read through a text mirror of the same document), `partial` (opened but paywalled or truncated), or `search-only` (seen only in search results, not opened). Search-only items carry a "(search-only)" tag in the text and are never used as sole support for a figure.
- Facts are separated from forecasts. Forecasts are labelled with who made them.
- Three kinds of material are weaker than the rest and are marked where used: trade-press retellings of paywalled reports (the Financial Times on KPMG's own audit fee; the Wall Street Journal on private-equity deal values), vendor publications, and commentary pieces.
- Where firm figures are in local currency or US dollars, the source's basis is stated. Percentages computed here from source figures are marked "(computed)".

## 1. Summary

- **Audit revenue is still growing at the large firms; the fee-pressure evidence is anecdotal so far.**
  - KPMG reported audit revenue up 6.0% in local currency to US$14.1bn for the year ended 30 Sep 2025 [KPMG-REV-2025, functions table].
  - Deloitte reported audit and assurance growth of 5% in local currency for the year ended 31 May 2026 [CPAPA-DELREV-2026, service-line paragraph].
  - EY reported assurance up 3.0% in local currency to US$18.9bn for the year ended 30 Jun 2026 [EY-REV-2026, "Assurance" sentence].
  - PwC's UK, Middle East and Channel Islands group reported audit revenue up 1% to £1.483bn for the year ended 30 Jun 2026 [PWCUK-FY26-2026, group line-of-service paragraph].
  - Assurance was the slowest-growing service line at EY in FY26 [EY-REV-2026, service-line sentence].
- **The best-documented "AI discount" cases are not audit fees.**
  - Reports say KPMG International pressed its own auditor, Grant Thornton UK, for a lower fee citing AI. The reported fee fell 14%, from US$416,000 to US$357,000, but the account comes from secondary retellings of a Financial Times story [TFS-KPMGFEE-2026, opening paragraphs; ONLYCFO-KPMGFEE-2026, "KPMG Inadvertently" section].
  - PwC's chief AI officer said clients asked for "fair share" of AI efficiencies and that gains in systems-integration work were "in large part" showing up as price discounts [BBL-PWCPRICE-2025; SFN-PWCPRICE-2025, paragraphs 3-4]. That is consulting work, not audit.
- **Public-company audit buyers are testing prices.**
  - In the FERF 16th annual survey (95 finance professionals; fieldwork Sep 2025 to Jan 2026), organizations that ran a competitive review reported fee reductions of 25-30% even without switching firms. One-third of respondents said they were considering a change of auditor [FERF-2026, "RFPs deliver results" and "One-Third" sections].
  - The same survey puts the median S&P 500 audit fee at US$7.96m for FY2024 [FERF-2026, "Audit Fees" section].
- **Audit firms still price on time.**
  - In the 2025 AICPA and CPA.com benchmark (54 firms with complete data), 87% of firms track realization and 76% track billable hours, while only 13% track the share of engagements on value-billing agreements (fixed fee billed in advance) [CPACOM-ATR-2025, Theme 4 chart].
  - Firms plan to raise fees: 74% of respondents listed "increasing fees" as an anticipated growth strategy [CPACOM-ATR-2025, growth-strategies chart].
- **Private-equity capital is restructuring the mid-tier, and AI is part of the stated rationale.**
  - Ten of the top 20 US firms were reported to be PE-backed or about to be, after Crowe (KKR; closed 7 Aug 2026) and Eide Bailly (Reverence Capital Partners) [CFOBREW-PE-2026; BIG4AF-CROWE-2026].
  - On 29 Jul 2026 Grant Thornton Advisors (New Mountain-backed) agreed to buy CBIZ in a US$5bn deal including debt [HL-GTCBIZ-2026].
  - The PCAOB's inspections director said in Dec 2025 that over half of 90+ significant PE-related deals since 2020 occurred in 2025, and that inspectors will examine quality control at PE-backed firms [AT-PCAOBAI-2025, "PE warning" section].
- **The leverage pyramid is being squeezed from both ends, but the evidence is firm announcements and press, not audited ratios.**
  - Bloomberg Law reported on 23 Apr 2026 that KPMG would cut its US audit partners by 10% (headline) to raise productivity [BBL-KPMGPARTNERS-2026].
  - UK graduate intake at the Big Four fell between 2023 and 2025 [SFN-GRADCUT-2025; FORTUNE-PWCUK-2025].
  - Deloitte UK's new-joiner count fell from 6,800 (FY2023) to 3,160 (FY2025) [YAHOO-DELUK-2025].
- **Independent evidence that AI changes audit fees is thin and mixed.**
  - A 2026 working paper on US public companies found client AI adoption has no systematic effect on audit fees overall, with process-oriented AI lowering fees [UBC-CLIENTAI-2026].
  - A Chinese A-share study found AI adoption by the audited company lowered audit fees [LAI-2025].
  - A Management Science paper (summarised by its authors) found audit offices that adopted AI increased auditor headcount [ISCA-LAWSHEN-2025].
  - None of these measures firm-side AI use and fees in the same engagements.
- **Internal audit budgets are under strain independent of AI.**
  - In the Internal Audit Foundation's 2026 North American Pulse, 19% of functions reported budget cuts for 2025 (11% in 2024) and 18% reported staff cuts [JOFA-IAPULSE-2026, opening paragraphs; OPTRO-IAFUND-2026].
  - The source attributes no cause to AI. Cuts rose in a year when AI "took more of a foothold" [JOFA-IAPULSE-2026, paragraph 3].

### 2.2 Fee levels and fee pressure

**Fee benchmarks (facts, with survey limits).**
- The FERF 16th annual audit fee report benchmarks FY2024 fee disclosures for about 470 S&P 500 companies: median audit fee US$7.96m, mean US$12.2m, top quartile of companies paying 58.5% of all S&P 500 audit fees [FERF-2026, "Audit Fees" section].
- Fee intensity falls with scale: companies with US$1-5bn revenue paid 0.116% of revenue in audit fees; companies above US$5bn paid 0.057% [FERF-2026, "Audit Fees" section].
- FERF reports that organizations that ran a competitive review, even without switching firms, reported fee reductions of 25-30%. This comes from the 95-respondent survey and is self-reported, not from a count of engagements [FERF-2026, "RFPs deliver results"].
- 51% of FERF respondents had worked with their current auditor for over ten years, and one-third were contemplating a change, citing rising fees, "increasingly transactional" relationships and no proactive strategic dialogue [FERF-2026, "One-Third" section].
- FERF's lead researcher framed the AI question as "where are those savings going?" [FERF-2026, researcher quotation]. Respondents were evenly split on whether auditors' AI had improved audit quality [FERF-2026, "AI in the Audit" section].
- A finance executive's newsletter (opinion, not data) gives private-company rules of thumb: Big Four starting prices of roughly US$100,000 or more, and second- and third-tier firms about 15% cheaper. The author states these are his own general guesses [ONLYCFO-KPMGFEE-2026, "Private Companies" section].

**The KPMG and Grant Thornton UK episode (reported, not verified against the primary).**
- Trade retellings of a Financial Times report from February 2026 say KPMG International paid Grant Thornton UK US$357,000 for its 2025 audit versus US$416,000 for 2024, a cut of about 14%, after KPMG threatened to change auditor and argued that AI should lower the fee [TFS-KPMGFEE-2026, "KPMG asked its auditor" and "And it worked" sections; ONLYCFO-KPMGFEE-2026, opening sections; MYCPE-KPMGFEE-2026, summary paragraph].
- The landscape recorded that the Verdantix commentary carried no fee figures. The figures here come only from the retellings above, which cite the Financial Times and disclosure filings. The original FT piece was paywalled and was not opened (see Gaps).
- Reported reactions are opinion: an industry consultant argued AI should raise quality-based fees, and a newsletter author argued every audit client now has a template for renegotiation [TFS-KPMGFEE-2026, "Paradox" section; ONLYCFO-KPMGFEE-2026, opening section].
- This is a single fee for a small, simple client that is itself an accounting network. It is not evidence of a market-wide price move.

**PwC's reported price cuts (consulting, not audit).**
- Bloomberg reported on 27 Jun 2025 that PwC had cut prices for some services after clients pointed to its AI use. PwC's chief AI officer said gains in systems-integration work of about 30% were "in large part showing up as price discounts", and that the reductions had "plateaued" as PwC shifted to quality and value messaging [BBL-PWCPRICE-2025, opening paragraphs; SFN-PWCPRICE-2025, paragraphs 3-5].
- Neither report names clients or amounts, and neither concerns audit.

**Price rises still dominate planning.**
- The CPA.com benchmark reports median audit-practice revenue growth of 8% among responding firms, and 89% of firms anticipate growth over the next three years; 74% name "increasing fees" among their strategies (multiple answers allowed; 54 firms with complete data) [CPACOM-ATR-2025, growth chart].
- FRC data show average UK PIE audit fees rising 7% in 2024 [FRC-MARKET-2025, paragraph 12]. Neither dataset isolates AI effects.

**Price competition from PE-backed firms (practitioner view).**
- A UK managing partner quoted in Raconteur said some buyout-backed firms undercut fees to win clients (his illustration: a £25,000 quote against a £30,000 market rate), while others prioritise revenue per client and larger accounts [RACONTEUR-PE-2025, "Audit quality at risk" section]. This is one interviewee's view, offered as an example, not measured data.
- The same article says the UK audit watchdog raised its assessment of the risk from private-equity ownership from medium to high [RACONTEUR-PE-2025, "Audit quality at risk" section]. The primary FRC statement was not located.

### 2.3 Audit pricing models: hours, fixed fee, value

- Time-based metrics dominate in the 54-firm benchmark: realization 87%, billable hours 76%, hours 76%, utilization 72%. Leverage is tracked by 33%, audit margin per engagement by 28%, deliverable-based accountability by 22%, and share of engagements on value-billing agreements by 13% [CPACOM-ATR-2025, Theme 4 chart].
- The report says that as automation and AI shorten tasks, "realization, utilization and billable hours are becoming less relevant", and it recommends audit margin per engagement, deliverable-based accountability and client readiness [CPACOM-ATR-2025, Theme 4 text and Step 1 table]. These are the authors' recommendations, not firm behaviour.
- The same report defines audit margin per engagement as revenue minus cost to serve, "independent of the old budget" [CPACOM-ATR-2025, Step 1 table]. Only 28% tracked it.
- 66% of firms tell clients about their technology as a differentiator [CPACOM-ATR-2025, as summarised in the landscape (CPACOM-ATR-2025, S2); the survey chart itself was not re-read for this line].
- The Thomson Reuters Institute's tax and audit action paper (survey of tax and audit professionals) says nearly half feel pressure to generate financial gains from AI and that one-third say their firms have not yet adapted commercial models [TRI-FOP-2026, paragraph beginning "Many tax & audit professionals"]. The paper names three strategic paths (elevate, scale, reimagine), and the first aims to "command premium fees" based on expertise [TRI-FOP-2026, "Finding your strategic path"].
- Firm leaders' public positions on audit pricing are not in the opened sources. The only audit-pricing statements found are the KPMG episode (above) and the retellings' quotations.
- Fieldguide, an AI vendor, markets its audit platform on helping firms with "pricing pressures" and "margins", but the opened vendor material gives no measured pricing result [FIELDGUIDE-2026, CEO quotation; landscape V7]. (The blog title "Pricing Pressures? Here's How AI Can Help" was seen only as a link title in a cached page; the post itself returned 404 when fetched.)

### 2.4 Margins, realization, leverage and partner economics

**Partner pay (UK disclosures; US networks do not publish).**
- PwC UK: distributable profit per partner rose 8% to £935,000 in FY26 (£865,000 in FY25); revenue in the UK grew 2% to £4.365bn [PWCUK-FY26-2026, bullet list].
- PwC UK had reported £862,000 for FY24, down 5% from £906,000, and a prior-year figure of £920,000; it said it was investing over £100m into platforms including AI [CITYAM-PWCUK-2024, paragraphs 3-6].
- EY UK: average distributable profit per partner rose 9% from £723,000 to £787,000 in FY25; profit before tax £679m [EYUK-FY25-2025, "Distributable profit" paragraphs].
- Deloitte UK: profits rose 4% in FY25 and average profit per equity partner rose to £1.05m; it appointed 20 fewer partners [YAHOO-DELUK-2025, final paragraphs].
- Partner profit rose in all three UK firms in the latest year reported while headcount and hiring fell (below). The sources do not attribute partner profit changes to AI.

**The pyramid.**
- UK Big Four graduate and school-leaver schemes were cut between 2023 and 2024-25. Reported figures: KPMG 1,399 to 942 (-29%), Deloitte 1,700 to 1,400 (-18%), EY 1,800 to 1,600 (-11%), PwC 1,600 to 1,500 (-6%) [SFN-GRADCUT-2025, paragraph 4]. The article links the cuts to AI taking over entry-level tasks and to offshoring [SFN-GRADCUT-2025, paragraphs 3 and 8]; its figures come from an unnamed source and were not checked against firm reports.
- PwC UK's senior partner said entry-level numbers were lower "this year" (1,300 versus 1,500, i.e. 200 fewer), citing the wider slowdown in investment, hiring and deal-making. He also said AI is "reshaping roles" but that new tools and skills investment were, "for now", offsetting more serious disruption [FORTUNE-PWCUK-2025, paragraphs 3-7].
- Deloitte UK's number of "new colleagues" fell from 6,800 (FY2023) to 3,160 (FY2025) [YAHOO-DELUK-2025, final paragraphs].
- PwC's global headcount was about 364,000 at FY25 (down about 5,600 on the year per the Financial Times as quoted), and the firm did not restate its 2021 pledge to add 100,000 jobs [CPAPA-PWCREV-2025, headcount paragraphs].
- EY's headcount rose 2.2% to 415,182 in FY26 [CPAPA-EYREV-2026, final paragraph]. KPMG's headcount was 276,030 at 30 Sep 2025 on a continued-operations basis [KPMG-REV-2025, headcount table].
- Bloomberg Law reported on 23 Apr 2026 (headline) that KPMG would cut 10% of its US audit partners to reshape the practice; the partly paywalled text quotes the firm tying the move to "a multi-year strategy to align the size, shape and skills of our team to the power of our audit platform" [BBL-KPMGPARTNERS-2026, headline and first paragraphs]. A search result summary (search-only) puts the US audit practice at about 1,400 partners and managing directors.
- A Bloomberg Government opinion piece (28 Apr 2026) quotes KPMG's audit chief technology officer as saying that within two to three years there will be "next to no human beings" doing routine audit testing, and that he will no longer hire a graduate to create workpapers. It is an opinion column and it does not give the primary source of the quotations [BGOV-KSHAPE-2026, paragraphs 3 and 5].
- A Financial Times analysis reported by Computerworld found nearly 7% of Big Four job postings in 2025 required AI expertise (under 2% in 2022) and auditing roles were just under 3% of postings; one firm noted a posting could apply to several positions [CW-BIG4HIRING-2026, paragraphs 1-3].
- Only one-third of benchmarked US firms track leverage [CPACOM-ATR-2025, Theme 4 chart]. No published leverage ratio (staff per partner) was found for any network (see Gaps).
- Academic counter-evidence: in a Management Science study (authors' summary), US audit offices that adopted AI, identified by AI-skilled hiring, increased auditor headcount relative to non-adopters, concentrated among junior and mid-level auditors, and AI-audited clients had fewer serious restatements [ISCA-LAWSHEN-2025, paragraphs 5-7]. The authors' mechanism is that AI lets offices take on more clients. The summary does not report fee effects.

### 2.5 Private-equity investment and consolidation

**Structure.**
- Non-CPA investors generally cannot own audit practices, so deals use an alternative practice structure: a licensed CPA firm performs attest work and a separate advisory entity, which takes the investment, holds tax, consulting and other non-attest businesses and provides services to the CPA firm under a management services agreement [CPAPA-GTNM-2024, paragraphs on alternative practice structure; BIG4AF-CROWE-2026, "The structure is important"; LINFORD-PE-2026, "The Legal Loophole"].

**Deals (facts as reported).**
- Grant Thornton US sold a stake to a group led by New Mountain Capital, closing 31 May 2024; the Wall Street Journal reported a 60% stake in the US unit focused on the non-audit business, with terms undisclosed by the firm [CPAPA-GTNM-2024, opening paragraphs]. Grant Thornton had been the seventh-largest US firm by revenue; Baker Tilly's earlier deal (about US$1bn for just over 50%, led by Hellman & Friedman and Valeas) was previously the largest [CPAPA-GTNM-MAR-2024, paragraphs 1-4].
- On 29 Jul 2026 Grant Thornton Advisors agreed to acquire CBIZ for US$55 per share in cash, a transaction valued at US$5bn including debt, with New Mountain investing incremental equity; CBIZ's benefits and insurance segment is to be separated into a standalone entity [HL-GTCBIZ-2026, opening paragraphs]. Closing had not been reported at the page date.
- Crowe: KKR's investment closed on 7 Aug 2026 into a new entity, Crowe Advisory LLC, with Crowe LLP remaining the licensed attest firm. The Wall Street Journal reported a majority stake valuing Crowe at nearly US$3bn; Crowe's annual revenue was reported at about US$1.39bn. Price and percentage were not disclosed by the parties [BIG4AF-CROWE-2026, "A Deal Reportedly Worth Nearly $3 Billion"]. This source is a blog retelling the WSJ.
- Eide Bailly (revenue US$840m, valued at about US$1.8bn per the WSJ) sold a majority share to Reverence Capital Partners. Its COO said the firm planned to double revenue in three to four years and that a capital sponsor was the "number one driver" because of technology change [CFOBREW-PE-2026, paragraphs 2-4].
- CFO Brew, summarising the WSJ on 26 Jun 2026, counted ten of the top 20 US firms as owned or soon to be owned by private equity [CFOBREW-PE-2026, opening paragraph]. Both Crowe and Eide Bailly said they wanted capital to invest in AI [CFOBREW-PE-2026, paragraph 4].
- Citrin Cooperman (Blackstone, valuation over US$2bn per a commentary) and others are listed in a compliance firm's blog post. Its claim that over half of the top 30 firms had taken or were discussing PE money is the author's estimate [LINFORD-PE-2026, "The Numbers"].
- Europe: Accountancy Europe's June 2025 information paper reports PE deals in accountancy rising from 10-20 a year before 2022 to over 100 in 2023 and about 200 in 2024. About 60% of deals target accounting, tax and advisory firms and 40% target firms with audit and assurance, and PE investment in audit firms tripled in 2023 and quadrupled in 2024. In the Netherlands, 21% of non-PIE audit firms were PE-backed in early 2025, with a projection of 30% by year-end [ACCTEU-PE-2025, summary paragraphs]. Only the summary page was opened.
- The UK: 27% of the top 60 accountancy firms had taken PE funding, per a survey cited in Raconteur, and 46% of UK firms in that survey were open to PE [RACONTEUR-PE-2025, paragraphs 2 and 5]. The Raconteur article says Grant Thornton UK was acquired for £1.5bn [RACONTEUR-PE-2025, paragraph 1].

**Why firms say they are selling (stated reasons).**
- Capital for technology, acquisitions and succession [AT-PCAOBAI-2025, Botic quotation; CFOBREW-PE-2026, paragraphs 3-4; BIG4AF-CROWE-2026, "Why Does an Accounting Firm Need Private Equity?"].
- Partner liquidity: the Crowe commentary notes traditional partners have no large liquidity event at retirement and that a sale monetises accumulated enterprise value. It adds that the economics for future partners may differ [BIG4AF-CROWE-2026, "The Partnership Model Is Changing"]. This is blog commentary.
- Regulators describe the same drivers. The PCAOB's acting chair said PE capital can finance recruitment, retention, succession and technology including AI, and warned that investor returns and eventual sale could shift incentives "so that profitability outweighs audit quality" [AT-PCAOBAI-2025, Botic quotations].

**Regulatory concerns (facts and positions).**
- PCAOB inspections director Christine Gunia (Dec 2025): "more than 90 significant PE-related transactions and firm mergers since 2020", over half of them in 2025. She said PE could pressure profitability, "reduced staffing on audit engagements, fewer specialists", and said inspectors will focus on quality control at firms that have undergone PE or alternative practice structure changes, and on AI, in 2026 [AT-PCAOBAI-2025, "PE warning" section].
- Botic also warned that PE roll-ups could reduce the number of firms auditing public companies and concentrate market power [AT-PCAOBAI-2025, Botic quotations]. A Deloitte DART summary lists PE investment and independence among inspection focus areas [DART-SECPCAOB-2025, PCAOB staff panel paragraph].
- AICPA's Professional Ethics Executive Committee voted on 19 Dec 2025 to seek comment on alternative-practice-structure revisions (significant influence versus control; network-firm definitions); comments closed 30 Apr 2026, per the commentary [LINFORD-PE-2026, "AICPA Finally Steps In"]. Later PEEC steps are in the landscape and were not re-opened here (JOFA-PEEC-2026).
- ICAS called for a multi-stakeholder review of audit-firm ownership rules in the UK, saying the priority is audit quality and independence "regardless of where investment in audit firms comes from" [ACCOUNTANT-ICAS-2025, opening and recommendation paragraphs].
- The FRC's December 2025 market update says access to private capital "could, in the right circumstances" benefit the UK audit market, and that it is monitoring developments [FRC-MARKET-2025, paragraph 37].
- Evidence on PE effects on fees or quality is not yet available in the opened sources. A search result (search-only) mentions a University of Amsterdam project on PE and audit quality and fees. A 2024 European Accounting Review study (search-only) found more profitable audit firms deliver higher audit quality.

- The scale of the mid-tier AI commitments (RSM's US$1bn against a combined US$5bn revenue after its planned merger) is large relative to firm size [RSM-1B-2025, opening paragraphs]. This is a computed observation only (US$1bn over three years against US$5bn annual revenue), not a statement the source makes.
- In the benchmark survey of smaller firms, the top blockers were staff not trained (66%), poor client data (60%), no access to client systems (53%) and budget (53%) [CPACOM-ATR-2025, as summarised in the landscape (S2)]. Smaller firms cannot match network-level budgets.

### 2.7 AI-native entrants and vendors (capital and structure)

- Modus: announced US$85m of seed and Series A funding led by Lightspeed (April 2026). It describes itself as an AI-native audit technology platform and holding company that invests in accounting firms. It said it had already invested in a top-200 firm with more than US$30m revenue and expected its platform to "more than double" that firm's organic growth rate in 2026 (a company expectation) [MODUS-2026, paragraphs 1-4].
- Repodo (Denmark): founders of the bank Lunar raised €8.2m (US$9.6m) to launch an AI-powered audit firm targeting small and medium companies first [REPODO-2026, paragraphs 1-2]. The report notes auditing has been slow to change partly because regulation requires qualified auditors to keep responsibility [REPODO-2026, paragraph 2].
- Fieldguide: US$75m Series C led by Goldman Sachs Alternatives' growth equity group, valuing the company at US$700m, total funding US$125m. The company says its software is used by about half of the top 100 US firms (company claim) [FIELDGUIDE-2026, paragraph 1; search summary for the "half" claim is search-only].
- Fieldguide's CEO said agents are "in production in live engagements" and that it is making a "big push" into financial audit [FIELDGUIDE-2026, CEO quotation].
- Modus and Repodo are prototypes of two different models: a software-plus-roll-up holding company working through alternative practice structures, and a greenfield AI-built audit firm. Neither source gives pricing, margins or audit revenue.
- No published price list or fee comparison for any AI-native assurance provider was found.
- The Grant Thornton platform (Gtap) is a firm-built example: US audit launch described as private-company audits first, public companies "next year", within a platform of nearly 25,000 people operating under an alternative practice structure [GT-GTAP-2026, paragraphs 3-7].
- CFO-side buyers are also targets of the vendors' marketing: a newsletter author, a finance executive, predicts AI-first mid-tier firms will "significantly cut fees" and that Big Four trust gives them pricing power [ONLYCFO-KPMGFEE-2026, "Big 4 Pricing Power"]. This is an opinion and a forecast by that author.

### 2.8 What the research says about AI and audit fees

- Client-side AI: a 2026 working paper (under review; listed by its author's blog on 21 Apr 2026) uses US 10-K text for 2010-2022 and finds client AI adoption "does not lead to systematic changes in audit fees", consistent with offsetting efficiency and risk effects. Process-oriented AI adoption leads to lower fees; product-oriented AI raises monitoring and subsequent restatements (not material misstatements) [UBC-CLIENTAI-2026, abstract].
- A study of Chinese A-share firms for 2011-2022 found corporate AI adoption significantly reduces audit fees, more so in state-owned enterprises; the proposed mechanism is reduced information asymmetry [LAI-2025, abstract]. Differences in market and measurement mean results may not transfer.
- Auditor-side AI: the Management Science summary reports headcount growth, better going-concern and internal-control assessments and fewer restatements for AI-adopting offices. Fees are not reported in the opened text [ISCA-LAWSHEN-2025, paragraphs 5-8].
- No opened study measures audit-firm pricing change caused by the firm's own AI use. This is the central evidence gap.

### 2.9 Implications for in-house internal audit budgets (evidence)

- Funding trend: the Internal Audit Foundation's 2026 North American Pulse (373 leaders, per the article) reports 19% of functions had budget cuts in 2025 (11% in 2024), 18% staff cuts (11% in 2024), 23% budget increases (34% in 2024) and 45% saying funding was "mostly" or "completely" sufficient (53% in 2024). The levels were described as rivalling the 2008-09 financial crisis [JOFA-IAPULSE-2026, opening paragraphs; OPTRO-IAFUND-2026, third paragraph].
- The JofA article notes that 59% of leaders who considered the function fully or almost fully aligned with strategy said funding was mostly or completely sufficient, against 29% of those only somewhat aligned. Financial services functions had fewer cuts (9%) than privately held non-financial companies (28%) [JOFA-IAPULSE-2026, paragraphs 4-8].
- A vendor poll (Optro, April 2026, over 200 leaders) found 64% reported current-year budgets decreased or flat; staffing took the biggest hit among those facing cuts (20%), then co-sourcing and specialist support (17%). Functions reporting administratively to the CFO were more likely to have had cuts (27%) than those reporting to the CEO (16%) [OPTRO-IAFUND-2026, paragraphs 3-8]. Optro sells audit software; the figures are directional. The page is undated; its metadata is June 2026.
- Only 26% of CAEs said they had full autonomy over how their budget is spent, and 36% said the CFO had final approval of the budget (same Optro poll) [OPTRO-IAFUND-2026, paragraphs 4-5].
- Co-sourcing is common: in the 2024 IIA Pulse (survey fielded Nov-Dec 2023), about 60% of functions used outsourcing or co-sourcing, 32% of the smallest functions and 74% of the largest, mostly for technology-related work [IIA-PULSE-2024, executive summary].
- The 2024 Pulse said budget increases outpaced decreases (36% versus 13%) before the 2025 turn [IIA-PULSE-2024, executive summary].
- No independent evidence was found that Big Four or mid-tier co-sourcing rates are falling because of AI. A vendor blog (DSG.ai, which sells a competing model) asserts that firms' AI cost savings "have not yet" been passed on to co-sourcing buyers and quotes Big Four co-sourcing rate ranges; the page contains date errors elsewhere, and its figures are not used here [DSG-BIG4IA-2026, "Model 2" section].
- The FERF finding that audit clients who competitively tendered obtained 25-30% lower fees [FERF-2026] concerns external audit buyers, not internal audit, but it shows how a CFO who sees an auditor's AI claims is likely to behave when reviewing the internal audit co-source budget.
- The Thomson Reuters Institute reports that over a quarter of tax and audit professionals would not take a job at a firm without professional-grade AI and almost one in three would consider leaving if AI expectations were unmet within two years [TRI-FOP-2026, paragraph 3]. Applied to internal audit, this is a talent-cost signal, not a finding about internal audit.

## 3. What it means for an AI transformation conversation

### 3.1 Implications

1. **Separate the three economic questions.** For an assurance leader (external or internal), AI touches (a) unit cost of delivery, (b) price realised, and (c) capital structure. The opened evidence is strongest on (c) (PE deals), moderate on (a) (announced investments, headcount moves) and weakest on (b) (price realised), where there is one disputed anecdote.
2. **Do not assume AI savings reach the fee line soon.** Large-firm audit revenue grew 1-6% in the latest reported years [KPMG-REV-2025; CPAPA-DELREV-2026; EY-REV-2026; PWCUK-FY26-2026], and 74% of benchmarked firms plan fee increases [CPACOM-ATR-2025]. But buyers are reading the same announcements: FERF shows CFOs asking "where are those savings going?" [FERF-2026]. The likelier near-term channel is negotiation and tendering, not a published price cut.
3. **Time-based economics are the binding constraint.** With 87% tracking realization and 13% on value billing [CPACOM-ATR-2025], firms whose model rewards hours face a direct conflict with efficiency. A leader who adopts AI without changing how the practice is priced and measured will either hide the savings (and risk client pushback) or give them away (and compress margin).
4. **The pyramid changes shape before the fee does.** The visible moves are on inputs: graduate intake, partner counts, offshoring [SFN-GRADCUT-2025; BBL-KPMGPARTNERS-2026]. The regulator raised the apprenticeship question directly: where do future auditors build skepticism if routine work is automated [AT-PCAOBAI-2025, Botic quotation]? A transformation plan needs a pipeline answer, not only a tooling answer.
5. **Capital is the scarce input for mid-size firms.** Network commitments run to billions (KPMG US$4.2bn, Deloitte more than US$3bn); mid-tier commitments are US$1bn-scale [KPMG-REV-2025; CPAPA-DELREV-2026; RSM-1B-2025]. The firms that took PE capital named AI as part of the reason [CFOBREW-PE-2026]. A small practice that is not funded this way should decide which AI capabilities to buy from vendors versus build [CPACOM-ATR-2025 shows 66% cite untrained staff as a blocker].
6. **PE brings a return requirement that regulators are watching.** PCAOB inspectors say they will test quality control at PE-backed firms [AT-PCAOBAI-2025]. For a client or audit committee, firm ownership is now a due-diligence topic alongside AI tooling.
7. **For in-house internal audit, the budget story is mostly about funding sufficiency and CFO control, not AI substitution.** Budget cuts rose in 2025 [JOFA-IAPULSE-2026], 36% of CAEs report CFO final approval of the budget [OPTRO-IAFUND-2026], and strategically aligned functions report better funding [JOFA-IAPULSE-2026]. AI is more likely to be argued for as capacity within a flat budget than as a reason for a larger one. That is an inference from the survey pattern, not a finding in any source.
8. **External-audit fee changes feed internal audit indirectly.** If external auditors rely more on tools and on client data, client-readiness work (data quality, system access) rises; the benchmark shows poor client data (60%) and no system access (53%) as top blockers [CPACOM-ATR-2025, as summarised in the landscape (S2)]. Internal audit and finance may be asked to carry some of that readiness work. No source measures this transfer.

### 3.2 Interview probes

For a firm leader (partner, practice leader):
1. How is the engagement priced today: hours times rate, fixed fee, or a value agreement? What share of engagements are on fixed or value terms, and has it changed in two years?
2. When a client or a procurement team says "your AI should lower the fee", what do you say, and has any client asked? Has any fee been reduced or any scope traded for savings?
3. Where do hours saved by AI go: lower budgeted hours, more testing at the same hours, or less staff? Who decides, and is it visible in realization and margin?
4. How is the junior and associate pipeline planned if routine testing is automated? What changes in hiring, ratios and the path to partner?
5. What AI spend is capitalised, who funds it (partners, debt, an outside investor), and what return is expected within what period?
6. If you have outside capital or are considering it: what is the structure for the attest entity, how are quality and independence protected, and what do inspectors ask?

For a CAE or head of internal audit:
7. How did your budget and headcount move in the past two years, who has final approval, and does AI feature in the case for or against funding?
8. What share of your plan is co-sourced, and have you reopened the co-source terms since your providers began announcing AI tools? What did you ask for?
9. If AI let you cover more of the audit universe at the same cost, how would you show that to the CFO and audit committee, in coverage, hours or findings?
10. How do you tell what your external auditor's AI use means for the work you are asked to do for them (data, evidence requests, readiness)?

### 3.3 Objections and evidence-based responses

| Objection | Evidence-based response |
|---|---|
| "AI will collapse audit fees; audit firms must cut prices now." | Audit revenue grew at all four networks in the latest years reported (1-6% depending on firm and basis) [KPMG-REV-2025; CPAPA-DELREV-2026; EY-REV-2026; PWCUK-FY26-2026]. The one reported AI-linked audit fee cut is a single small client, from secondary retellings [TFS-KPMGFEE-2026]. Buyer pressure is real (25-30% reductions after tenders in a 95-person survey [FERF-2026]), but it is not shown to be caused by AI. |

| "The billable hour is dead." | In a 54-firm benchmark, 76% still track billable hours and 13% track value-billed engagements [CPACOM-ATR-2025]. Reports of moves away from the hour are commentary and recommendations, not measured behaviour [CPACOM-ATR-2025, Theme 4 text]. |

A budget cut is not evidence of AI-enabled capacity. Only 26% of CAEs have full budget autonomy [OPTRO-IAFUND-2026]. |
| "Our auditor's fee should fall because they use AI." | Possible, and some buyers are acting on it [TFS-KPMGFEE-2026]. Counter-arguments in the same sources: firms have invested heavily and the technology may raise quality [TFS-KPMGFEE-2026, "Paradox" section]. Half the FERF respondents were split on whether AI improved quality [FERF-2026]. Ask the auditor for evidence of changed hours and quality, not a general claim. |

- **Primary Financial Times and Wall Street Journal reports** on the KPMG/Grant Thornton fee episode and on the Crowe, Eide Bailly and Grant Thornton deals were paywalled and not opened. Fee and valuation figures rest on trade retellings. KPMG International's and Grant Thornton UK's filings, which the retellings cite, were not located.
- **No audit-specific evidence on how AI changes pricing.** No survey of audit fee models (hourly versus fixed versus value) beyond the 54-firm CPA.com benchmark. No fee-per-hour trend, no data on scope changes, and no public statement by a Big Four audit leader on audit pricing found. PwC's reported discounts relate to consulting.
- **Margins, realization and leverage.** No published audit margin, realization rate, or staff-per-partner ratio for any network. UK partner pay is available for PwC, EY and Deloitte (latest years differ); US network partner pay and KPMG UK and Deloitte UK FY26 figures were not found. PwC's FY26 global revenue was not found. KPMG's FY26 results are due about December 2026.
- **Audit revenue by service line for PwC and Deloitte globally** was not found in the opened sources; Deloitte's audit-and-assurance dollar figure was not stated.
- **Grant Thornton Advisors, CBIZ and the other PE-backed platforms' deal terms, post-deal fee behaviour and quality outcomes** are not public. No empirical study of PE ownership and audit fees or quality was found (a University of Amsterdam project was only seen in a search result).
- **US deal counts.** The only counts are the PCAOB's (over 90 significant deals since 2020, mostly 2025) and the WSJ-based "ten of the top 20". There is no independent deal tracker in the opened sources. The Accountancy Europe full paper and the FRC's own statement raising PE risk from medium to high were not opened.
- **AI-native entrants.** No pricing, margin, client or audit-revenue disclosures. Oath Verified and other ventures named in the landscape were not opened. Modus' alternative-practice-structure investments are described only in company and press terms.
- **AI investment levels.** All figures are announced commitments, several not AI-specific (KPMG, PwC). BDO's US$1bn is a trade-press mention only. No source reports AI spend by firm per audit or per partner.
- **Internal audit budgets.** The 2026 Internal Audit Foundation Pulse itself was not opened; the figures come from a JofA article and a vendor blog that cite it. No Gartner internal-audit budget data was reachable. No independent evidence that internal audit co-sourcing rates have moved because of AI. Vendor blog (DSG.ai) is not relied on.
