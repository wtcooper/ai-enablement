# Method topic: pe-value-creation

Stream M (method) · Run: wave1 · Written 2026-10-16

**Topic:** How private-equity operating partners and value-creation teams transform an acquired business: investment thesis, outside-in and commercial/operational diagnostics, value creation plans (VCP), value levers and EBITDA bridges, 100-day plans, PMO/value-creation-office cadence and KPI tracking, portfolio playbooks reused across companies, and how PE firms in 2025–2026 build AI into value creation. Translated for a business-unit leader or head of AI transforming a unit top-down.

**Feeds phases:** frame, breakdown-and-rollup, prioritize, business-case, plan-and-control.

**Reading notes.** Refs are `PUBLISHER-SHORT-YYYY` and match the Sources table (§9). A locator follows each ref in brackets. "Self-reported" means the respondent or the owner reported it; "vendor-published" means a seller of related services published it; "independent" means a third party measured it. Almost all AI outcome data in this topic is self-reported or vendor-published. Access values: `full` (page or PDF text read), `abstract-only` (abstract or landing page read), `search-only` (only search-result snippets read; such figures are never used as primary evidence).

## 1. Summary

- **PE's playbook is a closed loop, not a document.** Thesis from diligence → value creation plan (an initiative-by-initiative EBITDA bridge with owners, baselines, dates) → 100-day plan that activates the top levers → standing cadence (weekly early on, monthly or quarterly reviews, board) → mid-hold reset. The loop is the transferable part. [BDEMERSON-2026 §"working VCP"; SBI-2025; ACHARYA-2009 §Table 10 summary; BAIN-MIDYEAR-2026 §principles]
- **The plan starts before the deal closes.** In a 2013 survey of 170+ PE executives, almost 90% of firms used a 100-day plan, and most created it during diligence or right after the acquisition. Interview data on 66 UK deals shows GP time heaviest in diligence (1.44 GP FTE) and falling to 0.41 GP FTE after the first 100 days. For a unit leader: do the diagnostic and draft the plan before asking for the mandate. [GT-2013 p.1–2; ACHARYA-2009 §7.2 Table 10 (4)]
- **Returns now need operations.** Bain's rule of thumb "12 is the new 5": a deal that needed about 5% annual EBITDA growth for 2.5x over five years in the 2010s now needs about 10–12%. Operational lever surveys point the same way (self-reported). The pressure to "show the number" is what makes PE method rigorous about baselines, owners and proof. [BAIN-GPER-2026 §"12 is the new 5"; SIMONK-2025]
- **Academic evidence supports operations as a real driver, with caveats.** Across 701 exits, operational sources were about half of value created (EBITDA growth 37%, free cash flow 10%) versus leverage 31% and multiple effect 18% (an older, mostly pre-2013 sample). Sales growth, not cost cutting, was the largest EBITDA source in a French mid-market study and in PE investors' own survey answers (self-reported). [CAPDYN-2014 Fig.1; UNQUOTE-2016; GOMPERS-2016 §6.3]
- **AI enters the PE method in four places:** (1) diligence (AI disruption/opportunity triage of the target: Bain's revolution/transformation/augmentation sort and five questions); (2) the VCP (AI levers beside pricing, procurement, sales); (3) the operating model (fund-level AI teams and shared playbooks across portfolio companies); (4) the exit story (measured AI proof points, not narrative). [BAIN-DILIGENCE-2025; FTI-PLAYS-2024 §Plays 1–3; BAIN-TECH-2026]
- **Reported AI value is real but thin and mostly self-reported.** 66% of 550+ PE leaders report AI benefits within 12 months (up from 34%), but only 31% call their AI implementation efficient; 24% report revenue growth from AI; 45% still pilot. Vista's own portfolio data shows lower R&D, sales and customer-success staffing intensity in subsets of companies. No independent measurement of AI value in PE portfolios was found. [FTI-VCI-2026; GT-AI-2026; VISTA-2026]
- **What does not transfer:** control of the board, authority to replace management (CEO replaced in 39% of deals in the first 100 days in one sample), management equity stakes, a hard exit date and leverage. A unit leader gets the method, not the forcing functions, so the advisor must help them build substitutes (sponsor, funded targets, incentives, a dated review). [ACHARYA-2009 Table 10 (1),(5); GT-2013 p.6]
- **Biggest practical gap for the advisor:** PE's best-documented AI move is a *portfolio-level diagnostic plus shared playbook plus standard KPIs*. The unit-level equivalent (a heat map of exposure and opportunity across your own sub-units, with common measures) is recommended by practitioners but not yet backed by measured results. [BCPARTNERS-2025; ELIXIRR-2026; SIMONK-2025]

## 2. Core concepts in plain language

**2.1 Investment thesis → value creation plan (VCP).** The thesis says why the asset is worth more under new ownership; the VCP turns it into named initiatives, each with baseline, target, owner and date, reconciled each quarter against the model the deal was priced on. Practitioners describe it as "an EBITDA bridge with names attached." *Knowledge work / AI:* the unit's equivalent is a bridge from today's cost, capacity or margin to a target, built bar by bar from initiatives, not from a top-down percentage. AI initiatives are bars in the same bridge, not a separate programme. [BDEMERSON-2026 §working VCP, §quarterly review]

**2.2 Value levers and the EBITDA bridge.** Equity value change is usually decomposed into EBITDA growth, multiple change and leverage/cash. Research on 701 exits found operational sources about half of value created; multiple expansion was partly market (7 of 18 points) and partly firm-driven (11 of 18). Critics of the standard bridge argue it misattributes (Kroll proposes an alternative; search-only). *Meaning for a unit:* a bridge is a communication device and a discipline, not a causal proof; use it to force specificity and to total the portfolio of initiatives, and be honest about overlap between bars. [CAPDYN-2014 Fig.1, p.3; KROLL-2025 (search-only)]

**2.3 Full-potential and outside-in diagnostics.** Before buying, PE sponsors (and the consultancies that serve them) test where revenue, operational and technology levers could create a step change, using customer research, competitor moves, benchmarks and even working prototypes. Bain's term is "full potential diligence." Bain urges moving "from full-potential diligence to execution on Day 1." *For a unit leader:* run an outside-in view (customers, competitors, what a new entrant could build) alongside the inside-out process map; they find different things. [BAIN-GPER-2026 §new math; BAIN-DILIGENCE-2025 §What type of AI risk]

**2.4 The 100-day plan.** A short, high-intensity plan that converts the highest-conviction VCP levers into workstreams with owners, dollar targets and early wins, and installs the management system (operating reviews, KPI dashboards, forecast protocols, decision rights). One accountable person per plan. The logic: people are most receptive to change right after the deal. *Meaning:* a unit leader's equivalent window is the first quarter after a mandate or budget is granted. [GT-2013 p.1–3; SBI-2025 §100-day]

**2.5 Operating partners and the "value-creation office".** Operating partners (ex-CEOs, COOs, functional experts) either sit in house (KKR Capstone, about 100 full-time operating professionals) or are a network of advisers; they shape the plan in diligence, work with management on implementation (KKR says 70%+ of Capstone's work is that), sit on boards and supervise cadence. No source defined a formal "value-creation office" with stage gates; the closest descriptions are the quarterly value review and weekly early cadence. *Meaning:* the head of AI plays the operating-partner role for a unit: outside-in challenge, plan owner, cadence keeper. [KKR-CAPSTONE §overview; UMBREX-2025 §models, §responsibilities; BDEMERSON-2026]

**2.6 Cadence and KPI tracking.** Evidence on actual cadence: in 66 UK deals, 92% of GPs interacted with the CEO weekly or more in the first 100 days and 68% had at least one board meeting a month (interview data). A 2025 industrials/business-services survey reports value creation plans moving from annual reviews (53% → 42%) to 3–6 month reviews (20% → 39%). Operationally, PE standardizes KPIs so initiatives can be compared across companies. [ACHARYA-2009 Table 10 (4); SIMONK-2025]

**2.7 Portfolio playbooks and cross-company learning.** Leading firms compare initiatives across the portfolio (which move fastest, which lag, which have the biggest impact) and reuse what works; this needs standardized KPIs and consistent reporting. For AI, FTI describes four operating models from fully decentralized to fully centralized and found 40% of PE firms in its survey managing AI at portfolio-company level. Deloitte advises a "fast follower" stance: reuse validated AI use cases, don't scale frontier ones without evidence. *Meaning for a head of AI:* build a use-case library and common measures across sub-units; a use case proven in one team is the cheapest to replicate. [SIMONK-2025; FTI-PLAYS-2024 §Play 2; DELOITTE-2026 p.4]

**2.8 Mid-hold reset.** Bain: the middle of the hold is where value creation gets lost, because the original VCP "runs out of steam" or conditions change; the fix is to re-underwrite and launch a fresh VCP, and to concentrate resources on winners ("more value in turning a 3x deal into a 5x deal than a 1x into 1.5x"). *Meaning:* schedule a re-plan at a fixed point, and be willing to stop work in sub-units that aren't paying back. [BAIN-MIDYEAR-2026 §principles]

**2.9 AI as risk and as lever, both in the plan.** Bain sorts targets into *revolution* (business model at risk; under 10% of 300+ companies in its study), *transformation* (substantial business-model change) and *augmentation* (about half). It asks five questions (model, volumes and pricing, basis of competition, product improvement, cost savings) and says most acquirers report an AI diligence has convinced them to walk away from a deal. *Meaning:* the first frame for a unit is "which parts of this unit does AI threaten, change or merely help?" before any use-case hunt. [BAIN-DILIGENCE-2025 §categories, §five questions]

**2.10 Proof points and the exit story.** Buyers want measured AI traction, not strategy. Bain: the question in diligence is no longer "do they have an AI strategy" but "can they show me the proof points." Software KPIs (ARR, NRR) stop capturing AI impact when AI is priced on usage; Bain recommends separating AI revenue into traditional ML, add-ons and agentic products and tracking AI's variable cost. *Meaning:* design measurement into the plan from day one, with AI-attributable benefits and costs separated from baseline. [BAIN-TECH-2026 §KPIs]

**2.11 What does not transfer from PE to a business unit.** Control and incentives drive much of PE's execution: sponsors own most ordinary equity, management owns about 15% of ordinary equity in the UK sample, CEO turnover is high (69% of deals had a CEO replacement at some point), and there is a dated exit. A unit leader has none of these by default. [ACHARYA-2009 §7.1 and Table 10 (1),(5)]

## 3. Expert moves

Format: **move** — when to use it — what it reveals — source ref [locator]. "BU:" gives the translation for a unit leader or head of AI.

1. **Write the plan as an EBITDA (or capacity/margin) bridge where every bar is an initiative with baseline, target, owner and quarter** — when making the case — reveals which bars are unowned or overlapping, and that "no single bar carries the hold." BU: bridge from today's cost-to-serve or hours to target, one bar per initiative. — BDEMERSON-2026 [§working VCP]
2. **Start the plan in diligence, not after** — at the frame stage, before asking for the mandate — reveals gaps between expectations and operating reality while the data room is open and teams are still receptive. BU: draft the 100-day plan while negotiating scope. — GT-2013 [p.1–2, Exhibits 1–2]; ACHARYA-2009 [§7.2, Table 10 (4)]; BDEMERSON-2026 [§working VCP]
3. **Run a "full potential" pass: revenue levers, operational levers and technology levers, not just the cost case** — frame — reveals upside the deal case misses. BU: score the unit on growth, cost and product, not only automation. — BAIN-GPER-2026 [§new math]
4. **Sort each sub-unit into revolution / transformation / augmentation before designing levers** — frame — reveals which parts need reinvention versus tool adoption (Bain: "each quarter of delay can add up to a full year of catch-up" for transformation). BU: classify each service line or team. — BAIN-DILIGENCE-2025 [§categories]
5. **Ask Bain's five AI questions: will the business model be upended; will volumes and pricing change; will the basis of competition change; what product improvements are possible; are there meaningful cost savings** — frame/diagnose — reveals threat and upside in one pass. BU: swap "business model" for "why this unit exists" and "pricing" for how internal customers are charged. — BAIN-DILIGENCE-2025 [§five questions]
6. **Ask "who benefits from the efficiency: the company or the customer?" for any services line** — frame — reveals whether productivity gains will be competed away through price. BU: do internal clients expect budget cuts if you automate? — BAIN-DILIGENCE-2025 [§Question 2]
7. **Look for many people doing similar knowledge-work tasks** (call centres, developers, sales reps, recruiters, creatives, case managers) — diagnose — reveals where AI cost savings concentrate. — BAIN-DILIGENCE-2025 [§Question 5]
8. **Check the pricing model: per-seat, cost-plus or hours-based revenue is exposed when AI reduces the labour behind it** — frame — reveals revenue-at-risk that a cost-saving plan hides. BU: chargeback and headcount-based budgets. — BAIN-DILIGENCE-2025 [§Question 2]
9. **Build an outside-in prototype of the target's core function** — frame/diagnose — reveals whether the moat sits in code, data, workflow or ecosystem. Reported: Bain uses AI-generated replicas of target software in PE diligence (reported secondhand from FT). BU: prototype your own unit's core deliverable with off-the-shelf AI to see what an entrant could do. — BAIN-DILIGENCE-2025 [§healthcare example]; PEWIRE-BAIN-2026 [full article]
10. **Triage "what could AI take to zero" using three tests: unit-economics compression, moat erosion, new-entrant route** — frame — reveals terminal-risk assets worth board time versus ordinary competitive noise. Practitioner opinion, not measured. — ELIXIRR-2026 [§question 1]
11. **Disrupt-and-opportunity assessment of every sub-sector and portfolio company, then targeted strategies** — diagnose — reveals where AI disruption is rapid versus where AI unlocks immediate value. BC Partners describes doing this across its portfolio. BU: do it across sub-units. — BCPARTNERS-2025 [Q&A, pillar 2]
12. **Ask the diligence question "can they show me the proof points?" instead of "do they have an AI strategy?"** — diagnose and business case — reveals narrative versus numbers. — BAIN-TECH-2026 [§diligence]
13. **Separate AI-attributable revenue and cost into buckets (traditional ML, add-ons, agentic) and track variable costs (hosting, third-party models, fully loaded AI staff)** — business case/plan — reveals whether AI changes cost structure and scales efficiently. BU: keep AI benefits and run costs in their own ledger. — BAIN-TECH-2026 [§KPIs]
14. **Pick the highest-conviction levers for the 100-day plan; give each a workstream, owner and dollar target; name one person ultimately accountable** — plan — reveals whether the unit can deliver the annual plan. — SBI-2025 [§execution]; GT-2013 [p.3 quote, p.6 and Exhibit 8]
15. **Install operating reviews, KPI dashboards, forecast protocols and decision rights inside the first 100 days** — plan-and-control — reveals issues before they become performance risks. — SBI-2025 [§execution]
16. **Weekly contact with the unit's executive owner early on; at least monthly formal review** — plan-and-control — in 66 UK deals (interview data), 92% weekly-or-better CEO contact in the first 100 days and 68% monthly board meetings. BU: weekly with unit head, monthly with the sponsor. — ACHARYA-2009 [Table 10 (4)]
17. **Taper sponsor time deliberately: heavy before and just after the start, lighter later** — plan — reveals realistic load (2.96 FTE in diligence, 0.73 in the first 100 days, 0.41 afterward in the sample). — ACHARYA-2009 [Table 10 (4)]
18. **Quarterly written value review: read the bridge against the underwriting case, initiative by initiative, what landed, slipped, changed, and what the exit model now says** — control — reveals drift early; funds that skip it "discover their bridge for the first time" in a buyer's quality-of-earnings. — BDEMERSON-2026 [§quarterly review]
19. **Shorten the review cycle when the environment shifts** — control — a 2025 survey reports expected shift from annual to 3–6 month VCP reviews (self-reported intentions). — SIMONK-2025 [§dynamic VCP]
20. **Do a mid-hold reset: re-underwrite, launch a fresh plan, and stop spreading effort evenly** — mid-run — reveals which units are worth more investment. — BAIN-MIDYEAR-2026 [§principles]
21. **Compare initiatives across the portfolio: which move fast, which lag, which have the biggest financial impact** — rollup — reveals patterns to double down on; requires standardized KPIs and willingness to be benchmarked. BU: compare AI initiatives across your sub-units on the same measures. — SIMONK-2025 [§portfolio-driven]
22. **Reuse validated use cases first ("fast follower"); don't scale frontier use cases without evidence and agentic governance** — prioritize — reveals cheap wins versus risk. BU: library of proven, owned use cases. — DELOITTE-2026 [p.4]
23. **Choose where AI ownership sits (decentralized, one company as centre of excellence, fund-specific, centralized) and centralize first the policies and performance management; leave local use cases local** — plan — FTI's ordered list starts with risk/security/privacy policies, then performance management, then use-case prioritization. Consultant framework, not tested. — FTI-PLAYS-2024 [§Play 2]
24. **Hire or appoint a fund-level AI lead who builds repeatable playbooks and ties pilots to EBITDA/growth** — org design — practitioner observation (Egon Zehnder); BC Partners has a Head of AI & Digital in portfolio operations; KKR Capstone lists "AI strategy and execution." — EZ-2026 [§portfolio-first]; BCPARTNERS-2025 [Q&A]; KKR-CAPSTONE [§digital value creation]
25. **Put an AI-exposure diagnostic into the 100-day plan and hold the owner accountable for it the way they are for working capital** — plan — practitioner recommendation. — ELIXIRR-2026 [§what the firms getting this right are doing]
26. **Choose AI operating ratios the plan will be judged on** (staff per $10M revenue, accounts per customer-success manager, quota attainment, cycle time) — business case/control — Vista's measurement stack. Provenance: owner-published, subsets of portfolio only. BU: pick two or three unit ratios before starting. — VISTA-2026 [pp.9–12, Exh.3.1–3.2]
27. **Diagnose management capability and decision rights first** — frame — one-third to two-fifths of sample deals replaced the CEO or CFO in the first 100 days; GT-2013 warns that "competent" does not mean "right role." BU: check that the plan's owners hold the authority their bars need. — ACHARYA-2009 [Table 10 (1)]; GT-2013 [p.6]
28. **Ask whether failure risk is controllable** — control — in a 2025 survey, 67% of initiative failures were attributed to controllable reasons (planning, resourcing, management; over-optimistic targets; poor buy-in). BU: pre-mortem on those three. — SIMONK-2025 [§controllable]
29. **Score management practices (monitoring, targets, incentives) as a first-contact diagnostic** — diagnose — the World Management Survey grid scores three areas; PE-owned firms scored better than family or government-owned ones (association, not causation). BU: apply to the unit's own routine. — BLOOM-2012 [§management practices]; BLOOM-2009 [abstract]
30. **Anchor on the quick, visible wins that confirm the strategy** — plan — "even modest" early wins establish credibility with the board/sponsor. — SBI-2025 [§momentum]

## 4. Question bank

Tags: scope **[team / unit / org]**; journey stage **[new / optimize / prove]** borrowed from Deloitte's PE stages (new = start of ownership, optimize = mid-hold, prove = exit). Phases follow the advisor's list.

### Frame

- What is the thesis in one sentence: why is this unit worth more, or worth less, in three years? **[unit, org · new]** [BDEMERSON-2026; BAIN-GPER-2026]
- What would a "12 is the new 5" ask look like here: what improvement rate does the sponsor actually need, versus what has been assumed? **[unit, org · new]** [BAIN-GPER-2026]
- Which sub-units fall in revolution, transformation or augmentation? How do you know: customer research, competitor moves, a prototype? **[unit · new]** [BAIN-DILIGENCE-2025]
- If a well-funded entrant rebuilt your core output with current AI, what would they build and in how long? **[unit · new]** [BAIN-DILIGENCE-2025; PEWIRE-BAIN-2026]
- How is the unit paid for its work (per hour, per seat, per ticket, cost-plus)? What happens to revenue or budget if the work takes half the effort? **[unit, org · new]** [BAIN-DILIGENCE-2025 Q2]
- Who captures the efficiency gain: the unit, its internal clients, or its external customers? **[unit · new]** [BAIN-DILIGENCE-2025 Q2]
- Where are many people doing near-identical knowledge tasks? **[team, unit · new]** [BAIN-DILIGENCE-2025 Q5]
- Is leadership treating AI as a priority with a track record of shipping, or as a topic? **[unit, org · new]** [BAIN-DILIGENCE-2025 specialty-software case]
- Who is the single accountable person, and what authority do they have over hiring, budget and process? **[team, unit · new]** [GT-2013 p.3; ACHARYA-2009]
- What do you have that a PE owner would have and a unit leader usually doesn't (board control, incentives, a dated exit)? What will substitute? **[unit, org · new]** [ACHARYA-2009 Table 10 (5)]

### Breakdown and roll-up

- Can you break the target into initiatives so that each has one owner, a baseline, and a date? Which bars overlap? **[team, unit · new]** [BDEMERSON-2026]
- Which measures are standardized across sub-units, and which does each team define for itself? **[unit, org · optimize]** [SIMONK-2025]
- If you ranked all initiatives across sub-units by speed and impact, what patterns would appear? **[unit, org · optimize]** [SIMONK-2025]
- Which use cases have already been proven in one team and could be reused elsewhere? **[unit, org · optimize]** [DELOITTE-2026 p.4; FTI-PLAYS-2024]
- Who owns AI policy, tooling, data and performance management: central, shared centre of excellence, or each team? **[unit, org · new]** [FTI-PLAYS-2024 Play 2]
- Are your sub-units' systems and data definitions consistent enough to compare? **[unit, org · new]** [SIMONK-2025]
- Which sub-units' AI exposure is different enough to warrant separate plans? **[unit · new]** [BCPARTNERS-2025; BAIN-DILIGENCE-2025]

### Prioritize

- Which three to five levers carry most of the bridge? What is your confidence in each? **[unit · new]** [SBI-2025; BDEMERSON-2026]
- Where would you stop spending: which sub-unit is a 1x-to-1.5x story versus a 3x-to-5x story? **[unit, org · optimize]** [BAIN-MIDYEAR-2026]
- Which initiatives rely on the unit's own data or workflow knowledge (hard to copy) versus a generic tool anyone can buy? **[unit · new]** [ELIXIRR-2026; BAIN-DILIGENCE-2025 Q3]
- Which proposed AI use cases have evidence from elsewhere, and which are frontier? **[team, unit · new]** [DELOITTE-2026 p.4]
- Do you have more initiatives than owners can run? **[unit · optimize]** [BAIN-MIDYEAR-2026 (resources limited)]
- Is the AI lens only cost, or also revenue, product and decision quality? **[unit, org · new]** [BAIN-MIDYEAR-2026; FTI-PLAYS-2024 Play 1]
- Which levers are within management's control versus dependent on market conditions or capital structure? **[unit · new]** [SIMONK-2025]

### Business case

- What are the baselines (last quarter's actual costs, volumes, cycle times), and who signed off? **[team, unit · new]** [DELOITTE-2026 p.7; BDEMERSON-2026]
- Show me the proof points: what has actually changed in a metric, and over what period? **[team, unit · optimize/prove]** [BAIN-TECH-2026]
- Can you separate AI-attributable benefit from the rest, and include run costs (infrastructure, model fees, AI staff)? **[unit · optimize]** [BAIN-TECH-2026]
- What ratio will show the effect (items per FTE, accounts per manager, cycle time), and have you seen it move anywhere else? **[team, unit · new]** [VISTA-2026 pp.9–12]
- What would the next owner or the sponsor's successor need to see to believe this? **[unit, org · prove]** [BAIN-TECH-2026; DELOITTE-2026 p.7]
- If the case includes added revenue, what is the mechanism: new price, new product, share gain? **[unit · new]** [BAIN-DILIGENCE-2025 Q4]
- What does the case assume about hiring or redeploying people, and who owns that decision? **[unit, org · new]** [CPAPA-2026 (role changes in accounting firms)]

### Plan and control

- What are the 100-day workstreams, owners and dollar targets? Which early wins are within reach? **[team, unit · new]** [SBI-2025; GT-2013]
- Who meets whom, how often, with which dashboard? Weekly flash, monthly review, quarterly value review? **[team, unit · new]** [ACHARYA-2009 Table 10 (4); BDEMERSON-2026]
- What is the incentive for the plan's owners, and what does it reward? **[unit, org · new]** [GT-2013 pp.5–7; ACHARYA-2009]
- What is the trigger to re-plan, and who decides? **[unit · optimize]** [BAIN-MIDYEAR-2026]
- How will you know in 60 days that an initiative is off track? **[team · new]** [GT-2013 p.6 (TJC quote)]
- How heavy is the change load on the team? Do they have capacity for the plan? **[team · new]** [GT-2013 p.10]
- What is your AI governance posture: could you show an auditor how AI is controlled within 90 days? **[unit, org · optimize]** [GT-AI-2026]
- Which AI use cases are in production versus pilot, and what is the rule for moving between? **[unit · optimize]** [GT-AI-2026; DELOITTE-2026 p.4]

## 5. Common traps

| # | Trap | Why it happens | Evidence | How to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **A plan without an execution engine.** Strategy is fine; resourcing, ownership and management bandwidth are missing. | Plans are written by deal or strategy teams; delivery is assumed. | In a 2025 survey of deal teams and operating partners (industrials and business services; n not stated), 67% of initiative failures were controllable: planning/resourcing/management, over-optimistic targets, lack of alignment. Self-reported. [SIMONK-2025 §controllable] | Name owners with authority; write the bridge with baselines; run a pre-mortem on the three failure types. |
| 2 | **Optimistic or shaky targets.** | Targets set at the investment case, not from the operating baseline. | Same survey. [SIMONK-2025] | Baseline from last period's actuals; confidence rating per initiative. |
| 3 | **"Competent person = delivered task."** | Assigning by seniority rather than fit. | GT-2013 quotes an advisor calling that assumption "bad" and stressing the right people in the right roles. In the UK deal sample the CEO changed in the first 100 days in 39% and the CFO in 33% of deals. [GT-2013 p.6; ACHARYA-2009 Table 10 (1)] | Assess ownership fit early; make the "accountable person" explicit. |
| 4 | **Bolt-on AI features and point solutions.** | Easier than redesign. | Bain: companies seeing greatest impact "are not simply layering AI tools onto existing processes"; FTI: point solutions have given incremental improvement. Elixirr cites Chegg's 2023 AI study tool as a failed wrapper (company revenue and valuation figures there are unverified). [BAIN-MIDYEAR-2026 §principles; FTI-PLAYS-2024 §Play 1; ELIXIRR-2026 §Chegg] | Redesign the workflow; tie AI to a bar in the bridge. |
| 5 | **Cost-only lens.** | Cost savings are measurable early. | Bain-StepStone GP outlook (via Elixirr, secondhand): GPs' best AI returns in sourcing and diligence; inside portfolios benefits skew to cost savings, and nearly 40% expect no material financial impact in 2026. Bain: AI also accelerates product, sales, pricing and new revenue. [ELIXIRR-2026 §variable underpriced; BAIN-MIDYEAR-2026] | Include growth, product and decision-quality levers in the frame. |
| 6 | **Pilot sprawl.** | Easy to start, hard to scale. | GT: 45% still piloting (11 points above cross-industry). FTI: 31% describe AI implementation as efficient/mostly efficient. Analysys Mason predicts only about 20% of PE portfolio companies will fully succeed in 2026 rollouts (a forecast, not a measurement). [GT-AI-2026; FTI-VCI-2026; ANALYSYS-2025 body vs headline "<25%"] | Fewer, owned use cases; explicit promotion rule from pilot to production. |
| 7 | **No milestones or KPIs for AI.** | AI strategy treated as a theme. | In FTI's survey, 36% of PE firms that have an AI strategy have no specific milestones or KPIs for AI's value-creation impact. [FTI-PLAYS-2024 §Play 3] | Put AI bars in the bridge with owners and dates. |

## 7. Worked examples

### 7.1 Two diligences, two answers (outside-in AI triage)

*Source: Bain, 2025.* A financial sponsor was diligencing an AI-native healthcare company. The Bain team built and tested a prototype "outside in," which convinced the sponsor that the target's technology might more easily be challenged by incumbents or new entrants. In a different diligence on a specialty workflow-software company, the team concluded AI was more opportunity than risk: the product was entrenched in customer workflows with high switching costs and loyal customers, defensible data and workflow moats, a management team treating AI as board-level, and a record of adding AI features customers were starting to pay for. Bain says the outcome is decided by evidence from customers, competitors and prototypes, not by sector label. [BAIN-DILIGENCE-2025 §introduction, §specialty-software case]
*For a unit leader:* sort each service line the same way. A line that is a thin layer on generic tools (the first case) needs reinvention thinking; a line embedded in client workflow with proprietary data (the second) needs adoption and defence. Check with a prototype before promising either story.

### 7.2 Measuring AI across a portfolio the way an owner would

*Source: Vista Equity Partners, July 2026 outlook; all figures owner-reported.* Vista organizes evidence into two dimensions: AI's effect on product innovation and monetization, and AI's effect on internal productivity and operating leverage. For the internal side it tracks ratios per $10M of revenue (R&D FTEs, S&M FTEs), ARR per customer-success manager and expense as a share of revenue, over subsets of portfolio companies that choose to report. Two named examples: AlertMedia built an AI discovery-coaching workflow that scores sales calls and reports a 40% increase in quota attainment; Xactly deployed AI role-play and a value-selling motion and reports a 26% shorter sales cycle. Vista notes metrics are not standardized industry-wide and that companies are encouraged but not obligated to report. [VISTA-2026 pp.3, 9–12; Exh.3.1–3.2]
*For a unit leader:* pick two or three ratios up front (for example, items handled per FTE, cycle time, cost per case), agree the baseline, report them the same way across sub-units, and publish the denominators. The Vista pattern also shows two kinds of gain: capacity (output per head) and quality (better coaching, better win rates), which the advisor should keep separate in the case.

### 7.3 A portfolio-level AI operating model, and what each level owns

*Source: FTI Consulting (published Dec 2024, updated May 2026); consultant framework with client illustrations, not independently tested.* FTI describes four operating models for a PE firm's AI: decentralized, one portfolio company as a centre of excellence, fund-specific, and fully centralized. It lists nine components in the order they should first be consolidated at firm level: policy and guidelines for risk, security, trust and privacy; collaboration with leadership on performance of AI against the investment thesis; monitoring prioritization and investment in use cases; data federation between portfolio companies; general-model use cases; custom or fine-tuned model learnings; compute and infrastructure; shared engineering and partner advisers; and the playbook for workforce change management and adoption. It also reports 40% of PE firms in its survey manage AI at portfolio-company level, and 36% of those with an AI strategy have no milestones or KPIs. [FTI-PLAYS-2024 §Play 2]
*For a unit leader or head of AI:* decide what you centralize first (policy, measurement) and what stays in each team (use-case design), then write that into the plan.

### 7.4 PE roll-ups in a professional-services setting (context, not method)

*Source: Capstone Partners via CPA Practice Advisor, 28 July 2026.* Accounting-firm M&A hit a record 194 transactions in 2025 (+26%), with private equity accounting for 54.8% of deals in the year to date versus 38.9% a year earlier. The report says outside capital increasingly funds transformation, with investors weighting whether firms can use capital to deploy AI tools, automate workflows, strengthen controls and improve service delivery, and says AI is leading firms to reorient manager and director roles toward oversight, advisory engagement and client management. This is trade-press summary of an advisory report; no firm-level value creation results were found. [CPAPA-2026]
*For an advisor:* it shows the pattern PE uses in knowledge work (platform plus add-ons, then AI-enabled delivery change), but there is no outcome data here to cite for method quality.

## 8. Mapping to phases

### Frame
- Use PE's *thesis → full-potential → AI exposure sort* to open: ask why the unit is worth more or less in three years, what the plan must deliver per year ("12 is the new 5" shows how ambition gets set from return maths), and where AI threatens, transforms or augments each sub-unit.
- Surface forcing functions the leader lacks (sponsor, incentives, dates) and plan substitutes.
- Moves: 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 8, 9, 10, 27. Questions: Frame section.

### Breakdown and roll-up
- Decompose the target into initiatives with one owner, baseline and date; total them as a bridge. Roll up across sub-units on standardized KPIs so patterns (fast, slow, high-impact) show.
- Decide which AI capabilities are central (policy, measurement, tooling) and which are local. Re-use proven use cases first.
- Warning: standardizing measures across units is hard and takes governance. Show denominators.
- Moves: 1, 21, 22, 23, 24. Questions: Breakdown and roll-up section. Evidence: SIMONK-2025, FTI-PLAYS-2024, VISTA-2026.

### Prioritize
- Concentrate on the three to five highest-conviction levers; give each a confidence level; stop spreading effort; separate reuse of proven cases from frontier bets; include growth and product levers, not only cost.
- Moves: 14, 20, 22, 25, 28. Evidence: BAIN-MIDYEAR-2026, DELOITTE-2026, SIMONK-2025.

### Business case
- Build the case as a bridge with named initiatives and baselines; separate AI-attributable benefit and run cost; choose operating ratios; prepare proof points the sponsor or successor can check.
- State the method and the uncertainty (bridges are not proof); avoid headline figures from vendor surveys unless dated, sampled and labelled.
- Moves: 1, 12, 13, 26. Evidence: CAPDYN-2014 (and its attribution caveats), BAIN-TECH-2026, VISTA-2026.

### Plan and control
- Convert top levers into a 100-day plan with workstreams, owners, dollar targets, early wins and the management system (reviews, dashboards, forecasts, decision rights). Weekly contact early, monthly review, quarterly written value review, mid-hold reset, incentives.
- Add an AI-exposure diagnostic to the plan and an AI governance check.
- Moves: 14–20, 25, 28, 30. Evidence: ACHARYA-2009, SBI-2025, BDEMERSON-2026, BAIN-MIDYEAR-2026, GT-AI-2026.

## 10. Gaps

- **No independent measurement of AI value creation in PE portfolios.** All AI outcome figures here (FTI, GT, L.E.K., Vista, Accordion/Ramp) are self-reported or vendor-published. Vista's data covers subsets with voluntary reporting. No error-rate, parallel-run or counterfactual evidence found.
- **Blocked or paywalled primary sources.** McKinsey (Bridging PE's value creation gap; Global Private Markets 2026), BCG PE AI report, Alvarez & Marsal North America and Europe 2026 value creation reports, PwC value-creation material, Kroll, Harvard Law forum post on VCP content (a search summary reported strategy-group shares and diminishing returns to plan detail; not read), Kaplan–Strömberg JEP 2009, ILPA pages, PEI survey data, and the FTI full reports. Search-snippet content was not used as evidence for any figure in §6.
- **Blackstone and KKR portfolio AI claims** (LLM spend growth, 300-strong analytics community, AI across 225+ companies) appear only in secondary commentary. No primary transcript passage was read. Excluded from evidence.
- **EQT's AI programme** appears only as an event page and an AI-generated third-party summary (35+ digital/AI experts, systematic measurement). No primary slides or transcript read.
- **"Value-creation office" / PMO structure.** No source defined formal PMO roles, stage gates, or escalation rules for PE value creation. The closest are cadence descriptions (weekly early, monthly review, quarterly value review) from interview data (UK, 1996–2004), a 2025 consultancy note, and practitioner articles. A head of AI building a unit PMO is extrapolating.
- **Academic evidence is old for the method.** Core value-creation decompositions use deals up to 2013 or earlier; the 100-day interview data is from UK deals in 1996–2004; GT's 100-day survey is 2013. None tests AI.
- **Evidence for transfer to non-PE settings** (a business unit inside a corporate, or a professional-services firm) is missing: no source studies whether PE-style plans work without ownership control and equity incentives.
- **Books** were read via publisher or secondary summaries only (Mastering Private Equity, The Private Equity Playbook, Private Equity at Work). No book chapters were read; no claims here depend on them beyond identification of the material.
- **Talks.** No conference transcript or podcast transcript on value-creation cadence was obtained. EQT's Value Creation Day and BC Partners' Q&A are the nearest, and the first is summary only.
- **Dates unresolved:** Grant Thornton 2026 AI Impact Survey month; McKinsey and Kroll publication dates; Heinrich paper issue; Umbrex original sources for the 18%→47% operations-share figures.
