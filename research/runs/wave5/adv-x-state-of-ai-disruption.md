# State of AI and disruption in advisory and outsourced services (cross-cutting)

Archetype: `advisory` (advisory and outsourced-services side of accounting and advisory firms, private middle market). Run date: 2026-10-20. Wave 5. Topic id: `adv-x-state-of-ai-disruption`.

Conventions in this file:
- Source refs are `PUBLISHER-SHORT-YYYY` and reuse the landscape's refs where one existed. New refs follow the same form. Locators are section headings, paragraph positions or table names, because the pages are web articles without page numbers.
- Each entity is tagged: **[top-100/mid-market firm]**, **[Big Four]**, **[PE-backed platform]** (private-equity or venture-backed), **[AI-native provider]**, **[vendor]**, **[body]** (regulator, standard-setter, professional body), **[research]**.
- Every figure says who measured it. "Self-reported" means the named organization reports its own result. Forecasts say who made them.
- Module ids used for routing: client-accounting-services (CAS), risk-controls, transactions, valuation, forensic-disputes, fpa-performance, private-client.
- Tense words are kept as the source has them: "announced", "piloting", "in production", "expects".
- Firm operations (the firm's own pricing, staffing, billing, PE ownership terms) belong to professional-services; attest work to assurance; tax technical work to tax. They appear here only where they set the conditions for advisory work, and are tagged "route".

## 1. Summary

- **Use is near-universal, embedding is not.** Vendor-sponsored practitioner surveys put AI use at 88-98% of firms, but only 30% say AI is embedded as their default (INTUIT-ACCTTECH-2026) and only 21% have an AI policy or strategy (KARBON-AISTATE-2026). On the client side, RSM's middle-market survey finds 86% of AI-using organizations have integrated AI at least partly but only 36% have it fully embedded across core processes (RSM-MMAI-2026). The same gap exists on both sides of the engagement.
- **CAS is the entry point for continuous advisory, and the evidence is practitioner testimony plus trade data.** Withum says it built a $70M CAS business in seven years and that advisory "sells best next to CAS" (ACCTODAY-CASKEYS-2026). Accounting Today and Woodard report CAS margins of 40% or more at some firms (WOODARD-TWR-2025). No independent dataset on whether CAS converts into advisory fees was found.
- **Capital is reshaping who competes.** IFAC counts 177 direct PE investments and 875 roll-up acquisitions in accounting firms in 2015-2025 (CPAPA-IFACPE-2026). CPA Trendlines' tracker follows 525 events and about $49B of transacted enterprise value to May 31, 2026 (CPATRENDLINES-TRACKER-2026). Venture-backed AI-native roll-ups (Current, Multiplier, Modus) are a separate buyer group. Grant Thornton Advisors' announced $5B CBIZ acquisition is explicitly tied to funding AI (GT-CBIZ-2026).
- **Incumbents are partnering with AI-native platforms rather than only competing with them.** CLA (Top 20) announced a co-built, firm-trained model with Digits for its CAS practice (ACCTODAY-CLA-2026). Botkeeper's wind-down in February 2026 shows the vendor layer is fragile (CPAPA-XENDOO-2026).
- **Middle-market demand is real but not yet a measured "AI-enabled advisory" demand.** Clients rate AI-enabled quality as essential (78%) while 6% say most providers deliver it, and about a third plan to reassess providers within 12 months (TR-FOP-2026, self-reported by Thomson Reuters). RSM's survey shows 40% of AI-spending increasers reallocate budget from external consulting (RSM-MMAI-2026): a demand risk for advisory.
- **The junior pipeline is the clearest structural strain.** Stanford/ADP data show hiring of 22-25-year-olds in AI-exposed occupations 19% below trend (STANFORD-CANARIES-2026, not accounting-specific). Big Four UK graduate intakes fell 6-29% from 2023 to 2025 (SCOTFIN-BIG4-2025, Telegraph-sourced). Forvis Mazars warns that thin, AI-made reports and depleted junior ranks will hurt later (FORVIS-QOE-2026). Evidence that firms are abandoning early-career hiring is weak (ICAEW-HOWTO-2026).
- **Structure limits what advisory can be sold to audit clients, and the rule is not final.** Crowe, Grant Thornton and others split attest and non-attest entities (CROWE-KKR-2026). AICPA PEEC was still discussing a second exposure draft in August 2026 (JOFA-PEEC2-2026), and IESBA has a PE workstream (IESBA-PE-2026).
- **The business-model change is mostly forecast.** The common scenario is repricing from hours to value, fixed or subscription fees as AI compresses routine work. Evidence is intent (69% of surveyed tax professionals "anticipate" value-based, hybrid or fixed fees, CPACOM-BLUEJ-2026) and executive comments (BLOOMBERG-PRICING-2026), not disclosed fee data.

### 2.2 AI-native outsourced accounting and finance firms

Reports $428M revenue, 228 partners, over 1,800 employees, 67 offices, No. 28 on the 2026 Top 100, backed by Thrive Holdings. States that AI lets local firms bring "Fortune 500-caliber" advisory to Main Street businesses. Acquired firms keep ownership and local control. Thrive's in-house team works with OpenAI on tools for tasks such as data mapping and memo writing. A Crete-owned firm's president said AI tools saved his team "hundreds of hours every month" in audit testing (REUTERS-CRETE-2025; self-reported; this is attest work, route to assurance). That was the plan as announced; the 2026 article gives no purchase total.
- CPA Trendlines' tracker lists Thrive Capital / Bessemer as the second most active capital backer with 28 tracked deals (Alpine Investors / Ascend is first with 39) (CPATRENDLINES-TRACKER-2026, "Most Active Capital Backers" table).

A Lightspeed partner said the approach works best on small firms, and that a 200-accountant firm is less likely to adopt at a high rate.
- **Modus [AI-native provider].** Formed with $85M seed and Series A capital; announced Apr 7, 2026 and classified by the tracker as a capital event, "AI-native audit" (CPATRENDLINES-TRACKER-2026, April 2026 section). Attest-adjacent; route to assurance.

- **Basis [vendor].** $100M Series B at $1.15B valuation, Feb 24, 2026. - **Atlas [vendor].** $6M seed (Accel, Stellaris), Apr 9, 2026. Positions AI as a "junior accountant" in partner firms, claims "efficiency gains exceeding 5X on targeted workflows" in early deployments (ATLAS-SEED-2026; self-reported, early stage).

### 2.3 Private-equity consolidation and the advisory growth thesis

**Scale (facts, with the measuring body)**
- IFAC research (reported Mar 2026): PE investments touched more than 1,000 accounting firms worldwide in the past decade. 177 direct investments facilitated 875 roll-up acquisitions, a total of 1,052 transactions, 2015-2025. In 2025 each direct investment produced 7.6 additional transactions, a "consolidation index" four times its 2021 level. About 25% of direct PE investment involves assurance practices serving public-interest entities; under 10% for all transactions including roll-ups; more than half of PE transactions do not involve firms offering audit or assurance (CPAPA-IFACPE-2026, paragraphs 3-9).
- CFO Brew restated IFAC's findings and added that 2025 saw the first PE-to-PE "flip" of an accounting firm (Citrin Cooperman), that Baker Tilly became the sixth-largest US firm after acquiring Moss Adams with PE owning just under half of the combined firm, and that Cherry Bekaert's January 2026 Tarsus deal was its 15th since taking PE money in 2022 (CFOBREW-PE-2026, "Zoom out" paragraph). CFO Brew's sentence that about 900 transactions occurred "in 2025" misstates IFAC (875 roll-ups across 2015-2025); use the IFAC-based figures.
- CPA Trendlines (trade research publisher), data through May 31, 2026: 525 tracked events, 473 acquired firms, about 72 platforms, 69 capital sources, $49B transacted enterprise value on about $24B annual revenue and close to 96,000 staff; trailing-12-month deal count peaked at 226 in March, with monthly counts easing in April and May. The tracker says the window is "a floor, not a ceiling" (CPATRENDLINES-TRACKER-2026, opening paragraphs and "deceleration" paragraph). Methodology is the publisher's; counts differ from other trackers.

**What buyers are buying (April-May 2026 tracker narrative)**
- April 2026 deals tilted toward capability acquisition: forensic accounting, federal contracting, cybersecurity, IT transformation, tax advisory inside wealth platforms, and AI-native audit infrastructure. The tracker names EisnerAmper's acquisition of KLG Business Valuators & Forensic Accountants, J.S. Held's acquisition of CMM, LLP, and Sikich's acquisition of Jefferson Wells U.S. (reported $100M, 300+ employees, $76M 2025 US revenue) (CPATRENDLINES-TRACKER-2026, April 2026 section). The tracker reads this as PE-backed platforms buying "defensible advisory niches" tied to litigation, valuation and forensic work. That interpretation is the tracker's.
- All May 2026 deals were acquisitions or mergers; Grant Thornton Advisors bought MCA Connect, a Microsoft consulting firm of about 350 professionals (CPATRENDLINES-TRACKER-2026, May 2026 section).

**The stated thesis from the buyers**
- Grant Thornton Advisors' CEO links the $5B CBIZ acquisition (announced; called "pending" on Sep 2, 2026; close expected Q4 2026) to funding AI and training. He says mid-tier firms use third-party capital and acquisitions to fund and recoup technology costs, and that AI governance advisory demand "already" exists and will grow as regulators give guidance. Listed professional-services shares fell earlier in 2026 on automation fears; the CEO says "the market has it wrong" and cites margin expansion and share gains, both self-reported without figures (GT-CBIZ-2026, paragraphs 1-14).
- Current's CEO frames roll-ups as either "cost extraction on a deadline" (the traditional PE playbook, in his words) or topline growth with a long holding period (ACCTODAY-CURRENT-2026, paragraph 5).
- The Accounting Technology Lab hosts (Randy Johnston, Brian Tankersley) said late-career partners with unfunded buyouts are turning to PE, and that PE then forces technology modernization (CPAPA-TECHLAB-2026, discussion on "AIPE"; podcast Jul 1, 2026; opinion).
- IFAC lists possible risks (higher fees, more investment in advisory than audit) and opportunities (better-resourced, more resilient firms) as hypotheses, not findings (CFOBREW-PE-2026, paragraph 2).

**Independence limits (relevant to which advisory services a PE-backed firm can sell to its own audit clients)**
- Crowe's structure after the KKR close (Aug 7, 2026): Crowe Advisory LLC provides tax, advisory and other non-attest services; Crowe LLP stays a licensed CPA firm for attest. The release footer says some services may not be available to attest clients (CROWE-KKR-2026, release text and legal footer). **[Top 15 firm, PE-backed platform; independence restriction stated.]**
- AICPA PEEC voted Dec 19, 2025 to issue an exposure draft on alternative practice structures that distinguishes "significant influence" from "control" by investors over a non-attest entity and changes the "network firm" definition; comments closed Apr 30, 2026; the draft would be effective one year after adoption (AICPA-PEEC-APS-2025, paragraphs 1-4). On Aug 10, 2026 the Journal of Accountancy reported PEEC would continue discussions at an October interim meeting on a second exposure draft, including a proposed interpretation prohibiting attest-client investment in firms (JOFA-PEEC2-2026, paragraphs 1-3). The October meeting outcome was not found. The rule is therefore not final.
- IESBA launched a PE-in-accounting-firms workstream in March 2026 to assess whether standard-setting on APS is needed, with an update due at its June 2026 meeting, and agreed to develop non-authoritative guidance on technology and AI (IESBA-PE-2026, paragraphs 1-4). The June update was not found.
- Which specific advisory services are restricted for attest clients (for example bookkeeping, valuations, internal-audit outsourcing) was not confirmed against the AICPA, SEC or PCAOB texts in this run; treat as `(unverified)` and confirm with assurance.

**Counter-narratives**
- CPA Trendlines argues PE is not "taking over" the profession: platform firms are rolling up the mid-market with institutional capital (CPATRENDLINES-76X-2026, paywalled; only the free opening was read; opinion).
- Woodard (Apr 2025 podcast on 2025 Top 100 data): growth across the Top 100 fell to 4.89%, with the largest firms slowing "largely due to declines in advisory services", while smaller firms grew 14-15% (WOODARD-TWR-2025, opening paragraphs of the body; 2025 Top 100 data). Advisory is not a one-way growth story.

### 2.4 Middle-market client demand for AI-enabled services

- **Client-side adoption (RSM Middle Market AI Survey 2026).** 1,030 senior leaders in the US (827) and Canada (203), fieldwork Mar 5-16, 2026; middle market defined as $30M-$10B revenue in the US ($30M-$1B in Canada). All respondents use AI (adoption from piloting to full integration), so the headline figures are among AI users, not all middle-market companies. 86% have partially or fully integrated AI; 97% report satisfaction; 54% say AI exceeded ROI expectations; 58% plan to invest $1M or more this fiscal year; 84% expect spending to increase next year; only 36% have AI fully embedded in core processes; 45% focus on clear near-term value and 17% on enterprise transformation. Among those increasing spend, 43% reallocate from BI and analytics, 41% from cybersecurity and 40% from external consulting services. Top inhibitors across all respondents: data quality and availability (34%), security and privacy (30%), legacy integration (28%), talent and skills (28%). 88% expect their workforce size and composition to look fundamentally different within two to three years. RSM sells middle-market consulting, so it has a stake in the "scale AI" framing (RSM-MMAI-2026, introduction page "methodology" and findings paragraphs; CPAPA-RSMMM-2026, paragraphs 2-9). **[Top-10 mid-market firm as survey sponsor.]**
  - Implication to test, not a finding: reallocation "from external consulting" reads as pressure on advisory, but the survey does not say the consulting is replaced by AI or by whom.
- **Large-company finance functions (KPMG).** 1,013 senior finance leaders in 20 countries, March 2026, organizations with revenue of at least $250M ($500M in the US); technology and financial services are 58% of the sample. Active AI use in finance rose from 30% to 75% since 2024; 71% say AI meets or exceeds ROI expectations; 36% name data quality the top barrier; 42% are "fully assurance-ready" and 29% track where AI adoption fails. The same release reports "assurance-ready" organizations show 33% vs 6% error-reduction improvement and 42% vs 14% confidence in scaling AI. The landscape suspected the 42% figure was conflated; the release in fact uses 42% for both the share fully assurance-ready and the scaling-confidence figure of assurance-ready organizations (KPMG-AIFINANCE-2026, key-findings bullets and "assurance-ready" paragraphs). This is a larger-company sample than private mid-market and the survey is the firm's own **[Big Four]**.
- **Clients' view of their providers (Thomson Reuters Institute, Jun 22, 2026).** 78% of corporate clients consider AI-enabled quality improvements very important or essential; 6% say most of their providers deliver it. Within 12 months 32% will reconsider provider relationships, a third of them with more than $1M in annual work at risk; Thomson Reuters applies this to the US legal and CPA markets to produce "up to $143B" in client revenue under reconsideration. The $143B is Thomson Reuters' own extrapolation, covers legal and accounting, and rests on a survey of professionals including in-house respondents (TR-FOP-2026, paragraphs 3-4 and footnote). A "not preparing to reassess" reading is also possible; the survey shows stated intent, not switching.
- **What clients ask their accountants.** Intuit's survey: three in five respondents say clients ask for proof of AI data protection always or frequently; only 33% proactively explain how AI supports their work and 49% address it only when asked. 84% agree strong AI data security is becoming a competitive advantage. 41% name trust and liability the top reason clients will keep paying for human expertise, ahead of complexity management (31%) and empathy (22%) (INTUIT-ACCTTECH-2026, client-trust section; practitioners' perceptions of clients, not client data).
- **Fractional and interim finance.** CFO Dive (Aug 6, 2026) cites Business Talent Group's April 2026 report: interim C-suite requests up 151% since 2021, interim CFO demand up 14% year on year, with controls, accounting and audit skills most in demand. A San Diego fractional CFO says AI has made companies more comfortable with part-time finance leadership and that clients have shown little desire so far to replace him with AI; he bills hourly and sees AI as making relationships stickier (CFODIVE-FRACTIONAL-2026, paragraphs 1-12; one practitioner's experience, not a sample). Relevant to CAS and fpa-performance.
- **Governance demand.** Grant Thornton's CEO says AI governance work is already driving demand (GT-CBIZ-2026, paragraph 3). Grant Thornton LLP launched an Enterprise Assurance Services practice with provisional AIUC-1 accreditation on Sep 23, 2026 (GT-EAS-2026, opening paragraphs). That is assurance sold to non-audit clients; route to assurance and the risk-controls module only where it is advisory.

### 2.5 The junior talent pipeline

- **Labor-market evidence (not accounting-specific).** Stanford Digital Economy Lab, using ADP payroll data through June 2026 (Aug 2026 version): employment of workers aged 22-25 in AI-exposed occupations is 19% below where it would be had it tracked less-exposed peers; no comparable gap for experienced workers; no evidence of economy-wide displacement; the gap works mainly through reduced hiring, and it is concentrated where AI substitutes for rather than complements tasks. The authors call it "early, descriptive" evidence, not causal, and note it attenuates when controlling for education and has some pre-trends (STANFORD-CANARIES-2026, abstract and introduction; search results indicate earlier versions reported 13% in Aug 2025 and 16% in Nov 2025, not re-read). The appendix lists Accountants among the highest-exposure large occupations (Table A.6) and "Accountants and Auditors" among example most-exposed occupations on the automation-usage measure; no accounting-specific estimate was found.
- **Firm intake.** Telegraph-sourced figures (Jun 23, 2025): UK graduate schemes at KPMG fell 29% (1,399 in 2023 to 942), Deloitte 18% (1,700 to 1,400), EY 11% (1,800 to 1,600), PwC 6% (1,600 to 1,500); Indeed data show UK accountancy graduate adverts 44% lower than 2023. The article attributes the cuts to AI taking entry-level tasks and to offshoring and cost cutting (SCOTFIN-BIG4-2025, paragraphs 2-5). UK-only and 2025. **[Big Four.]**
- **Counter-evidence on intent.** ICAEW (Apr 2, 2026): all Big Four plan to hire at least 1,000 junior people this year, EY expecting 1,600; talent advisors say firms recognise they must keep building the pipeline, citing the 2008 experience when closed apprenticeship intake led to management-talent shortages about five years later (ICAEW-HOWTO-2026, paragraphs 2-4). ICAEW's June 2026 piece says it is unclear how much AI contributed to Big Four cuts, once offshoring and cost reduction are factored in (ICAEW-VOICES-2026, paragraph 2).
- **Postings.** A Financial Times analysis, reported by Computerworld (May 19, 2026), found nearly 7% of Big Four job postings in 2025 required AI expertise (under 2% in 2022) versus just under 3% for auditing roles (COMPUTERWORLD-FT-2026, paragraphs 1-2). **[Big Four.]**
- **Small and mid-sized employer view.** BambooHR (Mar 24-Apr 9, 2026, 1,248 US respondents at small and mid-sized businesses plus six years of workforce data): a third of new accounting and finance hires quit within a year; senior-to-entry-level hiring ratio of 3:1. BambooHR's CFO attributes part of the quitting to unclear entry-level remit as seniors use AI tools to do work juniors used to do (CFOBREW-JUNIORS-2026, paragraphs 1-4; the interpretation is the CFO's).
- **Practitioner survey.** Intuit: 77% report at least one hiring struggle (80% in 2025, 94% in 2024); hardest to fill are candidates with 1+ years (46%) and 5+ years (42%); 56% say a new entry-level hire takes six months or more to reach full billable capacity; 38% prioritize strategic judgment and 22% AI fluency; for entry-level signals, technology certifications (53%) and AI, automation and data skills (53%) edge CPA status (50%) (INTUIT-ACCTTECH-2026, hiring section). Karbon: 91% of respondents believe graduates are more likely to join firms using AI (KARBON-AISTATE-2026, "Talent magnet" bullet; a belief, not a hiring measurement).
- **Advisory-specific warning.** Forvis Mazars US (Sep 8, 2026, quality of earnings): firms that over-rely on automation produce "thin and unsupported conclusions" that look professional; "depletion of junior ranks" will create future holes in executive-level insights; AI also increases the prevalence of lower-quality, lower-cost QoE products. It also says an EBITDA adjustment can be worth "many times" the fee (FORVIS-QOE-2026, "talent" and closing paragraphs; firm opinion, no data). **[Top-10 mid-market firm; transactions module.]**
- **Role redesign.** Juniors review AI outputs and spot anomalies instead of keying data, per Robert Half in ICAEW-HOWTO-2026 (paragraphs 8-9); junior accountants interviewed by ICAEW warn that juniors asked to oversee AI too early may approve bad output (ICAEW-VOICES-2026, MacDonald quote). Woodard/Accounting Today: firms' stated problem is shifting from "we can't find people" to "how do we do it with fewer people" (WOODARD-TWR-2025, staffing section, 2025).
- Route: staffing models, offshoring and compensation belong to professional-services.

### 2.6 Business-model scenarios: compliance work becoming continuous advisory

Everything in this section is a scenario or a stated intention unless marked as a fact.

**Facts that anchor the scenarios**
- CAS is defined by the AICPA/CIMA as outsourced or co-sourced financial operations that shift the firm from transactional provider to an embedded, ongoing resource (JOFA-CASTIPS-2025, paragraph 8).
- Withum's route: a very small CAS team seven years ago, now $70M in CAS revenue; advisory including transaction advisory, cybersecurity, risk work, health-care consulting and investment banking "comes out of" CAS; AI used for variance finding and real-time dashboards. Same speaker says "complete automation of the tax process" is coming and warns of larger firms "coming down market" using AI (ACCTODAY-CASKEYS-2026, paragraphs 3-10; firm leader's statements at Accounting Today's Firm Growth Forum, May 2026) **[Top 100 firm; CAS, fpa-performance, transactions, risk-controls.]**
- Intuit survey: manual data cleanup is the top advisory blocker (30%) (INTUIT-ACCTTECH-2026), which is the mechanism by which automating CAS bookkeeping is supposed to free advisory capacity.

## 3. What it means for an AI transformation conversation

### 3.1 Implications for a leader in an advisory or outsourced-services business

1. **Treat "we use AI" as the starting condition, not a position.** If use is near-universal (INTUIT-ACCTTECH-2026, KARBON-AISTATE-2026), the leader's real question is the share of recurring work that runs through repeatable, reviewed AI workflows and whether the data underneath is clean. The surveys name data cleanup and tool integration as the blockers (INTUIT-ACCTTECH-2026; KPMG-AIFINANCE-2026; RSM-MMAI-2026).
2. **CAS is a platform decision as well as a service line.** Choose between buying an AI-native ledger and model (CLA/Digits pattern), building on a general platform (CBIZ/Microsoft pattern), or staying on the existing stack. Vendor failure (Botkeeper) makes exit plans part of the decision.
3. **Capital is a strategic option, and it has independence consequences.** The same capital that funds AI (GT/CBIZ, Crowe/KKR) comes with the APS structure, so the advisory arm can grow while the attest arm limits what it can sell to attest clients. The PEEC rule is still moving.
4. **Clients now ask about AI.** Practitioners report clients asking for proof of AI data protection and only a third of firms volunteering an explanation (INTUIT-ACCTTECH-2026). A one-page statement of tools, review steps and data handling is a low-cost response the surveyed firms say is missing.
5. **Pipeline planning is a client-quality issue as well as an HR one.** The advisory-specific warning is that cheap, AI-made work lacks the experienced judgment that creates value, and that thinning junior ranks removes the people who become reviewers (FORVIS-QOE-2026). The wider data show reduced entry-level hiring in exposed occupations without proof that accounting firms are abandoning intake (STANFORD-CANARIES-2026; ICAEW-HOWTO-2026).
6. **Plan for scenarios rather than a single forecast.** The scenarios in 2.6 are not mutually exclusive: a firm can see compliance fees compress, CAS grow, an AI-native entrant take part of small-client bookkeeping, and platform consolidation, all in one planning period.

### 3.2 Interview probes (ask the leader)

1. "What share of your recurring CAS work is run through a documented AI-assisted workflow with a named reviewer, and what share is individuals using tools on their own?" (separates the 30% embedded from the 54% situational, INTUIT-ACCTTECH-2026.)
2. "Which part of the stack would you have to replace if your ledger or automation vendor failed in a month?" (Botkeeper, CPAPA-XENDOO-2026.)
3. "When a CAS client's books are clean and current, what advisory work have you actually sold to that client in the last year, and at what fee basis?" (tests the "advisory sells best next to CAS" claim, ACCTODAY-CASKEYS-2026.)
4. "Where do your clients, and the CFOs you serve, put their consulting and AI budget today, and do you know whether any of it is moving away from you?" (RSM-MMAI-2026 reallocation; TR-FOP-2026 reconsideration.)
5. "What happens to your first-year hires' work, and who reviews AI output ten years from now?" (STANFORD-CANARIES-2026; FORVIS-QOE-2026; ICAEW-VOICES-2026.)
6. "Does anyone outside your firm hold an equity stake, or do you expect to take outside capital, and which advisory services could you then not sell to audit clients?" (CROWE-KKR-2026; JOFA-PEEC2-2026.)
