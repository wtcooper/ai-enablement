# Independence, pricing and client data in advisory (cross-cutting topic)

Archetype: `advisory` (advisory and outsourced services in accounting and advisory firms, private middle market). Run date: 2026-10-20. Wave 5. Topic id: `independence-pricing`.

Conventions. Source refs follow `PUBLISHER-SHORT-YYYY` and reuse the landscape's refs where they exist (`landscape-advisory.md`). A locator follows each ref in brackets. "Opened" means the page or PDF was fetched and read in this run (directly, through `curl`, or through the r.jina.ai mirror). Vendor, firm and survey figures are self-reported unless marked `independent`. Quotations are under 25 words. `(unverified)` marks a fact resting on one secondary source or on sources that disagree. Forecasts are labelled with who made them.

Routing. The firm's own pricing, staffing, billing and PE ownership go to `professional-services`. Attest independence technical work goes to `assurance`. Tax technical work (Circular 230, section 7216, the AICPA tax-services interpretation) goes to `tax`. They appear below only where they set what an advisory leader can sell or price.

## 1. Summary

- **Independence limits are tied to the client, not the service line.** A firm that audits a client faces service restrictions on that client. Under SEC Rule 2-01(c)(4), these include bookkeeping, financial-information-system design, valuation, internal audit outsourcing, management functions, investment-banking-type services and expert services for advocacy. Most are barred unless the results will not be subject to audit procedures (SEC-RULE2-01-2026). The same advisory service can be sold freely to a non-audit client.
- **No AI-specific independence rule was found.** The AICPA's August 2026 ethics article says existing nonattest and information-systems rules still apply to AI work. It says developing or hosting an AI system for an attest client can raise independence concerns, and tells readers to use the conceptual framework where the Code is silent (JOFA-ETHICSAI-2026). IESBA's 2023 technology revisions already treat hosting or storing an audit client's data as assuming a management responsibility (IESBA-TECH-2023).
- **Contingent and outcome-linked fees are a hard edge for audit clients.** SEC Rule 2-01(c)(5) and PCAOB Rule 3521 bar contingent fees for any service to an issuer audit client (SEC-RULE2-01-2026; PCAOB-RULES3520-2026). The AICPA Code restricts them for firms that perform audits, reviews and certain other engagements (NYSSCPA-CONTFEE-2015). Outcome-based pricing is being promoted in the market without that caveat in most of the material read.
- **Pricing practice is slow to move, while stated intent has moved.** In a BILL/NewtonX survey of more than 200 firm leaders (late 2025), fixed fee (66%) and hourly (60%) still dominated and 68% were ruling out new pricing strategies or undecided (BILL-AIAMBITION-2026). In a Blue J/CPA.com survey of more than 1,000 US tax professionals, 69% anticipated moving to value-based, hybrid or fixed-fee billing (CPACOM-BLUEJ-2026). The JofA states that 90% of CAS practices sell fixed-fee recurring services, without naming a source for the figure (JOFA-CASTIPS-2025).
- **Who keeps AI-driven time savings is an open question that regulators have started to address.** The IRS Office of Professional Responsibility says billing for time not actually spent, or double billing for AI-assisted tasks, may violate Circular 230 section 10.27, depending on the facts. It says cost savings should be passed on openly (IRS-OPR-2026). A named RSM partner posed the same question as a margin-or-price-cut dilemma (BBLAW-PRICING-2026). No firm-level fee, realization or margin data on AI was found.
- **Client-data duties turn mainly on which AI environment is used.** The AICPA Code's confidential client information rule requires asking whether client consent is needed before client data goes into an AI tool (JOFA-ETHICSAI-2026). Vendors state that commercial and enterprise products are not used for model training by default (ANTHROPIC-PRIVACY-2026; MS-COPILOTPRIV-2026), but 34% of professionals in one Thomson Reuters survey use AI their organization has not approved (TR-FOP-2026).
- **Clients are asking about data protection.** In Intuit's 2026 survey of 725 US accounting and bookkeeping professionals, three in five said clients ask for proof of AI data protection always or frequently, yet 33% proactively explain how AI supports their work (INTUIT-ACCTTECH-2026).
- **The structure is moving as fast as the technology.** Crowe closed KKR's investment in its non-attest arm on Aug 7, 2026 (CROWE-KKR-2026). The AICPA ethics committee plans a second exposure draft on alternative practice structures, including a proposed ban on attest-client investment in firms (JOFA-PEEC-APS2-2026). IESBA has opened a PE workstream (IESBA-PE-2026).

## 2. Findings

### 2.1 Which advisory and CAS services are restricted for the firm's own attest clients

**Rule sources and who they cover.** Each regime applies to the firm's own audit or attest clients only. A firm can sell the same service to a client it does not audit.

**What the SEC staff has said about scope.**
- The SEC staff FAQ says there is a rebuttable presumption that a prohibited service will be subject to audit procedures. Materiality of a subsidiary does not overcome that presumption (PWC-SECFAQ-2019 [Q3, issued Aug 13, 2003]). The version read is hosted on PwC Viewpoint, last updated Jun 27, 2019.
- The FAQ also says providing accounting and financial statement templates for the client's use in financial reporting can count as participating closely (PWC-SECFAQ-2019 [Q1, revised 2019]).
- For entities under common control, the staff has not objected to applying the "not subject to audit" exception in a private-equity group context where operations are autonomous (PWC-SECFAQ-2019 [Q5, issued Jun 27, 2019]).
- There is no cure for prohibited services performed in a prior period (PWC-SECFAQ-2019 [Q2]).

**Firm-built software as an analogy, not an AI ruling.**
- The staff FAQ treats a tax-return software module that generates information for tax accruals and disclosures as financial information system design. Licensing it to an audit client is prohibited whether or not it feeds the general ledger (PWC-SECFAQ-2019 [Q9]).
- By contrast, licensing return-preparation software with that single function was treated as a permissible tax service requiring audit committee pre-approval (PWC-SECFAQ-2019 [Q8]).
- Whether a firm-built AI agent that drafts journal entries or variance explanations for an audit client falls under this reasoning has not been addressed in the sources opened. No SEC or PCAOB statement on AI-specific independence was found.

**What AICPA says about AI and independence.**
- The Aug 5, 2026 JofA ethics article says providing AI-related nonattest services, such as developing or hosting an AI system for an attest client, can raise independence concerns. It says the existing nonattest and information-systems rules still apply. Where the Code does not address a situation, members should identify threats, evaluate significance, apply safeguards and document the analysis (JOFA-ETHICSAI-2026 [section "AI services can raise independence concerns for attest clients"]).
- The same article points to an AICPA ethics staff article, "AI through an ethics lens", in the members-only ethics library, which was not opened (JOFA-ETHICSAI-2026 [closing section, via a search summary]).

**Operating practice.** Independence is monitored in systems, not left to engagement teams.
- A 2016 JofA article describes independence monitoring built into client-intake and billing systems. Mayer Hoffman McCann required a client to be entered in the independence system before a client number could be assigned (JOFA-NONATTEST-2016 [section "Client evaluation and documentation"]).
- It describes RSM's annual client acceptance and evaluation process that can trigger national office consultation (JOFA-NONATTEST-2016 [same section]).
- It also describes private-equity relationships as the hardest affiliate monitoring problem, because there may be little public information on the entities and how they are related (JOFA-NONATTEST-2016 [section "Affiliates and network firms"]).
- This is older than the 12-18 month window and is used as an authoritative foundation for how firms operationalise the rules. It predates AI.

### 2.2 Alternative practice structures (briefing note only; owned by professional-services)

- AICPA PEEC voted Dec 19, 2025 to issue the exposure draft "Proposed Revisions Related to Alternative Practice Structures". It would distinguish "significant influence" from "control" by investors over the nonattest entity and revise the "network firm" definition. Comments were due Apr 30, 2026. The draft would take effect one year after adoption (AICPA-APSNEWS-2025 [body]; AICPA-PEEC-APS-2025 [body]).
- After the public comments, PEEC discussed changes in August 2026, including revising the "network firm" definition and adding a new interpretation prohibiting attest client investment in firms. It agreed to continue at an interim October meeting on issuing a second exposure draft (JOFA-PEEC-APS2-2026 [body]). The outcome of that October meeting was not found.
- IESBA launched a workstream on PE investment in accounting firms on Mar 26, 2026 to assess whether standard setting is needed. It also agreed to develop non-authoritative guidance on technology and AI. An update was due at its June 2026 meeting (IESBA-PE-2026 [body]). The June update was not found.
- IESBA staff published "Emerging Technologies: A Characteristics-Based Approach to Ethical Considerations for Professional Accountants" on Jul 15, 2026. It covers AI, among other technologies. The PDF was not opened (IESBA-EMERGING-2026 [publication page]).
- Crowe closed KKR's investment on Aug 7, 2026. Crowe Advisory LLC provides tax, advisory and other non-attest services. Crowe LLP remains a licensed CPA firm providing attest services. Crowe states it practices in an alternative practice structure in accordance with the AICPA Code and notes that some services may not be available to attest clients (CROWE-KKR-2026 [press release body; legal notice]).
- Grant Thornton describes the same split: Grant Thornton LLP (licensed CPA firm, audit and assurance) and Grant Thornton Advisors LLC (not a licensed CPA firm, non-attest only) (GT-EAS-2026 [boilerplate]). Its pending $5B CBIZ acquisition was announced Jul 29, 2026 and is expected to close in Q4 2026 (GT-CBIZ-2026 [body]; announcement date from the landscape).
- In an APS, the attest firm must treat the affiliated nonattest firm's services as its own for independence. Mayer Hoffman McCann's general counsel said it considers CBIZ's services as if it provided them (JOFA-NONATTEST-2016 [section "Affiliates and network firms"]).

**Named-firm statements.**
- Bloomberg Law (Mar 5, 2026) reports that top firms are developing value and outcome-based pricing models as AI tools threaten billable hours. - BST & Co. (Albany, NY; regional firm, ranking not stated in the source): the CAS leader said billable hours per person dropped from about 1,700 a year to 500-600 while CAS revenue grew 47%. - Withum (top-100 firm) says its CAS practice reached $70M revenue in seven years and that "advisory sells best next to CAS" (ACCTODAY-CASKEYS-2026 [body]). - Grant Thornton Advisors' CEO said "we're seeing margin expansion in our business and we're also taking share", and said scale matters because AI investment is expensive (GT-CBIZ-2026 [body]). This is a CEO's statement of a trend with no figure. Dated Sep 2, 2026 per the landscape.

**Margins.**
- The Woodard Report podcast (Apr 30, 2025) says some firms see CAS margins "over 40%" per Accounting Today's report. Joe Woodard said his consulting data shows firms achieving 60-70% gross profit margin. Neither figure is independently audited or tied to a sample in the transcript (WOODARD-TWR-2025 [section "The margins are real"]).
- Karbon's 2026 State of AI report (n≈593 professionals; vendor-run) reports average time savings of 60 minutes a day per employee. Only 21% of firms have an AI policy or strategy (KARBON-AISTATE-2026 [summary and key findings]).
- The best peer-reviewed evidence found is Choi and Xie in the Journal of Accounting Research (June 2026, vol 64, issue 3). Survey of 277 accountants plus field data on 79 SMEs and over 200,000 transaction records: GenAI use is associated with productivity gains, reallocation from routine data entry to communication and quality assurance, more granular ledgers and faster month-end closing. A framed experiment found that relying on non-consensus AI recommendations can increase error risk (STANFORD-JAR-2026 [abstract]). It is about bookkeeping-type work on a platform serving SMEs and does not measure pricing or margin.
- The investment side of the margin equation: KPMG's Fisher said AI investment costs often cannot be attributed to individual projects (BBLAW-PRICING-2026 [section "Pricing Dilemma"]). Grant Thornton's CEO said CBIZ would give flexibility to invest more in technology and AI (GT-CBIZ-2026 [body]). In the same piece he said share prices of listed professional-services firms fell earlier in 2026 on automation fears.
- Client side: in Thomson Reuters's Future of Professionals 2026 (over 1,800 professionals and C-level executives, 60+ countries; vendor-run), 77% of corporate clients say AI-enabled quality from their professional service providers is essential, just 5% say providers deliver it, and 31% will reconsider those relationships within 12 months (TR-FOP-2026 [key findings]). The sample mixes professions, so this is adjacent evidence for accounting and advisory.
- Why clients keep paying: in Intuit's survey, 41% cited trust and liability as the top reason clients will pay for human expertise as AI takes on routine work. Complexity management was next at 31% and empathy 22% (INTUIT-ACCTTECH-2026 [section "Trust and Transparency"]).

**Outcome-based and vendor-side pricing.**
- Digits (AI-native accounting platform; vendor) announced outcome-based pricing for accounting firms on Apr 7, 2026. Firms pay for a client only when 95% or more of that client's transactions are "zero-touch", meaning neither created nor edited by a human accountant before close. The company says one top-400 firm of about $10M revenue with over 100 CAS clients raised automated handling from about 75% to 98% in 2025 (DIGITS-OUTCOME-2026 [body]). All figures are vendor-published. This is the vendor's price to the firm, not the firm's price to its client. It moves automation risk from the firm to the vendor, and the firm's client pricing is a separate decision.
- A Jan 8, 2026 Bloomberg Tax commentary by Smith + Howard's CEO forecasts "advisory work with fees tied to measurable outcomes". It says that for outcome-linked fees to work, firms need clear baselines for each metric, target ranges with floors and ceilings, simple formulas, and data directly from systems. It adds that audit firms cannot accept contingent fees tied to financial results (BBTAX-SH-2026 [sections "Outcome-Based Fees", "Questions of Independence"]). The piece is a forecast by one firm CEO. It says pilot programs exist and names none.
- A vendor-written Thomson Reuters blog describes value pricing as a price set by value to the client and fixed fee as a set price per service, and says fixed-fee structures "don't offer the same margins". It is an explainer, not evidence (TR-VALUEPRICING-undated [sections "What is value-based pricing?", "What is fixed-fee pricing?"]).
- Fractional CFO demand: Business Talent Group (a Heidrick & Struggles company) reports interim C-suite requests up 151% since 2021 and interim CFO demand up 14% year over year (report cited by CFO Dive, Aug 6, 2026). The fractional CFO CFO Dive interviewed charges hourly and sees AI as a way to build stickier relationships (CFODIVE-FRACTIONAL-2026 [body]).

**Rules that constrain pricing.**
- Contingent fees. SEC Rule 2-01(c)(5) and PCAOB Rule 3521 prohibit contingent fees or commissions on any service to an issuer audit client (SEC-RULE2-01-2026 [(c)(5)]; PCAOB-RULES3520-2026 [Rule 3521]). The AICPA Code proscribes contingent fees for services where the member or firm performs an audit or review of a financial statement, certain compilations, or an examination of prospective financial information. Fees set by courts or based on results of judicial proceedings and some IRS-examination fees are not contingent fees (NYSSCPA-CONTFEE-2015 [text on Section 1.510, revised Code effective Dec 15, 2014]). This is a 2015 description of the Code. The current text was not opened.
- Range quotes are not automatically contingent. The NYSSCPA article says a range based on effort rather than result is not a contingent fee (NYSSCPA-CONTFEE-2015 [paragraph beginning "In addition"]).
- Reasonable fees and AI-shortened work. The IRS OPR (Jun 24, 2026) applies Circular 230 section 10.27(a) (no unconscionable fee in a matter before the IRS). It says billing for manual labor or time not actually spent, or double billing for AI-assisted tasks, may violate it, "depending on the facts". It says cost savings should be passed on openly and AI activities disclosed in general or specific terms as needed (IRS-OPR-2026 [Regulatory Framework item 2]). This closes the landscape's `(unverified)` flag on the OPR fee point. The scope is matters before the IRS, so it is routed to `tax` for application.
- Adjacent profession: a third-party summary of ABA Formal Opinion 512 (Jul 29, 2024) says an hourly biller may charge only for time actually spent, so efficiency gains cannot be billed as time worked, and that boilerplate engagement-letter language is not enough for informed consent (EZEL-ABA512-2024 [Q&A on billing; confidentiality section]). It is secondary and is about lawyers. The ABA primary document was blocked. It is cited only as a signal of how a sister profession's ethics body treated the same question.

### 2.4 Client-data confidentiality when AI handles client books and records

**Professional-conduct duties.**
- AICPA. The Code's confidential client information rule (ET 1.700.001) means members should ask before pasting client data into any AI system: is it confidential client information, is consent required, and if so has the client specifically consented. The article warns that some public generative AI tools use inputs to train future models, and says many firms steer staff to approved tools with clear data-handling protections (JOFA-ETHICSAI-2026 [section "AI can create confidentiality risks quickly"]).
- IESBA Code. Confidentiality requires appropriate protection in "collection, use, transfer, storage or retention, dissemination and lawful destruction". The accountant must take reasonable steps so that personnel and those from whom advice and assistance are obtained comply with the duty (IESBA-TECH-2023 [R114.1(d), 114.1 A1]).
- IESBA names the situations in which authorization is sought: where information is used for training purposes, product or technology development, research, or benchmarking. It lists what to communicate, preferably in writing: nature of the information, purpose, who will undertake the activity, and whether the client will be identifiable in the output (IESBA-TECH-2023 [114.3 A3]).

**Statutory and regulatory duties (US).**
- FTC Safeguards Rule (16 CFR Part 314). An accountant or other tax preparation service in the business of completing income tax returns is a "financial institution" under the rule. Firms must oversee service providers by selecting capable ones, requiring safeguards by contract, and periodically assessing them. They must notify the FTC within 30 days of discovering an unencrypted-information breach involving at least 500 consumers (FTC-SAFEGUARDS-2026 [§314.2(h)(2)(viii); §314.4(f); §314.4(j)(1)]). The rule protects "customer information" about individual consumers, so it does not cover business-client data as such (FTC-SAFEGUARDS-2026 [§314.2(c)-(d)]).
- Section 7216. A tax return preparer may not disclose or use tax return information without the taxpayer's signed written consent that names the preparer, the recipient or use, and the information. Consent for disclosure to a preparer outside the US is required before disclosure (TREAS-7216-2008 [§301.7216-3(a)(1), (a)(3)(i)(B)-(E)]). Whether an AI vendor is a "recipient" is not addressed in the text read. Routed to `tax`.
- IRS OPR says GenAI can compromise client privacy when data generated for one client is repurposed to answer an inquiry about another. It says practitioners must "strictly handle all client data using only secure, enterprise-approved AI" and that willful mishandling may lead to Circular 230 discipline (IRS-OPR-2026 [intro; item 6]).

**What vendors state about their own terms.** These are vendor-published, and contracts and plan tiers govern in practice.
- Anthropic: by default it does not use inputs or outputs from commercial products (Claude for Work, the API, Claude Gov) to train its models. Consumer plans are covered on a separate page. Feedback submitted by thumbs up or down can be used and stored for up to five years (ANTHROPIC-PRIVACY-2026 [page dated Aug 18, 2026]).
- Microsoft: prompts, responses and data accessed through Microsoft Graph in Microsoft 365 Copilot are not used to train foundation LLMs. Interaction data is stored and admins can set retention policies with Purview. Copilot services opted out of Azure OpenAI abuse monitoring (MS-COPILOTPRIV-2026 [sections on training, abuse monitoring, stored data]).
- OpenAI's enterprise privacy page returned an access block and was not read.

**Shadow AI and policy gaps (measured).**
- 34% of professionals use AI their organization has not approved, and 41% lack access to tools that meet professional accountability standards (TR-FOP-2026 [key findings]; vendor-run, mixed professions).
- Only 21% of firms have an AI policy or strategy (KARBON-AISTATE-2026 [summary]; n≈593, vendor-run, Karbon-user bias acknowledged in the landscape).
- Data security tops client-facing concerns at 39% (RAMP-CALCPA-2026 [key findings]; Ramp with CalCPA, 400+ California professionals). 56% use GenAI but only 10% have it integrated across operations.
- Three in five say clients ask for proof of AI data protection always or frequently; 33% proactively explain AI use; 49% only when asked; 84% agree strong AI data security is becoming a competitive advantage (INTUIT-ACCTTECH-2026 [section "Trust and Transparency"]). It is Intuit-run, so its respondent pool skews toward its users.
- ACCA's Oct 14, 2026 report (n=1,178, 135 countries): 61% rank AI use as the top ethical challenge, up from 32% in 2024. Confidentiality is among the listed AI-related ethical challenges (ACCA-ETHICS-2026 [key findings]).

**Controls practitioners propose.**
- JofA (Oct 2026) reports a "Baseline Security Checklist" by Sanjay Chadha of SAV Associates, with 12 areas scored to 75 points. It urges enterprise versions of AI tools, which may provide stronger organizational data protections, contractual assurances and administrative controls than consumer versions. It also recommends data classification, least-privilege access for agents, activity monitoring, human approval for higher-risk actions, tested incident response, and vendor risk review including assurance reports (JOFA-CHECKLIST-2026 [sections "AI and vibe coding", "Data classification and governance"]).
- A CPA Practice Advisor piece (Aug 13, 2026, by a Texas A&M faculty member) proposes five written controls before agents run a client close: an agent inventory with a named owner, replayable evidence, risk-scaled human sign-off, reading third-party assurance closely, and monitoring with an exit threshold. It notes that vendor claims such as 90% match rates are marketing until client data reproduces them (CPAPA-AGENTSCLOSE-2026 [five numbered controls]).
- Source conflict on SOC guidance. The Aug 13 piece says the AICPA had not yet published guidance specific to AI use in SOC reporting. JofA reported on Oct 13, 2026 that the AICPA had issued, earlier in 2026, guidance on the effect of service organizations' AI use on SOC 1 and SOC 2 engagements (CPAPA-AGENTSCLOSE-2026 [control 4]; JOFA-PRACTICEAID-2026 [closing paragraph]). The issue date of the SOC guidance was not found. Treat the Aug 13 statement as superseded or inaccurate `(unverified)`.
- New assurance offering. On Oct 19, 2026 the AICPA and the AI Underwriting Company announced a joint CPA-led engagement combining a SOC 2 report with AIUC-1 certification. Multiple CPA firms are already delivering it, and a full launch is expected in early 2027 (JOFA-AIUC-2026 [body]). KPMG's survey found 42% of 1,013 senior finance leaders fully assurance-ready for AI-enabled finance processes, and 29% track where AI adoption fails (KPMG-AIFINANCE-2026 [section "Assurance readiness powers AI performance"]). The release uses 42% for a second measure (confidence in scaling AI among assurance-ready organizations, against 14% for others). This resolves the landscape's `(unverified)` flag: both uses are in KPMG's own release.

**Where data custody meets independence (the firm's own audit clients).**
- IESBA says storing or hosting an audit client's data, being the only access to its information system, taking custody of its records so that its own records are incomplete, and providing back-up or disaster recovery for its data are examples of assuming a management responsibility (IESBA-TECH-2023 [606.3 A1]). The Code also notes that collecting, receiving, transmitting and retaining data provided by an audit client in the course of an audit or to enable a permissible service does not by itself assume management responsibility (IESBA-TECH-2023 [606.3 A2]).
- Reading these with the AICPA's warning about hosting AI systems for attest clients (JOFA-ETHICSAI-2026), a firm that runs a client's books inside a firm-hosted AI environment faces a data-custody question as well as a confidentiality question. This is this report's reading of the cited paragraphs, not an authoritative interpretation. No source tested it for an AI platform.

**Failure example.** The IRS OPR cites a 230-plus-page report that Deloitte Australia prepared for the Australian government, published in July 2025, which contained invented quotes and references. Deloitte Australia reportedly resolved the matter by agreeing to refund part of its fee (IRS-OPR-2026 [fabricated-output paragraph]). CPA Practice Advisor dates the refund agreement October 2025 (CPAPA-AGENTSCLOSE-2026 [control 3]). It is a fee and quality example rather than a confidentiality one.

## 3. What it means for an AI transformation conversation

### 3.1 Implications

1. **Start the advisory conversation with who the client is.** The independence analysis depends on whether the client is also an audit client, whether it is an issuer, and whether it is a PIE under IESBA. A leader who cannot answer "which of our advisory clients are audit clients, and under which regime" is not ready to deploy AI on those clients' books (SEC-RULE2-01-2026; IESBA-TECH-2023).
2. **Treat firm-built or firm-hosted AI as a possible nonattest service in its own right.** The AICPA flags development and hosting of AI for attest clients. The SEC staff's software-licensing FAQ is the closest analogy for agents that generate financial-statement information. No AI-specific ruling exists, so firms need documented conceptual-framework analysis (JOFA-ETHICSAI-2026; PWC-SECFAQ-2019).
3. **Outcome pricing and independence collide in a predictable place.** Outcome-linked fees are widely promoted. They are barred for issuer audit clients and restricted in the AICPA Code for audit and review clients. A firm needs a rule for which clients are eligible before it advertises outcome pricing (BBTAX-SH-2026; SEC-RULE2-01-2026; NYSSCPA-CONTFEE-2015).
4. **Pricing changes lag AI adoption, so the efficiency gain currently lands wherever the firm's contract puts it.** With fixed fees or subscriptions the firm keeps the gain until the client renegotiates. With hourly billing the OPR says the gain should be passed on in tax matters (BILL-AIAMBITION-2026; IRS-OPR-2026). Leaders should decide this deliberately. The RSM partner's framing is a useful way to put the question (BBLAW-PRICING-2026).
5. **Client data handling is both a duty and a selling point.** Clients ask for proof (INTUIT-ACCTTECH-2026). The professional codes require attention to consent and approved tools (JOFA-ETHICSAI-2026; IESBA-TECH-2023). A one-page AI and data statement is the concrete deliverable.
6. **Controls over clients' agents are a service line, and the firm's own audit clients are a restricted market.** Demand signals are strong (KPMG-AIFINANCE-2026; GT-CBIZ-2026 [Peko forecast]). Independence determines who can sell the service to whom.

### 3.2 Interview probes

1. "List your advisory and CAS clients that are also attest clients. For each, which regime applies, and who signs off when a new service is added?" Listen for a system versus memory.
2. "Where does client data go when your people use AI: which tools, which plan tier, who approved them, and what do your engagement letters say about AI?" Compare with the 34% unapproved-use and 21% policy figures.
3. "When AI cuts the time on a recurring deliverable, what happens to the fee, the margin and the staff incentive?" Probe whether the firm has priced any CAS or advisory engagement on value, a fixed fee or a subscription, and whether it can show realization before and after.
4. "Have you built any agent or dashboard you provide to clients? Does any client also receive an audit, review or other attest service from you or an affiliate?" Probe the custody and hosting question.
5. "If a client asked tomorrow for proof of your AI data protection, what would you hand over?" Probe whether the answer is a SOC report, a policy, vendor terms or nothing.
6. "Are you or an affiliate in an alternative practice structure, or considering outside investment? What does your attest entity require of the advisory entity's AI use?" Route detail to `professional-services`.

### 3.3 Objections and evidence-based responses

| Objection | Response from the evidence |
|---|---|
| "Independence rules stop us from selling this to audit clients anyway, so AI changes nothing." | The restrictions follow the services. AI changes which services a firm provides: hosting, agent development and data custody may be new nonattest services (JOFA-ETHICSAI-2026; IESBA-TECH-2023 [606.3 A1]). Most advisory clients are not audit clients, and they are where AI-enabled CAS and advisory are priced and sold (ACCTODAY-CASKEYS-2026). |
| "Clients will keep paying by the hour or by the existing fixed fee, so we do not need to rethink price." | Most firms have not changed pricing (BILL-AIAMBITION-2026). Yet 69% of surveyed tax professionals anticipate a move (CPACOM-BLUEJ-2026), 77% of corporate clients in TR's survey say AI-enabled quality is essential and only 5% say providers deliver it (TR-FOP-2026), and the OPR has put AI-shortened billing in the fee-reasonableness frame for tax matters (IRS-OPR-2026). |
| "AI efficiency is pure margin." | The one measured quantity is vendor-side: Digits prices only on achieved automation (DIGITS-OUTCOME-2026). A firm CEO claims margin expansion without a figure (GT-CBIZ-2026). Named partners describe the margin-versus-price question as unresolved (BBLAW-PRICING-2026), and investment costs are hard to attribute to projects. No independent margin data was found. |
| "Outcome-based pricing is the answer." | It is the direction of vendor and commentary material (BBTAX-SH-2026; DIGITS-OUTCOME-2026). It needs baselines and system data, and it is restricted for audit clients (SEC-RULE2-01-2026 [(c)(5)]). No firm-level result on outcome-based client fees was found. |
| "We use an enterprise AI product, so client data is fine." | Vendors state commercial products are not used for training by default (ANTHROPIC-PRIVACY-2026; MS-COPILOTPRIV-2026). That does not satisfy the Code's consent question, the FTC's service-provider oversight (contract and periodic assessment) or section 7216 consent (JOFA-ETHICSAI-2026; FTC-SAFEGUARDS-2026 [§314.4(f)]; TREAS-7216-2008). It also does not stop unapproved tools being used (TR-FOP-2026). |
| "Clients are not asking about our AI." | Three in five professionals in Intuit's survey say clients ask for proof of AI data protection always or frequently, which is a self-report by accountants about their clients (INTUIT-ACCTTECH-2026). |
| "Faster work means we need fewer controls." | In KPMG's survey, 42% of finance leaders are fully assurance-ready and organizations that are assurance-ready report higher error reduction (33% vs 6%) (KPMG-AIFINANCE-2026). The OPR and the Deloitte Australia episode show the cost of unreviewed output (IRS-OPR-2026). |
