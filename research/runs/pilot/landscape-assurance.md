# Landscape scan: assurance

- **Archetype:** assurance. External audit and other assurance services: independent examination of financial statements and internal control over financial reporting (ICFR) for public and private companies, plus other assurance (SOC reports, sustainability/ESG assurance). Performed by audit firms of all sizes (Big Four, mid-tier, small practices). Also relevant to in-house internal audit functions whose work overlaps (controls testing, risk assessment).
- **Proposed modules:** acceptance & independence · planning & risk assessment · controls testing · substantive testing · specialists (IT audit, valuation) · completion & reporting · quality management · other assurance (SOC, sustainability)
- **Prepared:** 2026-10-16 (pilot wave)
- **Method note:** Every source below was found and summarized through web search result extracts. Page fetches failed for all hosts in this session (see §14 Gaps), so no primary PDF was opened end to end. Dates and figures are as reported in those extracts. Items marked `(unverified)` came from a single secondary source or conflicting sources. Source types use the controlled vocabulary (`conference`, `regulator`, `standard-setter`, `professional-body`, `firm`, `vendor`, `academic`, `survey`, `press`, `podcast`, `community`, `job-posting`).

## 1. Summary

- **The Big Four moved from GenAI assistants (2024) to firm-wide agent rollouts (2025–2026).** KPMG put AI agents into KPMG Clara for about 95,000 auditors in April 2025, including agents for substantive procedures. EY announced multi-agent AI in EY Canvas for about 130,000 assurance staff on 7 April 2026 and targets end-to-end coverage by 2028. Deloitte launched a "connected agentic intelligence" network in Omnia for about 85,000 practitioners on 24 June 2026. PwC said in October 2025 that it expects end-to-end AI-driven audit automation within 2026, with a single global agentic platform about 18 months later. Grant Thornton US (Gtap, May 2026) and RSM US ($1B over three years, June 2025) show the mid-tier following, mostly with commercial tools.
- **Regulators have moved from "spotlights" to principles-based guidance on GenAI and agents.** The UK FRC led: AI-in-audit guidance and a thematic review of tool certification (26 June 2025), then the first regulator guidance on generative and agentic AI (30 March 2026), then an Audit Tech & AI Sandbox (opened 4 June 2026). India's NFRA issued ten principles that cover agentic AI (July 2026). The IAASB's exposure drafts of ISA 330/500/520 (August 2026, comments due 15 December 2026) are explicitly technology-neutral. The PCAOB, under new Chair Demetrios (Jim) Logothetis, asked for public comment on AI in its 2026 strategic-plan and standard-setting requests. Its technology-assisted-analysis amendments take effect for fiscal years beginning on or after 15 December 2025, and QC 1000 takes effect on 15 December 2026. The AICPA published a practice aid, *AI Considerations for Financial Statement Auditors*, on 13 October 2026, and TQA 9561 on AI in SOC examinations on 11 September 2026.
- **Controls testing is the most active venture and product space.** Funding and launches came in a dense run within one year. Fieldguide raised $75M in February 2026, Petual $20M in April and Andera $37M in June. Optro, formerly AuditBoard, bought Midship in May 2026 and extended autonomous testing beyond SOX in October 2026. RSM launched Beacon with Andera in August 2026, and Workiva released testing agents in September 2026. COSO's February 2026 GenAI internal-control guidance makes AI-touched controls a new ICFR testing object.
- **Adoption is broad but shallow.** Gartner (August 2026) found that 93% of audit leaders use some AI but only 38% have an AI strategy. Only 30% use GenAI for audit testing, against 60% for drafting reports. Among US CPA firms, CPA.com (December 2025) found that 75% have no documented transformation plan and nearly a quarter use no AI.
- **Business-model pressure is visible.** In a case reported by the FT (February 2026), KPMG pushed its own auditor to cut its fee about 14% on AI grounds. Big Four UK graduate intakes fell in 2025. An FT analysis of 2025 job listings found more AI postings than audit postings at the Big Four. New services are forming around assurance over AI itself, such as ISO 42001 certification and the CAQ's *Role of the Auditor in AI*.

## 10. Module check

The proposed list follows the ISA and PCAOB engagement lifecycle well, so practitioners will recognise it. Recommended changes, with reasons:

1. **Controls testing: keep it, but define it to cover both the external auditor's work and management's or internal audit's work, and include walkthroughs and ITGCs.** Vendor and firm activity runs across both sides. Andera, Petual, Optro, Workiva and RSM Beacon sell to internal audit and SOX teams. PwC's Advanced Walkthrough Assistant and its planned controls-testing automation, and KPMG's SOX point of view, address the external audit. The work steps are the same: walkthrough, test of design, sample selection, attribute testing, deviation evaluation, workpapers. ITGC testing should sit here, not under "specialists". Microsoft's change-management testing agent, IFIAR's ITGC findings and RSM Beacon's scope all treat ITGCs as controls testing. **Add an explicit sub-topic, "testing AI-enabled controls"** (COSO Feb 2026; KPMG SOX 2030; AICPA practice aid Part 1), because AI inside client processes is becoming a new kind of control to test.
2. **Specialists: narrow it to IT audit specialists for complex IT, data extraction and SOC 1 reliance, plus valuation, actuarial and tax specialists.** Moving ITGCs out keeps this module about using the work of experts, which ties to AS 1105 Appendix A, AS 1210 and ISA 620. Evidence for valuation is thin (IVSC, Commerford et al.).
3. **Add a cross-cutting module or sub-module: "data & evidence acquisition"**, covering requests for client-provided items, ERP data extraction, and confirmations. Many vendors (DataSnipper, Circit, Validis, Inflo, Thomson Reuters Confirmation) and the PCAOB technology-assisted-analysis amendments (reliability of electronic information) sit here. It feeds both controls testing and substantive testing.
4. **Split quality management into (a) firm-level quality management and AI tool governance** (ISQM 1 and QC 1000 technological resources, tool certification, FRC thematic review, IAASB non-authoritative material) **and (b) engagement supervision & review of AI-assisted work** (ISA 220, FRC GenAI guidance "human review of outputs", automation bias research). Practitioners and regulators treat them separately. Most AI risk guidance lands in (b).
5. **Call out journal-entry and fraud testing** inside substantive testing, or in planning & risk assessment. It is the most mature AI use case in audit (the FRC's illustrative example, EY's 1.4 trillion JE lines, MindBridge) and has its own standards (ISA 240, AS 2401).
6. **Split other assurance into three parts: SOC/attestation, sustainability assurance, and assurance over AI systems.** SOC is close to controls testing (TQA 9561; EY SOC polling). Sustainability has its own standard (ISSA 5000, effective Dec 2026) and a different evidence base. Assurance over AI is a new business line: ISO 42001 and 42006 certification, the CAQ's *Role of the Auditor in AI*, ACCA/EY, and ICAEW's AI Assurance Conference.
7. **Acceptance & independence: keep it, but expect it to stay thin.** Little public AI evidence exists beyond Intapp-type tools and IESBA's technology revisions. It could merge with "engagement setup" (planning) if deep dives find little.
8. **For internal audit, add "issue writing and reporting" under completion & reporting.** Gartner's top GenAI use in internal audit is drafting issues and reports (60%). Uniper reported gains on report writing.
